Snorkel over a reef off Tioman or Redang, and the clear water, the protected fish and the "no fishing, no anchoring" rules you were briefed on are not just good manners — they are the law. The statute behind them is the **Fisheries Act 1985**, Act 317 in Malaysia's statute book, and it governs far more than tourism: it is the backbone of how Malaysia manages fishing and protects marine life across its waters.

This guide gives a plain-language overview of what the Act does, who enforces it, and why it matters whether you fish, dive or run a coastal business.

## What the Act is

The Fisheries Act 1985 is Malaysia's **principal federal law on fisheries and marine conservation**. It replaced earlier fisheries legislation and brought the licensing of fishing, the management of fisheries resources, and the protection of marine habitats under a single framework. The Act is organised into several Parts; marine parks and marine reserves are dealt with specifically in **Part IX**.

It is administered by the **Department of Fisheries Malaysia** (Jabatan Perikanan Malaysia), headed by the **Director-General of Fisheries**, who coordinates and supervises fisheries and marine park matters in Malaysian waters.

## Licensing: fishing is a permitted activity, not a free-for-all

A core principle of the Act is that **fishing in Malaysian waters requires a licence**. The Act sets out the machinery for licensing — how licences are issued, refused, suspended, cancelled and validated — and distinguishes between **local** and **foreign** fishing vessels, with stricter controls on foreign vessels operating in Malaysian waters.

This licensing regime is how the government manages fishing effort, protects the livelihoods of licensed local fishers, and keeps unlicensed and foreign vessels out of protected zones.

## Marine parks and reserves

One of the Act's most publicly visible powers is the creation of **marine parks and marine reserves**. Under Part IX, the Minister may establish these protected areas by **gazette order**, with aims that include protecting aquatic life, supporting scientific research, and regulating recreation and other activities to prevent irreversible damage to the marine environment.

In practice, Department of Fisheries materials describe marine park zones as extending generally **two nautical miles** from the lowest sea level around designated islands, with some zoned at one nautical mile. Within these zones, **activities that damage coral reefs and marine ecosystems are prohibited** — which is why anchoring on reefs, collecting coral or shells, and fishing are restricted or banned inside the parks.

Separately, the Minister can restrict fishing in specific locations through the **Fisheries (Prohibited Areas) Regulations**, which have been amended over the years to add or remove named waters.

## Why it matters

The Act touches several groups directly:

- **Fishers and boat operators** need the right licence and must stay out of prohibited and protected zones.
- **Divers, snorkellers and tour operators** are bound by marine park rules — no fishing, no anchoring on reefs, no taking of marine life.
- **Conservationists and researchers** rely on the Act's protected-area powers as the legal basis for Malaysia's marine protected areas.

For the coastal and island context where these rules bite hardest — the east-coast islands and their monsoon-shaped seasons — see the [east coast islands guide](/en/tourism/east-coast-islands-guide/).

## A note on the current text

Legislation changes. The widely circulated English reprint of Act 317 was current as of 1 November 2012, and the Act has been amended since; the Malay-language text is the authoritative version. Before relying on any specific section, licensing category, zone boundary or penalty, confirm the current consolidated Act with the Attorney General's Chambers and the Department of Fisheries (see the verification note on this page).

## What's next

The Fisheries Act 1985 is the quiet legal framework behind both Malaysia's fishing industry and its marine parks — the reason a reef is protected and a trawler is licensed.
