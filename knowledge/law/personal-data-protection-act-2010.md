The **Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709)**, known as the **PDPA**, is Malaysia's principal law for the protection of personal data processed in **commercial transactions**. It was passed by Parliament in 2010 and came into force on **15 November 2013**, making it the country's first comprehensive, cross-sectoral data-protection statute. It is enforced by the **Personal Data Protection Commissioner** through the Personal Data Protection Department (Jabatan Perlindungan Data Peribadi, **JPDP**), based in Putrajaya.

## Who and what the Act covers

The PDPA governs any person who processes — or has control over or authorises the processing of — personal data in respect of commercial transactions. It reaches organisations established in Malaysia and, in defined circumstances, those outside Malaysia that use equipment in Malaysia to process such data.

Two important carve-outs sit in **section 3**:

- It **does not apply to the Federal Government or State Governments**.
- It does not apply to personal data processed by an individual purely for **personal, family or household** affairs, including recreation.

## The seven data-protection principles

**Section 5** requires compliance with seven principles, each fleshed out in later sections of the Act:

| Principle | Core requirement |
|---|---|
| General | Process personal data only with consent and for a lawful, directly related purpose |
| Notice and Choice | Inform individuals in writing of the purposes and their rights |
| Disclosure | Do not disclose data for purposes other than those notified, without consent |
| Security | Take practical steps to protect data from loss, misuse or unauthorised access |
| Retention | Do not keep data longer than necessary for its purpose |
| Data Integrity | Keep data accurate, complete, not misleading and up to date |
| Access | Allow individuals to access and correct their own data |

Failing to comply with the principles is an offence. The **original penalty** was a fine up to **RM300,000** and/or imprisonment up to **2 years**.

## The 2024 amendments

The **Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024** is the most significant overhaul since the Act began, moving Malaysia's framework closer to international standards. Its provisions came into operation in stages during 2025. Key changes include:

- **New terminology:** "data user" is replaced by **"data controller"** throughout the Act.
- **Higher penalties:** breach of the principles now carries a fine up to **RM1,000,000** and/or imprisonment up to **3 years**.
- **Mandatory data-breach notification:** a data controller must notify the Commissioner of a personal-data breach as soon as practicable, and notify affected individuals where the breach is likely to cause significant harm.
- **Mandatory Data Protection Officer (DPO):** data controllers and processors must appoint at least one DPO and notify the Commissioner.
- **Data portability:** individuals may request their data be transmitted to another controller, subject to technical feasibility.
- **Biometric data** is expressly added to the definition of **sensitive personal data**.

## What's next

If you handle personal data in Malaysia, map your processing against the seven principles, appoint a DPO, and put a breach-response procedure in place. For the authoritative text and the latest guidelines, consult the JPDP portal at [pdp.gov.my](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/akta/pdp-act-2010-en/).
