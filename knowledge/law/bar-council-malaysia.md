Malaysia does not have one bar. It has three — one for the Peninsula, one for
Sabah, one for Sarawak — and the body most people mean when they say "the Bar
Council" governs only the first. Getting that geography right is the first thing
this page does.

## One country, three bars

The **Legal Profession Act 1976** created an independent Bar for **Peninsular
Malaysia**, replacing the colonial-era 1947 Ordinance. It does not extend to East
Malaysia. Lawyers there belong to separate bodies under separate legislation:

- **Sabah** — the **Sabah Law Society**;
- **Sarawak** — the **Advocates Association of Sarawak**.

All three appear in the Fourth Schedule to the Companies Act 2016 as approved
bodies, but each governs admission and practice only within its own
jurisdiction. A lawyer admitted in the Peninsula has no automatic right to
practise in Sabah or Sarawak.

## The Malaysian Bar and its Council

Every advocate and solicitor admitted in Peninsular Malaysia becomes a **member
of the Malaysian Bar** automatically. The **Bar Council** is the body the
membership elects to run the profession between the Bar's general meetings. Its
composition is drawn from elected members together with the chairs of the State
Bar Committees and office-bearers such as the President and Vice-President.

The **purposes of the Malaysian Bar**, set out in s.42 of the Act, go beyond
guild self-interest. They include **upholding the cause of justice without regard
to its own interests or those of its members**, maintaining and improving
standards of conduct and learning, representing and protecting the interests of
the profession, and maintaining a Compensation Fund for clients wronged by a
practitioner. That first purpose is why the Malaysian Bar frequently speaks on
rule-of-law and access-to-justice questions.

## Admission, practice and discipline

- **Admission** is governed through the **Qualifying Board**, which sets entry
  qualifications and oversees pupillage (the period of supervised practice before
  full admission).
- **Practice** requires a current **annual practising certificate** — the Sijil
  Annual — without which an admitted lawyer may not practise in that year.
- **Discipline** runs through the **Advocates and Solicitors Disciplinary
  Board**, a body separate from the Bar Council, which investigates complaints and
  imposes sanctions; serious cases can reach the courts.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming the Bar Council governs all Malaysian lawyers.** It covers the
  Peninsula; Sabah and Sarawak have their own bodies.
- **Using "Bar Council" and "Malaysian Bar" interchangeably without care.** The
  Bar is the whole membership; the Council is its elected executive.
- **Thinking admission in one jurisdiction carries to the others.** It does not.
- **Treating the Bar Council as the disciplinary decision-maker.** Formal
  discipline is the Disciplinary Board's function.
