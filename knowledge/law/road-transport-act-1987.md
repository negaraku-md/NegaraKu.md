The **Road Transport Act 1987 (Act 333)** is Malaysia's principal law governing motor vehicles and road traffic. It regulates vehicle registration and licensing, driving licences, periodic vehicle inspection, third-party insurance, and a wide range of traffic offences. The Act is administered and enforced mainly by the **Road Transport Department (Jabatan Pengangkutan Jalan, JPJ)**, and it received Royal Assent on **30 August 1987**.

## What the Act covers

The long title describes an Act to regulate motor vehicles and traffic on roads, to protect third parties against risks arising from the use of motor vehicles, and to co-ordinate and control means and facilities for transport. Its provisions are grouped into five main Parts:

| Part | Subject |
|------|---------|
| Part I | Preliminary (interpretation, application) |
| Part II | Classification, registration and licensing of motor vehicles and drivers (with Parts IIA and IIB on periodic inspection and foreign vehicles) |
| Part III | Roads |
| Part IV | Provisions against third-party risks from motor-vehicle use |
| Part V | Offences and miscellaneous provisions |

## Registration and licensing

The Act makes registration and licensing the foundation of lawful road use. Under **section 26(1)**, no person may drive a motor vehicle on a road unless they hold a driving licence authorising them to drive a vehicle of that class or description. Separately, using a motor vehicle without a valid **motor-vehicle licence (road tax)** is an offence under **section 23**. JPJ issues driving licences and registers vehicles under the framework set by this Act.

## Traffic offences and the 2020 amendments

Part V sets out the core traffic offences. The **Road Transport (Amendment) Act 2020 (Act A1618)** substantially increased penalties, particularly for dangerous and drink-driving. Key offences and their penalties include:

| Section | Offence | Penalty (first offence, as amended) |
|---------|---------|--------------------------------------|
| s.41 | Causing death by reckless or dangerous driving | 5-10 years' jail and RM20,000-RM50,000 fine |
| s.42 | Reckless and dangerous driving | Up to RM10,000 fine and/or jail; disqualification up to 5 years |
| s.43 | Careless and inconsiderate driving | Minimum RM5,000 fine |
| s.44 | Causing death or injury while under the influence | Heavy fines and long jail terms; minimum 10-year disqualification for death |
| s.45A | Exceeding the prescribed alcohol limit | Up to 2 years' jail and RM10,000-RM30,000 fine |

Under **section 45A**, having alcohol in the body exceeding the prescribed limit while driving, attempting to drive, or being in charge of a motor vehicle is an offence in itself, even where no accident, death or injury results.

## Enforcement and the KEJARA demerit system

Enforcement powers under the Act let JPJ officers and police stop, inspect, and seize vehicles that breach safety and licensing requirements. To deter repeat offending, the **KEJARA** demerit-points system — made under subsidiary rules stemming from Act 333 — began enforcement on **15 April 2017**. Drivers who accumulate demerit points for scheduled offences face graduated licence suspensions and, ultimately, revocation.

## What's next

For the specific rules and figures that sit under this Act, see JPJ's official portal for driving-licence classes, road-tax rates, and the current KEJARA scheduled offences, and consult the Attorney General's Chambers reprint for the authoritative statute text before relying on any section number in a legal matter.
