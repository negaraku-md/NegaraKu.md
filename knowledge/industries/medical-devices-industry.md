If you have ever worn a surgical glove, had blood drawn through a catheter, or recovered with the help of an orthopaedic implant, there is a reasonable chance the device was made in Malaysia. Beyond its famous rubber gloves, the country has quietly become South-East Asia's most important place to manufacture medical devices — and one of the more significant medtech manufacturing bases anywhere in the world.

This guide looks at the medical-device industry specifically: the manufacture of devices and medtech products. That is distinct from the broader [rubber industry](/en/industries/rubber-industry/), which covers gloves and latex in depth. Here the focus is the wider world of devices — catheters, implants, cardiac and diagnostic products — and the ecosystem of multinationals, local firms, regulators and incentives behind them.

## What is the medical-device industry?

A "medical device" is any instrument, apparatus, implant or in-vitro reagent used to diagnose, prevent, monitor or treat a medical condition — a definition that stretches from a simple syringe to a programmable cardiac rhythm device. In Malaysia the industry grew out of the country's strengths in rubber and in precision electronics manufacturing (the same skills base that powers the [electrical & electronics](/en/industries/electrical-electronics-industry/) and [semiconductor](/en/industries/semiconductor-industry/) sectors), and it now spans single-use disposables, implantable devices, diagnostic equipment and contract manufacturing.

## How big is it?

Malaysia's standing is best captured regionally. In a joint media release in October 2024, MIDA and the Association of Malaysian Medical Industries (AMMI) described Malaysia as the top medtech hub in South-East Asia, home to the region's largest domestic medical-device market — worth about **RM10.6 billion in 2023** — and to the region's highest concentration of multinational medtech companies.

On exports, figures vary and should be treated with care. Ministry of Health and media statements put 2024 medical-device exports at roughly **RM37 billion**, up about 31% on the previous year. A MATRADE official has separately been reported citing **US$8.7 billion** for 2024 and placing Malaysia among the world's top five medical-device manufacturing sites, alongside Costa Rica and Puerto Rico. These numbers do not reconcile neatly, and no single official headline total was confirmed for this guide, so each is flagged for verification.

The sector's human footprint is also substantial: AMMI's **91 member companies** alone are reported to support **over 54,000 jobs** and to account for more than half of the country's medical-device export contributions. Across the economy, ministry statements refer to more than 200 manufacturers.

## Product segments

Malaysia's device output clusters into a few broad families:

- **Single-use disposables** — rubber and synthetic gloves, catheters, tubing, syringes and wound-care products. Malaysia has long been described as a dominant supplier of catheters (an undated MATRADE overview claims around 80% of world demand) and of examination gloves.
- **Orthopaedics** — implants and instruments, with home-grown specialists such as Straits Orthopaedics and ABio Orthopaedics based in Penang.
- **Cardiovascular and cardiac-rhythm devices** — structural-heart components and related products made by multinational plants.
- **Diagnostics and in-vitro devices** — reagents, test kits and laboratory equipment.
- **Contract manufacturing and precision components**, plus specialised services such as sterilisation (Malaysia has the region's largest sterilisation capacity across ethylene oxide, gamma and electron-beam methods, and was the first in the region with an X-ray sterilisation facility), biocompatibility testing and packaging.

## Clusters

The industry is heavily concentrated in the north of the peninsula:

- **Penang** is the undisputed heartland, centred on the **Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone**, where the bulk of the state's medical-device workforce is employed. As space has tightened on the island, expansion has spread to the mainland at **Batu Kawan**.
- **Kulim, Kedah** adds advanced capacity within the Kulim Hi-Tech Park; the German glass-and-pharma-packaging group SCHOTT, for example, completed a Kulim plant in 2024.
- **Melaka** and the central region host additional manufacturers, and **Johor** is drawing growing investment, helped by spillover from neighbouring Singapore.

For the state context, see the profile of [Penang](/en/states/penang/), the cluster's anchor.

## Key players

Malaysia hosts a dense roster of global medtech names operating substantial plants, reported to include **B. Braun**, **Boston Scientific**, **Abbott**, **Smith & Nephew**, **Steris** and **Dexcom** (the exact count of top-tier multinationals present differs between sources and is flagged for verification). B. Braun's Penang site is a particularly prominent anchor, described by MIDA as a global centre of excellence for intravenous-access products and also making infusion solutions and surgical instruments.

Alongside the multinationals sits a layer of local manufacturers — orthopaedic specialists such as Straits Orthopaedics, and the glove majors (Top Glove, Kossan and others) whose scale underpins the disposables segment. This mix of foreign anchors and local suppliers is what gives the ecosystem its depth, with AMMI members reporting billions of ringgit in raw materials and services sourced from local SMEs.

## Regulator and incentives

Medical devices in Malaysia are regulated by the **Medical Device Authority (MDA)**, a statutory body under the **Ministry of Health**. The MDA was established under the **Medical Device Authority Act 2012 (Act 738)** and enforces the **Medical Device Act 2012 (Act 737)**. Under this framework, devices are risk-classified **A to D**, must be **registered** before being placed on the market, and the businesses that make, import, distribute or represent them must hold an **establishment licence**. Foreign manufacturers without a local presence must appoint an in-country **authorised representative**, and submissions are made through the MDA's online system (MeDC@St).

On the investment side, medical devices are a **MIDA-promoted activity** within the life-sciences and medtech space. Manufacturers can apply for mainstream incentives such as **Pioneer Status** (a partial-to-full exemption of statutory income for a set period) and the **Investment Tax Allowance** on qualifying capital expenditure, as set out in MIDA's guidance; the exact rates and eligibility should always be confirmed with MIDA because the promoted-activities list and terms change over time. The sector is also named a priority under the **New Industrial Master Plan 2030**, which aims to push Malaysia toward higher-value, more localised manufacturing. See the profile of [MIDA](/en/government/mida/) for how the agency administers these incentives.

## Outlook

The direction of travel is toward higher value. Malaysia's foundations — a deep disposables base, world-class sterilisation and testing services, and a skilled precision-manufacturing workforce — are being leveraged to attract more advanced, implantable and diagnostic work. A MATRADE chairman has projected the market growing at roughly 9.5% a year toward around US$4.5 billion by 2028, and the NIMP 2030 explicitly targets medtech as a priority (both figures are indicative and flagged for verification).

The main challenges are familiar: a persistent skills gap in the Penang corridor as investment outpaces talent supply, exposure to global demand cycles given the sector's export orientation, and regional competition from Singapore, Vietnam and others. But the trajectory is clear — Malaysia intends to move from being the world's workshop for disposables toward a broader, more sophisticated medical-device manufacturing hub.
