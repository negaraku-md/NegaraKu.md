CIMB is the bank you meet almost everywhere you turn in Malaysian commerce — and increasingly across Southeast Asia. It is a universal bank, meaning it does everything from a salaried worker's savings account to multi-billion-ringgit bond issues and cross-border acquisitions, and it does so not only in Malaysia but through large operations in Indonesia, Thailand and Singapore.

## Overview

CIMB Group Holdings Berhad is a Malaysian universal bank headquartered in Kuala Lumpur and listed on Bursa Malaysia. It is commonly ranked as Malaysia's second-largest bank by assets, behind [Maybank](/companies/maybank) and ahead of [Public Bank](/companies/public-bank), and it is one of the five largest banking groups in ASEAN by assets (UN Environment Programme Finance Initiative member profile). The group reported total assets of about RM755.1 billion as at 31 December 2024 and a staff strength of over 33,000 (figures as at 31 March 2025, per CIMB's corporate site), operating across roughly 15 markets.

Its largest shareholder is Khazanah Nasional, Malaysia's sovereign wealth fund, whose stake sat in the low-twenties-percent range through 2024 (CIMB substantial-shareholder disclosures; The Edge Malaysia) — a reminder that CIMB, for all its commercial reach, grew out of Malaysia's government-linked banking consolidation.

## History: the mergers that built CIMB

CIMB's name comes from Commerce International Merchant Bankers, the investment bank at the core of the modern group, but its banking lineage runs much deeper — back to Bian Chiang Bank, founded in Kuching, Sarawak, in 1924, which through decades of consolidation became part of what is today CIMB.

The decisive chapter came in the mid-2000s. In June 2005, CIMB announced the acquisition of the Bumiputra-Commerce group; the holding company Commerce-Asset Holdings was renamed Bumiputra-Commerce Holdings, and the plan was to fold its consumer and investment banks into a single universal bank (CIMB corporate history). In January 2006 the new CIMB Group was formed, combining investment banking, consumer banking, asset management and Islamic services under one roof. That same year the group acquired Southern Bank, and in September 2006 Prime Minister Abdullah Ahmad Badawi formally launched the group — completing a three-way merger of Commerce International Merchant Bankers, Bumiputra-Commerce Bank and Southern Bank, and introducing a new logo and the "Forward Banking" tagline.

The group then moved quickly across borders. Indonesia had already arrived through the 2002 acquisition of PT Bank Niaga; in 2008 Bank Niaga merged with Bank Lippo to create what was then the country's sixth-largest bank, rebranded CIMB Niaga, a move driven by Khazanah to comply with Bank Indonesia's single-presence policy. In 2008-2009 CIMB entered Thailand by acquiring BankThai (later CIMB Thai) and opened in Singapore. In a few years a Malaysian investment-banking house had become a pan-ASEAN universal bank.

## Business segments

CIMB runs as an integrated universal bank, with its businesses usually grouped into a handful of core franchises:

| Segment | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer Banking | Everyday retail banking — deposits, cards, mortgages, personal and SME lending, digital banking (the CIMB OCTO app) |
| Commercial Banking | Banking for small and mid-sized enterprises, trade finance and business lending |
| Wholesale Banking | Corporate banking, treasury and markets, and investment banking (advisory, debt and equity capital markets) |
| CIMB Islamic | Shariah-compliant financing, deposits and capital-markets products, run across the group |

The wholesale and investment-banking franchise is a direct inheritance of the group's merchant-banking origins, and CIMB has long been one of the region's more prominent ASEAN investment banks. CIMB Islamic, launched in 2003, embeds Islamic finance across the group rather than siloing it — consistent with Malaysia's position as a global Islamic-finance hub.

## Regional footprint

CIMB is one of the few genuinely regional ASEAN banks. Its presence is anchored by Malaysia and three large overseas operations:

- **Indonesia — CIMB Niaga:** one of Indonesia's larger banks and a major profit contributor; CIMB Niaga posted a FY2024 net profit of 8.7 trillion rupiah (The Star).
- **Thailand — CIMB Thai:** a listed Thai commercial bank within the group.
- **Singapore:** a wholesale and consumer banking hub for the region.

Beyond these, CIMB operates in markets including Cambodia, Vietnam and the Philippines, with selected presence in Greater China, the United Kingdom and other financial centres — part of a footprint the group describes as spanning around 15 markets.

## Scale and significance

For the financial year ended 31 December 2024, CIMB reported a record net profit of RM7.73 billion, up 10.7% year on year, with return on average equity of 11.2% and operating income of RM22.30 billion (CIMB FY2024 results). Its Common Equity Tier 1 ratio stood at 14.6% at year-end, a conventional marker of capital strength.

As one of Malaysia's "big three" banking groups alongside Maybank and Public Bank, CIMB is systemically important to the domestic financial system and a heavyweight on Bursa Malaysia, where it is consistently among the largest companies by market value. Because Khazanah is its anchor shareholder, the bank also sits at the intersection of commercial banking and national economic policy.

## Why it matters

CIMB matters on three counts. First, scale: as Malaysia's second-largest bank by assets, its lending and deposit decisions ripple through the whole economy. Second, reach: more than most Malaysian institutions, CIMB ties the country's banking system into the wider ASEAN economy, so its results are a useful read on regional, not just domestic, conditions. Third, ownership: Khazanah's stake makes CIMB a case study in how Malaysia's government-linked investment model shaped — and still shapes — the country's banking champions. For anyone trying to understand Malaysian finance, CIMB is one of the handful of institutions you cannot skip.
