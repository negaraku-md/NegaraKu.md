RHB is one of the names most Malaysians recognise on a high-street bank branch, an ATM card or a home-loan brochure — yet the group behind it reaches well beyond retail banking, into Islamic finance, investment banking, insurance and markets across Southeast Asia. This page is a neutral, factual overview of what RHB Banking Group is, how it is structured, who owns it and why it matters.

## Overview

RHB Banking Group is one of Malaysia's largest banking groups, led by RHB Bank Berhad, a public company listed on Bursa Malaysia under stock code 1066. It is commonly described as the fourth-largest fully integrated financial services group in Malaysia, after [Maybank](/companies/maybank), [CIMB](/companies/cimb) and [Public Bank](/companies/public-bank). The group is headquartered in Kuala Lumpur and provides a full range of banking and financial services through a set of specialised subsidiaries.

RHB reported total assets of about RM358 billion as at 31 December 2025, up from RM350 billion a year earlier, and a net profit of RM3.4 billion for the 2025 financial year (a 7.8% increase on FY2024), according to the group's results announcement. Its largest shareholder is the Employees Provident Fund (EPF), Malaysia's statutory retirement savings fund.

## History

RHB's roots run deep into Malaysian banking history. Its earliest ancestor is Kwong Yik Bank, established in 1913 in Kuala Lumpur and often cited as Malaya's first local bank. The modern group was assembled through a series of mergers:

- In 1997, Kwong Yik Bank's successor combined with DCB Bank — at the time described as the country's biggest-ever banking merger.
- In 1999, the group absorbed Sime Bank (the former United Malayan Banking Corporation, dating to 1959).
- In 2003, it merged with Bank Utama (Malaysia) Berhad.

A further transformation came in 2012, when RHB Capital Berhad acquired OSK Investment Bank in a share swap; the two investment-banking businesses were combined with effect from April 2013, substantially enlarging the group's capital-markets arm. In 2016, RHB Bank Berhad took over the listing status of RHB Capital Berhad to become the group's listed financial holding company. A proposed merger with the AmBank group was explored in 2017 but was called off after the parties could not agree terms.

## Business segments

RHB operates as a universal banking group, delivering services through dedicated legal entities and reporting across several business segments. The table below summarises the main building blocks.

| Entity / segment | Role |
| --- | --- |
| RHB Bank Berhad | Listed financial holding company and main commercial bank |
| Consumer / community banking | Retail deposits, mortgages, cards, personal and SME lending |
| Corporate & wholesale banking | Corporate lending, treasury, transaction and capital-markets services |
| RHB Islamic Bank Berhad | Shariah-compliant deposits, financing and wealth products |
| RHB Investment Bank Berhad | Investment banking, broking, advisory and research |
| RHB Insurance Berhad | General (non-life) insurance |

**Consumer banking** serves individuals and small businesses with everyday accounts, home and personal financing, and credit cards. In its FY2025 segment reporting, RHB's community-banking arm recorded pre-tax profit of about RM1.1 billion.

**Islamic banking** is a strategic pillar. Through RHB Islamic Bank, the group offers deposits and financing structured to comply with Shariah principles. RHB reported Islamic financing at 46.4% of its total domestic loans for FY2025 — a sign of how central Islamic finance has become to Malaysian banking generally.

**Investment banking** runs through RHB Investment Bank (RHBIB), which provides corporate advisory, equity and debt capital markets, stockbroking and research. The 2012-2013 OSK integration made RHBIB one of the more prominent domestic investment banks and strengthened its regional broking reach.

**Insurance** is provided by RHB Insurance, the group's general insurance subsidiary, which contributed a smaller but growing share of group earnings in FY2025.

## Regional footprint

RHB is not only a Malaysian bank. According to the group's own disclosures, it operates across several Southeast Asian markets: Malaysia (its home market), Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, Brunei, Cambodia and Laos. Singapore is its most developed overseas operation, offering capital-markets, broking and corporate-advisory services, while Cambodia hosts a commercial-banking network of branches in Phnom Penh and the provinces. This regional spread lets RHB serve cross-border trade and investment flows within ASEAN and diversifies its earnings beyond the domestic market.

## Scale & significance

By the standard measures used for Malaysian banks — total assets, profitability and market presence — RHB sits firmly among the country's leading institutions. Its roughly RM358 billion in total assets (as at 31 December 2025) and RM3.4 billion in FY2025 net profit place it in the top tier of domestic lenders, and it reported a return on equity of 10.5% for FY2025 with a dividend payout of 65%. The group operates more than 200 branches in Malaysia and employs a workforce reported in the region of 13,000 to 14,000 people.

Ownership is a distinctive feature. The EPF — which manages the retirement savings of most private-sector Malaysian workers — is RHB's largest shareholder. Its exact stake is disclosed in Bursa Malaysia substantial-shareholder notices and has shifted over time, reported broadly in the mid-to-high 30% range across 2025 and 2026. Because a national pension fund is the anchor investor, RHB's performance is closely watched as a matter of public interest, not only by private shareholders.

## Why it matters

RHB matters for three practical reasons. First, as one of Malaysia's largest banks it is a core part of the national financial system, and its lending and deposit activity touches households and businesses across the country. Second, its heavy weighting toward Islamic financing illustrates how mainstream Shariah-compliant banking has become in Malaysia — a market where Islamic and conventional finance operate side by side. Third, its EPF anchor ownership links the bank's fortunes to the retirement savings of millions of Malaysians, giving its results a significance beyond ordinary corporate reporting.

## What's next

- **Watch the financial filings.** Time-varying figures — total assets, profit, EPF's exact shareholding, branch and staff counts, and asset-based rankings — change each reporting period. Always confirm the latest numbers against RHB Bank Berhad's results announcements, annual report and Bursa Malaysia disclosures rather than relying on older figures.
- **Track the digital and regional push.** RHB has invested in digital banking (including a stake in a licensed digital bank) and continues to build its ASEAN footprint, so its mix of domestic and regional earnings is likely to keep evolving.
- **Follow consolidation talk.** Malaysian banking has a long history of merger speculation; RHB has twice been part of major merger discussions, so any renewed talk of industry consolidation is worth monitoring through official announcements.
