Few Malaysian companies are as closely tied to a single founder as Public Bank is to Tan Sri Teh Hong Piow. Starting with one branch in central Kuala Lumpur in 1966, he built a lender that grew into one of the country's largest banking groups — and one famous less for size than for caution, posting the banking sector's lowest bad-loan ratios decade after decade.

## Overview

Public Bank Berhad (registration 196501000672 / 6463-H) is a Malaysian banking group listed on Bursa Malaysia. It is commonly described as the country's third-largest bank by total assets — behind [Maybank](/companies/maybank) and [CIMB](/companies/cimb) — while ranking as the largest Malaysian bank by shareholders' funds and among the most profitable. Its total assets stood at about RM543 billion as at end-December 2024.

The group's reputation rests on a focused retail-and-SME model and conservative lending. It reported net profit of RM7.15 billion for the 2024 financial year (up from RM6.65 billion the year before), on revenue of RM27.21 billion, according to results published in February 2025. Customer deposits reached RM433.3 billion and loans RM397.7 billion at end-December 2024, and the group declared a second interim dividend of 11 sen per share for the year. Managing director and chief executive Tay Ah Lek leads day-to-day operations. The bank marks its 60th year of operations in 2026.

## History: Teh Hong Piow's founding and legacy

Teh Hong Piow was born in Singapore on 14 March 1930. He began his career in 1950 as a clerk at Overseas-Chinese Banking Corporation (OCBC), joined Malayan Banking (Maybank) as a manager in 1960, and was promoted to general manager in 1964 at the age of 34. In 1966 he left to found his own bank.

Public Bank began operating at its first branch on Jalan Gereja, Kuala Lumpur, on 6 August 1966, and listed on the Kuala Lumpur Stock Exchange — now Bursa Malaysia — on 6 April 1967, less than a year after opening. Teh chaired the group for most of the next five decades, stepping back from the chairmanship in his later years to serve as chairman emeritus, adviser and non-executive director. He died on 12 December 2022, aged 92, after more than seventy years in banking, and was survived by his wife, Puan Seri Tay Sock Noy, and four children. His investment vehicle, Consolidated Teh Holdings, remained the bank's single largest shareholder, and his insistence on prudent credit underwriting became the defining feature of the institution he left behind — a culture of caution that successive management teams have kept largely intact.

## Business segments

Public Bank is a universal banking group organised around a core of personal and small-business banking, with specialist subsidiaries extending into Islamic finance, investment banking, fund management and insurance.

| Entity | Business |
| --- | --- |
| Public Bank Berhad | Parent and principal commercial bank (retail, SME, corporate) |
| Public Islamic Bank Berhad | Wholly owned full-fledged Islamic (Shariah-compliant) bank |
| Public Investment Bank Berhad | Investment banking, stockbroking and advisory |
| Public Mutual Berhad | Unit-trust and private-retirement-scheme fund management |
| LPI Capital Bhd (44.15% stake) | General insurance (Lonpac Insurance) |
| Public Bank (L) Ltd | Offshore banking in Labuan |

**Retail banking** — mortgages, hire-purchase vehicle financing, credit cards and personal lending — is the group's backbone, and it holds leading market shares in residential property and passenger-vehicle financing. **SME banking** serves small and medium enterprises, long a Public Bank strength. **Public Islamic Bank** provides the Shariah-compliant counterpart to the conventional franchise. **Public Mutual** is one of Malaysia's largest private unit-trust managers and a major source of fee income, while the 2024 move to take a 44.15% stake in **LPI Capital** deepened the group's insurance exposure.

## Scale and reputation: asset quality

What distinguishes Public Bank among Malaysian lenders is not its balance-sheet size but its credit discipline. The group has for many years reported the lowest gross impaired-loan (non-performing loan) ratio in the Malaysian banking system — historically around 0.6%, well below the industry average — reflecting a cautious approach to the loans it writes.

That conservatism underpins strong, steady profitability and a long record of ratings at the top of the domestic scale. Pre-tax profit was RM8.93 billion for FY2024; excluding a one-off goodwill impairment on its Hong Kong operations, underlying pre-tax growth would have been higher. The combination of high asset quality, efficient cost control and a loyal retail deposit base is the group's signature. As one obituary summed up the founder's achievement, Teh had built the bank into "a modern and integrated financial institution."

## Regional footprint

Although overwhelmingly a Malaysian bank, Public Bank also operates a regional network built up over decades. Domestically it runs roughly 260 branches nationwide. Abroad, its presence is concentrated in Greater China and Indochina:

- **Hong Kong:** Public Bank (Hong Kong) Limited and Public Finance Limited, the group's longest-established overseas operation, plus a small presence in mainland China.
- **Cambodia:** Cambodian Public Bank Plc (known as Campu Bank), one of the larger foreign banks in the country, with affiliated insurance and securities units.
- **Vietnam:** Public Bank Vietnam Limited, a wholly foreign-owned bank.
- **Laos:** Public Bank Lao Limited, with a small branch network.
- **Sri Lanka:** a limited branch presence.

Group-wide, Public Bank serves on the order of nine million customers and employs more than 21,000 people.

## Why it matters

Public Bank sits in the top tier of Malaysia's banking system, so its lending, deposit rates and credit standards touch millions of households and small businesses. For savers and borrowers it is a mainstream choice for mortgages, car loans and SME financing; for investors it is a bellwether of Malaysian retail-banking health, watched for loan growth, margins and — above all — asset quality. Its founder-driven culture of conservative lending also offers a useful contrast with the larger, more diversified universal-banking models of Maybank and CIMB. Because the bank's figures move with each reporting period, anyone citing its assets, profit or market position should check the latest annual report and Bursa Malaysia filings rather than rely on older numbers.
