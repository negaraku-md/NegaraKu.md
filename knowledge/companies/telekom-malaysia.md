Almost every fibre broadband line, enterprise circuit and undersea data cable in Malaysia touches one company before it reaches anyone else. Telekom Malaysia Berhad (TM) is the national incumbent — the operator that built most of the country's fixed network and still owns the backbone that rivals and international carriers depend on.

## Overview

TM is Malaysia's incumbent fixed-line telecommunications and broadband operator, listed on Bursa Malaysia (stock code 4863) and classed as a government-linked company. It sells to three broad markets through distinct brands: consumers (the unifi fibre brand and the older Streamyx service), enterprise and government (TM One), and other operators and carriers (TM Global, its wholesale arm). Underneath all of them sits the asset that defines TM — a national fibre backbone and a submarine-cable network that carry much of the country's domestic and international traffic. Unlike [Maxis](/companies/maxis), which is primarily a mobile operator, TM's centre of gravity is fixed infrastructure and wholesale connectivity.

## History

TM's roots run back to the colonial-era Telecommunications Department. After independence the department was known as Jabatan Telekom, and the name Telekom Malaysia was adopted in 1984. The decisive shift came on 1 January 1987, when the service was corporatised as Syarikat Telekom Malaysia (STM) under the Telecommunications Service (Successor Company) Act 1985 — turning a government department into a company that could raise capital and operate commercially.

STM listed on the Kuala Lumpur Stock Exchange (now Bursa Malaysia) on 7 November 1990, one of the landmark privatisations of the era, with a reported market value of about RM27 billion. Over the following two decades TM expanded into mobile and regional markets, then separated the two. In 2008 TM demerged its mobile and international operations; the demerged entity, TM International Berhad, was renamed [Axiata](/companies/axiata) in 2009. That split left TM focused on fixed-line, broadband and wholesale infrastructure, while Axiata (parent of Celcom, now CelcomDigi) carried the mobile business.

## Business

TM organises its operations around customer-facing brands under its technology-services arm.

| Brand / unit | Market served | What it provides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| unifi | Consumers and small businesses | Fibre broadband, convergence (internet, voice, IPTV) and unifi Mobile |
| Streamyx | Consumers (legacy) | Older copper-based (ADSL/VDSL) broadband, being wound down as fibre grows |
| TM One | Enterprise and public sector | Managed connectivity, cloud, data-centre and digital solutions for corporations and government |
| TM Global | Operators and international carriers | Domestic and international wholesale: the national fibre backbone, leased capacity and submarine cables |

**unifi**, launched in 2010 as the country's first high-speed-broadband (HSBB) service, is TM's flagship retail brand and the vehicle for its fibre-to-the-home push. **Streamyx**, launched in 2001, was the mass-market broadband service of the DSL era and is gradually being retired. **TM One** serves enterprise and government customers with connectivity, cloud and data-centre services. **TM Global** is the wholesale backbone business — historically also referred to as TM Wholesale — that sells capacity to other telcos and operates Malaysia's international cable links.

## National role

TM's significance goes beyond its own subscribers because it owns infrastructure the whole sector uses. Its fibre backbone is the primary long-haul network linking towns and states, and TM Global operates Malaysia's submarine-cable landings, which connect the country to Asia, the Middle East, Europe and the United States. As stated on 31 January 2026, TM Global's reach spanned 33 submarine-cable systems totalling about 275,000 km, with cable landing stations including a fifth station at Morib, Selangor and another planned at Kuala Sedili, Johor.

TM is also a central delivery partner for **JENDELA** (Jalinan Digital Negara), the national digital-connectivity plan coordinated by the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC). JENDELA aims to extend fibre and mobile broadband across the country and retire legacy networks, and TM's fibre rollout is one of the largest contributors to the premises-passed targets. Because so much national and third-party traffic rides on TM's network, the company functions as a strategic piece of public infrastructure as much as a commercial operator.

## Scale and significance

TM reported group revenue of about RM11.71 billion for the financial year ended 31 December 2024 and net profit of roughly RM2.0 billion, with fixed-broadband subscribers of about 3.179 million (all FY2024 figures, which change over time). Its ownership reflects its status as a government-linked company: Khazanah Nasional and the Employees Provident Fund are its largest shareholders, alongside Kumpulan Wang Persaraan (Diperbadankan) and other institutions. Crucially, the government holds a single **Special Rights Redeemable Preference Share** — a "golden share" — that carries special rights over certain corporate decisions and senior appointments, a common feature of Malaysia's privatised strategic utilities.

## Why it matters

For most people and businesses in Malaysia, TM is the company behind the broadband line — and often behind their provider's line too. Its decisions on fibre coverage, pricing and backbone capacity shape what the rest of the market can offer, which is why its wholesale terms are closely watched by regulators and competitors. For investors and students, TM is a case study in how a former state telecoms department became a listed incumbent that still sits at the heart of national infrastructure policy.

## What's next

- **Fibre and JENDELA completion:** continued fibre expansion to remaining premises and rural areas under successive JENDELA phases, including Sabah and Sarawak, where coverage historically lags the peninsula.
- **Data-centre and submarine-cable growth:** TM Global is positioning Malaysia as a regional data hub, adding cable systems and landing stations and linking the data-centre boom to its backbone and international capacity.
- **Shareholding and governance:** watch TM's Bursa Malaysia filings for changes in Khazanah, EPF and other institutional stakes, and for how the Ministry of Finance exercises its special-share rights — rather than relying on older figures.
