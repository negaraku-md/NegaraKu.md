Walk into a Pantai or Gleneagles hospital in Kuala Lumpur, a Mount Elizabeth ward in Singapore, an Acibadem clinic in Istanbul or a Fortis centre in Delhi, and you are stepping into the same corporate family. That family is IHH Healthcare Berhad — a Malaysia-headquartered company that has grown into one of the largest private hospital operators in the world.

## Overview

IHH Healthcare Berhad is a private healthcare group incorporated and headquartered in Malaysia, with a major operating base in Singapore. It is dual-listed — primarily on Bursa Malaysia (stock code 5225) and secondarily on the Singapore Exchange (SGX: Q0F). The group's business is building and running hospitals and medical centres, together with laboratories, clinics and related healthcare services, under a portfolio of strong national and regional brands rather than a single global name.

Its reach is unusual for a company of Malaysian origin: the FY2024 annual report describes a network of more than 140 healthcare facilities, including over 80 hospitals, spread across roughly ten countries in Asia and Europe (as of the financial year ended 31 December 2024). Because those counts move with each acquisition and opening, the exact figures should always be read from the latest filing.

## History

IHH's modern form took shape under Malaysia's sovereign wealth fund. The holding company — originally Integrated Healthcare Holdings — was incorporated in 2010 by Khazanah Nasional, which assembled a set of existing healthcare assets into a single platform. Chief among them were Singapore's Parkway Holdings (the Mount Elizabeth, Gleneagles and Parkway network) and Malaysia's Pantai group.

In 2012 the group added Türkiye's Acibadem, a leading private hospital operator, bringing a large European and Middle Eastern footprint into the fold. That same year, in July 2012, IHH carried out a simultaneous dual listing on Bursa Malaysia and the Singapore Exchange. The offering raised on the order of US$2 billion and was described at the time as one of the world's largest healthcare IPOs and among the biggest global flotations of that year. The listing let investors subscribe in either the Malaysian or Singapore tranche and receive shares denominated accordingly.

Expansion continued after listing. In 2018 IHH acquired a controlling stake in India's Fortis Healthcare, giving it a major presence in one of the world's fastest-growing healthcare markets. The group has since continued to add capacity, including hospital acquisitions and greenfield builds across its home markets.

## Home markets and brands

IHH organises its operations around a handful of core markets, each anchored by recognisable brands rather than a single corporate identity. This brand-led structure lets long-established local names continue to carry patient trust.

| Market | Key brands |
| --- | --- |
| Malaysia | Pantai Hospitals, Gleneagles, Prince Court Medical Centre |
| Singapore | Mount Elizabeth, Gleneagles, Parkway (Parkway Pantai network) |
| Türkiye (and wider Europe) | Acibadem |
| India | Fortis Healthcare |

In Malaysia, the Pantai and Gleneagles chains and the Prince Court Medical Centre in Kuala Lumpur give IHH one of the country's largest private hospital footprints. In Singapore, the Parkway-branded hospitals — Mount Elizabeth and Gleneagles among them — are long-standing fixtures of the city-state's private medical sector and a well-known destination for medical travel. Acibadem extends the group deep into Türkiye and neighbouring European markets, while Fortis gives it scale across major Indian cities. The group also maintains laboratory and ancillary services that support these hospital networks.

## Ownership

IHH's share register reflects its origins and its international backing. Its two largest shareholders are Japan's Mitsui & Co and Malaysia's sovereign wealth fund, Khazanah Nasional.

Khazanah created and initially controlled the group, holding a clear majority before the 2012 IPO. Mitsui came in as a substantial strategic investor and progressively increased its position: in 2018 it bought an additional block of shares from Khazanah, which lifted it to roughly a third of the company and made it the single largest shareholder — a position it has held since, through its vehicle MBK Healthcare Management. Khazanah remains a significant long-term shareholder, and the balance of the stock is held by institutional and retail investors on the two exchanges. Precise ownership percentages shift with share placements and buy-ins, so current figures should be taken from the latest Bursa Malaysia and SGX disclosures rather than from older reports.

## Scale and significance

For the financial year ended 31 December 2024, IHH reported group revenue of about RM24.4 billion and net profit of roughly RM2.66 billion, according to its results release — figures that place it among the largest listed companies of its kind in the region. Its combined network of hospitals and beds across Malaysia, Singapore, Türkiye and India makes it, by common industry reckoning, one of the biggest private healthcare groups in the world by market value and bed count.

For Malaysia specifically, IHH matters on several levels at once: it is a large domestic employer and private-sector hospital provider; a rare example of a Malaysian-headquartered company that has become a genuine multinational in a high-skill service industry; and a vehicle through which national institutions (Khazanah) and a major foreign investor (Mitsui) jointly hold a globally significant asset.

## Why it matters

IHH sits at the intersection of healthcare, capital markets and national strategy. For patients, it operates many of the best-known private hospitals in Malaysia and Singapore and a large share of the region's medical-tourism capacity. For investors, it is a bellwether for Asian private healthcare, with earnings spread across currencies and regulatory systems. And for understanding Malaysia's economy, it is a case study in how a state investment fund built and then partly divested a world-scale company while keeping it headquartered at home.

## What's next

- **Capacity expansion:** The group has signalled plans to add thousands of beds across its markets over the coming years through new hospitals and acquisitions; its exact targets and progress are disclosed in each annual report.
- **Ownership balance:** Watch the relative stakes of Mitsui and Khazanah, which can shift through placements or further share purchases and are a recurring subject of market reporting.
- **Up-to-date figures:** Because hospital counts, bed numbers and revenue all change each reporting period, confirm any specific figure against IHH's latest Bursa Malaysia and SGX filings before relying on it.
