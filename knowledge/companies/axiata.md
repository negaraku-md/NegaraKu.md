Malaysia's biggest telecoms story is not a single network but a holding company that owns pieces of mobile operators in six countries. Axiata Group Berhad sits in an office tower at Kuala Lumpur Sentral and controls, part-owns or jointly runs the carriers that connect hundreds of millions of people across South and Southeast Asia.

## Overview

Axiata Group Berhad is a Malaysian-based regional telecommunications and digital holding company, listed on Bursa Malaysia under stock code 6888. It does not primarily run a network under its own name; instead it holds stakes in operating companies ("opcos") and associates across Asia and steers them as a portfolio. Its largest shareholder is Khazanah Nasional, the Malaysian government's sovereign wealth fund, with other state-linked institutions — Permodalan Nasional Berhad and the Employees Provident Fund — also among the top holders (stakes as summarised by public sources, undated; see verification note).

The group's reach spans Malaysia, Indonesia, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh and Cambodia, together with the edotco towers infrastructure business and digital units such as the Boost fintech platform and the ADA data-and-marketing company.

## History: from TM International to Axiata

Axiata's roots lie inside the former national monopoly. It was incorporated on 12 June 1992 as **TM International Berhad**, the international and mobile arm of [Telekom Malaysia](/malaysia). Over the following years it accumulated cellular stakes across emerging Asian markets, and in 2008 it was listed separately on Bursa Malaysia.

On 2 April 2009 the company rebranded as **Axiata Group Berhad**, signalling an identity independent of its former parent and a strategy centred on regional mobile growth rather than Malaysian fixed-line roots. The name has since become an umbrella brand carried by several of its operators, such as XL Axiata and Dialog Axiata.

## Regional operating companies

Axiata's structure is best understood as a parent holding stakes in separately managed operators, several of them listed on their own home exchanges. The table below lists the principal mobile businesses and Axiata's approximate ownership (figures are time-varying — see the verification note for dates).

| Operating company | Country | Brand / status | Axiata stake (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CelcomDigi | Malaysia | CelcomDigi (jointly controlled with Telenor) | ~33.1% |
| XLSmart | Indonesia | XL / Axis / Smartfren (jointly controlled) | ~34.8% |
| Link Net | Indonesia | Fixed broadband / fibre | ~79.5% |
| Dialog Axiata | Sri Lanka | Dialog | ~73.75% |
| Robi Axiata | Bangladesh | Robi / Airtel | ~61.82% |
| Smart Axiata | Cambodia | Smart | ~72.48% |

**CelcomDigi (Malaysia).** Axiata's home-market operator was for years Celcom, one of Malaysia's oldest cellular brands. In November 2022 Celcom was merged with Telenor's Digi to create CelcomDigi, Malaysia's largest mobile operator by subscribers. Axiata and Telenor each took about 33.1% and jointly control the merged entity; Digi was renamed CelcomDigi Berhad in February 2023. The merger put CelcomDigi head-to-head with [Maxis](/companies/maxis) and U Mobile in the Malaysian market.

**XLSmart and Link Net (Indonesia).** Axiata's long-standing Indonesian operator, XL Axiata, was combined with rival Smartfren in 2025 to form XLSmart, a jointly controlled business that reported a sharply larger subscriber base after the merger. Alongside it, Axiata holds a controlling interest in Link Net, a fixed-broadband and fibre company positioned as a second-largest "FibreCo" in Indonesia.

**Dialog Axiata (Sri Lanka).** Dialog is the market leader in Sri Lanka. In mid-2024 it completed the acquisition and amalgamation of Bharti Airtel's Sri Lankan operation, lifting its reported subscriber base past 20 million and giving Bharti a minority stake in Dialog in exchange.

**Robi Axiata (Bangladesh) and Smart Axiata (Cambodia).** Robi, which operates the Robi and Airtel brands in Bangladesh, and Smart in Cambodia round out the frontier-market portfolio. Both are majority-held and both have featured in the group's reporting as resilient contributors to profit.

## edotco and infrastructure

A distinctive part of Axiata is **edotco**, its telecommunications towers business — the physical masts, rooftops and power equipment that carriers rent to host their antennas. edotco is one of Asia's largest independent tower companies; Axiata held about 63% of it as of 2025, with Khazanah Nasional and the Retirement Fund (KWAP) holding the balance. One Edge Malaysia report in October 2025 put edotco's portfolio at "more than 47,000 towers" across its markets, after deals such as a US$940 million acquisition of roughly 13,000 Pakistan towers.

Because towers are a capital-heavy, cash-generative asset distinct from running a network, edotco has repeatedly figured in Axiata's balance-sheet planning, including reported moves to monetise part of the stake to reduce group debt.

## Scale and significance

Axiata describes itself as serving a very large regional customer base — a figure the company put at around 150 million across Asia in 2019, since grown through mergers and market expansion. For the nine months to 30 September 2025, the group reported profit of RM403.3 million, and it has emphasised cutting holding-company debt and simplifying its portfolio.

The group matters to Malaysia on three levels. It is a flagship of Khazanah's investment portfolio, so its performance feeds into state financial strength. It is a channel for Malaysian capital and management expertise into fast-growing Asian telecoms markets. And through CelcomDigi it is central to Malaysia's own mobile and 5G landscape, which is regulated by the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC).

## Why it matters

For anyone mapping Malaysia's economy, Axiata is a case study in how a formerly state-owned monopoly's offshoot became a pan-Asian investor. Its holding-company model — owning stakes rather than always running networks directly — means that headlines about "Axiata" often really concern a specific opco (CelcomDigi in Malaysia, Dialog in Sri Lanka) or a portfolio move (merging XL with Smartfren, selling edotco towers). Understanding which layer a piece of news belongs to is the key to reading the group correctly.
