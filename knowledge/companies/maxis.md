Almost every Malaysian with a phone has, at some point, been a Maxis or Hotlink customer. Behind those two familiar brands sits a single company — Maxis Berhad — that ranks among the country's largest telecommunications operators and has grown from a mobile-only carrier into a converged provider of mobile, home broadband and enterprise connectivity.

## What is Maxis?

Maxis Berhad is a Malaysian converged telecommunications company. Its core business is mobile network services, sold to consumers under two brands: **Maxis**, positioned as the premium brand for postpaid plans and home services, and **Hotlink**, the value brand aimed at prepaid users and younger, price-sensitive customers. Both brands run on the same underlying Maxis network.

Alongside mobile, Maxis sells **home fibre broadband** to households and a range of **enterprise** services — connectivity, cloud, Internet of Things and managed solutions — to businesses and government agencies. The company is listed on the Main Market of Bursa Malaysia under stock code 6012. It is a private-sector, publicly traded firm, not a government entity, though its principal operating licence and spectrum sit under Malaysian regulation.

## History: Usaha Tegas, Ananda Krishnan and two listings

Maxis traces back to the 1990s, when it was built up as the mobile arm of a telecoms group associated with Malaysian businessman **Tan Sri T. Ananda Krishnan** and his **Usaha Tegas** group. The business first listed on Bursa Malaysia (then the Kuala Lumpur Stock Exchange) in 2002 as Maxis Communications Berhad, and was by then one of the largest companies on the exchange.

In 2007 Ananda Krishnan took the company private and delisted it, reorganising the group so that its faster-growing overseas ventures (in markets such as India and Indonesia) were separated from the Malaysian mobile business. The Malaysian operation was then relisted on its own. According to Light Reading, Maxis returned to Bursa Malaysia in November 2009 as "Malaysia's leading integrated communications company," opening as the fifth-largest company on the exchange with a market capitalisation of about RM37.5 billion. The 2009 IPO raised roughly RM11.2 billion (around US$3.3 billion) and was widely reported at the time as the largest ever in Southeast Asia.

The relisted entity covered only the Malaysian mobile business; the international assets stayed with the privately held parent. Saudi Telecom Company (stc) has also been a long-standing shareholder of the parent group alongside the Usaha Tegas-linked interests. Exact, current shareholding percentages change over time and should be read from Maxis's latest Bursa Malaysia disclosures rather than from older figures.

## Business: mobile, home fibre and enterprise

Maxis today organises its business around three main areas.

| Segment | What it covers | Main brands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer Mobile | Postpaid and prepaid mobile plans for individuals | Maxis, Hotlink |
| Consumer Home | Home fibre broadband and 5G home internet | Maxis |
| Enterprise Business | Connectivity, cloud, IoT and managed services for businesses and government | Maxis Business |

For the financial year ended 31 December 2024 (FY2024), Maxis reported **service revenue of RM8,868 million**, up 3.5% year on year, and **profit after tax of RM1,396 million**, up 40.7% (a figure flattered by prior-year one-off adjustments), on EBITDA of RM4,122 million, according to the company's own results announcement. Within that, Enterprise Business service revenue reached RM1,610 million (up 6.8%) and Consumer Home service revenue RM993 million (up 7.4%), while consumer mobile subscribers totalled about **9.7 million** and total home connections about **784,000** as of year-end. These figures are time-varying; the latest quarterly and annual filings should be consulted for current numbers.

## 5G and the network

Malaysia took an unusual path on 5G. Rather than letting each operator build its own 5G network, the government created a single wholesale network operator, **Digital Nasional Berhad (DNB)**, from which the mobile carriers buy access. Maxis was the last of the major operators to commit: after its shareholders approved the deal, it signed a 5G access agreement with DNB and began offering 5G plans in August 2023, with the arrangement executed through its subsidiary Maxis Broadband Sdn Bhd.

The government then moved toward a **dual-network model**, under which a second 5G network would be built once DNB's coverage targets were met. In November 2024 the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) selected **U Mobile — not Maxis** — to roll out that second network, a decision Maxis said it would engage the regulator to understand. As a result, Maxis's 5G access and the shape of Malaysia's second network remain areas to watch, and readers should verify the current arrangement against MCMC announcements.

## Scale and significance

Maxis is one of a small number of operators that between them carry most of Malaysia's mobile and fixed-broadband traffic. Its main rivals are [Axiata](/companies/axiata) (whose Malaysian mobile business merged with Digi to form CelcomDigi), U Mobile and Telekom Malaysia. In a market where mobile penetration is very high and growth comes mainly from converged bundles — selling mobile, home broadband and digital services together — Maxis's push into home fibre and enterprise is central to its strategy.

As a large, dividend-paying company on Bursa Malaysia, Maxis is also a significant holding for Malaysian institutional investors and pension funds, which gives its results and its regulatory position a weight beyond the telecoms sector itself.

## Why it matters

Maxis sits at the intersection of two things that touch almost everyone in Malaysia: how people connect, and how the country builds shared digital infrastructure. Its brands reach millions of households, so its pricing, coverage and service quality shape everyday life. At the same time, its journey — from a mobile-only carrier to a converged operator, and from building its own network to buying access from a national 5G wholesaler — mirrors the policy choices Malaysia has made about who should own and operate critical infrastructure. Watching Maxis is therefore a useful way to track both consumer telecoms and the politics of Malaysia's digital economy.
