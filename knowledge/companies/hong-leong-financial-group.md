Malaysians know the Hong Leong name from a bank branch, a motorcycle, or a share price — but the banking business sits inside a two-tier listed structure that often confuses readers. At the top is Hong Leong Financial Group Berhad (HLFG), the financial holding company; beneath it is Hong Leong Bank Berhad (HLB), the commercial bank that most customers actually deal with.

## Overview

HLFG is the Bursa Malaysia-listed holding company that gathers the financial-services interests of the Hong Leong Group, the conglomerate controlled by Tan Sri Quek Leng Chan. It is not itself a bank. Instead it consolidates three distinct businesses: commercial banking (Hong Leong Bank), insurance and takaful (through Hong Leong Assurance), and investment banking and asset management (through Hong Leong Capital).

Hong Leong Bank is the group's flagship and one of Malaysia's five largest banking groups by assets. HLFG is its controlling shareholder, holding roughly 64% of HLB according to the bank's 2019 shareholder register (a figure that moves with corporate actions and should be checked against the latest Bursa Malaysia filing).

## History: Quek Leng Chan and the Hong Leong Group

The Hong Leong Group traces to a trading business founded in Singapore in 1963 by Quek Leng Chan and his cousins, part of a wider family enterprise that eventually split into Malaysian and Singaporean arms. In Malaysia the group grew under Tan Sri Quek Leng Chan into a conglomerate spanning financial services, manufacturing and property.

The banking business has a much older root. Hong Leong Bank descends from Kwong Lee Mortgage & Remittance Company, established in Kuching, Sarawak, in 1905 — one of the oldest financial institutions in the country. It later became Kwong Lee Bank, then MUI Bank under the MUI Group. The Hong Leong Group acquired MUI Bank on 3 January 1994, renamed it Hong Leong Bank, and listed it on the then Kuala Lumpur Stock Exchange (now Bursa Malaysia) on 17 October 1994 under stock code 5819.

The holding company above it was previously named Hong Leong Credit Berhad and was renamed Hong Leong Financial Group Berhad in July 2006, trading under stock code 1082.

## Structure: HLFG versus HLB

The single most important distinction for readers is that HLFG and HLB are two separate listed companies, one owning the other.

| Entity | Listing (Bursa code) | Role in the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hong Leong Financial Group (HLFG) | Bursa Malaysia (1082) | Parent financial holding company |
| Hong Leong Bank (HLB) | Bursa Malaysia (5819) | Commercial banking (the flagship) |
| HLA Holdings / Hong Leong Assurance | Unlisted (under HLFG) | Life and general insurance, takaful |
| Hong Leong Capital | Bursa Malaysia | Investment banking, broking, asset management |
| Hong Leong Investment Bank | Unlisted (under Hong Leong Capital) | Investment banking |

So when results season reports "Hong Leong Bank's net profit," that is the bank subsidiary; when it reports "HLFG's net profit," that is the consolidated group — bank plus insurance plus investment banking, net of minority interests. HLFG's earnings are dominated by HLB, which is why the two tend to move together.

## Business segments

**Commercial banking (Hong Leong Bank).** This is the core. HLB offers retail, business, Islamic and wholesale banking across Malaysia and the region, and contributes the large majority of HLFG's profit.

**Insurance (HLA Holdings).** HLFG's insurance holding company, HLA Holdings, sits over Hong Leong Assurance Berhad — among the larger domestic life insurers — and Hong Leong MSIG Takaful, a Shariah-compliant insurance joint venture. This arm gives the group exposure to protection and savings products alongside banking.

**Investment banking (Hong Leong Capital).** Separately listed Hong Leong Capital Berhad houses Hong Leong Investment Bank (corporate finance, stockbroking, treasury) and Hong Leong Asset Management (fund management). It is the smallest of the three pillars by earnings and is more cyclical, rising and falling with capital-market activity.

## Regional footprint

Hong Leong Bank is one of the more internationally spread Malaysian banks. Beyond its home market it operates through HL Bank in Singapore, a branch in Hong Kong, Hong Leong Bank Vietnam, and Hong Leong Bank (Cambodia).

Its most distinctive overseas asset is in China. HLB bought into Bank of Chengdu in 2008 — an initial 19.99% stake for about RM877.5 million — which was diluted to around 18% after Bank of Chengdu's Shanghai Stock Exchange listing in January 2018. The holding stood at about 17.8% as of December 2025, when HLB said it was studying a partial sale of up to 5%, a process that could extend into its financial year ending 30 June 2027. HLB also holds a 49% interest in Sichuan Jincheng Consumer Finance, a consumer-finance joint venture formed with Bank of Chengdu in 2010. These China interests are strategic associates rather than controlled subsidiaries, and their equity contribution is a notable swing factor in HLB's results.

## Scale and significance

Hong Leong Bank is firmly inside Malaysia's "big five" banking groups by assets, which also include [Maybank](/en/companies/maybank), [CIMB](/en/companies/cimb), [Public Bank](/en/companies/public-bank) and [RHB](/en/companies/rhb-banking-group). On a Wikipedia-compiled table of assets at 31 December 2023, HLB ranked fifth with about RM265 billion in assets, behind RHB; after HLB completed its acquisition of EON Bank on 6 May 2011, some contemporary reporting described it as the country's fourth-largest lender. The precise ordering among the non-Maybank four varies by year and metric, so current rankings should be checked against the banks' latest disclosures.

At the group level, HLFG reported a record profit before tax of RM6.10 billion and net profit of about RM3.25 billion for its financial year ended 30 June 2025, with total assets of around RM355.2 billion; HLB's own net profit for the same year was about RM4.27 billion. These figures are time-varying and should be verified against the audited annual reports.

## Why it matters

The Hong Leong group illustrates how Malaysia's banking sector is organised around a handful of family- or institution-controlled groups, each built as a holding company over a listed bank. Understanding that HLFG is the holding company and HLB is the bank avoids a common error — double-counting or confusing group and bank earnings. It also explains why Quek Leng Chan's control is exercised through the listed HLFG rather than directly over the bank, and why corporate actions (such as the Bank of Chengdu sell-down) are debated at both levels.

## What's next

- **Bank of Chengdu sell-down.** Watch whether HLB proceeds with disposing of up to 5% of its Bank of Chengdu stake, how it is priced, and whether proceeds flow to shareholders — a process the bank said could run into FY2027.
- **Succession and control.** Reporting has flagged longer-term succession questions around the Quek family's control of the group; any change would be disclosed through Bursa Malaysia filings.
- **Current figures.** For live shareholding percentages, asset size, branch counts and segment earnings, always consult HLFG's and HLB's latest Bursa Malaysia announcements and annual reports rather than relying on older figures.
