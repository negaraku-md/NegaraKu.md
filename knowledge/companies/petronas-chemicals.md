Almost every bag of fertiliser spread on a Malaysian farm, and a large share of the plastic resins moulded in the country's factories, trace back to one company: Petronas Chemicals Group Berhad. It is the chemicals arm of the national oil company, the biggest integrated chemicals producer in Malaysia, and — unusually for a state-linked business — a separately listed company that anyone can buy shares in.

## What is Petronas Chemicals Group?

Petronas Chemicals Group Berhad (PCG) is Malaysia's largest integrated producer of chemicals and petrochemicals. It is a listed subsidiary of [PETRONAS](/companies/petronas) — the national oil and gas corporation, Petroliam Nasional Berhad — which remains PCG's majority shareholder while the rest of the shares trade publicly. PCG takes feedstock such as natural gas and refinery streams from the wider PETRONAS group and converts them into olefins, polymers, fertilisers, methanol and specialty chemicals, selling into both domestic and export markets.

The company is headquartered at the PETRONAS Twin Towers in Kuala Lumpur and is listed on the Main Market of Bursa Malaysia under the ticker PCHEM.

## History: from a PETRONAS division to a listed company

PCG's roots lie in PETRONAS's long-running push to build a domestic petrochemical industry around the country's natural gas reserves, with integrated complexes developed at Kerteh in Terengganu and Gebeng in Pahang from the 1980s and 1990s onward. These scattered chemical businesses were consolidated under the Petronas Chemicals Group banner (the holding company was founded in 1985, per corporate records) ahead of a public listing.

The defining moment came in 2010. PCG shares began trading on the Main Market of Bursa Malaysia on 26 November 2010, with PETRONAS acting as the selling shareholder. The offering raised approximately RM14.78 billion (about US$4.7 billion including the over-allotment option, exercised in full in December 2010) and was described at the time as the largest initial public offering ever in Southeast Asia. PETRONAS retained a majority stake, so PCG became a publicly traded company while still sitting firmly inside the national oil group.

## Business segments

PCG organises its operations into three core reporting segments, supported by an "Others" category for corporate and shared activities.

| Segment | What it produces | Example uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Olefins & Derivatives | Ethylene, propylene and polymers such as polyethylene and polypropylene | Plastics, packaging, consumer and industrial goods |
| Fertilisers & Methanol | Urea, ammonia and methanol | Agriculture; feedstock for downstream chemicals and fuels |
| Specialties | Advanced and specialty chemicals, animal nutrition, coatings and additives | Higher-value industrial and consumer applications |

The **Olefins & Derivatives** segment is the traditional backbone of the business, turning gas-derived feedstock into the building-block chemicals and plastics that underpin much of modern manufacturing. The **Fertilisers & Methanol** segment makes Malaysia a significant regional supplier of nitrogen fertilisers and methanol.

The **Specialties** segment is the newest and strategically most important growth area. It was transformed by PCG's acquisition of Perstorp Holding AB, a Sweden-based specialty chemicals company. PCG completed the RM7.31 billion deal on 12 October 2022, buying the entire equity interest from a vehicle controlled by private equity firm PAI Partners. Management framed the purchase as the centrepiece of its "stepping-out" strategy — a deliberate move up the value chain from commodity petrochemicals into higher-margin specialties, including sustainability-driven products such as Perstorp's "Project Air" sustainable-methanol initiative.

## Major sites

PCG's production is concentrated at a small number of large, integrated complexes, backed by a wider international footprint added through Perstorp.

- **Kerteh, Terengganu** — one of the two original integrated petrochemical complexes, built around olefins, polymers and derivatives fed by natural gas from offshore Terengganu.
- **Gebeng, Pahang** — the second long-established complex, hosting plants across the olefins, derivatives and specialties value chains.
- **Pengerang Integrated Complex (PIC), Johor** — the newest and largest hub, part of PETRONAS's Pengerang Integrated Complex in southern Johor. PIC gives PCG additional olefins, derivatives and specialty-chemical capacity and is central to the group's growth.

Together these sites make Kerteh, Gebeng and Pengerang the three anchors of Malaysia's petrochemical map, with Perstorp adding manufacturing and sales operations across Europe, Asia Pacific and North America.

## Scale and significance

PCG is the dominant chemicals producer in Malaysia and a sizeable player regionally. Its total combined production capacity was reported at about 16.8 million tonnes per annum as at 30 June 2026 (per PCG's 2026 corporate presentation), up from an older figure of 15.4 mtpa — a reminder that capacity figures move as plants are added or debottlenecked. The group employed around 4,500 people as of late 2026.

As a listed company, PCG reports audited results. For the financial year ended 31 December 2024 it recorded a profit after tax of roughly RM1.3 billion, in what the company and analysts described as a challenging market for commodity petrochemicals. Because earnings, capacity, headcount and the exact PETRONAS shareholding all change over time, readers should confirm current figures against PCG's latest annual report and Bursa Malaysia filings rather than relying on any single snapshot.

## Why it matters

PCG sits at the junction of three things that matter to Malaysia's economy. It is a key part of how the country captures more value from its natural gas — instead of merely exporting raw hydrocarbons, PCG converts them into chemicals and plastics worth far more. It is a major employer and exporter, and a cornerstone of the industrial clusters at Kerteh, Gebeng and Pengerang. And as a listed subsidiary of a state-owned parent, it offers a rare public window into part of the PETRONAS group, with disclosures that investors and researchers can scrutinise directly.

## What's next

Several threads are worth watching. First, the Specialties pivot: how successfully PCG integrates Perstorp and grows higher-margin products will shape whether it can cushion the swings of the commodity petrochemical cycle. Second, decarbonisation — PCG has tied initiatives such as sustainable methanol to a broader net-zero-by-2050 ambition, and progress there will matter to both regulators and customers. Third, the balance between commodity and specialty earnings as global chemical markets stay volatile. For current numbers on capacity, profitability and PETRONAS's shareholding, always check PCG's latest Bursa Malaysia disclosures.
