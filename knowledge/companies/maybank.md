Open a current account almost anywhere in Malaysia and there is a good chance it carries the tiger's-head logo of Maybank. Malayan Banking Berhad is the country's largest bank — by the assets on its balance sheet and by what the stock market says it is worth — and one of the biggest banking groups in Southeast Asia. This profile sets out what the group does, where it operates and who owns it.

## What is Maybank?

Maybank is a universal banking group: it takes deposits and lends, but also runs investment banking, Islamic banking, insurance, asset management and brokerage under one roof. The holding company, Malayan Banking Berhad, is listed on [Bursa Malaysia](/finance) under stock code 1155 and is a component of the benchmark FTSE Bursa Malaysia KLCI index. The group describes its purpose as "Humanising Financial Services," the phrase it has used to frame its strategy for more than a decade.

As at 31 December 2025 the group reported total assets of roughly RM1.05 trillion and, for the 2025 financial year, a record net profit of RM10.51 billion on net operating income of RM30.38 billion (The Star, February 2026). It employs more than 43,000 people and operates around 2,600 branches worldwide. Because all of these figures move from year to year, treat them as point-in-time snapshots and check the latest annual report for current numbers.

## How did Maybank start?

Maybank was founded in 1960 — the bank dates its heritage to that year — by the businessman Khoo Teck Puat together with associates, several of whom had banking backgrounds. It opened its first branch in Kuala Lumpur and expanded quickly across the newly independent Federation of Malaya. In 1962 the company was listed on the Kuala Lumpur Stock Exchange, the forerunner of today's Bursa Malaysia. Over the following decades it grew from a domestic lender into a regional group, pushing into Singapore and, later, Indonesia, and building out investment banking and Islamic finance alongside its core retail business.

## What are the group's main businesses?

Maybank organises itself as a universal bank, and its revenue comes from several distinct engines:

- **Consumer and business banking.** The retail and SME franchise — everyday accounts, cards, mortgages, hire purchase and business lending — is the group's largest by customer numbers. Its digital front end is the widely used **Maybank2u** online and mobile banking brand, complemented by the MAE app.
- **Islamic banking.** [**Maybank Islamic**](/finance) is the group's Islamic-banking subsidiary and one of the largest Islamic banks in the world by assets; it was named Global Islamic Bank of the Year 2026 in The Banker's awards (The Star, June 2026). Malaysia operates a dual banking system in which Shariah-compliant banking runs alongside conventional banking, and Maybank is a major player in both.
- **Global and investment banking.** Maybank Investment Bank and the regional Maybank Investment Banking Group (the former Maybank Kim Eng franchise) handle corporate lending, capital markets, advisory, treasury and stockbroking across the region.
- **Insurance and takaful.** The group's insurance arm, **Etiqa**, sells conventional insurance and Islamic takaful products across Malaysia and the wider region.
- **Asset management and other services.** Maybank Asset Management and related units round out the group.

## Where does Maybank operate?

Maybank's reach is what most distinguishes it from a purely domestic bank. It operates across all ten ASEAN member states and maintains a presence in other Asian markets and global financial centres, for a presence in roughly 18 countries. Its three home markets are Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia. In Indonesia the group operates through the listed subsidiary PT Bank Maybank Indonesia Tbk; in Singapore it is a long-established full-service bank. This ASEAN-wide network makes Maybank one of the region's genuinely cross-border banking groups, rather than a national champion with a few overseas outposts.

## Who owns Maybank?

Maybank is majority-owned, in effect, by Malaysia's government-linked investment institutions. Its largest shareholders are funds managed by Permodalan Nasional Berhad (PNB) — above all Amanah Saham Bumiputera (ASB), the national unit trust — together with the Employees Provident Fund (EPF), the country's retirement savings fund. Reported stakes shift with every disclosure: in 2026, ASB was cited as holding roughly 30% and EPF around 12%, with PNB-managed holdings in aggregate reported near the low 40s in percentage terms. These proportions change frequently, so always check the latest Bursa Malaysia filings for the current register. The upshot is that a large share of Maybank's profits and dividends flows back to ordinary Malaysian savers through these funds.

## Why it matters

Maybank sits at the centre of Malaysia's financial system. As the largest bank, its lending, deposit rates and credit decisions ripple through the wider economy, and it is supervised by [Bank Negara Malaysia](/finance), the central bank. Its scale in Islamic finance also makes it important to Malaysia's ambition to be a global hub for Shariah-compliant banking. For comparison, Malaysia's other large listed banks include [CIMB](/companies/cimb) and [Public Bank](/companies/public-bank), which together with Maybank dominate the domestic market.
