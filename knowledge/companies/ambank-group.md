If you bank in Malaysia, you have very likely seen the AmBank name. Behind it sits AMMB Holdings Berhad, the Bursa Malaysia-listed company that heads one of the country's larger banking groups — and whose story runs from 1970s Arab-Malaysian merchant banking, through a long Australian partnership, to a landmark 2021 settlement tied to the 1MDB scandal.

## Overview

AmBank Group is the banking group headed by **AMMB Holdings Berhad**, listed on Bursa Malaysia under the ticker AMBANK. It is a universal banking group whose principal operating subsidiaries are **AmBank (M) Berhad** (the commercial bank), **AmBank Islamic Berhad** (its Islamic bank) and **AmInvestment Bank Berhad** (investment banking), complemented by life and family-takaful ventures (AmMetLife and AmMetLife Takaful) and a general-insurance interest through Liberty General Insurance (AmBank Group corporate site).

The group reported total assets of about **RM199.0 billion as at 31 March 2025** (FY2025 group financial highlights) and describes serving **over three million** individual and corporate customers. It is commonly ranked **Malaysia's sixth-largest banking group by assets** (The Edge Malaysia), placing it behind the larger universal banks such as [Maybank](/companies/maybank) and [CIMB](/companies/cimb) but firmly among the country's established lenders — see [Malaysia's big banks](/companies/malaysias-big-banks) for how the tiers compare.

## History: from Arab-Malaysian roots to AmBank

The group's lineage reaches back to **1975**, when the business that grew into AmBank was founded in the Arab-Malaysian merchant-banking tradition of the era. AMMB Holdings Berhad became the listed holding company of the then **Arab-Malaysian Banking Group**, and over the following decades the retail-facing brand was simplified to **AmBank**. Today AmBank's corporate materials describe "50 years of expertise in supporting the economic development of Malaysia" (AmBank Group corporate site), consistent with that 1975 origin.

A defining feature of the group's modern history was its Australian shareholder. **Australia and New Zealand Banking Group (ANZ)** became a major strategic shareholder in AmBank, building a holding of roughly **21.7%** and placing a nominee on the board. That partnership unwound over 2024. In **March 2024** ANZ sold a **16.5%** block (a trade valued at over RM2.1 billion), cutting its holding to about 5.2%. On **31 May 2024** ANZ announced the sale of its **remaining 5.2%** — some 171.02 million shares — via a block trade at **RM4.10 per share**, for about RM701 million, with settlement expected on 5 June 2024 (ANZ, 31 May 2024). ANZ's chief financial officer described the disposal as "a significant milestone in delivering on our strategy to simplify the Bank." The Star reported that the sale marked ANZ's full exit from AMMB (The Star, 6 June 2024). Around that time the Employees Provident Fund was reported as AMMB's largest shareholder, at about 14.33% (The Star).

## Business segments

AmBank Group runs as a universal bank, offering a full range of conventional and Islamic financial services across the following principal areas:

| Entity / segment | Role | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AMMB Holdings Berhad | Listed holding company | Group parent, Bursa Malaysia (ticker AMBANK) |
| AmBank (M) Berhad | Commercial bank | Retail, business/SME and wholesale banking, treasury and markets |
| AmBank Islamic Berhad | Islamic bank | Shariah-compliant banking, financing and Islamic capital markets |
| AmInvestment Bank Berhad | Investment bank | Corporate finance, capital markets, funds and wealth |
| AmMetLife / AmMetLife Takaful | Insurance ventures | Life insurance and family takaful |
| Liberty General Insurance | Associate/interest | General insurance |

In practice the business divides into personal and business banking (including SME lending and treasury and markets), Islamic banking and Islamic capital markets, investments and wealth (AmWealth, AmEquities and private banking), and insurance and takaful (AmBank Group corporate site). The group has positioned business and SME banking as a particular growth priority in its recent strategy.

## The 2021 1MDB-related settlement

In February 2021 the AmBank Group reached a high-profile settlement with the Malaysian government over its role in transactions connected to **1Malaysia Development Berhad (1MDB)**, the state fund at the centre of a global corruption investigation.

On **26 February 2021** the Ministry of Finance announced that AMMB Holdings had agreed to **"a RM2.83-billion global settlement on all outstanding claims and actions"** relating to the AmBank Group's involvement in 1MDB (Ministry of Finance Malaysia). The ministry stated that the settlement was **separate from** a RM53.7 million penalty that Bank Negara Malaysia had earlier imposed and that AmBank had paid, and that it **did not affect** Malaysia's claims against individuals such as Jho Low and Jasmine Loo, who would continue to be pursued. The ministry also noted that AmInvestment Bank was required to strengthen its due-diligence procedures for corporate proposals.

AMMB booked a RM2.83 billion provision for the fourth quarter of its financial year ended 31 March 2021. The Edge Malaysia reported that the sum was equivalent to nearly 30% of the banking group's market capitalisation of about RM9.51 billion at the time, and that the settlement related to the group's handling of a RM5.0 billion bond issue by 1MDB in 2009 (The Edge Malaysia). Finance Minister Tengku Zafrul said the settlement would enable a faster recovery of funds than prolonged litigation (Ministry of Finance Malaysia).

## Scale and significance

AmBank Group is one of the long-established pillars of Malaysia's banking system. With total assets of about **RM199.0 billion as at 31 March 2025** (FY2025) and FY2025 net profit of roughly RM2.0 billion, it is smaller than the country's top-tier universal banks but materially larger than most non-bank lenders. Its corporate site cites over three million customers, more than 8,000 employees and around 163 branches (figures as at 31 March 2026). All of these figures vary over time and should be confirmed against the group's latest annual report and Bursa Malaysia filings.

Its significance also lies in its history: the 1970s Arab-Malaysian merchant-banking roots, a nearly two-decade strategic partnership with a major Australian bank, and a settlement that became one of the most-cited corporate episodes of the 1MDB affair.

## Why it matters

For customers and businesses, AmBank is a full-service bank with a notable presence in business and SME banking, Islamic finance and investment banking. For investors and researchers, AMMB Holdings is a cleanly listed proxy for a mid-sized Malaysian banking franchise whose ownership has recently shifted from a strategic-shareholder model (ANZ) to a more widely held register — a change that affects governance, free float and strategic direction. The 2021 settlement remains a reference point in Malaysia's wider 1MDB recovery effort.

## What's next

- **Post-ANZ ownership.** With ANZ fully exited, watch Bursa Malaysia substantial-shareholder disclosures for how the register settles and whether any new strategic investor emerges.
- **Business-banking strategy.** The group has signalled growth ambitions in business and SME banking and in wealth; its quarterly results will show whether that focus is lifting returns.
- **Capital and dividends.** Track AMMB's capital ratios and dividend policy in each set of results, and confirm current asset, profit and headcount figures against the latest audited annual report rather than relying on older numbers.
