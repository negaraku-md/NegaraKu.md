Every year-end, roughly 11.4 million unit holders await the same announcement: how much will the annual Amanah Saham Bumiputera (ASB) distribution be? It is a financial ritual deeply rooted in the nation's economic policy — and behind it stands an institution called Permodalan Nasional Berhad.

## What is PNB and how did it begin?

Permodalan Nasional Berhad (PNB) was established on 17 March 1978 as an instrument of the government's New Economic Policy (NEP). It followed the establishment of Yayasan Pelaburan Bumiputra (YPB) on 9 January 1978, which was created to foster equity ownership among the Bumiputera community.

According to PNB's official website, its mandate is to enhance the economic wealth of the Bumiputera community and all Malaysians, for the prosperity of the nation. The underlying model is simple: shares in large Malaysian companies are acquired and transferred into trust funds, then sold back to investors in the form of smaller, more affordable units.

A year later, on 22 May 1979, Amanah Saham Nasional Berhad (ASNB) was established as PNB's wholly-owned unit trust management company. PNB remains the investment manager for these funds, while ASNB acts as the fund manager dealing with unit holders.

## What is the role of ASNB and its funds?

ASNB manages 18 unit trust funds, which fall into two main categories:

- **Fixed-price funds** — including ASB and Amanah Saham Malaysia (ASM), where the price per unit is maintained at RM1.00.
- **Variable-price funds** — including ASN Equity, ASN Imbang and ASN Sara, whose unit prices fluctuate according to market value.

The scale of ASNB's operations is among the largest in the local industry. The table below summarises the key figures as reported in PNB's press release dated 19 December 2025:

| Indicator | Figure (as at 2025) |
| --- | --- |
| PNB assets under management (AUM) | RM364 billion (up 4.5%) |
| Number of ASNB unit trust funds | 18 funds |
| Units in circulation | ~280 billion units |
| Unique account holders | 13 million |
| ASNB branches | 32 branches |
| Agents (including banks) | 2,700+ agents |
| Total ASNB distribution (2025) | RM15.3 billion |

Note: the same press release refers to units in circulation of "approximately 280 billion units across 15 million accounts" in its corporate notes, while stating the number of **unique account holders** stands at 13 million in its main text. This discrepancy reflects that a single individual may hold more than one account; it is not reconciled in the source and should be reviewed by a human reviewer.

## How does ASB work?

Amanah Saham Bumiputera was launched on 2 January 1990. Its key features, as stated in the official ASB Product Highlights Sheet (PHS) — except for the investor eligibility criteria, which are detailed in the ASNB Master Prospectus and **not** in the PHS:

- **Fund category:** Mixed Asset; the fund type is Income.
- **Unit price:** Fixed at RM1.00 per unit. It is **not** a capital guaranteed fund.
- **Eligibility:** Open to Bumiputera individuals — Malays, natives of Sabah and Sarawak, and the Orang Asli. These eligibility criteria are stated in the ASNB Master Prospectus, not in the ASB PHS.
- **Asset allocation:** Up to 90% in equities, up to 50% in other asset classes, and a minimum of 5% in cash or liquid assets.
- **Benchmark:** Maybank 12-Month Fixed Deposit Rate.
- **Annual management fee:** 0.35% per annum of the Net Asset Value (the deed permits up to 1.0%).
- **Trustee:** AmanahRaya Trustees Berhad.

Effective 25 May 2026, ASB introduced two unit classes: Class A (without zakat deduction) and Class B (with a zakat payment facility for Muslim investors). Both classes retain the same fixed-price fund features.

## How much is the ASB distribution and how is it calculated?

For the financial year ended 31 December 2025 (FY2025), ASNB declared an income distribution of **5.75 sen per unit** for ASB. This payout totalled **RM10.4 billion** and benefited **11.4 million unit holders** — the highest annual distribution quantum since ASB's inception. This figure brings ASB's cumulative income distribution to RM206.7 billion.

According to PNB's press release, the ASB distribution calculation is based on the **minimum average monthly balance** held throughout the fund's financial year. The distribution rate is not guaranteed and may rise or fall, as it depends on the profits realised by the fund as well as market conditions.

The following table shows the ASB distribution per unit for the three most recent financial years, as reported in PNB's press release:

| Financial year (31 Dec) | Distribution per unit (sen) |
| --- | --- |
| 2025 | 5.75 |
| 2024 | 5.75 |
| 2023 | 5.25 |

Note: in the ASB PHS, this annual headline figure is presented in two components (income distribution + bonus) that add up to the same headline rate; PNB's press release presents the combined headline figure as above.

For FY2025, the Maybank 12-Month Fixed Deposit Rate benchmark recorded an average of 2.29% (according to PNB's press release, an average up to 28 November 2025), making the spread of ASB's distribution over the benchmark 346 basis points. (The ASB PHS lists a benchmark figure of 2.28% for the same period; this small difference should be reviewed.) This performance was achieved in a challenging local equity market environment, with the FBM KLCI recording a price return of -0.3% for the year-to-date period in 2025 as stated in the body of the press release.

## What is the LEAP-6 Strategic Plan?

LEAP-6 is PNB's current strategic plan. According to the President and Group Chief Executive Officer of PNB, 2025 was the second year of the plan's implementation. The stated priorities include growing net client inflows, delivering sustainable income distributions to unit holders, and strengthening PNB's proprietary capital.

As a result, PNB's AUM rose 4.5% to RM364 billion in 2025. PNB stated that it is on track to achieve its target of **RM400 billion in AUM by 2027**. Its investment strategy includes diversification into global assets, private investments and real estate. PNB also expressed a cautious stance on the 2026 investment outlook following global capital market uncertainty.

## Where does PNB sit within the economic policy structure?

This section aims to explain the structure, not to evaluate it. PNB and ASB exist within an affirmative action framework arising from the NEP — a policy whose fundamental objective, as stated in PNB's official materials, is to foster Bumiputera equity ownership and reduce poverty.

The structure of ASNB's funds reflects the two dimensions of this mandate. Funds such as the ASB series are reserved for Bumiputera investors based on the eligibility criteria stated in the ASNB Master Prospectus. At the same time, PNB frames its role more broadly — namely to benefit the Bumiputera community **and** all Malaysians — and its portfolio also encompasses other funds under ASNB's management.

Targeted equity ownership policies such as this form part of an ongoing public policy discussion in Malaysia, with various parties putting forward differing views on their design and effects. This article takes no position on that matter; it merely explains how these institutions and funds are structured and function based on official sources.

## What comes next

For readers who wish to understand more, the most authoritative documents are the ASNB Master Prospectus and the Product Highlights Sheet (PHS) of each fund, which can be obtained at the ASNB website, its branches and its agents. The investor eligibility criteria in particular are contained in the ASNB Master Prospectus, not in the PHS. Each annual distribution declaration is announced through PNB's official press release at the end of the financial year.
