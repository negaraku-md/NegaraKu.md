Most monarchies in the world answer one and the same question in one and the same way:
who becomes the next king? The answer is usually descent, and the term is usually for
life.

Malaysia answers it differently. The Supreme Head of the Federation is elected — by the
Rulers themselves, from among themselves, for a term of five years, according to a list
that is rearranged after every election. That arrangement is written into the Third
Schedule of the Federal Constitution.

## At a Glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Yang di-Pertuan Agong |
| Constitutional position | Supreme Head of the Federation (Article 32(1)) |
| Elected by | Conference of Rulers (Article 32(3)) |
| Term of office | Five years |
| Eligibility | A reigning Ruler of a state (Third Schedule, Part I) |
| Election procedure | The Third Schedule and the Conference of Rulers' Regulations |
| Oath of office | Before the Conference of Rulers, in the presence of the Chief Justice (Article 37(1)) |
| Deputy | Deputy Yang di-Pertuan Agong (Article 33) |
| Consort | Raja Permaisuri Agong (Article 32(2)) |
| Seat | Istana Negara, Kuala Lumpur |

## Why This Mechanism Has No Parallel

Nine states in Malaysia have a Ruler. Four others — Melaka, Penang, Sabah and Sarawak —
do not have a Ruler, and their respective State Constitutions provide for the Yang
di-Pertuan Agong to be Head of the religion of Islam in that state (Article 3(3)).

The Fifth Schedule provides that the Conference of Rulers consists of the Rulers **and**
the Yang di-Pertua Negeri. But for the business of electing and removing the Yang
di-Pertuan Agong, the Constitution limits participation to the Rulers alone. Only a
reigning Ruler may be elected; the Yang di-Pertua Negeri are not candidates.

That is what makes the arrangement extraordinary: nine hereditary kings vote among
themselves to decide who will hold the highest federal office, and they repeat that
exercise every five years.

## How the Election Works

Paragraph 6 of the Fifth Schedule directs the Keeper of the Rulers' Seal to summon the
Conference of Rulers **not later than four weeks before** the Yang di-Pertuan Agong's
term of office expires, and also whenever a vacancy occurs.

Part I of the Third Schedule then governs the election itself:

1. **Eligibility.** A Ruler is qualified for election unless he is not of full age, has
   notified the Keeper of the Rulers' Seal that he does not wish to be elected, or the
   Conference of Rulers by secret ballot decides that he is unsuited by reason of
   infirmity of mind or body or any other reason.
2. **A threshold of five votes.** Such a resolution may not be passed unless at least
   five members of the Conference vote in support of it.
3. **Offer according to the list.** The Conference of Rulers offers the office to the
   Ruler qualified for election whose state stands first on the election list. If he
   does not accept it, the offer moves to the next state, and so on until a Ruler
   accepts it.
4. **Declaration.** Once the offer is accepted, the Conference of Rulers declares him
   elected, and the Keeper of the Rulers' Seal notifies the decision in writing to both
   Houses of Parliament.

The official Conference of Rulers portal explains that the voting is conducted in secret
using unnumbered ballot papers, marked with pen and ink, and that a Ruler needs to obtain
a majority of five votes before the office is offered to him. The Keeper of the Rulers'
Seal handles the distribution and counting of the ballot papers.

After being elected, Article 37(1) requires the Yang di-Pertuan Agong to take and sign
the oath of office in Part I of the Fourth Schedule before the Conference of Rulers and
in the presence of the Chief Justice of the Federal Court, before exercising his
functions. The oath is witnessed by two persons appointed by the Conference of Rulers.

## The Election List: A Rotation That Moves

This is the part most often misunderstood. The Constitution does not set out a fixed
rotation. It sets out a list that **changes**.

Section 4 of the Third Schedule reads, in effect, as follows:

- For the first election, the list contains the states of all the Rulers, in the order
  of precedence recognised among them at that time.
- After every election, any state that preceded the state of the elected Ruler is moved
  to the end of the list, and the state of the elected Ruler is **dropped**.
- When there is a change of Ruler for a state that is on the list, that state is moved
  to the end of the list.
- When no state remains on the list, the list is **reconstituted**: it once again
  contains the states of all the Rulers, with the states whose Rulers had held the
  office of Yang di-Pertuan Agong arranged in the order in which they held that office.
- After every election under the reconstituted list, the state that preceded the state
  of the elected Ruler is moved to the end, and the state of the elected Ruler is placed
  last.

The Conference of Rulers portal records that the first round ended when the Sultan of
Perak became the ninth Yang di-Pertuan Agong, and that elections thereafter followed the
list reconstituted on the basis of the precedence of states whose Rulers had held the
office, from the first to the ninth.

The Yang di-Pertuan Agong presently on the throne is the **seventeenth**. According to
official reports, Sultan Ibrahim Sultan Iskandar began his reign on 31 January 2024, and
the Installation Ceremony was held at the Balairung Seri of Istana Negara on 20 July
2024, for a term of five years.

## Power on Advice, and Power in Discretion

Article 39 vests the executive authority of the Federation in the Yang di-Pertuan Agong.
Article 40 then explains how that authority is exercised — and this is where the most
important distinction in this whole topic lies.

**Article 40(1).** In carrying out his functions under the Constitution or federal law,
the Yang di-Pertuan Agong shall act in accordance with the advice of the Cabinet or of a
Minister acting under the general authority of the Cabinet, except as otherwise provided
by the Constitution. The same Article gives him the right, on his request, to any
information concerning the government of the Federation which is available to the
Cabinet.

**Article 40(1A).** Where the Yang di-Pertuan Agong is required to act in accordance
with advice, on advice, or after considering advice, he shall accept and act in
accordance with that advice.

**Article 40(2).** He may act in his own discretion in performing three listed
functions, and "in any other case mentioned in this Constitution".

| Category | Examples per the text of the Constitution |
| --- | --- |
| Discretion (Article 40(2)) | Appointing a Prime Minister; withholding consent to a request for the dissolution of Parliament; requesting a meeting of the Conference of Rulers concerned solely with the privileges, position, honours and dignities of the Rulers |
| On advice (Articles 40(1), 40(1A)) | Appointing other Ministers on the advice of the Prime Minister (Article 43(2)(b)); appointing judges on the advice of the Prime Minister after consulting the Conference of Rulers (Article 122B(1)) |

Article 43(2)(a) gives the measure for the appointment of the Prime Minister: the Yang
di-Pertuan Agong shall appoint as Prime Minister a member of the Dewan Rakyat who **in
his judgment** is likely to command the confidence of the majority of the members of the
Dewan Rakyat. It is the phrase "in his judgment" that makes this a matter of his own
consideration.

Article 43(4) completes the picture: if the Prime Minister ceases to command the
confidence of the majority of the members of the Dewan Rakyat, the Prime Minister shall
tender the resignation of the Cabinet, unless at his request the Yang di-Pertuan Agong
dissolves Parliament.

## Other Functions Provided by the Constitution

- **Parliament.** Article 44 provides that Parliament consists of the Yang di-Pertuan
  Agong together with the Dewan Negara and the Dewan Rakyat. See
  [Parliament of Malaysia](/en/government/parliament).
- **Sessions.** Article 55(1) requires him to summon Parliament from time to time, and
  not to allow six months to elapse between the last sitting of one session and the
  first meeting of the next session. Article 55(2) provides that he may prorogue or
  dissolve Parliament.
- **Royal assent.** Article 66(4) sets a period of thirty days to assent to a Bill by
  causing the Great Seal to be affixed to it. Article 66(4A) provides that if assent is
  not given within that period, the Bill becomes law when that period expires, as if it
  had been assented to.
- **Armed forces.** Article 41 makes him the Supreme Commander of the federal armed
  forces.
- **Power of pardon.** Article 42(1) gives the power to grant pardons, reprieves and
  respites for offences tried by court martial and offences committed in the Federal
  Territories of Kuala Lumpur, Labuan and Putrajaya.
- **The religion of Islam.** Article 3(5) makes him Head of the religion of Islam in all
  three Federal Territories. Article 3(2) provides that where the Conference of Rulers
  agrees that any act, practice or ceremony is to extend to the Federation as a whole,
  every other Ruler authorises the Yang di-Pertuan Agong to represent him as Head of the
  religion of Islam.
- **Proclamation of Emergency.** Article 150(1) provides that if the Yang di-Pertuan
  Agong is satisfied that a grave emergency exists, he may issue a Proclamation of
  Emergency.

## Restrictions the Constitution Places on the Office

Article 34 lists several restrictions while he holds office. The Yang di-Pertuan Agong
does not exercise his functions as Ruler of his own state except his functions as Head
of the religion of Islam; does not hold any office of profit; does not actively engage
in any commercial enterprise; and does not leave the Federation for more than fifteen
days without the consent of the Conference of Rulers, except during a State visit.

Article 32(1) provides that no proceedings may be brought against him in any court
except in the Special Court established under Part XV. Article 33A provides that if he
is charged with an offence in that Special Court, he ceases to exercise the functions of
Yang di-Pertuan Agong, and that period is still counted as part of the five-year term.

## Deputy Yang di-Pertuan Agong

Article 33 creates the office of Deputy Yang di-Pertuan Agong, likewise elected by the
Conference of Rulers for a term of five years or for the remainder of the term of the
reigning Yang di-Pertuan Agong. The Deputy exercises his functions during a vacancy in
the office or while he is unable to exercise his functions, but does not do so for an
absence expected to be less than fifteen days unless the Deputy is satisfied that it is
necessary or expedient.

Part II of the Third Schedule sets out how he is elected, and the Conference of Rulers
does not elect a Deputy while the office of Yang di-Pertuan Agong is vacant.

## Common Misunderstandings About This Office

**"The rotation follows alphabetical order or the size of the state."** No. It follows
the election list in section 4 of the Third Schedule, which is mechanically rearranged
after every election and after every change of Ruler of a state.

**"All members of the Conference of Rulers vote."** Not for the election of the Yang
di-Pertuan Agong. The Fifth Schedule includes the Yang di-Pertua Negeri as members of
the Conference, but the Constitution limits the function of electing and removing the
Yang di-Pertuan Agong to the Rulers.

**"He signs laws as a mere formality."** The Constitution provides a specific period,
not a formality without structure: thirty days under Article 66(4), with the
consequence stated in Article 66(4A).

**"Discretionary power means general power."** Article 40(2) lists the discretionary
functions specifically, and adds "in any other case mentioned in this Constitution" —
that is, a case mentioned by the Constitution, not an open-ended category.

**"The Conference of Rulers only elects the Yang di-Pertuan Agong."** Article 38(2)
also gives it other functions, including consenting to certain laws; and Article 38(4)
provides that no law directly affecting the privileges, position, honours or dignities
of the Rulers may be passed without the consent of the Conference of Rulers.
