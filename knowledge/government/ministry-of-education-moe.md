The Ministry of Education — Kementerian Pendidikan Malaysia, universally shortened to **KPM** — is the federal ministry that runs Malaysia's school system. If it concerns a national school, the curriculum a child studies, a public-school teacher's posting, or a school-leaving certificate like the SPM, it sits with KPM. What KPM does *not* do is run the universities: that belongs to a separate ministry, and the boundary trips up a lot of people.

## What KPM is responsible for

KPM's job is defined by the **Education Act 1996 (Act 550)**, which received royal assent on 15 July 1996 and came into force on 1 August 1996, replacing the earlier Education Act 1961. The Act establishes the **National Education System** and gives the ministry its authority over the machinery of schooling.

In practice that authority covers:

- **Curriculum** — setting the national curriculum used in government and government-aided schools.
- **Examinations** — administering national assessments and issuing certificates.
- **Schools** — establishing, registering and managing government and government-aided primary and secondary schools, including national schools (SK) and national-type Chinese and Tamil schools (SJKC, SJKT).
- **Teachers and staff** — training, posting and managing the teaching service, which makes KPM the largest ministry in the federal civil service by headcount (roughly 587,940 staff in 2022).

The system it governs runs from pre-school, through six years of primary and up to five years of secondary schooling, to the post-secondary (pre-university) stage. Malaysia has retooled its examinations in recent years: the **UPSR** primary-school exam was abolished in 2021 and the **PT3** lower-secondary assessment in 2022, both giving way to school-based assessment, while the upper-secondary **SPM** (Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia) remains the main school-leaving certificate.

## Where KPM's remit ends: schools, not universities

The single most common confusion is between KPM and the **Ministry of Higher Education (Kementerian Pendidikan Tinggi, KPT)**. KPM handles schools; KPT handles universities and tertiary institutions. The two have swapped shape several times — tertiary education was hived off to a new higher-education ministry in 2004, the two were merged back into a single Ministry of Education from 2013, then split apart again in 2015 — but as at 2026 they are separate ministries. So a question about a public university, a degree programme, or PTPTN-style tertiary study is a KPT matter, not a KPM one.

## Leadership

The ministry is led by a politically appointed **Minister**, supported by a **Deputy Minister**, with the permanent administration headed by a **Secretary-General** and the professional education service by a **Director-General of Education**. As at 2026 the Minister is **Fadhlina Sidek**, in office since 3 December 2022, and the Deputy Minister is **Wong Kah Woh**, appointed on 12 December 2023.
