When floodwaters rise on Malaysia's east coast each monsoon, the orange-uniformed personnel wading through the water to reach stranded families are usually from **APM**. The **Malaysia Civil Defence Force** — *Angkatan Pertahanan Awam Malaysia*, or **APM** — is the federal uniformed agency responsible for civil defence and disaster response: flood relief, search-and-rescue, humanitarian aid and safety support at large public events. It operates under the **Civil Defence Act 1951 (Act 221)** and, since 2015, the **Prime Minister's Department**, and it is built around one of the country's largest pools of trained volunteers.

## What APM is

APM describes itself on its official portal as "a national emergency response and civil protection agency," carrying out rescue, protection and humanitarian operations in response to enemy threats, disasters and emergencies ([civildefence.gov.my](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/categories/safety-and-community/public-safety/role-of-the-malaysia-civil-defence-force-apm)). Its motto is *Sedia, Pantas, Berintegriti* — ready, fast, with integrity. In English it is styled the Malaysia Civil Defence Force; the Malay name APM is the form most used in day-to-day news coverage.

Crucially, APM is a *civil* defence body, distinct from the armed forces and the police. Its focus is protecting the civilian population — before, during and after a crisis — rather than combat or law enforcement.

## History: from passive defence to JPAM to APM

Civil-defence work in what is now Malaysia dates to **1939**, when the British administration organised "passive defence" measures ahead of the Second World War, according to APM's official history ([civildefence.gov.my](https://www.civildefence.gov.my/apm-history/?lang=en)). The **Civil Defence Ordinance** was enacted in **1951**, and a dedicated Civil Defence Department was established on **24 March 1952** to administer it.

After independence the Ordinance became the **Civil Defence Act 1951 (Act 221)**. A significant turning point came in **1972**, when the Act was amended to add peacetime disaster relief to its original wartime civil-protection duties ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Civil_Defence_Force)) — the legal foundation for the flood and disaster work APM is best known for today.

The agency's name has changed several times. It was long the Civil Defence Department (*Jabatan Pertahanan Awam Malaysia*, **JPAM**). Amendments to the Act that took effect in **2016** reconstituted it as the **Malaysia Civil Defence Force**; Wikipedia records that "Until 31 August 2016 it was known as the Civil Defence Department." (The exact naming timeline differs slightly between the official history and secondary sources — see *verificationNeeded*.)

## What APM does

APM's statutory functions flow from the Civil Defence Act 1951. Its official "functions and roles" page ties its duties to specific provisions ([civildefence.gov.my](https://www.civildefence.gov.my/functions-and-roles/?lang=en)):

| Function | What it involves | Statutory basis cited |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disaster relief & recovery | Rescue, protection of life and property, and helping victims recover after disasters | Act 221, s.8(1) |
| Force management & readiness | Establishing, equipping, training and disciplining the Force and Service | Act 221, s.3(2)(a) |
| Public civil-defence training | Training as many people as possible in civil-defence knowledge | Act 221, s.3(2)(c) |
| Information & warnings | Assessing and issuing advisories and warnings to the public | — |

In practice this means **flood and disaster response** (evacuation, boat rescue, relief-centre support); **search-and-rescue**, delivered through its specialist arm **PASPA** (*Pasukan Khas Pertahanan Awam*), which handles urban search-and-rescue (USAR), water, jungle, air and other high-risk rescue; **humanitarian relief** nationwide; and **public-event safety**, where APM personnel are a routine presence at festivals, sporting events and mass gatherings. During the northeast monsoon, APM runs round-the-clock operations centres and places thousands of personnel on standby across flood-prone states.

## Where APM sits in the disaster-management framework

Oversight of the Civil Defence Force moved to the **Prime Minister's Department** in 2015, having earlier sat under the Ministry of Home Affairs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Civil_Defence_Force)). APM's national headquarters is in Sungai Merab, Kajang, Selangor, and it is organised into state and district commands plus civil-defence units nationwide.

APM is one of several agencies in Malaysia's disaster-management machinery and is not the overall coordinator. That role belongs to the **National Disaster Management Agency (NADMA)**, also under the Prime Minister's Department, which runs the national disaster control centre and links state operations centres during major events. Within that structure, APM acts as **secretariat to the district and state disaster-management committees**, according to a NADMA statement reported during flood operations ([The Vibes](https://www.thevibes.com/index.php/articles/news/50533/disaster-management-committees-to-coordinate-post-flood-actions-nadma)). In short: NADMA coordinates and directs at the policy and inter-state level, while APM is one of the front-line operational responders on the ground.

## Volunteers and how to join

A defining feature of APM is its **volunteer base**. Alongside its career officers, the force relies heavily on trained volunteers (*sukarelawan*), and recent government statements have described a membership numbering in the tens of thousands, with stated expansion targets (exact current figures should be verified — see *verificationNeeded*). Volunteers receive civil-defence and rescue training and are mobilised for floods, emergencies and event duties.

People interested in volunteering can approach their nearest APM state or district office or the official portal (**civildefence.gov.my**) for current intake requirements, which typically cover age, residency and a basic training commitment.

## Why it matters

Malaysia faces recurring natural hazards — above all seasonal flooding driven by the northeast monsoon, alongside landslides, storms and other emergencies. APM is one of the agencies the public encounters most directly when disaster strikes: the uniformed responders at the relief centre, the boat crew during an evacuation, the safety team at a large event. Understanding that APM is a *civil* protection force governed by the Civil Defence Act 1951, that it works under NADMA's coordination rather than independently, and that much of its strength comes from community volunteers helps set realistic expectations about who does what when an emergency unfolds.
