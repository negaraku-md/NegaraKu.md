The **Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI)** is the Malaysian federal ministry responsible for planning, formulating and implementing policy on investment, international trade and industrial development. In short: MITI sets the rules and strategy, negotiates Malaysia's trade agreements, and steers the country's industrial direction — while its statutory agencies do the front-line work of promoting and approving specific investment and export deals.

## What MITI does

MITI's core functions cover four broad areas: driving foreign and domestic investment, promoting Malaysia's exports of manufactured goods and services, negotiating and administering bilateral, regional and multilateral trade agreements, and developing the manufacturing and industrial base. It is the lead ministry for Malaysia's participation in trade blocs and pacts such as the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) and the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP).

The ministry is a **policy body**, not a service counter. Investors seeking manufacturing licences or incentives deal with MIDA; exporters seeking market access support deal with MATRADE. MITI provides the framework those agencies operate within.

## The 2023 rename

For over three decades the ministry was the **Ministry of International Trade and Industry**. In April 2023 the Cabinet approved a rename to the **Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry**, publicly announced on 12 April 2023, to put investment front and centre. Because both names shorten to **MITI**, the acronym was kept — so the same familiar four letters now stand for a slightly different mandate.

## Key agencies under MITI

MITI oversees a family of statutory bodies and government-linked companies. The two most prominent are:

| Agency | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **MIDA** (Malaysian Investment Development Authority) | Promotes and evaluates investment in manufacturing and services; the focal point for federal investment promotion since 1 January 2024. |
| **MATRADE** (Malaysia External Trade Development Corporation) | Malaysia's national export promotion agency, established under the MATRADE Act 1992. |

Other bodies in MITI's portfolio include the Malaysia Productivity Corporation (MPC), SIRIM Berhad and the Department of Standards Malaysia (standards and quality), the Export-Import Bank of Malaysia (EXIM Bank) and Malaysian Industrial Development Finance (MIDF) for trade and industrial financing, and sector institutes such as the Malaysia Steel Institute.

From **1 January 2024**, MITI centralised federal-level investment promotion and marketing functions under MIDA, so that the various investment promotion agencies stopped duplicating one another and investors faced a single national entry point.

## Industrial policy: NIMP 2030

MITI's flagship industrial blueprint is the **New Industrial Master Plan 2030 (NIMP 2030)**, launched in September 2023. It sets Malaysia's industrial direction through 2030 using a mission-based approach built around four missions — advancing economic complexity, digital transformation, the push toward net zero, and safeguarding economic security and inclusivity. NIMP 2030 targets around **RM95 billion** in total investment and roughly **3.3 million** jobs over its span.

## Leadership

MITI is headed by a Cabinet minister supported by a deputy minister and a secretary-general. In the December 2025 Cabinet reshuffle, **Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani** was appointed Minister of Investment, Trade and Industry, with **Sim Tze Tzin** as deputy, succeeding **Tengku Zafrul Abdul Aziz**, who had led the ministry since December 2022. The ministry's headquarters is **Menara MITI** at No. 7, Jalan Sultan Haji Ahmad Shah, Kuala Lumpur, within the KL Metropolis development.

## What's next

If you are pursuing a specific investment or export activity, the practical next step is the relevant agency rather than the ministry itself — see the pages on **MIDA** (investment approval and incentives) and **MATRADE** (export promotion). For how MITI's industrial policy fits within Malaysia's broader development planning, see the **Ministry of Economy** and its national plans.
