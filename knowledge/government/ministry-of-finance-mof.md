The Ministry of Finance is the federal ministry that manages the money of the Government of Malaysia — the revenue it collects, the money it spends, and the debt it raises. In Malay it is the Kementerian Kewangan; its core administrative body is the Treasury, or Perbendaharaan. This page is a short reference to what the ministry is, which laws empower it, and which agencies sit beneath it. For the detail on the Budget and national debt, see [Ministry of Finance (Treasury)](/government/ministry-of-finance-treasury).

| --- | --- |
| Name | Kementerian Kewangan Malaysia / Ministry of Finance (MOF) |
| Core machinery | Treasury of Malaysia (Perbendaharaan) |
| Minister of Finance | Dato' Seri Anwar Ibrahim (Prime Minister) |
| Top civil-service officer | Secretary General of Treasury |

| Fiscal-responsibility law | Public Finance and Fiscal Responsibility Act 2023 (Act 850) |

Office holders change with Cabinet reshuffles; verify the current Minister of Finance II, Deputy Minister and Secretary General of Treasury at `mof.gov.my`.

## What the ministry does

The Ministry of Finance is responsible for both sides of the government's balance sheet. It formulates national fiscal policy, prepares and tables the annual Federal Budget, manages the raising and repayment of government borrowings, controls government accounting through the Treasury, and supervises the financial management of the other ministries, departments and statutory bodies. It is also the lead ministry for the financial system, overseeing the tax authorities, the capital market, and public-sector financing through its agencies.

## The statutes that empower it

Three laws frame the ministry's work:

- **Financial Procedure Act 1957 (Act 61)** provides for the control and management of the public finances of the Federation and the States. It establishes the mechanics of public money — the Consolidated Fund, the Contingencies Fund, the role of controlling officers, and the annual statement of accounts.
- **Minister of Finance (Incorporation) Act 1957 (Act 375)** makes the Minister of Finance a body corporate — the **Minister of Finance (Incorporated)**, known as MOF Inc. or MKD. This corporate person has perpetual succession and can hold property, sue and be sued, and enter contracts. It is the vehicle through which the federal government holds shares in government-linked companies and other commercial assets.
- **Public Finance and Fiscal Responsibility Act 2023 (Act 850)**, in force since 1 January 2024, binds the ministry's fiscal management to principles of responsible fiscal management and medium-term numerical targets.

## Departments and agencies under it

The ministry is organised into **departments** that are part of the civil service and **agencies** that are mostly statutory bodies or government-owned companies with their own boards.

| Type | Body | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Department | Treasury of Malaysia | Core machinery of the ministry |
| Department | Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD) | Indirect tax — SST, excise, customs |
| Department | Accountant General's Department | Government accounting and payments |
| Department | Valuation and Property Services Department (JPPH) | Valuation of government real estate |
| Agency | Inland Revenue Board (LHDN) | Direct tax — income tax, stamp duty, RPGT |
| Agency | Bank Negara Malaysia | Central bank; monetary policy and banking regulation |
| Agency | Securities Commission Malaysia | Regulation of the capital market |
| Agency | Employees Provident Fund (EPF) / KWAP | Retirement savings and public-sector pensions |
| Agency | Malaysia Deposit Insurance Corporation (PIDM) | Deposit and takaful insurance |

Two agencies are most often confused because both collect taxes: **LHDN** for direct tax and **Customs** for indirect tax. Both sit under this ministry but are separate authorities.
