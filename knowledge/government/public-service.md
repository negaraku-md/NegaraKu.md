answer: "Malaysia's public service is run by two separate federal bodies with different jobs. The Public Services Commission (Suruhanjaya Perkhidmatan Awam, SPA) is a constitutional commission: Article 144(1) of the Federal Constitution gives it the duty to appoint, confirm in service, place on the pensionable establishment, promote, transfer and exercise disciplinary control over officers within its jurisdiction — the general federal public service, plus the state public services of Melaka, Pulau Pinang, Perlis and Negeri Sembilan. The Public Service Department (Jabatan Perkhidmatan Awam, JPA) is an administrative department, not a constitutional commission: it plans the size and structure of the public service, designs schemes of service, sets salaries, allowances, training and pension policy, and runs the national training institute, INTAN. In short, JPA designs the system a civil servant works inside; SPA decides who fills a post, confirms them in it, and disciplines them if something goes wrong."

A civil servant's career in Malaysia passes through two federal bodies that
most people have never heard of, and neither of them is the ministry the
officer actually works in. One decides whether that person gets appointed,
confirmed, promoted or disciplined. The other decided, long before the
appointment happened, what the post pays, what training it requires, and
whether the post should exist at all.

Those two bodies are the Public Services Commission (SPA) and the Public
Service Department (JPA). They are easy to confuse — both have "public
service" in the name, both sit near the centre of the federal government,
and both deal with the same civil servants. They are not the same thing.

## At a glance

| | SPA | JPA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full name | Suruhanjaya Perkhidmatan Awam (Public Services Commission) | Jabatan Perkhidmatan Awam (Public Service Department) |
| What it is | Constitutional commission | Administrative department |
| Legal basis | Article 144(1), Federal Constitution | Not a constitutional body — an executive department |
| Established | 31 August 1957 | Traces to 1934; took the name JPA on 15 August 1968 |
| Core job | Appoint, confirm, promote, transfer, discipline | Plan, design and manage HR policy for the public service |
| Jurisdiction | General federal public service; state public services of Melaka, Pulau Pinang, Perlis, Negeri Sembilan | Federal public service policy generally |

## SPA: the commission that decides who and what happens to them

SPA's authority does not come from an Act of Parliament that a later
Parliament could quietly amend — it comes from the Federal Constitution
itself. **Article 144(1)** gives a Commission of this kind the duty to
**appoint, confirm in service, place on the permanent or pensionable
establishment, promote, transfer and exercise disciplinary control** over
members of the service or services within its jurisdiction. SPA was
established on **31 August 1957**, on the eve of Merdeka, to carry out that
duty for Malaysia's general public service.

That jurisdiction has a specific boundary. SPA's authority extends to the
**general public service of the Federation**, and beyond that, to the
**state public services of Melaka, Pulau Pinang, Perlis and Negeri
Sembilan** — states that do not run a separate state public service
commission of their own. Other services sit under their own, separate
commissions or councils created by the same Part of the Constitution: the
education service has the Education Service Commission (SPP), and the
police force, the armed forces and the judicial and legal service each have
their own arrangements. "Civil service" in everyday speech covers all of
these; SPA's Article 144(1) duty covers only its own slice of it.

In practice, that makes SPA the body a candidate deals with at entry —
sitting the exam, the interview, the medical and physical checks — and the
body an officer deals with again at confirmation, promotion, transfer, or
if disciplinary proceedings are opened against them. What SPA does not do is
decide how many posts exist, what a post pays, or what training an officer
receives before or after appointment. That is JPA's job.

## JPA: the department that designs the system

JPA is not a constitutional commission — it is a federal administrative
department, and its lineage is older than SPA's. It traces back to the
**Malayan Establishment Office**, set up in **1934** under Sir John Huggins
as Malayan Establishment Officer. The office was renamed several times as
Malaya moved toward and past independence — Federation Establishment
Officer (from 1954, under H. G. Turner from 1957), Principal Establishment
Officer, then **Ketua Pegawai Perjawatan** from 1962. In **1967** the office
was renamed Establishment Office of Malaysia, and the name **Jabatan
Perkhidmatan Awam (JPA)** was first used on **15 August 1968**. Its head has
carried the title **Ketua Pengarah Perkhidmatan Awam (KPPA)** since
Pekeliling Perkhidmatan Bil. 2 Tahun 1968, dated **7 October 1968** — a
title still in use today.

JPA's own corporate materials group its functions into three areas:

- **Perancangan (Planning)** — sizing the public service through structured
  human-resource projections, and determining the organisational and
  remuneration implications of exercises such as privatisation.
- **Pembangunan (Development)** — setting organisational and career-path
  policy, building succession plans, and setting training policy.
- **Pengurusan (Management)** — administering recruitment, placement,
  remuneration, promotion, retirement benefits, service conditions and
  employer–employee relations, then formulating, monitoring and evaluating
  the policies that flow from all of this.

Put simply: before SPA ever appoints anyone to a post, JPA has already
decided what scheme of service that post belongs to, what salary grade it
carries, and what qualifications and training it requires.

## Where JPA's reach shows up day to day

A few of JPA's functions are visible even to officers who never deal with
the department directly:

- **INTAN** (Institut Tadbiran Awam Negara, the National Institute of
  Public Administration) — the public service's central training
  institute, listed among JPA's own principal service links.
- **JUSA placement** — JPA places officers into JUSA grades, the most
  senior tier of the public service's premier/top-management structure.
- **HRMIS** — the government's human-resource management information
  system, which JPA administers for public-service HR data.
- **Pekeliling Perkhidmatan** (Service Circulars) — JPA is the department
  that issues the circulars setting out service conditions, salary
  adjustments and administrative reforms across the public service.

## Common mistakes

**Treating SPA and JPA as interchangeable.** They are not alternates for
the same function. SPA's constitutional duty is limited to appointment,
confirmation, promotion, transfer and discipline. Everything about how a
post is designed, graded, paid and trained for sits with JPA.

**Assuming SPA covers every civil servant in Malaysia.** It covers the
general federal public service plus four specific states' public services.
Education, police, the armed forces and the judicial and legal service each
have their own constitutional arrangements, separate from SPA.

**Assuming either body's current ministerial reporting line is fixed.**
Both SPA and JPA have historical roots as central agencies connected to the
Prime Minister's Department, and Cabinet reshuffles periodically move which
minister oversees which central agency. This page deliberately does not
name a current reporting ministry for either body — see [The Prime
Minister's Department](/en/government/prime-ministers-department) for why
that changes over time.

## What's next

If your question is about an individual civil servant's appointment,
confirmation, promotion or a disciplinary matter, that sits with SPA. If
your question is about how a post is graded, what it pays, what training
pathway it follows, or pension policy, that sits with JPA. For the
constitutional machinery both bodies operate inside, read [The Prime
