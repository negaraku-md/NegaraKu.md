MAICSA is the body most Malaysians name when asked who company secretaries
answer to — which is a useful shorthand and a misunderstanding. MAICSA qualifies
people. SSM licenses them. The two functions are separate, and the gap between
them is where a surprising amount of confusion about the profession sits.

## At a glance

## Standing under the Companies Act 2016

Section 235(2)(a) qualifies a person to be a company secretary if they are a
member of a body set out in the Fourth Schedule. MAICSA states that the
Government prescribed it as an approved body in the Gazette under that
provision.

The Fourth Schedule lists seven bodies in total:

1. Malaysian Institute of Chartered Secretaries and Administrators (MAICSA)
2. Malaysian Institute of Accountants (MIA)
3. Malaysian Bar
4. Malaysian Association of Company Secretaries (MACS)
5. Malaysian Institute of Certified Public Accountants (MICPA)
6. Sabah Law Society
7. Advocates Association of Sarawak

MAICSA's position at the head of that list reflects its subject-matter focus
rather than any superior status — membership of any of the seven satisfies
s.235(2)(a) equally.

*Note on naming: the Fourth Schedule to Act 777 refers to the Sabah Law Society,
while SSM's Part K FAQ refers to the Sabah Law Association. The statutory
wording governs.*

## Qualification is not authorisation

This is the distinction the entity page exists to make.

MAICSA's own profile states that its Fellow and Associate members are qualified
to act as named company secretaries **upon application to the Companies
Commission of Malaysia for a practising certificate under s.241** of the
Companies Act 2016.

So the sequence runs:

1. Become a Fellow or Associate of MAICSA — this satisfies s.235(2)(a).
2. Apply to SSM under s.241 for a practising certificate, currently RM100.
3. Only then may the person act as a company secretary.

Section 238(1)(c) closes the loop from the other direction: a person who ceases
to hold a s.241 practising certificate is disqualified from acting, whatever
their MAICSA standing. Membership and certification are independent, and losing
either stops you acting.

## The alternative route

MAICSA membership is not the only path. Section 235(2)(b) qualifies a person
licensed by SSM under s.20G of the Companies Commission of Malaysia Act 2001 —
the licence route, valid for three years under s.20G(5) and renewable no later
than 30 days before expiry. Practitioners on that route hold no professional
body membership at all, and are equally entitled to a s.241 practising
certificate.

## What MAICSA does beyond qualifying members

MAICSA operates as a technical and professional standards body. It publishes
technical announcements to members on regulatory developments — its November
2024 announcement on SSM's Company Secretary Information Data being one example
of material that circulates to practitioners well before it reaches general
guidance.

That technical output is well regarded and largely member-facing, which is why
authoritative Malaysian secretarial practice is often harder to find publicly
than the subject deserves.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating MAICSA as the regulator.** SSM licenses, registers and issues
  practising certificates. MAICSA confers membership.
- **Assuming MAICSA membership alone permits practice.** Section 241 requires a
  practising certificate first.
- **Confusing MAICSA with MACS.** They are distinct bodies, both in the Fourth
  Schedule.
- **Reading the Fourth Schedule as MAICSA and MIA only.** There are seven
  approved bodies.
- **Citing ICSA as a current name.** The international body is now The Chartered
  Governance Institute.

## What's next

To check whether a specific person may lawfully act, the membership is the wrong
thing to verify — look for a current s.241 practising certificate and check the
secretary through SSM's Company Secretary Information Data. The qualification
guide sets out both routes and the October 2025 changes to certificate renewal.
