## At a glance

| Metric | Value | Reference year | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Statutory minimum wage (monthly) | RM1,700 | In force since 1 Aug 2025 | Minimum Wages Order 2024, P.U.(A) 376 |
| Statutory minimum wage (hourly) | RM8.72 | In force since 1 Aug 2025 | Minimum Wages Order 2024, P.U.(A) 376 |
| Median individual salary & wages | RM2,793/month | 2024 | DOSM Salaries & Wages Survey Report 2024 |
| Mean individual salary & wages | RM3,652/month | 2024 | DOSM Salaries & Wages Survey Report 2024 |
| Median household income | RM7,017/month | 2024 | DOSM Household Income Survey Report 2024 |
| Mean household income | RM9,155/month | 2024 | DOSM Household Income Survey Report 2024 |

These three series measure different things and should not be read as one
ladder. The minimum wage is a legal floor set per employee. Median/mean
salary is what an individual earns from work. Median/mean household income
pools every income source across everyone living in one household — DOSM
records an average of 1.8 income earners and 3.7 persons per household for
2024.

## Why median and mean are both shown

Malaysia's income and wage distributions are right-skewed: most earners sit
below the average, and a smaller number of high earners pull the mean upward.

- Individual salary: median RM2,793 vs mean RM3,652 — the mean is about 31%
  above the median.
- Household income: median RM7,017 vs mean RM9,155 — the mean is about 30%
  above the median.

Whenever only a mean ("average salary") is quoted, it will read higher than
what a typical worker or household actually takes home. The median is the
better number for "what does a typical person/household earn" — the mean is
useful mainly for totals (e.g., estimating aggregate wage bills).

## The minimum wage floor

The Minimum Wages Order 2024 (P.U.(A) 376) was gazetted on 4 December 2024
under the National Wages Consultative Council Act 2011 and set:

- RM1,700 a month
- RM8.72 an hour
- RM65.38 / RM78.46 / RM98.08 a day for a six-, five- or four-day working week
  respectively

It applied from 1 February 2025 to MASCO-classified professional employers and
employers with five or more employees, and to every remaining employer from
1 August 2025 — the small-employer deferment has since ended. Full rules on
scope, deductions and payment timing are covered in
[Minimum Wage and the Rules on Paying Wages](/en/employment/minimum-wage-malaysia).

At RM1,700, the minimum wage sits at roughly 61% of the 2024 median individual
salary of RM2,793 — a gap, not a coincidence, since the median is pulled up by
mid-career and skilled wages that the floor was never meant to track.

## Household income vs individual salary — why they diverge so much

A median household income of RM7,017 looks nearly two and a half times the
median individual salary of RM2,793. The difference is mechanical, not a
contradiction:

- The household figure **sums every earner in the home** — DOSM's 2024 survey
  records an average of 1.8 income earners per household.
- It also includes **non-salary income**: self-employment, property, investment
  returns and current transfers, not only wages.
- The two surveys use **different sampling units** (households vs individual
  salaried/wage employees), so they are not simply the same pie sliced two ways.

## Common mistakes

- **Quoting the mean as "the average salary" without noting the median.** The
  gap between them (around 30% in both series above) is large enough to
  materially mislead a budget or a pay comparison.
- **Comparing a personal salary directly against the household income figure.**
  A single-income household should be benchmarked against median individual
  salary, not median household income.
- **Assuming the minimum wage tracks the median.** It is a statutory floor set
  through a separate legal process (the National Wages Consultative Council),
  not a fixed percentage of any DOSM income series.
- **Treating either DOSM figure as current-year.** Both releases above report
  on 2024 as the reference year; check the release date against the year you
  are quoting, since DOSM issues each report roughly 9–12 months after the
  reference year closes.
