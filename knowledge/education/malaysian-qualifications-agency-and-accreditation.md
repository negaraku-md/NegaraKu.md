Two students graduate with the same-sounding diploma. One qualifies for a government job and a master's place; the other is turned away at the door. The difference is often a single line in a public register: whether the Malaysian Qualifications Agency accredited the programme.

## What does the Malaysian Qualifications Agency actually do?

The Malaysian Qualifications Agency (MQA) is the statutory body responsible for quality assurance in Malaysian higher education. It was established on 1 November 2007 under the Malaysian Qualifications Agency Act 2007, formed by merging two earlier bodies: the National Accreditation Board (Lembaga Akreditasi Negara, LAN) and the Quality Assurance Division of the Ministry of Higher Education.

MQA's job is not to run universities. It sets and enforces the standards that programmes must meet. In practice that means it:

- implements the Malaysian Qualifications Framework (MQF) as the national reference point for qualifications;
- develops the criteria and standards used to judge programmes;
- accredits courses that meet those standards; and
- maintains the Malaysian Qualifications Register (MQR), the public record of accredited qualifications.

The Act gives MQA the authority to accredit programmes, supervise higher education providers, and maintain the register. As the framework states, "No programme will be accredited unless it is in compliance with the Framework."

## What is the Malaysian Qualifications Framework (MQF)?

The MQF is the map. It classifies every qualification in the country into a single, coherent structure so that a diploma from one college and a diploma from another mean the same level of learning. First published in 2007 and enforced from 2011, it is now in its 2nd Edition.

The framework has **eight levels**, each with one official qualification title. It spans two pathways — the academic sector and the Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) sector — using the same level titles for both.

The load at each level is measured in credits. In Malaysia, **one credit equals 40 notional learning hours** — the total time an average student needs, across lectures, self-study, practicals and assessment, to achieve the learning outcomes. Each level carries a mandatory minimum credit load:

| MQF level | Qualification | Minimum credits | Typical duration |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|------------------|
| 1 | Certificate | 15 | — |
| 2 | Certificate | 30 | — |
| 3 | Certificate | 60 | — |
| 4 | Diploma | 90 | 2 years full-time |
| 5 | Advanced Diploma | 40 | ~1 year full-time |
| 6 | Bachelor's Degree | 120 | 3 years full-time (minimum) |
| 7 | Master's Degree (coursework/mixed) | 40 | ~1 year full-time |
| 8 | Doctoral Degree (taught/mixed component) | 80 | ~3 years full-time |

A few things the table cannot show. Foundation and pre-university programmes (such as matriculation or A-Levels) sit outside the framework but are benchmarked to a minimum of 50 credits. Research-based master's and doctoral degrees carry no credit value — the master's normally takes about two years and the doctorate about three. And professional degrees often exceed these minimums: a bachelor's programme accredited jointly with a body such as the Board of Engineers Malaysia or the Malaysian Medical Council typically runs longer and carries more credits.

## Provisional or full — which accreditation matters?

MQA accreditation comes in two stages, and confusion between them causes needless worry.

**Provisional Accreditation** is, in MQA's words, "an exercise to determine whether a programme has met the minimum quality requirements preliminary to Full Accreditation." It is the checkpoint a *new* programme must pass before it can enrol students at all. It is a required condition for the Ministry of Higher Education to approve the programme.

**Full Accreditation** is "an assessment exercise to ascertain that the teaching, learning and all other related activities of a programme... have met the quality standards and are in compliance with the MQF." A programme can only be fully accredited once its first cohort has moved through — typically reaching the final year — so that MQA can see the programme actually delivers its promised outcomes.

The practical takeaway: **provisional does not mean second-rate.** A newly launched degree will legitimately hold only provisional status. What you should confirm is that the status is *current* and *listed*. Both statuses lead to registration in the MQR.

Established institutions may also earn **self-accrediting status** after demonstrating mature internal quality-assurance systems through an institutional audit. MQA then empowers those universities' senates to accredit their own programmes on the MQF, and the programmes are registered in the MQR.

## How do you verify a qualification is recognised?

Do not rely on a glossy brochure or a marketing claim of "MQA-approved." Check the source.

1. **Search the MQR.** The Malaysian Qualifications Register at [www2.mqa.gov.my/mqr](https://www2.mqa.gov.my/mqr/) is free and public. Look up the programme by name or by institution. If it is accredited, it appears there with its status and awarding institution.
2. **Confirm the exact programme, not just the university.** Accreditation attaches to a specific named programme, not to the institution as a whole. "University X is reputable" is not the same as "this diploma at University X is accredited."
3. **Check professional recognition separately** if you plan to practise a regulated profession such as engineering, medicine, law, accountancy or architecture. Ask whether the relevant professional body recognises the programme, which is a distinct step from MQA accreditation.
4. **Ask for the MQS.** On completing an accredited programme, providers issue a Malaysian Qualification Statement (MQS) detailing the learning outcomes, accreditation status and awarding institution.

Why it matters: recognition is what lets a graduate enter public-sector employment, win scholarships, articulate into a higher qualification, and have the credential accepted abroad. An unaccredited qualification can close all of those doors at once.

## What's next

If you are choosing a course, open the MQR and confirm the programme's status *before* you pay a deposit — provisional or full is fine, absent is not. If a programme you are interested in is not listed, ask the institution directly for its MQA reference and status in writing, and treat vague answers as a warning sign. For regulated professions, contact the relevant professional body to confirm the graduate route to registration. Employers and HR teams can use the same register to validate a candidate's stated qualification in minutes.
