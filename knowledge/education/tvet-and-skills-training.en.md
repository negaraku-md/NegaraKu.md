Many Form 3 students and their parents see only one path after secondary
school: SPM, STPM, a degree. But the country actually runs a whole other
education system — with its own institutions, its own certificates and its
own laws — that is rarely explained properly: TVET, or Technical and
Vocational Education and Training.

The most common confusion isn't about whether TVET is good or not. It's
about **who administers what**. An ILP is not the same as a community
college. A vocational college is not the same as a polytechnic. And the
certificate you end up with at the end of that path — the SKM — isn't
issued by any of those colleges themselves, but by a separate department
under yet another ministry.

## Three ministries, four institutional pathways

TVET in Malaysia is not supervised by a single body. It is spread across
three ministries, each with its own institutions:

| Pathway | Administered by | Typical entry | Qualification level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vocational College (KV) | Ministry of Education Malaysia (Technical and Vocational Education and Training Division) | After Form 3 | Malaysian Vocational Certificate (SVM) → Malaysian Vocational Diploma (DVM) |
| ILP, ADTEC, JMTI (ILJTM) | Department of Manpower (Jabatan Tenaga Manusia, JTM), Ministry of Human Resources | Varies by programme and NOSS | Certificate to diploma, certified as SKM/DKM/DLKM by JPK |
| Community College | Department of Polytechnic and Community College Education (Jabatan Pendidikan Politeknik dan Kolej Komuniti, JPPKK), Ministry of Higher Education | SPM, more flexible entry | Community-college-level certificate, short community courses (PSH) |
| Polytechnic | JPPKK, Ministry of Higher Education | SPM with certain credit requirements | Diploma and above |

**Vocational College (KV)** is the earliest-entry pathway — after Form 3,
not after SPM. Students take the Malaysian Vocational Certificate (SVM),
recognised as equivalent to SPM, while also receiving the Malaysian Skills
Certificate (SKM) Level 3 at the same time. Eligible SVM graduates can
proceed to the Malaysian Vocational Diploma (DVM), a diploma-level
qualification (Malaysian Qualifications Framework Level 4) that allows
progression to a bachelor's degree. There is a network of vocational
colleges offering a range of programmes throughout the country.

**ILP (Industrial Training Institute), ADTEC and JMTI** — known
collectively as ILJTM — come under the Department of Manpower (Jabatan
Tenaga Manusia, JTM), Ministry of Human Resources. The first ILP, in Kuala
Lumpur, is one of the country's earliest TVET institutions. These
institutions offer full-time courses and short courses that focus directly
on the needs of the industrial sector.

**Community Colleges and Polytechnics**, both under JPPKK, Ministry of
Higher Education, share administration but differ in focus. Community
colleges focus on certificates with more flexible entry and shorter study
periods, while polytechnics focus on diploma programmes and above.

## SKM isn't a school certificate — it's a separate system

This is the part most often misunderstood: the **Malaysian Skills
Certificate (SKM)** is not issued by the KV, ILP or community college
itself. It is issued by the **Department of Skills Development (Jabatan
Pembangunan Kemahiran, JPK)**, an agency under the Ministry of Human
Resources, under powers granted by the **Akta Pembangunan Kemahiran
Kebangsaan 2006 (Akta 652)**. This Act also established the National
Skills Development Council and set out the **National Occupational Skills
Standard (NOSS)** — a reference document that outlines the competencies
that must be mastered for each skills area, and forms the basis for every
SKM certificate issued.

This certification system has five levels:

- **SKM Levels 1, 2 and 3** — Malaysian Skills Certificate, from basic
  skills to skilled/supervisory level.
- **DKM (Level 4)** — Malaysian Skills Diploma.
- **DLKM (Level 5)** — Malaysian Advanced Skills Diploma.

What is less well known: the SKM can be obtained through **three different
pathways**, not solely by enrolling at an institution:

1. **Training at an accredited training institution** — the usual pathway,
   through ILP, ADTEC, JMTI, a vocational college or a private training
   centre accredited by JPK.
2. **Workplace-based training** — workers are trained and assessed
   directly at the workplace according to the relevant NOSS.
3. **Recognition of Prior Achievement (PPT)** — for individuals who
   already have years of experience in a field but have never held a
   formal certificate, allowing existing skills to be assessed and
   certified without having to undergo full training again.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming all TVET institutions fall under the same ministry.** KV
  comes under the Ministry of Education; ILP/ADTEC/JMTI come under the
  Ministry of Human Resources; community colleges and polytechnics come
  under the Ministry of Higher Education — application procedures and
  entry requirements are not necessarily uniform across the three.
- **Confusing SKM with an academic certificate such as SPM or a
  polytechnic diploma.** SKM is a separate skills certification issued by
  JPK, even though it can be obtained at the same time as studying at a KV
  or ILP.
- **Not realising the PPT pathway exists.** Workers who have long
  experience in a field often don't know their skills can be formally
  certified without having to go through a full course again.

## What's next

If you are a Form 3 student, start by comparing KV entry requirements on
the Ministry of Education portal with polytechnic/community college entry
requirements under JPPKK — these two pathways have separate application
dates and forms. If you are an adult worker who wants to certify existing
skills, contact JPK directly about the Recognition of Prior Achievement
(PPT) pathway for the relevant field. And if your employer sends you for
skills training funded through the HRD Corp levy, also read the guide on
the HRD Corp levy to understand how such training is financed at the
employer level.
