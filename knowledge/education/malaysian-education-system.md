Ask three Malaysians to describe "the education system" and you'll get three different maps. One talks about SK versus SJKC. Another talks about STPM versus matriculation. A third mentions an SKM certificate that nobody outside technical training has heard of. All three are describing the same system — just from different branches of it.

This article is the trunk those branches grow from: which ministry runs which stage, what each exam milestone actually gates, and where the post-SPM crossroads lead.

## Two ministries, one pipeline

The first thing the map needs is a boundary line, because Malaysian education is not run by one ministry.

The **Ministry of Education** (Kementerian Pendidikan Malaysia, KPM) is responsible for preschool, primary and secondary schooling, and also runs its own pre-university matriculation programme through its Matriculation Division. The **Ministry of Higher Education** (Kementerian Pendidikan Tinggi, KPT) is a separate ministry responsible for public and private universities, polytechnics and community colleges.

A third body sits above both once a qualification is awarded: the **Malaysian Qualifications Agency (MQA)**, which accredits post-secondary and higher-education programmes and maintains the **Malaysian Qualifications Framework (MQF)** — the common scale that lets a certificate, diploma or degree from a public or private institution be compared on paper.

## The ladder, stage by stage

| Stage | Approx. ages | Duration | Who runs it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Preschool | 4–6 | Up to 2 years, optional | MOE-registered kindergartens (government and private) |
| Primary | 7–12 | Planned as 6 years | MOE — national schools and national-type schools |
| Lower secondary | 13–15 | 3 years (Form 1–3) | MOE |
| Upper secondary | 16–17 | 2 years (Form 4–5) | MOE |
| Pre-university | 17/18–19 | 1–2 years | MOE (Form Six/STPM, matriculation) or private colleges |
| Tertiary | 19+ | 3–6+ years | KPT-regulated universities, polytechnics, community colleges |

The Ministry of Education's own description of the national system frames primary school as a planned six-year course that some pupils complete over five to seven years, and splits secondary school into lower secondary (Form 1–3, ages 13–15) and upper secondary (Form 4–5, ages 16–17).

## Preschool: optional, but the on-ramp

Preschool education in the national system covers children aged four to six. It is optional rather than compulsory, and is delivered through a mix of government-run and private or community kindergartens operating under Ministry of Education policy. There is no national exam at this stage — the milestone that matters is simply readiness to enter Year 1.

## Primary school: one system, three languages of instruction

Primary education is where Malaysia's best-known structural quirk appears. Pupils can attend a **national school** (Sekolah Kebangsaan, SK), where Bahasa Malaysia is the main medium of instruction, or a **national-type school** (Sekolah Jenis Kebangsaan, SJKC for Chinese-medium or SJKT for Tamil-medium). All of them are government schools under the Ministry of Education, following the same national curriculum framework — the medium of instruction differs, the ministry and the curriculum standard do not.

Primary school is planned as six years but the ministry's own framing allows completion anywhere between five and seven years, which matters for a small number of pupils who repeat a year.

## Secondary school: where the branching starts

Secondary school splits into two blocks:

- **Lower secondary** (Form 1–3, ages 13–15) — general exposure across subjects, still using Bahasa Malaysia as the main medium of instruction.
- **Upper secondary** (Form 4–5, ages 16–17) — students move into streams: pure science, arts/literature, Islamic studies, or technical and vocational programmes, preparing either for higher education or direct entry to work.

Within this, the Ministry of Education recognises several distinct school types beyond the ordinary day school: **fully residential schools** (Sekolah Berasrama Penuh), **national Islamic schools**, **special education schools**, and **sports schools**. Running alongside — but administratively separate — are institutions like MARA's own junior science colleges, which select students into a parallel track outside the standard MOE school list.

## The exam milestones — and the ones that no longer exist

This is the part of the map people get wrong most often, because it has changed. Two national exams that older Malaysians remember well no longer run:

- **UPSR** (Ujian Pencapaian Sekolah Rendah), the Year 6 primary-leaving exam, has been discontinued.
- **PT3** (Pentaksiran Tingkatan Tiga), the Form 3 lower-secondary exam, has also been discontinued.

The Ministry of Education has stated that both exams are "no longer relevant" and that it will instead strengthen ongoing **school-based assessment** at those levels rather than bring back a single national paper.

What remains — and what actually gates progress — is split across two separate statutory examination bodies:

| Exam | Level | Administered by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SPM (Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia) | End of Form 5 | Lembaga Peperiksaan Malaysia (Malaysian Examinations Syndicate) |
| STAM, SPMU (SPM repeat) | Upper secondary / Islamic studies | Lembaga Peperiksaan Malaysia |
| STPM (Sijil Tinggi Persekolahan Malaysia) | Form Six / pre-university | Majlis Peperiksaan Malaysia (Malaysian Examinations Council) |
| MUET (English proficiency) | Pre-university / degree entry | Majlis Peperiksaan Malaysia |

Lembaga Peperiksaan and Majlis Peperiksaan Malaysia sound interchangeable in casual conversation. They are not the same body: one sits inside the Ministry of Education's own structure and runs SPM-level papers; the other is a separate statutory body that runs the STPM and MUET.

## After SPM: the pre-university crossroads

SPM is the point where the branching becomes real. Broadly, four routes lead onward:

1. **Form Six + STPM** — two more years in a government school, examined by Majlis Peperiksaan Malaysia over three semesters.
2. **Matriculation** — a one- to two-year programme run directly by the Ministry of Education's Matriculation Division, a separate administrative track from Form Six.
3. **Private pre-university routes** — foundation programmes or A-Levels delivered by private colleges, assessed by the institution itself or a foreign examination board rather than by LP or MPM.
4. **TVET skills certification** — a parallel track outside both MOE pre-university routes: the **Malaysian Skills Certificate (SKM, Levels 1–3)** and the **Malaysian Skills Diploma / Advanced Diploma (DKM Level 4, DLKM Level 5)**, issued by the **Department of Skills Development (Jabatan Pembangunan Kemahiran, JPK)** — which sits under the **Ministry of Human Resources**, not the Ministry of Education or Ministry of Higher Education at all.

That last point is the one most explainers skip: Malaysia's "education system," once TVET is counted, actually runs through three ministries, not two.

## Tertiary education: many kinds of institution, one quality frame

Once a student clears SPM plus one of the pre-university routes above, tertiary study sits under the Ministry of Higher Education's remit: public universities, private universities and university colleges, polytechnics, and community colleges are all part of the system it oversees.

What ties all of them together on paper is the **Malaysian Qualifications Framework**, implemented by the Malaysian Qualifications Agency. The MQF defines qualification levels running from basic certificates through diplomas to bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees, with foundation and matriculation programmes sitting as preparatory routes into the bachelor's-degree level. Any accredited programme — public or private — is pegged to this framework, which is how a diploma from a private college and a diploma from a public polytechnic can be compared against a common standard.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming UPSR or PT3 results still matter today.** Both exams are discontinued; schools now use ongoing assessment instead.
- **Treating SPM and STPM as run by the same body.** SPM sits with Lembaga Peperiksaan Malaysia; STPM sits with the separate Majlis Peperiksaan Malaysia.
- **Assuming "matriculation" and "Form Six" are two names for the same thing.** They are different programmes under different administration, both leading toward a bachelor's degree.
- **Filing TVET certificates under the Ministry of Education.** SKM and DKM/DLKM certificates come from the Department of Skills Development under the Ministry of Human Resources — a third ministry entirely.
- **Assuming "polytechnic" and "community college" are interchangeable.** Both fall under the Ministry of Higher Education but are distinct institution types offering different qualification levels.
- **Confusing the Ministry of Education (schools) with the Ministry of Higher Education (universities).** They are two separate ministries with two separate portals, budgets and ministers.
