Your child is enrolled at a "free" government school — yet the PIBG form and a list of payments still arrive at your door. So what exactly must be paid, and what must not?

## What is the PIBG and is it mandatory?

The Parent-Teacher Association (PIBG) is an official institution that must exist at every government school and government-aided school. It is established under the **Education Act 1996** and the **Education (Parent-Teacher Association) Regulations 1998**. The association's uniform constitution, meanwhile, is issued through **Professional Circular Letter (SPI) No. 4/2004**.

PIBG membership is **automatic**. Every school teacher and every registered parent or guardian of a pupil becomes a member automatically. The headmaster or principal serves as an *ex-officio* adviser. The association's purpose, as stated in the constitution, is to advance pupils' welfare, strengthen cooperation between parents and teachers, and help expand teaching and learning facilities.

## Am I required to pay PIBG fees?

No. This is the point most often misunderstood. PIBG contributions are **voluntary and cannot be made compulsory**.

There are only three permitted sources of PIBG income:

- Member contributions — voluntary, cannot be made compulsory
- Contributions from outside individuals or organisations
- Proceeds from programmes or activities organised by the PIBG

Paying a contribution is **not a condition** of membership. A member who has not yet paid or does not pay a contribution still has the right to attend and speak at the Annual General Meeting. A school that collects "PIBG fees" by force is not complying with the regulations.

## What is the real cost of schooling at a government school?

Education at government primary and secondary schools carries no tuition fees. Most of the payments that remain are voluntary or subject to agreement.

| Item | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Tuition fees | None — not charged at government schools |
| Special fees | Abolished since 2008 (SPI No. 13/2007) |
| PIBG contribution | Voluntary — cannot be made compulsory |
| Additional payment package / activities | Voluntary, subject to parents' agreement |

Through SPI No. 13/2007, the government abolished special fees at government schools and provided an allocation to fund the activities that were previously paid for under those fees. It was replaced by a **system of additional payment packages** for certain services and facilities — and these payments must be agreed to, not imposed.

## Can a pupil be barred for not paying?

No. A pupil **cannot be barred** from:

- receiving textbooks (under the Textbook Loan Scheme),
- sitting examinations, or
- taking part in teaching and learning activities,

purely because the parents are unable or unwilling to pay a PIBG contribution. SPI No. 5/2001 also sets out the limits on PIBG powers at school, so that the association does not act beyond its role.

## What next

If your child's school imposes a payment stated to be "mandatory", ask the school to explain its basis and whether it is a voluntary contribution or an agreed additional payment package. Keep a receipt for every payment. Complaints about forced collections may be lodged with the State Education Department or the District Education Office. For further context, refer to the guide on enrolling your child in a government school and the overview of the Malaysian education system.
