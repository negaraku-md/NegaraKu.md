Every year, when the registration period opens, parents across Malaysia open the Ministry of Education Malaysia's (KPM) idMe portal to register their child for Year 1. One of the first choices they have to make: SK, SJKC or SJKT?

These three abbreviations are often misunderstood — some assume SJKC and SJKT are private schools, or that their curriculum is entirely different from SK's. This article explains what is actually the same, and what is actually different, between these three types of primary schools — descriptively, based on the official structure set by KPM.

## Three names, one category: government primary schools

SK (Sekolah Kebangsaan), SJKC (Sekolah Jenis Kebangsaan Cina) and SJKT (Sekolah Jenis Kebangsaan Tamil) all fall under the same category: **government primary schools or government-aided primary schools**, administered by the Ministry of Education Malaysia. According to KPM's official portal, all three types of schools exist as part of the same national primary education system, with a schooling period planned for six years.

This means teacher salaries, school administration, and pupil assessment across all three types of schools fall under the same KPM structure — not three separate systems that happen to exist alongside one another.

## What sets them apart: medium of instruction

The main difference between SK, SJKC and SJKT is the **main medium of instruction** — the language used to teach most subjects, not merely a language taught as a single subject.

| School type | Main medium of instruction | Compulsory language subjects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| National School (SK) | Bahasa Melayu | English |
| Chinese National-Type School (SJKC) | Chinese | Bahasa Melayu and English |
| Tamil National-Type School (SJKT) | Tamil | Bahasa Melayu and English |

According to KPM's official portal, SK is a primary school whose main medium of instruction is Bahasa Melayu, while SJKC and SJKT respectively use Chinese and Tamil as their main medium of instruction. At SJKC and SJKT, Bahasa Melayu and English are taught as compulsory subjects alongside the school's main medium of instruction.

## Same curriculum, different language of delivery

One of the most common misconceptions is to assume that SJKC or SJKT follows an entirely different curriculum from SK. In fact, all three types of schools follow the same **Standard Curriculum for Primary Schools (KSSR)**, set by KPM for all government and government-aided primary schools in the country.

The difference that exists is the language in which subjects are delivered — not the content or standard of the curriculum itself. Examinations and assessment for pupils from Year 1 to Year 6 across all three types of schools are likewise administered by the same KPM, following a uniform national primary school assessment framework.

## Legal framework

The standing of SK and national-type schools as part of the national education system is provided for under the **Education Act 1996 (Act 550)** — the principal statute governing government and government-aided educational institutions in Malaysia, including the establishment and maintenance of primary schools by type. The same Act underpins the rules for Year 1 pupil registration, including the eligible age for primary school entry.

## After primary school: pathways to secondary school

SK pupils continue directly to a national secondary school (SMK), which likewise uses Bahasa Melayu as its main medium of instruction.

SJKC and SJKT pupils, meanwhile, can continue to an SMK, or — specifically for some SJKC pupils — to a **national-type secondary school (SMJK)**, which is likewise a government or government-aided school under KPM. The transition in medium of instruction from Chinese or Tamil to Bahasa Melayu at the secondary level is supported through transition classes for pupils who need additional adjustment.

## Common misconceptions

**"SJKC and SJKT are private schools."** False. Both are government or government-aided primary schools under KPM, the same as SK — not private institutions.

**"SJKC is the same as a Chinese Independent High School."** Not the same. SJKC is a primary school within the government system that continues on to an SMK or SMJK. A Chinese independent high school, on the other hand, sits entirely outside the government system, running its own curriculum and examinations at the secondary level — a lineage entirely separate from SJKC.

**"SJKC or SJKT pupils don't learn Bahasa Melayu."** False. Bahasa Melayu is a compulsory subject at SJKC and SJKT, the same as English.

**"The SJKC/SJKT curriculum is entirely different from SK's."** All three types of schools follow the same KSSR; it is the language of delivery that differs, not the curriculum content.
