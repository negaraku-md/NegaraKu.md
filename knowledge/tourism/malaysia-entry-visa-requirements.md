You booked the flight, packed the bag, and only at the gate does the question land: does Malaysia want anything from you before you arrive? For most travellers the surprise cuts both ways — you probably do not need a visa, but you almost certainly need to file a small online form called the MDAC, and mixing those two up is the fastest way to a stressful arrival hall.

Malaysia runs one of the more generous entry regimes in Asia. Citizens of more than fifty countries walk in visa-free for a social visit. But "visa-free" and "no paperwork" are not the same thing, and the rules split along three separate questions: do you need a visa, do you need the MDAC, and does your passport clear the six-month bar. This guide walks each one in turn.

## Do you actually need a visa for Malaysia?

For a short holiday or business visit, the majority of travellers do not. Malaysia grants visa exemption for social visits to citizens of most Western nations, Commonwealth countries, and its ASEAN neighbours, with the permitted stay set country by country by the Immigration Department of Malaysia.

The exemption falls into broad tiers:

| Traveller group | Visa-free stay | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US, UK, most EU states, Australia, New Zealand, Canada | Set per nationality | Social visit — tourism plus permitted business meetings; check the Immigration Department country list for the exact number of days |
| Most ASEAN nationals | Less than one month | Per the ASEAN rule (except Myanmar); longer stays generally need a visa |
| Myanmar nationals | Shorter exemption | Excluded from the standard ASEAN exemption |
| India nationals | Visa-free until 31 December 2026 | Temporary exemption on the Immigration Department list; confirm before you travel |
| Other listed nationalities | No exemption | Must obtain a visa (usually eVISA) before arrival |

Two cautions on the table above. First, the exact number of days is fixed per country — the durations above are a general guide, not a promise for your specific passport, so check your nationality on the Immigration Department's country list before you rely on it. Second, the exemption covers *social visits only*: tourism, visiting family, attending meetings or a conference. It does not cover paid work, long-term study, or taking up residence — those need the correct pass or visa regardless of your nationality.

If your country is not on the exemption list — the Immigration Department's country page identifies the nationalities that must obtain a visa for any purpose, including several South Asian, African and other states — you move to the eVISA track below.

## How does the Malaysia eVISA (MYVISA) work?

Nationalities that require a visa apply online through Malaysia's official MYVISA / eVISA portal before they travel; there is no general visa-on-arrival for tourists from these countries. The portal offers two products:

- **Single Entry Visa (SEV)** — for one journey to Malaysia. Only nationalities that require a visa for each visit apply for this.
- **Multiple Entry Visa (MEV)** — for repeated journeys. It is open to all nationalities *except* Israel and North Korea, and allows a 30-day stay for each entry.

Key figures from the official portal:

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Application | Fully online via the MYVISA portal (register, complete form, upload documents, pay, print the eVisa note) |
| Single Entry Visa fee | RM105 plus a visa fee based on country of origin, plus up to a 1.7% payment-gateway charge |
| Multiple Entry Visa | Open to all nationalities except Israel and North Korea; 30 days per entry |
| Processing time | About two working days |
| Passport | Must remain valid (see the six-month rule below) |

Because processing takes a couple of working days and can run longer in busy periods, apply well ahead of departure rather than the night before. Print the approved eVisa note and carry it — you present it on arrival alongside your passport.

## What is the MDAC, and why does nearly everyone need it?

Here is the part that catches out even seasoned travellers. Separate from any visa question, almost every foreign visitor must submit the **Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)** — an online arrival declaration — before entering the country. It has been mandatory since 1 January 2024.

The essentials:

- **It is free.** The official MDAC costs nothing.
- **Submit it within three days before arrival**, online, at the official portal: `imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main`.
- **It is not a visa.** Filing the MDAC does not grant entry; it is a declaration of your personal, passport and travel details. If you need a visa, you still need the visa.

The MDAC and visa exemption are governed independently. Being visa-free does *not* exempt you from the MDAC — a US, British or Australian tourist enters without a visa but must still file the card. Treat them as two boxes to tick, not one.

### Who is exempt from the MDAC?

A specific set of travellers do not have to file the MDAC. According to the exemptions announced when the card launched, they include:

- Singapore citizens (at any entry point, by any mode of transport)
- Diplomatic passport holders
- Malaysian permanent residents
- Brunei General Certificate of Identity holders
- Thailand Border Pass holders

Note the trap for residents: MDAC exemption follows *passport nationality*, not residency. A Singapore permanent resident who is not a Singapore citizen must still file the MDAC — the exemption belongs to the Singaporean passport, not the Singapore address.

### The MDAC scam warning

The MDAC's popularity has spawned copycat websites. Malaysia's Immigration Department has repeatedly warned tourists to stay vigilant against fraudulent MDAC sites and to use only the official portal. These fake sites charge a "processing" or "express approval" fee for a form that is genuinely free, and they harvest your personal and passport data.

Protect yourself with three habits:

1. **Type the official URL yourself** — `imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main` — rather than clicking a search-engine advert.
2. **Refuse to pay.** Any site asking for a fee for the MDAC is not official.
3. **Distrust "express" or "guaranteed approval" offers.** The real MDAC has no such tier.

## What are the passport rules?

Regardless of visa status, one document rule applies to everyone:

| Requirement | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Passport validity | Valid for at least six months from the date of entry into Malaysia |

The six-month passport rule is strict and is enforced at check-in as well as at the Malaysian border — airlines routinely deny boarding to passengers whose passports fall short. Count six months from your *arrival* date, not your departure date, and renew early if you are close.

An immigration officer at the checkpoint retains discretion over admission and may ask questions about the purpose and length of your stay before admitting a social visitor.

## Can you use the autogates?

Malaysia has expanded automated immigration clearance to foreign travellers. As of the June 2024 expansion, citizens of **63 countries and regions** are eligible to use the autogates (e-gates) — including Australia, Canada, China, most of Europe, Hong Kong, Japan, Korea, New Zealand, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Taiwan, the UAE, the United Kingdom and the United States.

There is one binding condition: to use the autogate, you must have **submitted the MDAC at least three days before travel**. The card and the gate are linked — no MDAC, no gate. The autogate simply lets you scan your passport and pass through without a manual counter; first-time users may need a short enrolment at a counter before the gate will accept them on later trips.

## A quick decision framework

Work through these four questions in order and you have your answer:

1. **Is my passport valid for at least six months from arrival?** If not, renew before you do anything else.
2. **Is my nationality visa-exempt for a social visit?** If yes, no visa is needed — note your permitted number of days from the Immigration Department country list. If no, apply for an eVISA through the official MYVISA portal and wait for approval.
3. **Am I exempt from the MDAC** (Singapore citizen, diplomatic passport, Malaysian PR, listed border-pass holder)? If not — which covers nearly all tourists — file the free MDAC within three days before arrival on the official portal.
4. **Do I want to use the autogate?** If my nationality is among the 63 eligible and I filed the MDAC at least three days ahead, I can; otherwise I use a manned counter.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming visa-free means paperwork-free.** The MDAC is separate. Most visa-free tourists still must file it.
- **Paying for the MDAC.** It is free. Paying means you are on a scam site.
- **Filing the MDAC too early.** The window is within three days before arrival — submit it in that window, not weeks ahead.
- **Letting the passport slip under six months.** Enforced at boarding and at the border; measured from your arrival date.
- **Treating a visa-free social visit as a work permit.** Exemption covers tourism and meetings, not employment, long study or residence.
- **Confusing residency with nationality for exemptions.** Singapore PRs who are not Singapore citizens still need the MDAC.
- **Leaving the eVISA to the last minute.** Processing runs about two working days and can be longer — apply early.

## What's next

Confirm your specific nationality's visa status and permitted length of stay on the [Immigration Department of Malaysia's visa-requirement-by-country page](https://www.imi.gov.my/index.php/en/main-services/visa/visa-requirement-by-country/), since durations and temporary exemptions (such as the India exemption running to 31 December 2026) change. If you need a visa, start the [MYVISA eVISA application](https://rai.malaysia.gov.my/digital-services/apply---evisa) at least several working days before departure. Then, in the final three days before you fly, submit the free MDAC on the official portal at `imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main` and save the confirmation to your phone. With a passport comfortably clear of the six-month line, that is everything the border will ask for.
