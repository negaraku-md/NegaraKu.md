Drive up the mountain road out of the Kuala Lumpur heat and, somewhere past the halfway mark, the windows start to fog and the air turns cold. Within an hour of leaving the capital you can be standing in cloud at nearly 1,800 metres, in front of a neon resort city stacked on a mountaintop. That contrast — tropical lowland below, cool misty summit above, reachable in a morning — is the whole proposition of Genting Highlands. This guide covers what it actually is, how to get up there, what there is to do, how to treat the casino sensibly, and the mistakes that catch first-timers out.

## What exactly is Genting Highlands?

Genting Highlands is a hilltop resort city built on the peak of **Mount Ulu Kali**, in the **Titiwangsa Mountains** (the Main Range) of Peninsular Malaysia. According to Wikipedia, it sits at **approximately 1,800 metres (5,900 ft) above sea level** and straddles the border between two states: the eastern side lies in **Bentong District, Pahang**, and the western slopes in **Hulu Selangor District, Selangor**. People often file it under Pahang, which is where the resort is addressed, but strictly it sits on the state line.

The headline attraction is **Resorts World Genting (RWG)**, a large integrated leisure resort operated by **Genting Malaysia Berhad**. It was established by the late **Lim Goh Tong** — the company was incorporated in 1965 and the resort opened to the public in 1971 (Wikipedia) — and has grown into a self-contained city of hotels, theme parks, shopping, restaurants, shows and gaming. The whole thing is often referred to simply as "Genting."

Crucially, it is an **integrated resort**, not a national park or a quiet hill station. Where a place like [Cameron Highlands](/tourism/cameron-highlands-visitor-guide) is about tea gardens, strawberry farms and slow mornings, Genting is about built entertainment — rides, malls, concerts and casino floors — set at altitude. The two highlands suit very different moods, and it is worth being clear which you are after before you book.

## Why do people actually go up?

The single biggest reason is the **weather**. Malaysia's lowlands are hot and humid year-round, and Genting's altitude flips that. Wikipedia describes a spring-like subtropical highland climate with daily temperatures that **typically range between about 14 °C and 22 °C**, plus frequent mountain mist. For families from Kuala Lumpur, the novelty of needing a jacket at the equator is a genuine draw in itself.

The cool air comes bundled with the second reason: a dense concentration of **built attractions** in one place — theme parks, shopping, dining, live shows and a casino — that you can move between on foot, mostly indoors and sheltered from the rain. That combination of cool weather plus all-weather entertainment, barely an hour from the capital, is why Genting is one of Malaysia's most-visited destinations and an easy day trip or weekend escape from [Kuala Lumpur](/tourism/kuala-lumpur-visitor-guide).

The flip side of the weather is worth stating plainly: "cool and misty" often means **cold, grey and wet**. The summit is regularly wrapped in cloud, views come and go, and outdoor plans can be rained off. Pack a layer and keep an indoor backup plan. The upside is that the same cloud and chill are precisely what Kuala Lumpur families drive up for — an hour of winding road and the climate changes completely, which is the trick Genting has sold for half a century.

## What is there to do at Resorts World Genting?

The resort packs a lot onto the mountaintop. The main categories:

- **Theme parks.** There are two. The **Skytropolis Indoor Theme Park**, inside the First World Plaza, is the rainy-day, all-ages option (it was revamped and reopened in 2018, per Wikipedia). The **Genting SkyWorlds** outdoor theme park is the larger, headline park; it opened on **8 February 2022** after a long redevelopment (Wikipedia). Having one indoor and one outdoor park is deliberate — the indoor one carries the day when the weather closes in.
- **Shopping and dining.** **SkyAvenue** is the flagship lifestyle mall at the summit, with dining streets, boutiques and the upper cable-car station built into it (Wikipedia). There is also the outlet shopping at the mid-hill **Genting Highlands Premium Outlets** by the cable-car base.
- **Shows and entertainment.** The resort hosts concerts and live shows at its arena and theatres, alongside bars, clubs and arcades.
- **Hotels.** RWG runs several hotels on the mountain, including the very large **First World Hotel** (Wikipedia). Staying over turns a crowded day trip into a more relaxed visit and lets you catch the quieter early mornings.
- **The casino and the temple** — covered separately below.

Because what is open and operating shifts over time, check the current line-up of rides and attractions on the official Resorts World Genting site before you commit a day to any specific one.

## What about the casino — and who can go in?

Genting is the home of **the only legalised land-based casino in Malaysia** (Wikipedia). It operates as a lawful, licensed establishment, and gaming is a long-standing part of the resort's history — the original 1970s development paired a hotel with a small casino.

The entry rules matter, and they are specific. Malaysia applies Syariah law to Muslims, so **Muslims are not permitted to enter the casino**; entry is for **non-Muslims** only. Visitors must also meet a **minimum age** — widely reported as **21** — and are subject to **identity checks** (passport for tourists) and a **dress code** (casino entry rules are widely reported by travel and news sources). These rules can change and are enforced at the door, so confirm the current requirements directly with Resorts World Genting rather than relying on a figure here.

Two neutral points for planning. First, the casino is only one slice of a much larger resort — the theme parks, shopping, shows and cool weather stand entirely on their own, and plenty of families visit without ever going near a gaming floor. Second, if you do choose to play, treat it as paid entertainment with a fixed budget and **gamble responsibly**; this guide offers no view on whether you should.

## What is the Chin Swee Caves Temple?

Partway up the mountain sits one of Genting's most photogenic stops: the **Chin Swee Caves Temple**, a **Taoist temple** built into the hillside. According to the temple's official site, it was founded by Genting's own founder, **Lim Goh Tong**, who dedicated it to a venerated monk, Master Chin Swee, from his home province of Fujian in China; the temple was officially opened in **1994** (Chin Swee Caves Temple official site).

For visitors it is a free, calm counterpoint to the resort's neon: landscaped grounds, statues, a **climbable pagoda** and sweeping views out over the forested mountains on a clear day. It is easy to combine with the cable-car ride up, because the **Awana SkyWay has a dedicated Chin Swee station** where you can get off, look around, and continue on the same ticket (Resorts World Genting). Dress and behave respectfully, as you would at any active place of worship.

## How do you get there — driving or the Awana SkyWay?

Genting is **roughly an hour's drive from Kuala Lumpur** (Wikipedia), via the highway network and the mountain road that climbs to the summit. You can drive the whole way up, or take one of the coaches that run from the city. Confirm current coach services and timings with the operators before you travel.

The signature way up, though, is the **Awana SkyWay** — a gondola cable car that carries you from the mid-hill base to the summit through the forest canopy and, often, through cloud. Its shape is worth understanding:

- **Base station (near the Genting Highlands Premium Outlets).** You board at the mid-hill station, which adjoins the Premium Outlets car park, at roughly **1,105 m** altitude (Resorts World Genting; Wikipedia). This is where most people park or arrive by coach and switch to the gondola.
- **Chin Swee station (mid-way).** The line stops at the Chin Swee Caves Temple, where you can get off and reboard on the same ticket (Resorts World Genting).
- **Top station at SkyAvenue (summit).** The upper terminus, at about **1,725 m**, is built into the SkyAvenue mall at the top (Resorts World Genting; Wikipedia).

The current gondola system opened in **2016**, replacing a first-generation aerial tramway that ran from 1975 (Wikipedia). The full ride takes **around 10 minutes** (Resorts World Genting). The operator describes the line as about **2.8 km** long; Wikipedia lists an inclined length of 2,642 m, so treat the exact figure as approximate and confirm it with the operator. Some cabins have **glass floors** for an extra thrill, and the gondola runs in both directions, so you can ride up and drive down or vice versa.

Up at the summit everything is compact and largely connected under cover, so once you are there you mostly get around on foot between the hotels, malls and parks. The choice between driving all the way up and parking mid-hill to take the gondola often comes down to the weather and the crowds: on a clear, quiet day the cable car is the view; on a busy holiday weekend, the summit car parks and the gondola queues can both back up, so build in time either way.

## Who is Genting Highlands best for?

Genting suits some travellers far better than others, and being honest about the match saves disappointment.

| Good fit | Less good fit |
| --- | --- |
| Families wanting theme parks and cool weather | Travellers after quiet nature and solitude |
| Day-trippers and weekenders from Kuala Lumpur | People chasing untouched highland scenery |
| Shoppers, concert-goers, casino visitors (non-Muslim, of age) | Those who dislike crowds, queues and neon |
| Anyone wanting an easy, built-up escape from the heat | Trekkers and birdwatchers (go to a national park instead) |

In short: Genting is a **built entertainment resort at altitude**, best for families, day-trippers and anyone who wants rides, shopping and cool air without a long journey. If what you actually want is tea gardens, jungle trails and slow mornings, [Cameron Highlands](/tourism/cameron-highlands-visitor-guide) or a national park is the better call — and nothing stops you pairing Genting with a city stay in [Kuala Lumpur](/tourism/kuala-lumpur-visitor-guide) at the foot of the mountain.

## What do first-time visitors get wrong?

A handful of avoidable mistakes come up again and again:

- **Underestimating the weather.** "Cool and misty" often means cold, wet and socked in with cloud. Bring a jacket, do not count on clear summit views, and keep an indoor plan (the Skytropolis indoor park, the malls) for when it rains.
- **Going on a weekend or public holiday without expecting crowds.** Genting is one of Malaysia's busiest attractions, and school holidays and weekends bring long queues at the cable car, the car parks and the rides. Weekdays are calmer.
- **Not booking ahead in peak periods.** Hotels, theme-park entry and even the gondola can sell out or back up at busy times. Book accommodation and tickets in advance rather than turning up and hoping.
- **Assuming every attraction is open.** Rides and venues rotate in and out of service; check the current operating list on the official site before you build a day around one specific thing.
- **Misjudging the casino rules.** It is non-Muslims only, with a minimum age, ID checks and a dress code — do not assume you can walk straight in, and confirm the current rules officially.
- **Treating it like a nature trip.** Genting is a resort city, not a national park. If you want jungle and birdsong, this is the wrong mountain.

## What's next

Genting Highlands works best when you go in with the right expectations: an easy, cool-weather entertainment resort an hour above Kuala Lumpur, strongest for families, shoppers and day-trippers, and reached most memorably by the Awana SkyWay gondola with a pause at the Chin Swee Caves Temple. Before you set off, check current theme-park and cable-car operating details, hotel availability and — if it is relevant to you — the casino's entry rules directly with Resorts World Genting, since specifics change. For the wider trip, pair it with a stay in [Kuala Lumpur](/tourism/kuala-lumpur-visitor-guide), weigh it against the slower charms of [Cameron Highlands](/tourism/cameron-highlands-visitor-guide), and see the practical overview of [getting around Malaysia for tourists](/tourism/getting-around-malaysia-for-tourists) to plan the journey up.
