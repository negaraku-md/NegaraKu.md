Six sites in one country now carry UNESCO's stamp — and you can walk a centuries-old Dutch colonial square in the morning and stand inside a vast Bornean cave chamber a flight away. Malaysia's heritage is not a museum you look at; it is a living street you can eat, sleep and pray your way through.

This guide maps the country's UNESCO circuit, explains how to live inside the culture through the national Homestay Programme, and sets out the etiquette that keeps your visit welcome at mosques, temples and royal grounds.

## What are Malaysia's UNESCO World Heritage Sites?

As of 2025, Malaysia has six sites on the UNESCO World Heritage List. Only one — the twin cities of Melaka and George Town — is inscribed as a cultural property; the rest are natural or archaeological. Together they let a single trip run from prehistoric rock shelters to colonial shophouses to restored rainforest.

The Historic Cities of the Straits of Malacca (Melaka and George Town) were inscribed on 7 July 2008 under cultural criteria (ii), (iii) and (iv). According to George Town World Heritage Incorporated, the George Town core zone alone covers 109.38 hectares with a 150.04-hectare buffer, containing around 5,013 buildings — the reason its shophouse-lined lanes feel so densely layered with Malay, Chinese, Indian and European history built up over centuries of trade.

| Site | Year inscribed | State | Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kinabalu Park | 2000 | Sabah | Natural |
| Gunung Mulu National Park | 2000 | Sarawak | Natural |
| Historic Cities of the Straits of Malacca (Melaka & George Town) | 2008 | Melaka & Penang | Cultural |
| Archaeological Heritage of the Lenggong Valley | 2012 | Perak | Cultural (archaeological) |
| Archaeological Heritage of the Niah National Park Cave Complex | 2024 | Sarawak | Cultural (archaeological) |
| FRIM Forest Park Selangor | 2025 | Selangor | Natural |

The two newest additions show how broad "heritage" has become. The Niah Caves complex in Sarawak was recognised on 27 July 2024 at the 46th World Heritage Committee session in New Delhi — a cultural inscription honouring one of Southeast Asia's most important records of early human life. A year later, FRIM Forest Park Selangor was listed on 11 July 2025 at the 47th session in Paris, celebrated as one of the world's earliest large-scale tropical forest restoration projects on former tin-mining land, just northwest of Kuala Lumpur.

## How do you plan a heritage circuit across the country?

Malaysia's sites are spread across the Peninsula and Borneo, so most visitors pick a region rather than chasing all six.

- **Peninsula heritage loop:** Fly into Penang for George Town's shophouses and street art, drive south to Perak for the Lenggong Valley's archaeological galleries, and finish in Melaka for the Dutch-era Stadthuys and Jonker Street. FRIM in Selangor sits conveniently between them as a day trip from Kuala Lumpur.
- **Borneo nature-and-archaeology run:** Base in Sabah for Kinabalu Park, then fly to Sarawak for Gunung Mulu's cave systems and the Niah Caves near Miri.

Because George Town and Melaka are living cities, they reward slow walking over ticking off monuments. The George Town core is compact enough to explore on foot in a day, though the clan jetties, temples and museums easily fill two or three.

## What is the Malaysian Homestay Programme?

If a heritage site shows you the architecture, the Homestay Programme lets you live the culture. The Malaysian Homestay Experience Programme (PPHM) is a community-based tourism (CBT) initiative led by the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture (MOTAC). Rather than a hotel, you stay with a host family in a rural village and join their daily life.

According to MOTAC and the Government of Malaysia's official portal, PPHM is designed as "a bridge connecting tourists with the rich cultural heritage and traditions that reflect the uniqueness of Malaysia." Guests share meals cooked by traditional methods, help with farming or harvesting, and take part in local crafts, music and ceremonies. MOTAC publishes an annual Homestay Directory listing registered clusters, and operators must meet ministry guidelines to be registered.

A homestay is not a budget hotel with a village address — it is an invitation into a household. Treat your hosts' home, prayer space and customs with the same respect you would extend anywhere you are a guest.

## How should you behave at mosques and religious sites?

Malaysia is a multi-religious country where Islam is the official religion, and its mosques, temples and churches are active places of worship, not just attractions. A little preparation keeps you welcome.

Most mosques admit non-Muslim visitors outside prayer times. The core expectations are modest dress, covered hair for women, bare feet, and staying clear of the prayer hall while people are praying. Robes are commonly available for under-dressed visitors to borrow at major mosques such as the National Mosque (Masjid Negara) in Kuala Lumpur.

| Site type | Dress | Footwear | Key rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mosque | Long sleeves and trousers/skirt; headscarf for women | Remove shoes | No entry to prayer hall during prayers; avoid Friday midday |
| Hindu temple | Cover shoulders and knees | Remove shoes | Do not touch statues or offerings; walk clockwise |
| Chinese temple | Modest, cover shoulders and knees | Remove shoes at inner sanctum | Do not point feet at altars |
| Church | Smart-casual, modest | Usually kept on | Stay quiet during services |

A few universal courtesies apply everywhere: ask before photographing people at prayer, do not turn your back to an altar for photos, and step over — never on — thresholds and offerings.

## What about royal sites and cultural villages?

Malaysia's constitutional monarchy means palaces and royal grounds carry their own etiquette. Working palaces such as Istana Negara are generally not open to the public, so most visitors admire them from the gates; do not attempt to enter restricted grounds or photograph guards up close where signs prohibit it. Where royal museums exist, dress modestly and follow posted rules.

Cultural villages offer a lower-stakes introduction to tradition. Sarawak Cultural Village near Kuching and Mari Mari Cultural Village near Kota Kinabalu recreate the longhouses and daily crafts of Borneo's indigenous communities, while heritage quarters in George Town and Melaka run guided walks through clan houses and places of worship. These are curated experiences, but the same respect — modest dress, asking before photos — makes them richer.

## What's next

- **Confirm site logistics before you go.** Check current opening hours, guided-tour availability and any conservation closures directly with each site's managing body — for George Town, that is George Town World Heritage Incorporated.
- **Book homestays through official channels.** Use MOTAC's Homestay Directory to find registered PPHM clusters rather than informal listings, so your money supports vetted community operators.
- **Pack a scarf and slip-on shoes.** A light shawl and shoes you can remove easily cover almost every mosque and temple you will encounter.
- **Time your visits around prayers.** Avoid mosques around the five daily prayer times and especially Friday midday, when access for non-Muslims is limited.
