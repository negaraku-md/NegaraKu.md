When 3.2 million delegates fly in for a conference, they book hotels, hire translators, fill restaurants and sign deals — and Malaysia has spent more than a decade building the venues and the bureau to capture them.

That is the business behind the acronym MICE: Meetings, Incentives, Conventions and Exhibitions. It is tourism's high-yield cousin, where a single association congress can be worth thousands of premium visitors at once, and where Malaysia markets a specific promise — modern facilities at value-for-money prices, in the heart of Asia.

## Who coordinates business events in Malaysia?

The national convention bureau is the **Malaysia Convention & Exhibition Bureau (MyCEB)**, an agency under the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture (MOTAC). MyCEB describes itself as the one-stop hub for business and international sporting events, helping planners bid for, win and stage conventions, exhibitions, corporate meetings and incentive trips.

Unlike a venue, MyCEB does not host events itself. It is a promoter and facilitator: it supports bid submissions to international associations, connects organisers with local suppliers, and markets Malaysia at the global trade shows where destinations compete — IMEX Frankfurt, IBTM Barcelona, The Meetings Show in London and their Asia-Pacific counterparts.

The scale it reports is substantial. Since 2009, MyCEB says Malaysia has delivered:

| Metric (since 2009) | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Business events hosted | 3,105 |
| International delegates | 3,223,491 |
| Estimated economic impact | RM20.18 billion |

A narrower window shows how consistent participation pays off: MyCEB reports that from 2017 to 2023, its presence at The Meetings Show in London alone helped bring 6,625 delegates for an estimated RM116 million in economic impact.

## Where do the events actually happen?

Malaysia's business-events capacity is concentrated in a handful of purpose-built centres. Three anchor the map — two in Kuala Lumpur and one in Borneo.

| Venue | Location | Scale highlights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kuala Lumpur Convention Centre (KLCC) | KL city centre | Has hosted 1,200+ exhibitions and welcomed 23 million exhibition visitors; 2,000 on-site hotel rooms plus ~22,000 more within walking distance |
| MITEC | Near Dutamas (KL Metropolis), Kuala Lumpur | Opened 2016; 11 mega exhibition halls; 52,000 sqm exhibition space; 12,530 sqm column-free space (per CIDB); 13 meeting rooms; 1 Grand and 1 Junior Ballroom |
| Borneo Convention Centre Kuching (BCCK) | Kuching, Sarawak | Expanding via BCCK2 (see below) to serve East Malaysia |

**KLCC** is the flagship for association congresses, sitting beside the Petronas Twin Towers with a dense supply of walkable hotel rooms — a decisive advantage when a conference needs to house thousands of delegates near the plenary hall.

**MITEC** is the heavy-lifting exhibition venue. Purpose-built for large trade fairs, its column-free space and high floor-loading capacity (up to 5 tonnes per square metre) suit machinery, construction and industrial exhibitions that KLCC's footprint cannot absorb.

## What is happening in East Malaysia?

Business events are no longer only a Kuala Lumpur story. In Sarawak, the **Borneo Convention Centre Kuching** is building a second wing, **BCCK2**, to lift the state's capacity for large conventions and exhibitions.

According to trade press, BCCK2 will add a 5,936 sqm flexible main hall seating up to 9,500 people theatre-style or holding 340 exhibition booths, divisible into three halls, plus 20 breakout rooms and a five-level car park with 1,000 bays. A 70-metre climate-controlled sky bridge will link the two buildings, with completion targeted for 2028. The expansion signals a deliberate push to spread MICE demand beyond the Klang Valley and position Kuching as a gateway for Borneo-focused events.

## What is Malaysia's pitch to organisers?

Malaysia competes in a crowded Asia-Pacific field that includes Singapore, Bangkok and increasingly the Gulf. Its consistent message is **value** — comparable facilities and connectivity at lower cost — paired with English-language business fluency, a multicultural setting and a central position within ASEAN.

For an association weighing where to hold a 3,000-delegate congress, that translates into practical maths: a large purpose-built hall, thousands of adjacent hotel rooms, a bureau that helps assemble the bid, and a delegate spend that, on Malaysia's own positioning, stretches further than in pricier neighbouring hubs.

## What's next

Malaysia's business-events strategy points toward geographic spread and larger-format capacity. The BCCK2 build-out in Kuching, due in 2028, is the clearest near-term marker, while MITEC continues to absorb the country's biggest exhibitions and KLCC holds the association-congress core in the capital.

For planners, the practical next steps are to engage MyCEB early for bid support, match the event's format to the right venue (association congress versus mega-exhibition versus regional gathering), and confirm current published capacities and rates directly with each centre, as figures and facilities evolve with ongoing expansions.
