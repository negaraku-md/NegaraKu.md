Rain in Kuala Lumpur arrives fast and heavy, and an open-air plan can collapse in
minutes. It does not have to end the day. Two things make KL unusually good for a
wet-weather plan: the rail network runs through **covered, air-conditioned
stations and trains**, and it threads together a surprising amount of indoor
space — malls, museums and attractions you can hop between without stepping into a
downpour for long.

Below are three themed routes, ordered from driest to most exposed. One honest
caveat runs through all of them: a station being *connected* to a building is not
the same as the *whole journey* being covered, so where a stretch is outdoors, it
says so. And because hours and prices change, **check each attraction's own site
before you go** — many close one weekday or on public holidays.

## Getting around: the rail backbone

KL's **Klang Valley rail** — the **LRT** (Kelana Jaya, Ampang and Sri Petaling
lines), the **MRT** (Kajang and Putrajaya lines), the **KL Monorail** and **KTM
Komuter** — is the practical core of a rainy day, because the stations and trains
are enclosed and air-conditioned. Carry a **[Touch 'n Go](/transport/touch-n-go)** card and tap in and out;
contactless bank cards work on much of the network. Malaysian residents who travel
daily can use the **My50** unlimited monthly pass, but a one-off visitor will
usually just pay as they go. Useful interchanges where you change lines without
leaving the station include **Masjid Jamek**, **Pasar Seni** and **KL Sentral**.

## Route 1 — the mall core (the driest day)

This is the route to pick when the rain is relentless. The anchor is the
**air-conditioned KLCC–Bukit Bintang covered walkway**, an elevated, climate-
controlled pedestrian link (launched in 2012) that lets you move between the
Bukit Bintang area and **Suria KLCC** largely undercover.

- **Suria KLCC** — the big mall at the foot of the Petronas Twin Towers, linked to
  the **KLCC LRT** station by a **sheltered underpass**.
- **Pavilion KL** — in the Bukit Bintang retail core, by the **Bukit Bintang MRT**.
- **Berjaya Times Square** — linked **directly** to the **Imbi monorail** station
  by a **covered pedestrian bridge** — a genuine indoor-to-indoor transfer.
- **Mid Valley Megamall** — served **directly by KTM Komuter** (the Mid Valley
  station sits beside the mall), a short ride away for a change of scene.

The strongest covered links here are the KLCC LRT–Suria underpass, the Imbi–Berjaya
bridge and the KLCC–Bukit Bintang walkway. One stretch to note: the **Bukit Bintang
MRT and the Bukit Bintang monorail are separate stations**, so changing between
them means a short walk at street level.

## Route 2 — the museum cluster

A rewarding route, with an honest asterisk: these museums are reachable by rail,
but the **walks from the stations to the buildings are largely outdoors** (the
parkland around Masjid Negara and the Lake Gardens, the streets by Merdeka Square).
It is a good plan for light rain, less so for a downpour.

- **Islamic Arts Museum Malaysia** — an indoor museum near the National Mosque;
  the Museum's own site gives rail directions (a walk from **Pasar Seni** or the
  **Kuala Lumpur KTM** station, past Masjid Negara). The approach is partly in the
  open, so time it between showers.
- **Muzium Negara (National Museum)** — by the **Muzium Negara MRT**, which has a
  linked pedestrian walkway across to **KL Sentral**, so you can arrive via KL
  Sentral's many lines.
- **National Textile Museum** — an indoor museum in a heritage building near
  **Merdeka Square**, close to **Masjid Jamek LRT**.

Check each museum's hours and whether it is open — several close one weekday — on
its own website.

## Route 3 — science and family

- **Aquaria KLCC** — an indoor oceanarium inside the KL Convention Centre, reachable
  from **KLCC LRT** through Suria KLCC's sheltered underpass; it is popular, so book
  ahead.
- **Petrosains, The Discovery Centre** — an interactive science centre on an upper
  level of **Suria KLCC**, straight off the KLCC LRT and fully indoors.
- **National Science Centre (Pusat Sains Negara)** — a large indoor science museum,
  but **not rail-adjacent**: the nearest MRT is **Pusat Bandar Damansara**, then a
  short **feeder-bus or taxi** hop. Worth the extra leg for families, but it breaks
  the fully-undercover promise — save it for when the rain eases, or take a taxi
  door to door.

Aquaria and Petrosains keep you inside the KLCC core; the Science Centre is the one
stop that needs an outdoor transfer.

## Covered vs connected — the weather nuance

Plan honestly around one distinction:

- "A station or mall is **connected**" — supported by the operator or venue.
- "The **whole route** is step-free and continuously covered" — **verify**, because
  brief outdoor legs exist (the Masjid Negara parkland, the Bukit Bintang MRT-to-
  monorail change, the feeder bus to the Science Centre).

If you need **step-free** access the whole way, check lift availability on the
operator and venue sites before you travel.

## What's next

- Before you set out, confirm **current opening hours, prices and closed days** on
  each attraction's own website, and **book popular ones ahead**.
- Check the current fares and accepted payment on the **rail operator's** site, and
  carry a Touch 'n Go card.
- For the wider city beyond a rainy day, see the
  [Kuala Lumpur visitor guide](/en/tourism/kuala-lumpur-visitor-guide).
