An Indonesian family flies into Penang for a heart bypass, a couple from the Gulf comes for IVF, and a retiree from Australia books a full-body screening that would cost three times as much at home. Together they are part of a quiet industry that brought more than 1.6 million people to Malaysia in a single year — not to sightsee, but to get well.

Malaysia has spent more than a decade turning affordable, accredited private healthcare into an export. This guide explains how big the industry is, what people travel for, who keeps the standards high, and what a prospective patient should check first.

*This is general information about the industry, not medical advice. Always confirm treatment suitability, doctor credentials and total costs directly with a licensed hospital before travelling.*

## How big is Malaysia's medical-travel industry?

The sector has grown steadily into a multi-billion-ringgit contributor to the economy. Malaysia welcomed roughly **1.6 million healthcare travellers in 2024** — about **14% more** than the year before — generating **RM2.72 billion** in hospital revenue, which was up around **21%** on 2023's RM2.25 billion, according to the Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council (MHTC) and industry reporting. (The 14% figure describes the growth in traveller numbers; revenue grew at the faster ~21% rate.) Revenue climbed again to around **RM3.35 billion in 2025**.

Because each ringgit spent on treatment also flows into flights, hotels, food and accompanying relatives, the wider economic footprint is far larger than the hospital bills alone.

| Year | Healthcare travellers | Hospital revenue |
|------|----------------------|------------------|
| 2023 | — | RM2.25 billion |
| 2024 | ~1.6 million | RM2.72 billion |
| 2025 | — | ~RM3.35 billion (provisional) |

*Figures from MHTC and Free Malaysia Today. A dash means the figure was not published in the sources reviewed. MHTC separately cites a current headline of about 1.8 million healthcare travellers annually, an undated figure not tied to a specific year.*

MHTC has set a target of around **RM7 billion in healthcare-traveller revenue by 2030**. A larger figure of roughly **RM10–12 billion a year** is also cited, but the sources describe it as the sector's broader *economic impact* once spending on flights, hotels and accompanying relatives is included — MHTC's own industry-overview page, however, labels RM12 billion a 2030 "revenue goal", so the two framings should be treated with caution. MHTC has branded 2026 as the **Malaysia Year of Medical Tourism (MYMT 2026)** — the country's first national campaign year dedicated to the sector.

## Who coordinates it — and what does MHTC actually do?

The **Malaysia Healthcare Travel Council** is the government-backed agency that markets Malaysia as a healthcare-travel destination and acts as the single front door for international patients. Rather than treating anyone itself, it curates a network of vetted providers and smooths the journey around the clinical care.

Its role, in practice, covers:

- **Hospital matching** — helping patients find an accredited member facility for their condition.
- **Facilitation and concierge** — pre-treatment guidance, document review, airport coordination, language support and aftercare.
- **A "Special Lane"** for expedited handling of qualifying patients.
- **Quality assurance** — every hospital in the network is a vetted MHTC member.

The council coordinates a network of around **80 member hospitals and specialist centres** nationwide, drawing patients from over 60 countries.

## What do foreign patients travel to Malaysia for?

Malaysian hospitals advertise care across a wide range of specialties, and MHTC highlights **four centres of excellence** — oncology, cardiology, fertility and orthopaedics. A handful of areas do the heavy lifting in attracting international patients.

- **Cardiac care.** The **National Heart Institute (IJN)** is the flagship, a long-standing regional referral point for bypass surgery and complex cardiology.
- **Oncology.** Cancer diagnosis and treatment is a major draw, and oncology is one of MHTC's designated centres of excellence.
- **Fertility and IVF.** Assisted-reproduction services pull patients from across the region and the Middle East, particularly from countries where treatment is expensive, restricted, or subject to long public waiting lists.
- **Health screening.** Comprehensive executive check-ups are a common entry point — affordable, fast, and often the reason a first-time visitor returns for treatment.
- **Dental and cosmetic procedures.** Widely marketed to price-sensitive travellers, though patients should scrutinise credentials and follow-up arrangements as carefully as for any surgery.

The commercial pull is cost. MHTC states that treatment in Malaysia typically runs **30–60% below equivalent procedures** in Western markets such as the United States, United Kingdom and Australia.

## Which hospitals lead the field?

Penang has become one of the country's densest medical-travel clusters, and its standout is **Island Hospital**, which won the inaugural **Flagship Medical Tourism Hospital award in 2025** — an MHTC recognition assessed with independent input from Joint Commission International (JCI) and IQVIA. Other frequently cited names in the network include the National Heart Institute (IJN) for cardiac care and large private groups in the Klang Valley for oncology and general specialties.

Patients from neighbouring Southeast Asian countries — with Indonesia reported as the single largest source market — make up the bulk of arrivals, drawn by geographic proximity, cultural familiarity and established trust in Malaysian medicine.

## How is quality and safety assured?

Accreditation is the backbone of the industry's credibility. A prospective patient's first check should be whether a hospital holds a recognised independent accreditation and is an MHTC member. The main marks to look for:

| Accreditation | What it signals |
|---------------|-----------------|
| **JCI** (Joint Commission International) | The leading global standard for patient safety and hospital operations |
| **MSQH** (Malaysian Society for Quality in Health) | Malaysia's national hospital-accreditation body |
| **ACHS** (Australian Council on Healthcare Standards) | Independent Australian quality accreditation used by several Malaysian centres |

These schemes require independent audits and continuous review rather than one-off certification, which is what distinguishes an accredited hospital from a merely well-marketed one.

## What should a prospective patient check first?

Before booking, work through a short due-diligence list:

1. **Confirm accreditation** (JCI / MSQH / ACHS) and MHTC membership for the specific hospital.
2. **Verify the treating doctor's credentials** and experience with your exact procedure.
3. **Get the total cost in writing**, including consultations, tests, hospital stay and follow-up — not just the headline surgical fee.
4. **Plan aftercare** — how complications or follow-up will be handled once you return home.
5. **Use MHTC facilitation** to route enquiries through vetted providers rather than unverified agents.

## What's next

Malaysia has publicly committed to growing healthcare-traveller revenue to around RM7 billion by 2030 — more than double its 2024 level — and is using MYMT 2026 to court new markets across the Middle East, South Asia and East Asia. Expect more Flagship-designated hospitals, continued investment in Penang and the Klang Valley, and a growing emphasis on higher-value specialties such as oncology, cardiology and fertility. For a prospective patient, the practical takeaway is unchanged: start with accreditation, confirm costs in writing, and route enquiries through MHTC's member network.
