Step off the bus at Tanah Rata and the first thing you notice is your own breath — the air is cool enough that a jumper stops feeling ridiculous, even at the equator. That is the whole point of Cameron Highlands: a slab of temperate hill country stacked above tropical Malaysia, where tea grows in neat green corduroy over the ridges and strawberries ripen in roadside polytunnels.

## What exactly is Cameron Highlands?

It is Malaysia's best-known hill station, spread across a plateau in the northwest corner of Pahang. Tourism Pahang traces its "discovery" to 1885, when the surveyor William Cameron described the roughly 1,500-metre-high tableland; the area was then developed as a holiday retreat from the 1920s onward, which is why you still see mock-Tudor bungalows tucked among the tea.

The high ground keeps climbing. The highlands extend up to **Gunung Brinchang at 2,032 metres**, the summit that carries the road to the Mossy Forest. Because most of the plateau sits near 1,500 m, temperatures stay far below the sticky lowland norm — cool enough that the region grows a large share of Malaysia's temperate vegetables, cut flowers and strawberries.

## How do the three towns fit together?

Newcomers often assume "Cameron Highlands" is one town. It is really a chain of settlements strung along the main road, each with a different job.

| Town | Role | Character | Best base for |
|------|------|-----------|---------------|
| **Ringlet** | Gateway and farming service centre | Lowest of the three; where roads from Tapah and the Raub/Kuala Lipis side converge | Passing through; the BOH Ringlet tea garden is nearby |
| **Tanah Rata** | Main commercial and tourist town | Restaurants, shops, budget stays; about 6 km up from Ringlet (Tourism Pahang) | Most visitors — widest choice of food, lodging and tours |
| **Brinchang** | Highest of the three | Closer to the tea estates and the Mossy Forest; weekend night market | Tea, the summit road and cool-weather scenery |

Tanah Rata is the practical hub — it is also where the Cameron Highlands District Council (MDCH) is headquartered (39000 Tanah Rata), and where most guided tours depart. Tourism Pahang puts Tanah Rata just 6 km from Ringlet. Brinchang, being higher and nearer the plantations, suits travellers who want to be closest to the classic tea-and-forest scenery. Ringlet is more a working farm town and junction than a place to linger.

## What can you do on the tea plantations?

Tea is the region's signature, and **BOH Plantations** is the name you will see most. According to Cameron Highlands Online, BOH was established by the businessman J.A. Russell (James Archibald Russell) — a spelling confirmed on BOH's own site — and today runs three tea gardens: **Habu, Fairlie and Sungai Palas**.

For visitors, the two most accessible are:

- **Sungai Palas Tea Centre** (39100 Brinchang) — the postcard-famous one, with an overhanging viewing balcony, a cafe serving freshly brewed BOH tea, and free guided factory tours.
- **BOH Tea Garden at Ringlet** (39000 Ringlet) — the older estate on the Habu road, with similar tours and views.

BOH's official Sungei Palas page lists the tea garden hours as **8.30am to 4.30pm, Tuesdays to Sundays, closed on Mondays** (except for selected public and school holidays); it is worth arriving before noon on weekends to beat the crowds. The MDCH portal also lists **Bharat Tea** (the estate whose roadside "tea shop with a view" is a favourite photo stop) among the district's farm-and-garden attractions, so BOH is not your only option.

A typical tea-estate visit runs like this:

1. Walk the trail or terrace edge for photos over the tea rows.
2. Join the free factory tour to see plucking, withering, rolling and drying.
3. Finish with a pot of tea and scones at the cafe.

## Where do the strawberries and flowers come from?

The same cool climate that suits tea also fills the plateau with produce farms. The district council's official "Farm & Garden" list of places of interest includes **Strawberry Park**, vegetable farms, **Flower Nurseries** at Ringlet, a **Butterfly Farm**, and novelty stops like **EE Feng Gu** honey bee farm.

Most strawberry farms operate on a pick-your-own basis in season, usually paired with a cafe selling strawberry everything — juice, ice cream, jam — plus a nursery of cacti, roses or lavender next door. They cluster thickest along the Brinchang–Kea Farm stretch, which makes a natural half-day loop combined with a tea centre.

A rough agrotourism menu:

- **Tea** — BOH (Sungai Palas, Ringlet), Bharat, Sungai Palas estate
- **Berries and produce** — Strawberry Park and roadside pick-your-own farms; vegetable farms
- **Flora and fauna** — flower nurseries, butterfly and honey-bee farms, cactus and rose gardens

## Can you visit an Orang Asli village?

Yes — and it is one of the more distinctive things to do here. Cameron Highlands sits on the Pahang–Perak fringe, and the indigenous people of the surrounding forest are the **Orang Asli**, predominantly of the Semai group.

Penang Travel Tips describes forest-dwelling Orang Asli who "cultivate hill rice, and engage in hunting and gathering, and trade in forest produce such as petai, durian, rattan and resins." Guided cultural visits bring you into a village to meet residents, watch a **blowpipe demonstration**, and learn how forest plants and herbs are used. Ecological guides typically double as interpreters of both the culture and the surrounding jungle.

Treat these visits as a genuine cultural exchange: go with a reputable local operator, ask permission before taking photographs, and keep the tone respectful rather than voyeuristic. Some operators suggest bringing a small, useful gift; ask your guide what is actually appropriate rather than assuming.

## How do you get there and get around?

There is no airport or railway onto the plateau — you drive or are driven. According to Tourism Pahang, roads from **Tapah** and from the **Raub/Kuala Lipis** side converge at Ringlet before the route continues up through Tanah Rata to Brinchang. BOH's official travel guide adds a third approach: a separate road climbs from **Simpang Pulai** (Route 185) and enters the plateau from the north, reaching Kampung Raja first, above Brinchang. Public buses run up from Tapah to Tanah Rata for those without a car.

Once on the plateau, the settlements sit only a few kilometres apart along one main spine, so most people either self-drive, take taxis, or book the half-day and full-day tours that bundle a tea estate, a strawberry farm, the Mossy Forest and sometimes an Orang Asli village into a single loop.

## What's next

Cameron Highlands rewards a slow two- or three-day visit far more than a rushed day trip: base in Tanah Rata or Brinchang, split your time between a BOH tea centre, a strawberry-and-flower loop, and one guided village or Mossy Forest walk. Before you go, confirm current opening hours and Monday closures directly with each site, since seasonal and weather conditions on the plateau change plans quickly.
