You have landed, you are jet-lagged, and your phone shows no signal. Before you worry about the perfect nasi lemak, sort the boring-but-important things — a working SIM, the right emergency number, and knowing whether the sky is about to open. Here is the verified shortlist.

## What number do I call in an emergency?

**999.** Malaysia runs a single integrated line called the Malaysia Emergency Response System (MERS), which combined the old 991, 994 and 999 numbers into one when it launched on 1 October 2007. A single call reaches the police (PDRM), the ambulance service under the Ministry of Health, the Fire and Rescue Department (JBPM), and the Civil Defence Force (JPAM). Calls are free. Prank or false calls are an offence: the Civil Defence Force notes that under Section 233 of the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 a fake call can carry a fine of up to RM50,000, up to one year's imprisonment, or both — so use it only for genuine emergencies.

Save 999 in your phone before you need it.

## How do I get a working phone number?

Buy a prepaid SIM at the airport or any telco store, and bring your **original passport** for registration. Under the mandatory standard the regulator MCMC registered on 26 February 2026:

| Rule | Foreign visitor |
| --- | --- |
| Documents | Original passport (biometric verification) |
| Max SIMs per telco | 2 |
| Tourist SIM validity | Up to 3 months, then auto-terminated |

An eSIM from your telco or a travel-eSIM provider is a convenient alternative if your handset supports it and you only need data.

## When is the rainy season?

Malaysia is tropical and wet year-round, but the **Northeast Monsoon (November to March)** is the main rainy season. MET Malaysia flags it for heavy rain and large floods on the east-coast states — Kelantan, Terengganu, Pahang and east Johor — plus Sarawak and Sabah. If you plan to visit east-coast islands such as the Perhentians, Redang or Tioman in this window, confirm boat transfers and resort operations directly before booking, as rough seas frequently disrupt sailings. The **Southwest Monsoon (late May to September)** is generally drier. Check the MET Malaysia forecast before any boat or island plans.

## What about health and vaccinations?

This is general information, not medical advice — see a travel clinic ideally a month before you fly. The US CDC advises most travellers to Malaysia to be current on their **routine vaccines** and to consider **hepatitis A** and **typhoid**. Depending on your itinerary, ask about **hepatitis B**, **Japanese encephalitis**, **rabies**, and **antimalarial pills** — malaria prevention is advised only for rural, forested areas, not cities like Kuala Lumpur or Penang. No licensed traveller vaccine covers dengue, which is present across the country, so pack repellent (DEET or picaridin) and cover up at dusk.

## Do I tip, and how do I avoid scams?

Tipping is not part of the culture and is never expected; many hotels and sit-down restaurants already add a service charge to the bill, so rounding up or leaving small change is more than enough. For getting around, prefer e-hailing apps or insist a taxi runs its meter — flat "special price" quotes and "the meter is broken" are the classic overcharge lines. Keep your passport in the hotel safe and carry a copy.
