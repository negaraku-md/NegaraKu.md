Turn up at a Redang beach resort in December and you will likely find the boats gone, the jetty quiet and the dive shop shuttered. The east-coast islands run on a calendar written by the sea, not the tourist brochure — and by a set of marine-park rules most first-timers only discover at the ticket counter.

Perhentian, Redang, Kapas and Tioman are the four beach islands most travellers mean when they say "the east coast." All four sit inside gazetted marine parks, which shapes everything from what you pay to what you are allowed to touch once you are in the water.

## What makes these islands "marine parks," and why does it matter to you?

The protected zone is not just the beach. Malaysia's marine parks extend **generally two nautical miles from the lowest sea level** around each gazetted island — with a **one-nautical-mile** boundary for certain Terengganu islands — and they are managed by the **Department of Fisheries Malaysia** under the **Fisheries Act 1985 (Amended 1993)**. Nationwide, **49 islands** are gazetted as marine parks.

On the east coast, Terengganu's marine parks cover four archipelagos — **Pulau Redang, Pulau Perhentian, Pulau Kapas and Pulau Tenggol** — while Pahang's **Tioman** marine park is a cluster of **nine islands**: Pulau Tioman, Pulau Tulai, Pulau Labas, Pulau Sepoi, Pulau Seri Buat, Pulau Tokong Bahara, Pulau Gut, Pulau Cebeh and Pulau Sembilang.

What this means in practice: the reef you snorkel over is legally protected, a conservation charge is compulsory, and a list of "don'ts" applies the moment you enter the water.

## What will you actually pay before you board?

Every visitor pays a **conservation charge** set by the **Fees (Marine Parks Malaysia) Order 2017**, made under the Fees Act 1951. You pay it at a dedicated counter in the mainland jetty terminal — it is **separate from your boat fare** and is not bundled into a resort package.

| Visitor | Conservation charge |
|---|---|
| Malaysian (MyKad) adult | RM5.00 |
| Malaysian child (6–12) or senior (60+) | RM2.00 |
| Foreign adult | RM30.00 |
| Foreign child (6–12) or senior (60+) | RM15.00 |
| Disabled visitors | Free |

Island residents within the marine-park boundaries are exempt. The money is government revenue paid into the **Marine Parks and Marine Reserves Trust Fund**, used for managing and maintaining the parks and their visitor facilities.

If you are doing something beyond a day trip, other Fisheries Department rates apply:

| Activity | Fee |
|---|---|
| Camping | RM10.00 per night, per site (max 4 people) |
| Scuba diving (operator) | RM150.00 per month |
| Commercial filming / documentary | RM100.00 per day |
| Cruise vessel | RM100.00 per entry |
| Research | RM200.00 plus RM500.00 deposit |

## Which jetty do you sail from?

Each island has its own mainland departure point, so book the right one. Crossing times and boat fares change with the operator and season, so treat the pairings below as a starting point and confirm the details when you book:

- **Redang** — boats generally leave from **Merang** (Terengganu); some services also run from Kuala Terengganu.
- **Perhentian** — from **Kuala Besut** (Terengganu).
- **Kapas** — from **Marang** (Terengganu).
- **Tioman** — from **Mersing** or **Tanjung Gemok** (Pahang).

Remember that the boat fare and the marine-park charge are two separate payments — budget for both.

## What are the rules once you are in the water?

Marine parks are protected under the Fisheries Act 1985, and rangers do enforce the rules. The core statutory prohibitions mean that inside the park you may **not**:

- **Fish** — fishing is prohibited within marine-park boundaries.
- **Collect anything** — no taking any part of a marine organism, alive or dead, including coral pieces and shells.
- **Damage, touch or stand on coral.**
- **Anchor on or damage the reef** — boats should use mooring buoys or anchor only clear of coral, in designated areas.
- **Pollute or litter** — no dumping rubbish or contaminants into park waters.
- **Harass marine life** — no chasing, interfering with, or capturing animals.

Operators also enforce additional in-water restrictions — such as limits on high-powered water sports and keeping clear of marked boat-passage lanes — so check the posted rules and your operator's briefing before your first snorkel.

## Why does almost everything close in the monsoon?

The east coast faces the South China Sea, and from roughly **November to February** the **northeast monsoon** drives turbulent seas and heavy rain across Terengganu and Pahang. Sea crossings become unsafe and small-boat transfers unreliable.

There is no blanket government order to shut down. Instead, operators close voluntarily. Terengganu's state tourism office has said that hotels, resorts and guesthouses on the state's recreational islands — Redang, Perhentian, Kapas and Gemia among them — **suspend operations from October to February** for the monsoon, citing tourist safety in uncertain weather and turbulent seas, and using the downtime for repairs and maintenance.

The upshot for planning: treat **March to October** as the visiting season, with the calmest, clearest water generally falling in the middle of that window. If you see a resort advertising December availability, confirm boat transfers before you pay.

## How do the four islands compare?

Each island suits a slightly different trip:

| Island | State / jetty | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Perhentian | Terengganu / Kuala Besut | Two islands (Kecil and Besar); backpacker-friendly beaches plus quieter resorts |
| Redang | Terengganu / Merang | Resort-focused, package-heavy, postcard beaches |
| Kapas | Terengganu / Marang | Small, low-key, close to shore — good for a short escape |
| Tioman | Pahang / Mersing or Tanjung Gemok | The largest, with jungle interior, duty-free status and its own airstrip |

All four share the same non-negotiables: a conservation charge, the marine-park rulebook, and a season dictated by the monsoon.

## What's next

Before you book, lock down three things in order: **when** (aim for the March–October window and verify boat transfers if you are travelling near the monsoon edges), **which jetty** (match it to your island so you do not turn up in the wrong town), and **the fees** (budget the marine-park charge on top of your boat and accommodation). Keep MyKad or passport handy at the jetty counter — the conservation rate depends on it — and read the park rules before your first snorkel so a souvenir shell does not turn into a fisheries offence. For the legal backdrop, see the [Fisheries Act 1985](fisheries-act-1985) and the seasonal pattern in [the northeast monsoon](northeast-monsoon).
