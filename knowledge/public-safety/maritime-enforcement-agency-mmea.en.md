When a tugboat sinks in the middle of the Strait of Malacca or foreign fishermen intrude into Sabah's waters, it is a civilian agency in navy-blue uniforms that moves first — not the military. That is the Malaysian Maritime Enforcement Agency (MMEA), the nation's coast guard.

## What is the MMEA and why was it created?

Before the MMEA existed, various government agencies shared duties at sea, each with its own vessels and jurisdiction. A government study in April 1999 found "overlapping functions, overlapping jurisdictions, and uneconomical use of resources" among these agencies.

The solution was a single agency. The MMEA was established under the **Malaysian Maritime Enforcement Agency Act 2004**. The Act was gazetted on 1 July 2004 and came into force on 15 February 2005; the agency subsequently reached operational status through a soft launch on 30 November 2005 and was officially launched on 21 March 2006. It functions as a coast guard — a civilian enforcement agency, separate from the Royal Malaysian Navy, which focuses on defence. The MMEA was originally placed under the Prime Minister's Department and is now under the Ministry of Home Affairs.

## What are the MMEA's core duties?

The MMEA's official mission is to "enforce the law, save lives and property at sea" to ensure the safety of the Malaysian Maritime Zone. The three words in its motto — Guard, Protect, Rescue — sum up that role:

- **Guard:** enforcing federal law in the Malaysian Maritime Zone, including curbing smuggling, foreign fishermen's intrusions, and unauthorised entry.
- **Protect:** combating maritime crimes such as piracy as well as marine pollution, and maintaining public order in the nation's waters.
- **Rescue:** coordinating search and rescue (SAR) operations at sea.

As a comprehensive commitment to the public, the MMEA's Customer Service Mission is summed up as **QUICK, FIRM, FAIR**: carrying out its duties efficiently, enforcing the rules firmly, and treating all parties equally regardless of status.

## How does the MMEA coordinate search and rescue (SAR)?

When an emergency occurs at sea, the MMEA is the lead coordinating agency. This function is managed through the **Search, Rescue and Disaster Assistance Division (BCLBB)**, which handles operations to search for missing victims, evacuate patients, and provide disaster assistance in Malaysian waters.

## How is the MMEA organised along the coast?

To protect the long coastline of both the Peninsula and Borneo, the MMEA is organised by region and state. Its official directory covers the following state maritime teams:

- Kedah/Perlis, Penang, Perak, Selangor, Melaka & Negeri Sembilan, and Johor on the west and south coasts.
- Pahang, Terengganu, and Kelantan on the east coast of the Peninsula.
- Sabah and Sarawak in East Malaysia.

To ensure a continuous presence, the MMEA maintains deployments of assets at sea in every maritime region. This structure enables a single agency to cover vast waters without the resource-sapping overlap — the original problem that led to its establishment.

## Long-term vision

The MMEA's vision is "to be among the best maritime enforcement agencies in the world", in line with its mission to enforce the law and save lives and property at sea to ensure the safety of the Malaysian Maritime Zone.
