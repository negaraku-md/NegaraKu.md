When a house catches fire, a car is trapped in a flood, or a worker is
wedged in a confined space, one and the same number becomes the first link
between the victim and help — and at the end of that line, more often than
not, is Bomba. Understanding what the law actually empowers this department
to do explains why Bomba turns up at so many kinds of emergency, not just
fires.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Full name | Jabatan Bomba dan Penyelamat Malaysia (JBPM) / Fire and Rescue Department of Malaysia |
| Common name | Bomba |
| Type | Federal government department |
| Parent ministry | Ministry of Housing and Local Government (KPKT) |
| Enabling statute | Fire Services Act 1988 (Act 341) |
| Royal assent | 11 May 1988 |
| Gazetted | 9 June 1988 |
| In force | 1 January 1989 (P.U. (B) 701/1988), throughout Malaysia |
| Head of department | Director General of Fire Services (Ketua Pengarah Bomba) |
| Headquarters | Lebuh Wawasan, Presint 7, 62250 Putrajaya |
| Headquarters line | 03-8892 7600 |
| Emergency number | 999 (MERS 999) |
| Website | `bomba.gov.my` |

Act 341 was given royal assent on 11 May 1988 and gazetted on 9 June
1988, but only came into force throughout Malaysia on 1 January 1989.
The long title of the Act states its purpose succinctly: to make provision
for "the effective and efficient functioning of the Fire Services Department,
for the protection of persons and property from fire risks". The full text of
the Act can be consulted on the official portal of the Attorney General's
Chambers (AGC), which lists the latest reprint dated 1 April 2023.

## What is Bomba's job in law, exactly?

Most people think of Bomba purely as firefighters. Section 5 of the Act
gives a broader picture — it lists the department's official duties, and it
is that list which allows Bomba to be present at scenes where there is no
fire at all.

| Provision | Duty |
| --- | --- |
| Section 5(1)(a)(i) | Extinguishing, fighting, preventing and controlling fires |
| Section 5(1)(a)(ii) | Protecting life and property in the event of a fire |
| Section 5(1)(a)(iii)–(iv) | Ensuring adequate emergency exits and means of escape in certain premises |
| Section 5(1)(b) | Investigating the cause, origin and circumstances of a fire |
| Section 5(1)(c) | Carrying out humanitarian services, including protecting life and property in any disaster |
| Section 5(2) | Other duties imposed by law or directed by the Minister |

It is the humanitarian-services clause in Section 5(1)(c) that is the
statutory basis for Bomba's rescue work. This is in turn reinforced by
Section 19, which gives Bomba officers the power to act in "emergencies not
involving fire" — that is, emergencies that do not involve a fire at all.

## More than just putting out fires

Because of that mandate, Bomba has become a versatile response agency. In
the field, its role typically encompasses:

- **Firefighting** — residential, commercial, industrial and open fires.
- **Technical rescue** — road accidents, water rescue, confined spaces, and
  victims trapped in collapses.
- **Disaster relief** — floods, storms and major incidents, in line with the
  humanitarian-services duty under Section 5(1)(c).
- **Fire investigation** — determining the cause and origin of fires under
  Section 5(1)(b).
- **Fire safety and prevention** — inspections, fire certificates and public
  education, under the framework of Part III and Part V of the Act.

For purely medical emergencies, the lead agency within the 999 system is the
Ministry of Health's ambulance service; Bomba, for its part, provides rescue
response and on-scene support under its humanitarian mandate.

## How is Bomba organised and led?

The Act establishes a "Fire Services Department of the Federation" headed by
a Director General of Fire Services, assisted by Deputy Directors General,
Assistant Directors General and Directors (Section 3). Importantly for the
public: subsection 3(3) requires a **State Director of Fire Services for each
state** — so every state has its own Bomba administration, with fire stations
under it. Official statistics on the number of stations and personnel by
state are published by JBPM through `data.gov.my`.

Every Bomba officer is subject to the control and direction of the Director
General (subsection 3(5)), and the Director General may delegate his powers
in writing to any officer (Section 7).

## How do I contact Bomba?

| Need | Contact |
| --- | --- |
| Emergency (fire, rescue, disaster) | **999** — free |
| General enquiries / headquarters | 03-8892 7600 |
| State matters (fire certificates, inspections) | The respective State Fire and Rescue Department, via `bomba.gov.my` |

In an emergency, dial 999. The call is handled by MERS 999 (Malaysia
Emergency Response Services), a single platform connecting five agencies —
the police, Bomba, the Ministry of Health, the Malaysian Maritime Enforcement
Agency and the civil defence force. State your location precisely and the
type of emergency so that the call is routed to the correct agency.
According to the Malaysia Civil Defence Force (APM), every call made through
the 999 emergency line is free.

> **Nomenclature note:** the civil defence agency is now officially known as
> the **Malaysia Civil Defence Force (APM)** on its official website
> (`civildefence.gov.my`). The MERS 999 page at `malaysia.gov.my`, meanwhile,
> still lists it under the old name **Civil Defence Department (JPAM)** — the
> same body. Confirm the latest official nomenclature before relying on it in
> official documents.

## What next

If you are dealing with building fire safety — fire certificates, emergency
exits or inspections — refer to your State Fire and Rescue Department and the
provisions of Part V of Act 341. If your interest is Malaysia's emergency
response system as a whole, start with how **999 / MERS 999** routes calls
between the five agencies, and the roles of partner agencies such as the
Malaysia Civil Defence Force (APM) and the Royal Malaysia Police. For the
full text of the statutory duties and powers, refer to the Fire Services Act
1988 (Act 341) on the official portal of the Attorney General's Chambers
listed in the sources below.
