The balance in your banking app suddenly drops, or you have just tapped
"Confirm" on a transfer that now feels terribly wrong. Your heart pounds.
In that moment, only one thing genuinely changes the outcome: not panic, but
knowing which hotline to call — and calling it within the next few minutes,
not tomorrow.

Online scams are no longer a minor problem in Malaysia. Losses reached
**RM2.97 billion in 2025**, a surge of RM1.40 billion from RM1.57 billion the
previous year, with **66,204 cases** recorded — an 87% rise from 35,368 cases
in 2024. These figures were announced by Inspector-General of Police Tan Sri
Mohd Khalid Ismail on 15 June 2026. Non-existent investment schemes accounted
for the largest losses at RM1.47 billion, while phone scams remained the most
frequent with 28,388 cases. This guide explains what you must do immediately
after being scammed, how to check suspicious numbers, and how your report is
actually processed.

## What should you do in the first hour?

The flow of action is simple, and the order matters. Do it simultaneously if
you can — have someone next to you call the bank while you call 997.

1. **Call 997 immediately** if money has already been transferred. Do not wait
   to see whether it "goes through" or "succeeds" first.
2. **Contact your bank's scam hotline at the same time.** Bank hotlines operate
   24/7 and the bank is the party that can technically hold or freeze
   transactions and the recipient's account.
3. **Do not go looking for a separate police report.** From September 2025, a
   call to 997 itself counts as an official police report.
4. **Save all evidence.** Transfer receipts, screenshots of conversations, the
   scammer's phone number and account number — you will need them during the
   call.
5. **Do not delete anything and do not contact the scammer again.** Warning the
   scammer only speeds up their withdrawal of the money.

The 997 hotline is managed by the National Scam Response Centre (NSRC), a joint
operations centre that brings together the police, Bank Negara Malaysia, MCMC,
financial institutions and telecommunications companies under one roof to
respond quickly to online financial scams.

## Which hotline for which situation?

Not every problem goes to 997. This table maps situations to the correct
channel.

| Situation | Channel | Who handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Money has already been transferred to a scammer, or you suspect so | **997** | National Scam Response Centre (NSRC) — PDRM, BNM, MCMC and banks working together |
| Suspicious transaction on your account; want to freeze the card/account | **Your bank's scam hotline** (24/7) | Your own bank |
| Life-threatening emergency — police, fire, ambulance | **999** (**112** can also be dialled from a mobile phone) | Malaysia Emergency Response Services (MERS 999) |
| Passing on information or making commercial-crime enquiries | **013-211 1222** (CCID Infoline, WhatsApp/SMS only) | Commercial Crime Investigation Department (JSJK/CCID), PDRM |
| Checking an account or phone number before paying | **semakmule.rmp.gov.my** | JSJK/CCID, PDRM |

Note the crucial difference: **999 is not for financial scams** — that is 997.
Calling 999 for lost money only wastes precious time.

## Why does speed determine everything?

After money leaves your account, it usually moves quickly through a network of
"mule accounts" before being withdrawn as cash or converted into other assets.
As long as the money is still within the banking system and has not been
withdrawn, there is a chance it can be held or frozen.

That chance is highest in the **first few hours**. That is why a fast report is
far more effective than a complete but late one. Contacting the bank at the
same time as 997 is important because it is the bank that has direct control
over the account — 997 coordinates, but the bank ultimately presses the freeze
button.

For the same reason, "waiting a day to make sure" is a costly mistake. By
tomorrow, the money is usually gone.

## How is the report handled after you call 997?

A 997 call does not merely take your details and hang up. The NSRC functions as
a command room that coordinates several parties at once:

- **Tracing and holding the flow of money.** Recipient account information is
  passed to the banks involved to attempt to hold or freeze the balance.
- **Recording the report.** Since September 2025, the call is recorded as a
  police report, and an investigating officer will be assigned to your case.
- **Follow-up through the investigating officer.** The 997 hotline is for
  emergency response, not status enquiries. Case updates are made through the
  assigned investigating officer, not by calling 997 again.

After the call, keep the reference number or report number you are given. You
will need it for any follow-up, insurance claim, or enquiry with the bank.

## How do you check a suspicious account or phone number?

SemakMule (semakmule.rmp.gov.my) is a free public portal operated by the PDRM's
Commercial Crime Investigation Department (JSJK/CCID). It lets anyone check
whether a **bank account number** or **phone number** has been reported as
involved in commercial-crime cases.

Use this tool **before** you pay, not after. Example situations:

- A "seller" on social media gives you an account number to deposit for goods
  you have not yet seen — enter that number in SemakMule first.
- An overseas "employer" asks for a training or visa fee to be paid to a local
  account — check the account number and phone number.
- A call claiming to be from a bank or the authorities with a phone number you
  can check.

If the number is already flagged, that is a clear red flag. But remember: a
number that is *not* flagged does not mean it is safe — it may be a new mule
account that has not yet been reported. SemakMule confirms known danger; it
cannot confirm safety.

## What information should you have ready before making a report?

The call goes more smoothly if you prepare these details in advance. Based on
the official NSRC list:

- **The chronology of events** — what happened, from beginning to end.
- **Your personal details** — name, phone number, identity card number, and
  your own bank account number.
- **The suspect's information** — name, phone number, and recipient account
  number.
- **Transaction details** — the amount, date and time of each transfer.
- **The transfer receipt or slip** as evidence.
- **The URL links** shared by the scammer.
- **Communication evidence** — screenshots of conversations, call logs,
  messages.

One important warning the authorities emphasise: **the NSRC, the police and
banks will never ask for your username, password, PIN number, TAC or OTP.**
This is stated clearly on the official NSRC page at the NFCC. Anyone who asks
for them — even claiming to be from the scam response centre itself — is a
scammer. You give information *about* the case; you never give the keys to your
account.

## How does the CCID Infoline differ from 997?

For commercial-crime information or enquiries that are not the "money just
disappeared" emergency, the PDRM provides the **CCID Infoline** at
**013-211 1222**. This line has specific features you should understand:

- It operates **only via SMS and WhatsApp** — no voice calls are accepted.
- The operating hours are **8 am to 12 midnight** every day.
- It is for passing on information, complaints or enquiries relating to
  commercial crime such as online scams, Macau Scam, buy-and-sell fraud, and
  non-existent loans.
- It accepts only registered Malaysian telecommunications numbers, and reports
  must be genuine, not a prank.

So the simple order is: **997** for the emergency of money on the move; **the
bank** to freeze it; **the CCID Infoline** to pass on information or enquiries;
**SemakMule** to check before paying.

## Decision framework: which channel for me?

| Question | If YES | Channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Has money already been transferred to a scammer? | Call immediately | 997 + bank hotline |
| Is a life in danger right now? | Call immediately | 999 / 112 |
| Want to check a number before paying? | Check first | semakmule.rmp.gov.my |
| Have commercial-crime information/enquiries? | Send a message | 013-211 1222 |
| Lost documents, not money? | Make a report | Police station / PDRM e-Reporting |

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting to "make sure first".** The window for freezing money is measured
  in hours, not days. Call first, arrange the evidence later.
- **Calling 999 for financial scams.** That is not the channel — 997 is the
  right one, and 999 is only for life-threatening emergencies.
- **Assuming a separate report at a station is still mandatory.** Since
  September 2025, a 997 call itself counts as a police report for that case.
- **Handing over an OTP, TAC or password** to anyone who calls, even if they
  claim to be helping investigate your case.
- **Paying first, checking later.** SemakMule is useful before a transfer, not
  as a regret afterwards.
- **Calling back a number the scammer gave you** to "verify" — always use the
  official bank or agency number, not the number in that message.
