Call yourself an "accountant" in Malaysia without registering with one particular body, and you are in fact committing a criminal offence. That body is the Malaysian Institute of Accountants — the MIA — and it holds the sole authority to decide who counts as an accountant in this country.

The MIA is not merely a professional association. It is a statutory body born of legislation, and the designation you place after your name — as well as your right to open an accounting firm — depends entirely on it.

## What is the MIA and where does its power come from?

The MIA was established under the **Accountants Act 1967**. Section 3 of that Act creates the MIA as a body corporate, that is, a perpetual legal entity that can sue and be sued in its own name.

Because the MIA's authority comes from statute rather than from the constitution of a voluntary association, its decisions bind the entire profession. The MIA is the only organisation in Malaysia with the right to determine whether a person is qualified to be recognised as an accountant.

The Institute joined the **International Federation of Accountants (IFAC)** in 1977, and now has more than **32,000 members** across various industries in Malaysia and around the world. It is also a member of the ASEAN Federation of Accountants.

## Who oversees the MIA itself?

The MIA operates under the jurisdiction of the **Ministry of Finance**. This means the regulator of the profession is itself subject to government oversight.

For the audit of large companies, there is a further layer. Audits of public interest entities (PIEs) are overseen by the **Audit Oversight Board (AOB)**, which was established under Part IIIA of the Securities Commission Malaysia Act 1993 and began operating on 1 April 2010. The AOB registers PIE auditors, conducts inspections, and can impose sanctions. Financial reporting standards, meanwhile, are developed by the **Malaysian Accounting Standards Board (MASB)**, the standard-setting body established under the Financial Reporting Act 1997.

In short, the MIA regulates *who* can be an accountant and *how* they behave ethically; the AOB regulates the quality of public company audits; and the MASB sets the accounting standards.

## Why is the title "accountant" protected by law?

This is where the MIA's power is felt most keenly. The Accountants Act 1967 maintains an official register, and Section 13 divides registered members into three statutory categories:

| Category (Section 13) | Designation letters (Section 17) | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Chartered Accountant | C.A.(M) | The primary designation the MIA grants to fully qualified members |
| Licensed Accountant | L.A.(M) | A statutory category under the Act |
| Associate Member | A.M.(M) | A statutory category under the Act |

Sections 22 and 23 of the Act set out strict restrictions. No one may — unless they are registered under this Act and resident in Malaysia — practise or call themselves a chartered accountant, licensed accountant or associate member. The prohibition also extends to the use of the term "accountant" or any similar term in circumstances that could lead the public to believe the person is a registered accountant.

Breaching these provisions is not merely a disciplinary matter. Under **Section 26**, a person who contravenes Section 22 or 23 commits an offence and may, on conviction:

- **First offence:** a fine not exceeding **RM10,000** or imprisonment of up to **one year**.
- **Second or subsequent offence:** a fine not exceeding **RM20,000** or imprisonment of up to **two years**.

## How does someone become a Chartered Accountant Malaysia?

Becoming an MIA member requires a qualification recognised in the First Schedule to the Act. There are two main routes:

- **Examination route (Part I of the First Schedule):** pass one of the final examinations listed in Part I, *plus* have at least **three years** of relevant practical accounting experience; or
- **Recognised body route (Part II of the First Schedule):** become a member of one of the professional bodies recognised in Part II of the First Schedule (for example CIMA — the full list of other bodies such as ACCA, ICAEW, CPA Australia or MICPA should be verified against the current text of the First Schedule).

Alternatively, a person may pass the **MIA Qualifying Examination**.

Once registered, a member may use the C.A.(M) designation. But membership alone does not entitle a person to open a firm offering services to the public — that requires an additional step.

## What is required to open a public practice firm?

To offer public practice services (such as audit, tax or accounting for clients), an MIA member must hold a valid **practising certificate**. Before applying, the member must attend and complete the MIA Public Practice Programme.

For those who wish to become auditors, there is a clear chain of qualifications:

1. Become an MIA member registered as a C.A.(M).
2. Accumulate at least three years of relevant audit experience with an MIA-approved firm.
3. Obtain an MIA practising certificate (including an interview with the MIA Practising Certificate Committee).
4. The name is proposed to the Ministry of Finance, which issues the **audit licence under Section 263(2) of the Companies Act 2016**.

Renewing a practising certificate requires ongoing compliance, including Continuing Professional Development (CPD) hours. The practising certificate is a prerequisite qualification; the audit licence from the Ministry of Finance is the statutory authorisation that allows a person to actually audit. That audit licence is issued for a limited period (typically two years) and is subject to renewal.

## How does the MIA ensure its practitioners are ethical?

The Act gives the MIA the power to set ethical requirements, an investigation and disciplinary system, and a quality assurance review mechanism. The MIA exercises this power through the **By-Laws (On Professional Ethics, Conduct and Practice)**, which incorporate the International Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants.

The two main enforcement mechanisms are:

- **Practice Review Programme** — quality assurance for registered audit firms.
- **Investigation and Disciplinary System** — the MIA investigates complaints against members, and its Disciplinary Committee can impose sanctions such as a warning, a fine, suspension or cancellation of membership.

The By-Laws are updated from time to time to stay aligned with the International Code of Ethics. For example, the revised definitions of *Public Interest Entity* and *Listed Entity* take effect for audits of financial statements for periods beginning on or after **15 December 2024**.

## What are the next steps?

If you are a graduate or an aspiring accountant, check whether your qualification is listed in the First Schedule to the Accountants Act 1967, then plan your three years of practical experience towards C.A.(M) membership. If your goal is to open a firm or become an auditor, study the sequence of the MIA practising certificate before the Ministry of Finance audit licence.

If you are instead an employer or a client who wants to confirm that someone really is a registered accountant, the MIA provides an official register of members — the safest way to ensure that the title "accountant" being used is legally valid. Remember: the provisions of Sections 22, 23 and 26 mean that the title is not merely a label, but a protected qualification.
