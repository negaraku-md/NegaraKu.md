You have just registered a Sdn Bhd, the business is still small, and the company secretary has just sent an audit-fee invoice. The question is not "how much does an audit cost" — but "am I actually required to be audited?" For most private limited companies in Malaysia, the default answer is **yes**, and only a small group of companies truly qualify to escape it.

This article explains the basic rule under the Companies Act 2016: who must be audited, who qualifies for exemption, and how auditors are appointed, resign and are removed.

## What is the default rule — must every Sdn Bhd be audited?

The starting point is simple. **Section 267(1) of the Companies Act 2016 requires every private company to appoint an auditor for each financial year** for the purpose of auditing its financial statements. This is not optional — it is a statutory requirement that applies to every Sdn Bhd by default.

The appointed auditor is responsible under **section 266** (powers and duties of auditors) for reporting to the members of the company whether the financial statements give a true and fair view in accordance with the approved accounting standards, and whether proper accounting records have been kept. To carry out that duty, the auditor is given a right of access at all reasonable times to the company's accounting records, and a right to require information from the officers of the company.

So the default rule can be summed up like this: **an audit is mandatory, unless your company falls within a category that the Registrar has exempted.** The power to grant that exemption comes from **section 267(2)**, which allows the Registrar to exempt any private company from the requirement to appoint an auditor, subject to prescribed criteria and conditions.

## Who qualifies for exemption from audit?

The exemption criteria are now set out in **Practice Directive No. 10/2024**, issued by SSM on 16 December 2024. PD 10/2024 **revokes** the older PD 3/2017 and introduces an approach based on financial thresholds.

There are two routes to exemption: **dormant** companies, and companies that **meet the size thresholds**.

### Route 1: Dormant companies

A company that is dormant remains exempt from the audit requirement. A company is regarded as dormant in a financial year if it **carries on no business and has no accounting transaction occurring**. An "accounting transaction" means a transaction the record of which must be kept under section 245(1) of the Companies Act 2016 — but excludes transactions arising from a statutory obligation the company is required to pay (for example, statutory fees) and the related costs of complying with it.

Two situations qualify:

- A company that is **dormant from the time of its incorporation**; or
- A company that is **dormant throughout the current financial year and the immediately preceding financial year**.

### Route 2: Size thresholds (the two-of-three criteria)

For active companies, exemption depends on size. A company qualifies if it meets **at least two of the three** criteria below, for the current financial year **and** the two preceding financial years:

- **(a)** Annual revenue not exceeding the prescribed threshold;
- **(b)** Total assets in the statement of financial position not exceeding the prescribed threshold; or
- **(c)** The number of employees at the end of the financial year not exceeding the prescribed threshold.

The important thing to understand: these thresholds are **not fixed** — they rise in phases over three years to help companies adjust to the new framework.

## What are the phased thresholds under PD 10/2024?

This is the part most often misunderstood. The threshold depends on when the company's financial period **begins**, not when the statements are lodged. The table below is the core of the Practice Directive:

| Phase | Financial period begins | Year of submission | Revenue (not exceeding) | Total assets (not exceeding) | Employees (not exceeding) |
|------|------------------------|------------------|------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Phase 1 | On/after 1 Jan 2025 (up to 31 Dec 2025) | From 1 Jan 2026 | RM1,000,000 | RM1,000,000 | 10 |
| Phase 2 | On/after 1 Jan 2026 (up to 31 Dec 2026) | From 1 Jan 2027 | RM2,000,000 | RM2,000,000 | 20 |
| Phase 3 | On/after 1 Jan 2027 | From 1 Jan 2028 | RM3,000,000 | RM3,000,000 | 30 |

Remember, the revenue, total assets and number of employees for the **two preceding financial years** must also not exceed the maximum threshold for the relevant phase. The full RM3 million threshold only takes effect for financial periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

### How are "revenue", "assets" and "employees" defined?

These definitions matter because they determine whether you are truly under the threshold:

- **Annual revenue** includes revenue received and receivable in the year, but **excludes** credit entries for the reversal of earlier accounting entries, tax-related entries, reversals of earlier provisions, and gains from the derecognition of property, plant and equipment as well as investment property.
- **Total assets** means assets as defined in the approved accounting standards, including current and non-current assets.
- **Employees** means full-time employees at the end of each financial year — including local, foreign, contract and probationary employees — but **excludes** directors who also work full-time, shareholders who also work full-time, or family members/friends who are unpaid or receive irregular wages.

## Who CANNOT be exempt, however small?

Some companies do not qualify at all, regardless of size or level of activity. PD 10/2024 lists the exemption as not applying to:

- **Exempt private companies** (EPCs) that have elected to lodge an EPC status certificate with the Registrar under section 260;
- **Public companies**, including listed companies;
- **Private companies that are subsidiaries** of a public company; and
- **Foreign companies**.

This means, for example, that a Sdn Bhd wholly owned by a listed public company **must still be audited** even if it is dormant or micro-sized.

## Decision framework: does my Sdn Bhd need to be audited?

Use this sequence of questions to determine your company's position:

1. **Is the company a public company, a subsidiary of a public company, a foreign company, or an EPC that has lodged a certificate?** If yes → **must be audited.** Stop here.
2. **Is the company dormant from incorporation, or dormant throughout the current year and the preceding year?** If yes → **qualifies for exemption** (Route 1).
3. **Does the company meet at least two of the three thresholds (revenue, assets, employees) for the current year and the two preceding years, according to the correct phase?** If yes → **qualifies for exemption** (Route 2).
4. **Has any member holding ≥5% of the shares (or ≥5% of members), or the Registrar, sent written notice demanding an audit?** If yes → **must be audited** even if it qualifies for exemption.
5. If none of the exemption routes apply → **must be audited** under section 267(1).

Bear in mind: qualifying for exemption does not mean being automatically exempt. The company **elects** not to be audited. It can always appoint an auditor voluntarily — for example, if a bank or investor demands audited statements.

## Can shareholders compel an audit even if the company qualifies for exemption?

Yes — and this is an important protection for minority members. Even where a company qualifies for exemption, it **must still audit its accounts** if it receives written notice requiring an audit, given **not later than one month before the end of the financial year**, from:

- Any member holding in aggregate **not less than 5%** of the total issued shares (or of the shares of a particular class);
- **Not less than 5%** of the total number of members eligible to vote; or
- The **Registrar**, directing the company to audit its accounts.

## Even if exempt — what still needs to be lodged?

Audit exemption is **not** an exemption from lodging financial statements. This misunderstanding often causes compliance mistakes.

A company that elects for the exemption must:

- **Prepare financial statements** that comply with the approved accounting standards (section 244(1));
- **Lodge those unaudited statements** with the Registrar, together with the certificates required under sections 258 and 259;
- Include the **directors' report, statement by directors, and statutory declaration**, along with other lodgements under sections 251 and 252; and
- Attach the **audit exemption certificate** (Appendix 1 of PD 10/2024) signed by a director, confirming that the company qualifies for the exemption under section 267(2) and that members have not demanded an audit for that year.

For a private company, financial statements generally need to be **circulated within six months** of the end of the financial year (section 258(1)(a)), and the first set prepared within **18 months** of the date of incorporation (section 248(1)(a)). *(Both of these periods are listed for re-verification against the text of the Act — see `verificationNeeded`.)*

## How is an auditor appointed?

For companies that do need to be audited, the mechanics of appointment are governed by sections 267–270:

- **First appointment** — the directors may appoint an auditor before the first general meeting (or, for a private company, before the first financial statements are circulated).
- **Subsequent appointments** — the members appoint the auditor by ordinary resolution.
- **Failure to appoint** — if the company fails to appoint an auditor, the **Registrar may appoint** an auditor on the application of a member (section 268).
- **Automatic re-appointment** — for a private company, the Act provides for automatic re-appointment of the existing auditor, unless the members take steps to prevent it (section 270). *(Verify the exact terms of section 270 against the text of the Act — see `verificationNeeded`.)*

## How does an auditor resign or get removed?

The auditor's life cycle does not end with appointment. There are three main mechanisms:

**Resignation (section 281).** An auditor may resign by giving **written notice** to the company at its registered office. The auditor's office ends **21 days** after the notice is given, or on the later date stated in the notice, whichever is later.

**Notification to the Registrar (section 282).** After an auditor resigns, the company must notify SSM. The "Notification of Resignation of Auditor by Company" is submitted to SSM through the Corporate Registry System (CRS), accompanied by a copy of the resignation notice.

**Removal (sections 276 and 277).** The members of the company may **remove** an auditor at any time by **ordinary resolution** at a general meeting. However, **special notice** is required for that resolution (section 277); the company must inform the auditor and the Registrar, and the auditor has the right to submit written representations. After an auditor is removed or resigns, the directors must call a general meeting as soon as reasonably practicable to appoint a new auditor.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Several recurring mistakes often undermine compliance:

- **Assuming audit exemption means no filing.** Wrong — unaudited statements must still be lodged with the exemption certificate.
- **Using the threshold from the wrong phase.** The RM3 million threshold does not apply to financial periods beginning in 2025; that phase is still at RM1 million and 10 employees.
- **Forgetting to check the two preceding years.** Eligibility depends on the current year **and** the two previous financial years — not the current year alone.
- **Overlooking the category exclusions.** A subsidiary of a public company must still be audited however small it is.
- **Counting directors/shareholders as employees.** The definition of "employee" excludes directors and shareholders who work full-time — a miscount can lead a company to wrongly think it exceeds the threshold.
- **Thinking PD 3/2017 still applies.** PD 3/2017 has been revoked; it only remains applicable to statements with financial periods beginning on or before 31 December 2024.

## What's next

If you manage a Sdn Bhd, start by determining the **start date of the financial year** and matching it to the correct phase of PD 10/2024, then check the revenue, assets and employee figures for three financial years (the current one and the two preceding). If you meet two of the three criteria and no category exclusion applies, discuss with your company secretary whether to **elect** for the exemption — and make sure the unaudited statements are still lodged with the correct certificate.

If in doubt about eligibility, dormancy, or the effect of a shareholder notice, refer to the full text of the Companies Act 2016 and Practice Directive 10/2024 on the SSM portal, or seek advice from a licensed company secretary.
