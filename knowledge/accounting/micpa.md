MICPA is one of the oldest names in Malaysian accountancy, and one of the most
misread. People assume the body that trains certified public accountants is also
the body that licenses them to practise. It is not. MICPA qualifies people; the
Malaysian Institute of Accountants licenses them. Keeping the two apart is the
point of this page.

## A short history

The institute was incorporated on **26 July 1958** as the Malayan Association of
Certified Public Accountants, with twenty founding members. It became the
**Malaysian Association of Certified Public Accountants (MACPA)** on 6 July 1964,
and took its current name, **the Malaysian Institute of Certified Public
Accountants (MICPA)**, on 29 January 2002. Its professional education and
training programme dates back to 1961, which makes it one of the longest-running
accountancy qualifications in the country.

## Qualification, not a licence to practise

This is the distinction the page exists to make. MICPA confers the **CPA(M)**
designation — evidence that a person has met its examination and experience
requirements. It does **not**, by itself, confer the legal right to practise
accountancy in Malaysia.

That right runs through the **Malaysian Institute of Accountants (MIA)**, the
statutory regulator under the Accountants Act 1967. No one may call themselves an
accountant or run a public-accountancy practice without registering with MIA, and
an **audit licence** requires a further approval from the Minister of Finance
under s.263 of the Companies Act 2016. So a MICPA member who wants to practise
still registers with MIA — the qualification establishes competence, the MIA
registration confers the right to act.

## The MICPA-CAANZ programme

MICPA's current professional pathway is the **MICPA-CAANZ programme**, a joint
qualification run with **Chartered Accountants Australia and New Zealand (CAANZ)**
that originated in a 2009 agreement and was renewed in 2016. Completing it leads
to a qualification recognised by both bodies and, through CAANZ, access to the
**Global Accounting Alliance** — a network of chartered accountancy bodies whose
members enjoy reciprocal recognition in several major economies.

## Standing under the Companies Act 2016

MICPA is the **fifth body listed in the Fourth Schedule** to the Companies Act
2016, so its members are qualified to act as company secretaries under
s.235(2)(a) — on the same footing as MAICSA, MIA, the Malaysian Bar and the other
approved bodies. As with every Fourth Schedule body, that is a qualification: a
person must still obtain a s.241 practising certificate from SSM before acting.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating MICPA as the accountancy regulator.** MIA regulates and registers;
  MICPA trains and qualifies.
- **Assuming CPA(M) alone permits public practice.** Practice runs through MIA
  registration, and audit through a Ministry of Finance licence.
- **Confusing MICPA with MIA because both date their IFAC membership to 1977.**
  They are distinct bodies with distinct functions.
- **Reading "CPA" as the US credential.** CPA(M) is the Malaysian designation
  conferred by MICPA, not the American CPA.

## What's next

To check whether a specific person may lawfully practise, verify **MIA
registration and, for audit, a Ministry of Finance audit licence** rather than
the MICPA credential alone. For how the statutory regulator works, read the MIA
profile; for the body that sets the accounting standards those accountants apply,
read the MASB profile.
