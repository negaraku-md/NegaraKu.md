## What the Progressive Wage Policy is — and is not

The **Progressive Wage Policy** (Malay: *Dasar Gaji Progresif*, DGP) is a
government scheme that pays employers a temporary monthly **cash incentive** to
raise the wages of lower- and middle-income workers, on the understanding that
those workers are also upskilling. It was set out in a
[White Paper tabled in Parliament in November 2023](https://ekonomi.gov.my/sites/default/files/2023-11/Kertas_Putih_Cadangan_Dasar_Gaji_Progresif.pdf)
(Command Paper 69/2023) and is led by the Ministry of Economy.

The single most important thing to understand is that it is **voluntary**. An
employer chooses whether to join. That makes it the mirror image of the
[minimum wage](/en/employment/minimum-wage-malaysia/), which is a mandatory
floor every covered employer must pay. The Progressive Wage Policy does not
replace the minimum wage and does not set a new legal floor — it sits *above*
the floor and tries to pull pay upward through incentives rather than compulsion.

## How the incentive works

Under the White Paper, an employer that joins and meets the conditions receives
a fixed cash incentive for each eligible employee:

- **up to RM200 a month** for an **entry-level** employee, and
- **up to RM300 a month** for a **non-entry-level** employee,

paid for **12 months** per worker. The employer is expected to increase the
worker's actual salary by at least the same amount, so the subsidy is meant to
*kick-start* a raise rather than permanently fund it. The White Paper also noted
that the incentive rates would be **reviewed each year** against the
government's fiscal position.

The incentive is paid to the **employer**, not directly to the worker — the
worker benefits through the higher salary the employer commits to.

## Who is eligible

The scheme is aimed at **local employees earning between RM1,500 and under
RM5,000 a month** — the band above the minimum wage where wage stagnation is most
common. Eligibility is tied to the worker undergoing **approved skills training**,
which is the mechanism by which the policy links higher pay to higher
productivity rather than simply topping up salaries.

Participation conditions apply to employers as well; the pilot was oriented
toward local companies rather than large multinationals and government-linked
companies. (The precise training requirement and the current scope exclusions
are listed under *verification needed* below pending confirmation against the
official guidelines.)

## Progressive wage versus minimum wage

| | Minimum wage | Progressive Wage Policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Mandatory legal floor | Voluntary incentive scheme |
| Who is bound | All covered employers | Only employers who opt in |
| Mechanism | A floor set by gazetted Order | Government subsidy + employer top-up |
| Pay level | RM1,700/month (from 2025) | Targets the RM1,500–under-RM5,000 band |
| Linked to skills? | No | Yes — tied to upskilling |
| Duration | Permanent | Incentive runs 12 months per worker |

The two are designed to work together: the
[minimum wage](/en/employment/minimum-wage-malaysia/) stops pay falling below a
floor, while the Progressive Wage Policy tries to move workers *up* from that
floor as they gain skills.

## How employers join

Employers register **voluntarily** through the official portal,
[gajiprogresif.gov.my](https://gajiprogresif.gov.my). During the pilot, voluntary
registration opened on **15 May 2024** and incentive claims began in **June
2024**, with around a thousand employers taking part. An employer must satisfy
the scheme's eligibility and training conditions before it can claim.

## Where it stands

The policy moved from White Paper (2023) to a **pilot in 2024** and then to
wider rollout. **Budget 2025** allocated **RM200 million** to implement it in
2025, targeting about **50,000 workers**, with the government saying the 2025
mechanism would mirror the pilot.

Because the incentive rates are reviewed annually and the scheme is still
scaling, the exact participation numbers and wage outcomes change from year to
year — the latest official figures should be checked against the Ministry of
Economy's reporting (see *verification needed*).
