faq:
  - q: "How many public holidays must a Malaysian employer pay for?"
    a: "Eleven gazetted public holidays under s.60D(1)(a) of the Employment Act 1955, five of which are named by the Act itself, plus any day appointed as a public holiday for that year under s.8 of the Holidays Act 1951. The employer chooses the remaining six from the gazetted list and must exhibit a notice conspicuously at the place of employment before the calendar year begins, under s.60D(1A). Any other day may be substituted for one or more of those six by agreement between employer and employee."
  - q: "What happens when a public holiday falls on a Sunday or on another holiday?"
    a: "The proviso to s.60D(1) provides that where a public holiday falls on a rest day, or on another public holiday, the working day immediately following the rest day or the other public holiday becomes a paid holiday in substitution. Section 60D(1B) deals with a different case — a holiday falling during sick leave, annual leave or a period of temporary disablement — and requires the employer to grant another day as a paid holiday in substitution."
  - q: "Are state public holidays automatically paid holidays?"
    a: "Not automatically. Section 9(1) of the Holidays Act 1951 lets the State Authority appoint a day to be observed as a State public holiday in that State, and it is then observed there. Whether the employee is paid for it under the Employment Act depends on whether it is one of the eleven the employer has designated under s.60D — with the exception of the Ruler's or Yang di-Pertua Negeri's birthday for the State where the employee wholly or mainly works, which s.60D(1)(a)(iii) makes compulsory."
  - q: "Which state does the Ruler's birthday follow for a remote employee?"
    a: "Section 60D(1)(a)(iii) keys it to the State in which the employee wholly or mainly works under his contract of service, and gives Federal Territory Day where the employee wholly or mainly works in the Federal Territory. For fully remote staff the contractual place of work is the reference point, which is one reason to state it explicitly in the contract."

Two systems run in parallel and they answer different questions. The **Holidays Act
1951** decides which days are public holidays and where. The **Employment Act 1955**
decides how many of them an employee is paid for. Most holiday calendars published in
Malaysia only cover the first, which is why employers routinely pay for the wrong days.

## The Employment Act rule

Section 60D(1) entitles every employee to a paid holiday at the ordinary rate of pay on:

- **eleven of the gazetted public holidays**, five of which are fixed by the Act
- **any day appointed** as a public holiday for that year under s.8 of the Holidays Act
  1951

The five compulsory ones under s.60D(1)(a):

| # | Holiday |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | National Day |
| 2 | Birthday of the Yang di-Pertuan Agong |
| 3 | Birthday of the Ruler or Yang di-Pertua Negeri of the State in which the employee wholly or mainly works — or **Federal Territory Day** for employees working in the Federal Territory |
| 4 | Workers' Day |
| 5 | Malaysia Day |

The employer chooses the **remaining six**, and s.60D(1A) requires a notice specifying
them to be exhibited conspicuously at the place of employment **before the calendar year
begins**. Other days may be substituted for any of the six by agreement.

**Substitution.** Where a holiday under (a) or (b) falls on a rest day or on another
public holiday, the working day immediately following becomes the paid holiday instead.
Where it falls during sick leave, annual leave or a period of temporary disablement,
s.60D(1B) requires another day to be granted in substitution.

**Working on a holiday.** Section 60D(3)(a) requires **two days' wages at the ordinary
rate**, in addition to the holiday pay, *regardless that the period of work done on that
day is less than the normal hours of work*. Excess hours attract not less than three
times the hourly rate under s.60D(3)(aa). Those rate provisions are disapplied above
RM4,000 a month by First Schedule paragraph 1A — the **entitlement to the paid holiday
under s.60D(1) is not**.

## The Holidays Act structure

| Layer | Instrument | Who declares |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statutory list | First Schedule, s.3 | Parliament |
| Additional or substituted days | s.8 | the Minister, after consultation with the State Authority where a State is concerned |
| State public holidays | s.9(1) | the State Authority |
| Federal Territory public holidays | s.9(2) | the Minister |

The First Schedule already varies by state on its face: **Chinese New Year is one day in
Kelantan and Terengganu and two days in the other States**, and **Hari Raya Haji is two
days in Kelantan and Terengganu and one day in the other States**. Any calendar that
treats the statutory list as uniform is wrong before the State Authorities have declared
anything.

## Common mistakes

**Paying for every gazetted holiday in the state and calling it compliance.** The Act
requires eleven plus s.8 appointments, and the employer must nominate and display the
six discretionary ones.

**Skipping the s.60D(1A) notice.** It has to go up **before** the calendar year starts.

**Prorating holiday work.** Section 60D(3)(a) is explicit that two days' wages are due
even where the work was less than normal hours.

**Using head-office holidays for staff in another state.** The Ruler's birthday follows
the State where the employee wholly or mainly works.

**Treating the federal list as fixed.** Five of the 2026 federal dates are flagged in the
source as subject to change, and Islamic dates in particular move on sighting.

**Assuming the Kelantan and Terengganu variations are informal.** They are in the First
Schedule to the Holidays Act itself.

## What's next

Publish your s.60D(1A) notice for next year before 31 December, naming the six
discretionary holidays, and confirm each employee's contractual place of work so the
Ruler's-birthday limb resolves cleanly. For the rest of the leave picture see
[leave entitlements](/en/employment/leave-entitlements-malaysia).
