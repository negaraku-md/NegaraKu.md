Open a headhunting firm in Malaysia and start matching candidates with employers without a licence, and you are not merely breaching procedure — you are committing an offence that can lead to a six-figure fine. Every business that places job seekers with employers is subject to the **Private Employment Agencies Act 1981 (Act 246)**, enforced by the Labour Department.

## Who needs to be licensed?

The definition of a "private employment agency" is broad: it covers any party that, for a fee or reward, matches job seekers with vacancies. This means not only general placement agencies, but also **headhunting and executive search firms** that recruit professionals and management staff. Placing a Malaysian candidate with a local employer is already enough to make a licence a requirement.

## What are the three licence categories?

The amendments to Act 246 organise licences into three classes according to placement scope and the capital required:

| Licence | Placement scope | Paid-up capital | Cash guarantee |
|-------|-----------------|----------------|--------------|
| **A** | Job seekers within Malaysia only | RM50,000 | RM5,000 |
| **B** | Within and outside Malaysia; foreign domestic workers within Malaysia | RM100,000 | RM100,000 |
| **C** | Within and outside Malaysia; non-citizen workers within Malaysia | RM250,000 | RM250,000 |

A firm that only places Malaysian citizens with local employers — the common case for most domestic recruitment and headhunting agencies — needs only **Licence A**, the category with the lowest capital requirement.

## How much can be charged to a job seeker?

An agency cannot charge whatever amount it likes. For Malaysian job seekers, the placement fee is capped at **up to 25% of the candidate's first month's basic salary**. For **non-citizen** workers placed within Malaysia, the limit differs: the fee may not exceed **one month's basic salary** of the first month. These limits protect job seekers from being charged excessive amounts as a condition of obtaining employment.

## What other requirements must be met?

Besides capital and cash guarantee, applicants and licence holders are subject to several conditions:

- **Local ownership** — at least 51% of the company's shares must be held by Malaysian citizens.
- **Company name** — the name must be prefixed with the words "**Agensi Pekerjaan**".
- **Licence validity period** — an approved licence is valid for 24 months.
- **Application fee** — a processing fee of RM300 applies to applications.

## What is the penalty for operating without a licence?

Running a private employment agency without a valid licence is an offence. Following the amendments, the penalty was raised to **a fine of up to RM200,000 or imprisonment of up to three years, or both**. This makes licensing not just an administrative formality, but a legal prerequisite before any recruitment activity begins.

## What's next

- Confirm the correct licence category (A, B or C) for your business model before applying.
- Check the full paid-up capital and cash guarantee requirements at the **[JTKSM services page](https://jtksm.mohr.gov.my/en/services/private-employment-agencies)** for the latest official information.
- Job seekers can verify whether an agency is licensed by contacting the nearest Labour Department office before paying any placement fee.
