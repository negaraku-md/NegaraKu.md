Malaysia's Christian story has two centres of gravity, and neither is in the
capital. One runs back nearly five centuries to the Portuguese fort at Melaka;
the other sits across the South China Sea, in the longhouses and hill
communities of Sabah and Sarawak, where Christianity is not a minority faith at
all but the everyday religion of whole districts.

Christianity is Malaysia's **third-largest religion** — and understanding it
means looking east, to Borneo, more than to the peninsula.

## Numbers and where they are

At the 2020 Population and Housing Census, Christians were **9.1%** of the
population — about **2.94 million people** — behind Islam (63.5%) and Buddhism
(18.7%). But the national figure hides a sharp geography: **more than two-thirds**
of Malaysian Christians (about 70%) live in **Sabah and Sarawak**, and Sarawak is
the state where Christianity is the **largest single religion**, at around half
the population.

By community, Malaysian Christians are **majority Bumiputera** (about 59%) —
reflecting the indigenous churches of Borneo among the Kadazan-Dusun, Iban,
Bidayuh and others — with **Chinese** (about 27%) and **Indian** (about 4%)
minorities concentrated in the peninsula.

## Denominations

Malaysian Christianity spans the historic divides of the faith:

- **Catholic** — roughly half of all Malaysian Christians.
- **Protestant** — about 40%, including Anglican, Methodist, Baptist,
  Presbyterian and Lutheran churches, and the **Sidang Injil Borneo (SIB)**,
  one of the largest churches in East Malaysia.
- **Other traditions** make up the remainder.

## A mission history

**Roman Catholicism** arrived with the **Portuguese** in 16th-century Melaka,
leaving landmarks such as St Paul's on the hill. **Protestant** churches grew
later through two channels: **British-era missions** on the peninsula, and
**Chinese immigrant congregations** — Methodism, for instance, spread among
Fuzhou-dialect speakers.

The deepest roots, though, are in **Borneo**. Under the Brooke rulers of Sarawak
and the chartered administration of North Borneo, missionaries worked among the
indigenous peoples; today the Anglican, Methodist, Basel and SIB churches are
woven into Kadazan-Dusun, Iban and Bidayuh life. This is why the faith's centre
of gravity sits in the east — see
[the indigenous languages of Borneo](/en/arts-culture/borneo-indigenous-languages)
for the communities concerned.

## Institutions

The **Christian Federation of Malaysia (CFM)**, formed in 1985, is the main
umbrella body. It unites the three streams of Malaysian Christianity — the
**Catholic Bishops' Conference**, the **Council of Churches of Malaysia**
(mainline Protestant) and the **National Evangelical Christian Fellowship** —
and says it represents more than 95% of the country's churches, a large share of
them Bahasa Malaysia–speaking congregations in Sabah and Sarawak.

## Festivals and the legal frame

**Christmas** is a **nationwide public holiday**. **Good Friday** is a public
holiday **only in Sabah and Sarawak**. For the cultural observance, see
[Christmas in Malaysia](/en/arts-culture/christmas-malaysia).

Religious freedom is guaranteed by **Article 11** of the Federal Constitution.
One clause shapes Christian practice in particular: **Article 11(4)** allows
state law to control or restrict the **propagation** of religious doctrine among
Muslims. Separately, the use of the word **"Allah"** by non-Muslims has been the
subject of long-running litigation and differs by jurisdiction — an evolving
legal question rather than a settled one. In the Jill Ireland case the High
Court upheld a non-Muslim's right to use the word for personal religious use
(2021), and the federal government withdrew its appeal in 2023; earlier
litigation over the Catholic weekly *Herald* had reached a different outcome, so
the position remains case- and jurisdiction-dependent rather than a blanket
national rule. For how Articles 3 and 11 fit together, see
[Article 3 and religion](/en/law/article-3-religion).

## Common misconceptions

- **"Malaysian Christians are mostly Chinese or Indian."** In fact the majority
  are Bumiputera, concentrated in Sabah and Sarawak.
- **"Christianity is only a peninsular, urban faith."** The opposite — its
  heartland is rural and indigenous East Malaysia.
- **"Religion equals ethnicity."** They are measured separately in the census
  and do not map onto each other neatly.
