In the month of Panguni there is a night when the full moon fills the
sky. In Tamil tradition this luminous day holds a special place — it is
**Panguni Uthiram**. On this day, regarded as the anniversary of many
divine marriages, kavadi and chariot processions fill the streets at
many Murugan temples across Malaysia.

This article describes, in a neutral way, the timing of Panguni Uthiram,
the myths associated with it, and the way it is observed in Malaysia —
without explaining or evaluating the religious truth of any belief.

## The Time and the Name

Panguni Uthiram falls in the **month of Panguni** of the Tamil calendar
(generally March–April in the Gregorian calendar). **Uthiram** is the
name of a star (nakshatra); the day on which that Uthiram star aligns
with the **full moon (pournami)** is what is called Panguni Uthiram. It
is this astronomical configuration that lends the day its significance;
because its Gregorian-calendar date changes each year, it is customary to
consult a Tamil almanac (panchangam) or the local temple announcement for
the exact day.

## A Day of Divine Marriages

Because Panguni Uthiram is associated with various **divine marriages and
unions**, it is also called the day of marriages. Those commonly
mentioned in the traditions:

- **Murugan – Deivanai** (Devasena): After Murugan vanquished Surapadman,
  Indra gave his daughter Deivanai in marriage to Murugan — many temple
  traditions celebrate this day as that occasion.
- **Shiva – Parvati**
- **Rama – Sita**
- **Ranganatha (Vishnu) – Andal**

Besides these, some traditions also regard this day as the day
**Ayyappan** incarnated. Which myth is given precedence varies from
temple to temple and region to region.

## Customs of Worship

Many customs are seen at Murugan temples during Panguni Uthiram:

- **Kavadi** — devotees carrying kavadi as a vow is the central element.
  Bearing milk pots (paalkudam) and karakam are also present.
- **Fasting (vratham)** — some fast for days beforehand, keeping to
  simple food and purity.
- **Abhishekam** — special abhishekam rituals are performed for the main
  deity (moolavar) and the festival deity (utsavar).
- **Chariot procession (ther)** — carrying the festival deity on a
  chariot, or a silver or gold chariot, in procession is the climax of
  the day at many temples.

Because carrying kavadi takes precedence in this festival, in Malaysia
Panguni Uthiram is sometimes called the **Second Thaipusam**.

## Panguni Uthiram in Malaysia

Although Panguni Uthiram is relatively less known in Malaysia than
Thaipusam, at some temples it is celebrated on a very large scale:

- **Sri Marathandavar Bala Dhandayuthapani temple, Maran (Pahang)** — a
  temple nationally known for its Panguni Uthiram celebration. In 2024 it
  was held as the 93rd annual festival; it is celebrated with many days
  of special worship, milk pots, kavadi, and the arrival of great crowds
  of devotees.
- **Kapar Murugan temple (Selangor)** — a festival with kavadi and a
  chariot procession, resembling Thaipusam in appearance, takes place here.

Besides these, many Murugan and **Mariamman temples** across the country
observe this day on their own scale. Although **Batu Caves** is known as
Malaysia's largest Thaipusam centre, in some years groups of devotees
undertake pilgrimages on foot from there to temples such as Maran for
Panguni Uthiram.

## Panguni Uthiram and Thaipusam: Connection and Difference

Because both are festivals centred on Murugan that share the kavadi
tradition, they are often compared. But they fall on different days, with
different mythological backgrounds:

| Aspect | Thaipusam | Panguni Uthiram |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tamil month | Thai (January–February) | Panguni (March–April) |
| Primary myth | Murugan received the vel to destroy Surapadman | Divine marriages; Ayyappan's incarnation |
| Primary centre in Malaysia | Batu Caves, Penang Waterfall Hill | Maran (Pahang), Kapar (Selangor) |
| Shared tradition | Kavadi, milk pots, vows | Kavadi, milk pots, vows |

Through the shared tradition of kavadi the two are close; yet the day and
the foundational myth are each their own.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Panguni Uthiram and Thaipusam are the same."** No — they are two
  festivals that fall in different months, with different myths. The name
  Second Thaipusam points to the commonality of the kavadi tradition; it
  does not mean the two are one and the same.
- **"It refers to only one divine marriage."** Many divine marriages and
  unions are associated with this day; which one is primary varies from
  temple to temple.
- **"It is the same at all temples."** The scale and practices of the
  festival vary from temple to temple and from place to place.
