Ask most visitors which is Malaysia's oldest museum, and they'll guess Muzium Negara — the palace-roofed building that anchors Kuala Lumpur's museum row. They'd be off by eighty years. That title belongs to a modest gallery in Taiping, built while Muzium Negara's site was still occupied by its predecessor.

That gap between assumption and record is a useful way into Malaysia's museum landscape, because the "big three" people usually mean — the National Museum, the Islamic Arts Museum, and "the state museums" as an afterthought — turn out to have three completely different origin stories, funders, and governing bodies.

## Muzium Negara: built on a bombed-out predecessor

Muzium Negara did not start from empty ground. It occupies the former site of the Selangor Museum, part of which the Allied Air Force destroyed in a bombing raid on 10 March 1945. Two years after independence, in 1959, the government commissioned architect Ho Kok Hoe to design a replacement. Construction began in 1962, and the new building — its silhouette drawn from Malay palace and vernacular roof forms — was completed and officially opened on 31 August 1963 by the Third Yang di-Pertuan Agong, Tuanku Syed Putra Al-Haj.

Today it runs under the federal Department of Museums Malaysia (Jabatan Muzium Malaysia) and organises its permanent collection into four galleries:

| Gallery | Covers |
|---|---|
| Prehistory | Formation of the Malay Archipelago and Malaysia's major prehistoric archaeological sites |
| Malay Kingdoms | Early Malay states through the Melaka Sultanate's 15th-century height as a spice-trade and Islamic centre |
| Colonial Era | 446 years of Portuguese, Dutch, British and Japanese presence from 1511 onward |
| Malaysia Today | The road to independence and the nation since |

## The Islamic Arts Museum: a private museum, not a government one

Walk five minutes from Muzium Negara and the governance changes entirely. The Islamic Arts Museum Malaysia (IAMM) opened its doors in December 1998, and it exists because of one family's philanthropy rather than a ministry budget: it is a flagship project of the Albukhary Foundation, the private charity founded by businessman Syed Mokhtar Albukhary, and is directed by his brother, Syed Mohamad Albukhary.

IAMM holds more than 10,000 artefacts across 12 permanent galleries, organised by object type — textiles, ceramics, metalwork, arms and armour, manuscripts — rather than by geography. Its stated ambition is breadth beyond the Middle East: alongside Persian and Ottoman material, it gives deliberate weight to Islamic art from India, China and the Malay World, making the case that "Islamic art" was never a single regional tradition.

## The museums that came first

Neither of Kuala Lumpur's flagships is Malaysia's oldest institution. Two colonial-era museums predate the country itself by decades:

- **Perak Museum, Taiping** — established in 1883 under British Resident Sir Hugh Low, making it Malaysia's first and oldest museum. Its original building, delayed by insufficient funds, was only finished in 1886. It now runs four galleries: temporary exhibits, natural history, culture, and earthenware and indigenous crafts.
- **Sarawak Museum, Kuching** — opened to the public in the late nineteenth century under the second White Rajah, Charles Brooke, with input from naturalist Alfred Russel Wallace. It is the oldest museum in Borneo, predating Malaysia's formation by decades.

Sabah's state museum has a newer, two-stage history: it first opened on Gaya Street, Kota Kinabalu, on 15 July 1965, then moved to its present 17-hectare site at Bukit Istana Lama, officially opened by the Yang di-Pertuan Agong on 11 April 1984.

## Common mistakes

**Assuming one national body runs every museum.** Muzium Negara and Perak Museum both fall under the federal Department of Museums Malaysia. Sarawak and Sabah, by contrast, administer their own museums through separate state museum departments — a distinction worth knowing if you're chasing an artefact record or a research permission across state lines.

**Treating IAMM as a government museum.** Its polish and scale invite the assumption, but it was founded and is still directed by the Albukhary family, not a ministry.

**Assuming "national" means "oldest."** Muzium Negara is the country's symbolic flagship, not its longest-running institution — that distinction sits with Taiping, decades before the National Museum was even conceived.
