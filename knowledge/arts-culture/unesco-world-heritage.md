Ask how many World Heritage Sites Malaysia has and most sources will tell you four. That was true until 2024. The Niah Caves Complex was inscribed that July, and Forest Research Institute Malaysia Forest Park Selangor followed in 2025 — bringing the total to six.

More interesting than the count is the fine print. Every inscription carries a set of numbered criteria, and those numbers are the actual argument for why the site is on the List. Reading them changes what you think each place is famous for.

## The ten criteria, briefly

UNESCO maintains one combined list of ten selection criteria. Numbers (i) to (vi) cover cultural value — creative genius, interchange of human values, testimony to a tradition, an outstanding building or landscape type, traditional land use, association with living traditions. Numbers (vii) to (x) cover natural value — exceptional beauty, earth history, ongoing ecological processes, and habitat for biodiversity.

A property needs at least one. Which ones it gets is not decoration; it is the legal description of its Outstanding Universal Value, and it determines what the state party is obliged to protect.

## The two Borneo parks were listed for opposite reasons

Gunung Mulu, in [Sarawak](/en/states/sarawak), holds all four natural criteria — a rare sweep. Under (vii) UNESCO cites Sarawak Chamber, 600 m long by 415 m wide and 80 m high, the largest known cave chamber on earth. Under (viii) it cites the Melinau Limestone and the uplifted cave levels that record Borneo's tectonic history. Under (ix) and (x): seventeen vegetation zones, some 3,500 vascular plant species, and 109 palm species in twenty genera.

Kinabalu Park got only (ix) and (x). Mount Kinabalu at 4,095 m is the highest mountain between the Himalayas and New Guinea, and it looks the part — but UNESCO did not inscribe it for its beauty. The case rests on evolutionary process: an extreme altitudinal gradient, ultramafic soils, geographic isolation over short distances, and an estimated 5,000 to 6,000 vascular plant species including around 1,000 orchids.

## The cultural four are three different arguments

**Melaka and George Town** are one property, not two. Criterion (ii) covers the interchange between Malay, Chinese, Indian and three successive European colonial presences over more than 500 years; (iii) the living multicultural tradition; (iv) the shophouse and townhouse as a building type. Read [Penang](/en/states/penang) and the [Melaka Sultanate](/en/malaysia/melaka-sultanate) for the history behind that.

**Lenggong Valley** rests on depth of time: (iii) for a record of occupation spanning 1.83 million years, running through the Palaeolithic, Neolithic and Metal ages, and (iv) for undisturbed Palaeolithic stone tool workshops on a palaeolake shore. Perak Man, the near-complete prehistoric skeleton from Gua Gunung Runtuh, was recovered from that sequence.

**Niah** takes a different line — (iii) plus (v), the criterion for human interaction with the environment. UNESCO cites the longest known record of humans living with rainforest, at least 50,000 years, including the shift from foraging to rice farming and vegeculture.

**FRIM Forest Park** is the outlier. It is a cultural property under criterion (iv) alone: a lowland tropical forest deliberately grown from the 1920s on exhausted tin-mining land. It is listed not as nature, but as an early feat of ecological restoration.

## Common mistakes

- Treating Melaka and George Town as two sites. One serial inscription, later given a minor boundary modification.
- Assuming FRIM is a natural site. It is cultural — the forest is the artefact.
- Assuming Kinabalu was inscribed for scenery. Criterion (vii) was not applied.
- Confusing the World Heritage List with the Intangible Cultural Heritage lists, which are a separate convention — see [batik](/en/arts-culture/batik).

## What's next

Three properties sit on Malaysia's Tentative List: Taman Negara, Gombak Selangor Quartz Ridge, and Sungai Buloh Leprosarium. A tentative listing is only an intent to nominate — but given two inscriptions in two years, the six is unlikely to stay six for long.
