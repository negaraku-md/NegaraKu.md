A good pesilat can break an opponent's bones — or dance before a bride without touching anyone. Both are silat. That is what is hardest to grasp about this art: it is not merely a method of fighting, and not merely a ceremonial dance. It is both, in the same body, moved by the same steps.

UNESCO describes silat as a combat art for self-defence and survival that is rooted in the Malay world. But that same art, according to UNESCO itself, has "transcended its status as a self-defence art to become a performance art." This is the story of how a knowledge of war became a heritage of the world.

## What exactly is silat?

The most common mistake is to narrow silat down to a single definition. The Department of Culture and Arts (JKKN) describes it as a traditional Malay martial art that combines the elements of self-defence, dance, custom, music and life philosophy. It is not only physical movement; it is a way of carrying identity, discipline and cultural values.

UNESCO breaks these components down in greater detail. Besides physical and spiritual training, silat encompasses traditional Malay dress, silat musical instruments, and a variety of customs and ceremonies. This means that when someone learns silat, they are not merely learning to kick and parry — they enter a complete system of custom, with its own dress, music and code of conduct.

According to UNESCO, many silat movements draw inspiration from nature. The Department of National Heritage adds that its techniques are inspired by human anatomy, the movement of plants and the behaviour of animals. That is why silat styles are often named after elements of nature — and one of the most famous examples is Silat Harimau, which according to UNESCO imitates the aesthetic movements of the tiger in the art of attack and self-defence.

## Where did silat begin?

UNESCO traces the roots of silat to the Kingdom of Langkasuka, one of the earliest Malay kingdoms. The Department of National Heritage sets its timeline as beginning in the Langkasuka era and continuing throughout the age of the Malacca Malay Sultanate — a span covering almost the whole history of early Malay civilisation.

In its early days, UNESCO says, silat was practised by warriors who acted as enforcers of justice. It was the knowledge of those who bore both weapons and responsibility. It was this social standing that set silat apart from mere fighting technique: it was bound up with the concepts of honour, loyalty and leadership in traditional Malay society.

Today the context is different. The Department of National Heritage describes silat as an important social medium in Malaysian society, appearing at Malay wedding ceremonies as well as official state events. From the knowledge of warriors, it has become part of the face of everyday culture.

## Who teaches it, and how?

Silat is not taught in an ordinary classroom. According to UNESCO, training usually takes place in the evening or at night in an open space, led by a silat master and their assistant, known as the *jurukaka*.

The chain of transmission is clear: there are grandmasters and masters at the top, teaching instructors in the middle, and pupils learning from below. This arrangement matters because it explains why silat exists in so many styles — each school has its own lineage of masters, with its own movements, terminology and etiquette.

## How many silat styles exist?

Here two official sources give different figures — and both are correct, because they are counting different things.

| Source | Figure | What is counted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UNESCO | More than 150 silat styles | Known styles (schools of movement), many named after elements of nature |
| Department of National Heritage | More than 500 active silat schools | Silat schools (organisations/institutions) operating in Malaysia |

A "style" is a school of knowledge; a "school" (perguruan) is the organisation that teaches it. One style can be taught by many schools, and one school can teach more than one style. That is why the figure for schools is higher than the figure for styles.

Among the names listed in the records of the Department of National Heritage and JKKN:

- **Silat Cekak** — known for its practical and direct self-defence movements
- **Silat Gayung** (Silat Seni Gayong) — emphasises physical strength and the combat art
- **Silat Lincah** — fast and agile, suited to modern combat
- **Silat Buah Pukul** — belongs to the combat-art category
- **Silat Harimau** — imitates the movements of the tiger
- **Silat Pulut / Silat Tari** — the performance form at ceremonies
- As well as Sendi Naga, Cimande, Kunta and others

## Silat to fight, or silat to be performed?

This is the heart of the whole story. Silat is not one thing with one function — it is a family of forms that share a foundation of movement but differ in purpose.

The Department of National Heritage divides the art of silat into several main forms:

| Form | Purpose | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silat pulut / silat tari | Performance | Staged at ceremonies and traditional gatherings, accompanied by music |
| Silat buah pukul / combat art | Self-defence | The fighting form to protect oneself from threats |
| Silat olahraga | Sport | A regulated competitive event with points and winners |

The difference between silat pulut and combat silat is not merely one of style — it is a difference of intent. According to JKKN, silat tari or silat pulut is performed at wedding ceremonies and traditional rites, accompanied by the beat of drums and traditional musical instruments. Two pesilat "play" the flourishes of silat (bunga silat), displaying the beauty of movement without any intent to injure. The audience comes to enjoy, not to watch a real fight.

Silat buah pukul, or the combat art, meanwhile, as recorded by the Department of National Heritage, functions to protect oneself from the threat of an enemy. Here the very same movements — steps, evasions, parries — are used for real effect, not for spectacle.

This is the "two-faced life" that UNESCO refers to. The bunga silat performed before a bride, and the buah silat that can break a joint, grow from the same tree.

## How did silat become a world heritage?

Many assume silat "became a heritage" in 2019. In fact it happened in two stages: national recognition first, and only then international recognition.

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 2007 | The Department of National Heritage declared **Seni Persilatan Melayu** a **National Heritage** |
| 12 December 2019 | UNESCO inscribed **Silat** on the **Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity**, on Malaysia's nomination |

The National Heritage declaration in 2007 was recognition within the country — an official acknowledgement that the Malay art of silat is a cultural treasure that must be preserved. It preceded, and formed the basis for, the nomination to the UNESCO level.

The UNESCO inscription, in turn, took place at the 14th session of the Intergovernmental Committee of the 2003 Convention. Its element file carries the number 01504 in the UNESCO system, under Malaysia. What matters to understand: UNESCO did not "grant an award" to silat. It inscribed silat on the representative list — a register of living heritage that carries a responsibility of preservation, not merely a certificate.

## Who safeguards silat at the national level?

Behind the hundreds of independent schools, there is a single governing body. **PESAKA** — Persekutuan Silat Kebangsaan Malaysia, the National Silat Federation of Malaysia — is the body that coordinates, develops and promotes silat at the national level.

According to the website of the international body PERSILAT (the International Pencak Silat Federation), PESAKA was formed from four main silat associations:

- Seni Gayung Fatani Malaysia
- Silat Seni Gayung Malaysia
- Seni Silat Lincah Malaysia
- Seni Silat Cekak Malaysia

The PERSILAT website states that discussions towards its establishment began on 6 February 1978, and PESAKA was founded in 1983. Malaysia was also among the founding countries of the International Pencak Silat Federation (PERSILAT), established in 1979.

PESAKA's role leans more towards silat as a sport — training athletes, referees and technical officials for silat olahraga competitions at the national and international levels. This differs from the role of the Department of National Heritage and JKKN, which safeguard silat as a cultural heritage. Both roles are important, but they safeguard different sides of silat.

## Which "silat" do you mean?

Because the word "silat" encompasses so many things, misunderstanding comes easily. This simple framework helps:

- **If you mean the cultural heritage** — the form recognised by UNESCO and the Department of National Heritage, encompassing the combat art, the performance art, custom, music and dress.
- **If you mean the ceremonial performance** — that is silat pulut or silat tari, staged with musical accompaniment.
- **If you mean self-defence** — that is silat buah pukul or the combat art, with the real purpose of protecting oneself.
- **If you mean the competitive sport** — that is silat olahraga, administered by PESAKA and PERSILAT with rules, points and winners.

All four can rightly be called "silat". What distinguishes them is purpose and context, not the name.

## Common mistakes

**"Silat is just a dance."** Silat pulut is indeed a performance form, but it is only one of several forms. Silat buah pukul is a genuine combat art for self-defence, and silat olahraga is a competitive sport.

**"Silat was recognised by UNESCO in 2019, so that was its beginning."** National recognition as a National Heritage came earlier, in 2007. The UNESCO inscription on 12 December 2019 was the international stage built upon that foundation.

**"Silat and pencak silat are two different things."** In the sporting context, its governing body PESAKA is a member of PERSILAT (the International Pencak Silat Federation) — the term "pencak silat" is used widely for the same art across the Malay world, even though each country and school has its own name and style.

**"All silat styles are the same."** UNESCO mentions more than 150 styles, and the Department of National Heritage records more than 500 active schools. Each school has its own lineage of masters, movements and etiquette.

**"The UNESCO certificate guarantees silat will endure."** On the contrary. The representative list is a responsibility of preservation, not a guarantee. What keeps silat alive are the masters who still teach and the pupils willing to learn for years in an open space at night.

## Why silat still matters

Silat answers a question rarely asked about heritage: can something be useful and beautiful at once? Most heritage arts are left with only their beauty — performed, admired, but no longer functional. Silat is different. Its buah pukul can still defend a person; its bunga tari still adorns ceremonies; its olahraga still wins medals.

That is why UNESCO describes it as an art that has "transcended" its original status, not abandoned it. Silat did not stop being a combat art when it became a performance art. It added a face, rather than changing its face.
