An MM2H retiree who has driven for thirty years overseas, a new Employment Pass
hire who has never touched a manual gearbox, and a tourist on a two-week trip
all ask JPJ the same question and get three different answers. Since 19 May
2025 that gap got wider: JPJ stopped converting most foreigners' licences
altogether, and a lot of the advice still circulating online was written
before that change.

## What actually lets you drive here

Malaysia does not require every visitor to sit a local test. Section 28 of
the **Road Transport Act 1987** (Act 333) recognises a driving licence issued
by a country that is party to a treaty with Malaysia on mutual recognition of
domestic licences — in practice, this covers the Geneva Convention on Road
Traffic 1949 and the Vienna Convention on Road Traffic 1968. The licence is
deemed a Malaysian driving licence for as long as it stays valid in its home
country.

That is the legal foundation under all three practical routes:

| Route | Who it's for | What it requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Foreign licence alone** | Short-term visitors from a treaty country | A valid, unexpired home licence; a translation confirmed by the embassy or the issuing transport authority if it isn't in English or Malay |
| **International Driving Permit (IDP)** | Visitors whose home country issues one | A valid IDP obtained before entering Malaysia, alongside the underlying home licence |
| **ASEAN licence** | Nationals of ASEAN member states | The home country's own licence, recognised directly under the ASEAN mutual recognition arrangement — no IDP needed |

None of these give you an unlimited stay. They ride on top of your immigration
pass — once your visa or work pass lapses, so does your permission to drive
on that basis.

## The IDP is a translation, not a licence

An International Driving Permit is easy to misread as a stand-alone right to
drive. It isn't. JPJ's own description of the IDP is built around Malaysians
who already hold a **Competent Driving Licence (CDL)** valid for more than a
year, applying so they can drive **abroad** — the IDP is a standardised,
multilingual companion document to a real licence, not a replacement for one.
It is issued for one year at a time, renewable at any JPJ office or through
the Automobile Association of Malaysia (AAM).

The same logic runs in reverse for a foreign visitor arriving in Malaysia: the
IDP they hold from home makes their licence readable to a Malaysian officer
and internationally recognised on paper, but it is only as valid as the
underlying licence sitting behind it. Let the home licence lapse, and the IDP
lapses with it.

## Conversion: closed to most foreigners since May 2025

This is the part that has changed and where outdated blog posts cause the
most confusion.

Before 19 May 2025, a foreign national who had held a valid overseas licence
for long enough could apply to convert it directly into a Malaysian Driving
Licence (LMM) — no driving school, no local test. JPJ's currently published
conversion checklist, dated 19 May 2025, narrows that pathway to three
categories only:

1. **Malaysian citizens** holding a foreign licence (including a separate,
   more detailed checklist for Singapore-issued licences)
2. **Diplomatic corps** — non-diplomatic staff, consular officers and staff of
   international organisations, holding a valid Diplomatic Identification
   Card and a pass or visa valid for at least 90 more days
3. **Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H) participants**, holding a valid MM2H
   approval letter from the Immigration Department and a pass or visa valid
   for at least 90 more days

Both the diplomatic and MM2H pathways require the original foreign licence
(still valid on the day of application), plus an **original confirmation
letter** — either from the issuing country's embassy in Malaysia, or an
official driving licence record or extract from the foreign transport
authority. A photocopy of the licence on its own does not satisfy the
checklist.

**If you hold an Employment Pass, a Dependant Pass, or any other long-term
pass that isn't MM2H or diplomatic status, conversion is not currently open
to you.** This is the change that matters most for the ordinary expatriate
reader: JPJ's stated reason is road safety, and the policy applies regardless
of how long you've held a clean licence back home.

Separately, from 1 June 2026 JPJ widened processing of a different queue —
**Malaysian citizens** applying to convert a foreign licence back to a
Malaysian one — to all JPJ offices nationwide. That expansion is for
returning Malaysians, not foreign nationals, and it does not reopen the
conversion route for expatriates.

## If conversion isn't open to you

A foreign national who needs to drive long-term in Malaysia and doesn't
qualify for diplomatic or MM2H conversion must go through the same licensing
pathway as any first-time Malaysian applicant:

- Register with a JPJ-licensed driving school for the relevant vehicle class
- Complete the required theory classes and pass JPJ's computerised law test
- Hold a **Learner's Driving Licence (LDL)** through supervised practical
  lessons
- Pass the **practical driving test**, which issues a **Probationary Driving
  Licence (PDL)**
- Complete the mandatory **probationary period** before upgrading
  to a full **Competent Driving Licence (CDL)**

There is no shortcut for the number of years you've safely driven elsewhere —
the years of overseas experience that used to support a conversion no longer
have anywhere to be recognised in this pathway.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming an IDP alone lets you drive indefinitely.** It rides on your
  home licence and your immigration pass — both must stay valid.
- **Assuming any expatriate visa qualifies for conversion.** Since 19 May
  2025, only Malaysian citizens, diplomatic corps and MM2H participants are
  on JPJ's checklist.
- **Turning up with a photocopy of the foreign licence.** Diplomatic and
  MM2H conversion both require the original licence plus an original embassy
  or transport-authority confirmation letter.
- **Confusing the June 2026 announcement with an expat reopening.** That
  expansion applies to Malaysian citizens converting a foreign licence back,
  not to foreign nationals.
- **Assuming ASEAN nationals need an IDP.** Their home licence is recognised
  directly under the ASEAN mutual recognition arrangement.

## What's next

Work out which of the three groups you fall into — Malaysian citizen,
diplomatic corps, or MM2H participant — before you assemble any paperwork,
because the checklist and required letters differ by category and JPJ's
published list is the only current source of truth. If you fall outside all
three, budget for the full driving-school pathway rather than searching for a
conversion shortcut that no longer exists for your pass type. For the wider
set of long-term passes this interacts with — Employment Pass, MM2H tiers,
PVIP and Dependant Pass — see
