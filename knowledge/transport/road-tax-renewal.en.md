Many people call it "road tax" and treat it the way they treat income tax or sales tax — pay it, done, don't think about it again until next year. But its official name at JPJ is the Motor Vehicle Licence (LKM): a kind of **licence**, not a tax. This distinction is not just a matter of terminology. It explains why there is a strict time window for renewing it, and why a vehicle that has gone too long without an LKM cannot simply "pay and be done."

## What "road tax" actually is

The LKM is a licence under the Road Transport Act 1987 that allows a vehicle to be used lawfully on public roads in Malaysia — the same law that requires vehicle registration and a driving licence. Read more about the structure of this Act at [/en/law/road-transport-act](/en/law/road-transport-act).

In recent years, JPJ has also introduced e-LKM — a digital LKM record that is stored and can be displayed directly through the MyJPJ app, alongside the printed format which can still be chosen at renewal.

## When it can be renewed — and for how long

Per JPJ's official guidelines, the LKM **can only be renewed within the two-month period before its expiry date** — not earlier. For private vehicles, the permitted renewal period is either 6 months or 1 year.

Commercial vehicles have different period rules, since they are often subject to additional permits and inspection schedules.

## If it lapses for too long

There is no official limit on how long a vehicle can "rest" without an LKM before it is considered a technical issue — but JPJ sets one clear condition: **if the LKM has not been renewed for more than three years**, the vehicle must be brought in for inspection at Puspakom to obtain a B2 report (or a PG10 inspection specifically for individually-owned motorcycles). Only after that report has been obtained can the owner or their representative go to a JPJ office with the original registration documents and sufficient payment to proceed with renewal.

This means a vehicle left idle for years cannot simply be renewed online — it must go through a physical inspection first.

## Official channels for renewal

| Channel | How |
|---|---|
| MyJPJ app/portal | Log in, select the vehicle number, choose "Renewal", make payment online |
| JPJ counter/kiosk | State branch counters, eKhidmat kiosks, or JPJ Mobile |
| Pos Malaysia | Counters at post office branches (except Pos Laju), bring NRIC and current driving licence |
| MyEG | MyEG's online service for LKM and driving licence |

Valid vehicle insurance is a mandatory requirement before the LKM can be renewed — the online process will check insurance status automatically.

## Common mistakes

- **Trying to renew more than two months early.** JPJ's system will reject the application until it falls within that two-month window.
- **Assuming you can just pay online after it has lapsed for years.** Once it passes three years, the Puspakom/PG10 step is mandatory first — this is not optional.
- **Letting insurance lapse without noticing.** Without valid insurance, the LKM renewal application will be blocked even if payment is ready.
- **Overlooking vehicle-related summonses.** Many owners find that the system will not proceed with renewal until other issues related to the vehicle are resolved; read more about the types and channels for summonses at [/en/transport/traffic-summons-saman](/en/transport/traffic-summons-saman).

The actual rate payable is set according to engine capacity, vehicle type, and region (Peninsular Malaysia versus Sabah/Sarawak) based on JPJ's official rate calculation guidelines — the full schedule changes from time to time, so refer directly to the official JPJ calculator or the MyJPJ app for the current figures for your vehicle.

## What's next

Keep your LKM expiry date in your calendar, not just relying on memory — the two-month window is short, and a delay of years brings extra complications that can be avoided. If your vehicle has not been used for a long time or its LKM lapsed long ago, check your insurance and summons status first before going to Puspakom, so that one visit is enough to settle everything.
