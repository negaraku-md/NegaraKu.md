For the better part of a century, getting around Penang has meant cars, buses and the ferry across the strait. The Mutiara Line is the state's bet that an elevated urban railway can finally change that - and it is the first of its kind Penang has ever had.

It is also a project with a long and stop-start history: conceived under a state master plan, delayed by reclamation disputes, a pandemic and a change of government, then taken over by the federal government, renamed, and finally put into the ground in 2025.

## What exactly is the Mutiara Line?

The Mutiara Line is **Penang's first light rail transit (LRT) line**. It is designed as a **fully elevated railway of roughly 29.5 km**, with around **21 stations**, and is the anchor project of the **Penang Transport Master Plan (PTMP)** - the state's long-term blueprint for moving away from a car-dependent island.

The name honours Penang's nickname, the "Pearl of the Orient" (*mutiara* means pearl in Malay). It was previously known as the **Bayan Lepas LRT**, after the industrial and airport district in the island's south that an earlier alignment was built around.

As an urban metro-style line, it is a different animal from an intercity railway: short station spacing, frequent elevated services, and a role focused on daily commuting within greater Penang rather than long-distance travel.

## The route

The alignment stitches together the island's reclaimed south, the historic core of George Town, and the mainland city of Butterworth across the water.

| Segment | Stretch | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Island section | Silicon Island (PSR-A) to Komtar | The first civil works segment, reported at about 23.7 km with 19 stations |
| Cross-strait link | Macallum to Penang Sentral | A marine bridge of roughly 5.5 km across the Penang Strait to the mainland |

The line begins at **Silicon Island**, an artificial islet on the **Penang South Reclamation** (station code **PSR-A**), runs north across the island through **Komtar** - the central business landmark of **George Town** - and then crosses the **Penang Strait** on an elevated **marine bridge** from **Macallum** to **Penang Sentral** in **Butterworth** on the mainland. **Komtar, Macallum and Penang Sentral** are planned as interchange points, with Penang Sentral already a major bus-and-rail hub on the mainland.

The exact station count is still settling: MRT Corp's own project material lists **20 stations plus two provisional ones** (at PSR-A and Jalan Bagan Luar), while most news reporting rounds to **21**. That discrepancy is flagged for verification below.

## Who is building it, and who owns it

The delivery structure changed significantly over the project's life, and this is where the detail matters.

The owner and developer is the **Malaysia Rapid Transit Corporation (MRT Corp)** - the same federal, Ministry of Finance-linked company that delivered the **Klang Valley MRT and LRT lines**. MRT Corp took over the Penang project after the **federal government assumed control in 2024** and renamed it the Mutiara Line.

The **project delivery partner (PDP)** is **SRS Consortium**, a joint venture **led by Gamuda Berhad (60 percent)** with **Loh Phoy Yen Holdings and Ideal Property Development (20 percent each)**. SRS was originally appointed by the Penang state government back in **2015** as the PDP for the whole Transport Master Plan, and it holds the **main civil works contract for the first segment**.

So the line is best described as a **state-conceived, federally-delivered** project: dreamed up under the PTMP, but now built and owned through the federal MRT Corp machinery, with Gamuda's consortium doing the heavy civil engineering.

## The cost: a figure still being managed

Because the project has been re-scoped more than once, cost figures vary by source and date.

- An early **state-level estimate** put the line at around **RM10 billion**, excluding land acquisition.
- The **Cabinet-approved budget** after the federal takeover was about **RM13 billion**.
- In mid-2025, MRT Corp said it was running tenders to **keep the total cost below RM16 billion**, warning that without cost control it could climb toward **RM19 billion**.
- The **first civil works segment alone** (PSR-A to Komtar) was costed at roughly **RM8.3 billion**.

In other words, the same line can be quoted at RM10 billion, RM13 billion or near RM16 billion depending on which scope and which stage is meant - a distinction worth keeping in mind when comparing headlines.

## Signed, delayed, revived

The timeline is as much political and procedural as it is technical:

- **2015** - the Penang Transport Master Plan is adopted and SRS Consortium is appointed project delivery partner; the line is first introduced as the Bayan Lepas LRT.
- **2016-2019** - the route is submitted to the federal authorities and an environmental impact assessment is filed; conditional federal approval follows in 2019.
- **2020-2023** - progress stalls amid the COVID-19 pandemic, political instability, and disputes over the scale of the reclamation that would host the island terminus.
- **2024** - the **federal government takes over** the project, folds it under MRT Corp, scales the reclamation down to a single islet (**Silicon Island**), and renames it the **Mutiara Line**.
- **11 January 2025** - **groundbreaking**; the project is formally launched.
- **15 July 2025** - MRT Corp issues the **notice to proceed** to SRS Consortium, marking the official start of physical construction and starting a roughly **six-year clock**.
- **Q3 2027** - construction of the **Macallum to Penang Sentral** marine link is scheduled to begin, once its main contractor is appointed.
- **December 2031** - the **targeted opening** for passenger operations.

Physical progress was reported at around **16 percent in October 2026**. Because big rail timelines have slipped elsewhere in Malaysia, the opening date is a target to confirm, not a settled fact.

## How it compares to the Klang Valley

The most useful reference point is Greater Kuala Lumpur. There, MRT Corp and Prasarana have built up an **interconnected web** of MRT, LRT, monorail and commuter rail over several decades. Penang, by contrast, is starting from **zero urban rail** - the Mutiara Line is its first line, not an addition to an existing network.

The technology is familiar, though: an **elevated, electrified metro** on the same model as the Klang Valley lines, run by the same federal owner. The ambition is for the Mutiara Line to be the **first of several** PTMP lines, with the wider plan envisaging full build-out over the following decades.

## Common misreadings

- **Treating it as finished or imminent.** As of 2026 it was in early construction (around 16 percent); 2031 is a target.
- **Assuming it is part of the KL network.** It is a **standalone Penang system** under the same federal owner, not an extension of the Klang Valley lines.
- **Quoting one cost as "the" cost.** RM10 billion, RM13 billion and the sub-RM16 billion target describe different scopes and stages of the same project.
- **Confusing the island and cross-strait segments.** The RM8.3 billion first segment is the **island** civil works (PSR-A to Komtar); the **marine bridge** to Penang Sentral is a separate, later-tendered package.
- **Using the old "Bayan Lepas LRT" name as a different project.** It is the **same line**, renamed after the 2024 federal takeover.

## What's next

The signals to watch are concrete: MRT Corp confirming the final station count and total cost as tenders close, the award of the **Macallum to Penang Sentral** marine-link contract, and the first testing of completed viaduct and systems toward the **December 2031** target. Until trains are running, treat the opening date and cost ceiling as working targets to verify rather than settled outcomes.
