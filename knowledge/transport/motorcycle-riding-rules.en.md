You ride onto the Federal Highway on a motorcycle, see a "Motorcycle Lane" sign branching off to the left — and wonder whether you are required to follow it or may stay on the main carriageway. The answer hinges on one word: *gazetted*.

## When must a motorcycle use the dedicated lane?

On highways that have a **gazetted** dedicated motorcycle lane running alongside, riders are required to use that lane. Failing to do so is an offence under **Section 79(2) of the Road Transport Act 1987**, and the police (PDRM) actively enforce it — in one operation on the Federal Highway between kilometres 16 and 34, hundreds of summonses were issued to riders who refused to move into the motorcycle lane.

Among the highways with a gazetted dedicated motorcycle lane are the Federal Highway, the Guthrie Corridor Expressway (GCE) and the Kajang–Seremban Highway (LEKAS).

## Are motorcycles banned from the fast lane?

For an ordinary highway **without** a dedicated motorcycle lane, a review of the Road Transport Act 1987 found no specific regulation prohibiting motorcycles from using the rightmost lane (the fast lane). Large-capacity motorcycles may use the ordinary lanes like any other vehicle.

However, as a safety practice, slow motorcycles are advised to stay in the leftmost lane or to use the road shoulder depending on conditions. This is safety advice, not a statutory prohibition.

## Is there an engine-capacity limit?

As of publication, there is **no** engine-capacity restriction in force to determine which highways a motorcycle may enter. Proposals to limit highway access or speed limits by engine capacity have been raised (including a minister's request in 2016 to ban motorcycles from the fast lane), but no regulation has been gazetted to date.

## Helmets: what is mandatory?

A helmet is mandatory for all riders **and** pillion passengers, a requirement that has been in force since 1973.

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Law | Motorcycles (Safety Helmets) Rules 1973 (*Kaedah-Kaedah Motosikal (Topi Keledar) 1973*) |
| Accepted standard | MS 1 (Malaysian specification) or UN Regulation No. 22 |
| Compound | Up to RM300 for failing to wear an approved helmet |

Helmets certified only to DOT (the United States standard) do not meet Malaysian legal requirements. JPJ and the police are entitled to seize non-compliant helmets during roadblocks.

## Federal Highway background

The Federal Highway is cited as Malaysia's first highway to have a dedicated motorcycle lane. Its original route ran roughly 16 km from Kuala Lumpur to the Subang International Airport interchange, then was extended to Shah Alam and Klang by PLUS in 1992.

Today the lane carries roughly **100,000 riders a day**, far exceeding its original design capacity of around 20,000. The lane width ranges from 2.5 to 3.5 metres. The Ministry of Works (KKR), through JKR, continues to allocate funds to maintain and upgrade this lane.

## What's next

- Confirm whether the highway you use has a **gazetted** motorcycle lane — if it does, using it is mandatory.
- Check for the MS 1 (SIRIM) or UN R22 (ECE R22) label on your helmet before riding.
- Follow official announcements from MOT and JKJR on any proposed engine-capacity restriction or speed limit that could turn into law.
