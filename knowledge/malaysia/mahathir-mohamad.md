Mahathir Mohamad (born 10 July 1925) is Malaysia's longest-serving Prime
Minister and one of the defining figures of the modern nation. Trained as a
medical doctor, he entered politics through UMNO and rose to lead the country as
its **fourth Prime Minister** for 22 years, then returned decades later as its
**seventh**.

## First premiership (1981–2003)

Taking office on 16 July 1981, Mahathir presided over two decades of rapid,
state-led industrialisation. His government is associated with a series of
landmark projects — the Petronas Twin Towers, Kuala Lumpur International Airport,
the Multimedia Super Corridor and the [national car maker Proton](/industries/automotive-industry)
— and with the **"Look East"** industrial policy. In 1991 he announced
**[Vision 2020 (Wawasan 2020)](/malaysia/wawasan-2020)**, the goal of a fully
developed Malaysia by 2020.

The same period included events that remain contested in Malaysian politics: the
**[1987 Operation Lalang](/malaysia/ops-lalang-1987)** detentions under the
Internal Security Act, and the **1988 constitutional (judiciary) crisis**, which
ended with the removal of the Lord President of the Supreme Court. His tenure
closed in the aftermath of the **[1997–98 Asian financial crisis](/malaysia/1998-reformasi)**,
during which he imposed capital controls and dismissed his deputy and heir
apparent, **Anwar Ibrahim** — the event that launched the Reformasi movement. He
stepped down on 31 October 2003.

## Return and second premiership (2018–2020)

In 2018, at the age of 92, Mahathir came out of retirement to lead the opposition
Pakatan Harapan coalition, and the **[14th general election](/malaysia/2018-change-of-government)**
produced the first change of federal government since independence. He served as
the seventh Prime Minister from 10 May 2018 until his resignation amid the
February 2020 political crisis that brought down the government.

## Legacy

Mahathir is widely described as the "father of Malaysia's modernisation," and is
also a lasting subject of debate over the concentration of executive power,
judicial independence, and civil liberties during his first tenure. He turned
**100 on 10 July 2025**, the first Malaysian national leader to reach that age.
