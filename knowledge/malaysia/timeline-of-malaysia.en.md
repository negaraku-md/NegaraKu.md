Long before "Malaysia" had a name, this stretch of coast and archipelago was one
of the busiest crossroads on earth — where the monsoon winds handed ships between
India and China, and where sea-kingdoms, sultans, empires and colonisers each
left a layer. This timeline sets that story out in six eras. Each opens with the
shape of the age, then a short list of key moments that link to the full article.

## Sea-kingdoms and the old Malay world

Before any sultanate, the peninsula and its rivers were home to Hindu-Buddhist
trading polities that grew rich on the India–China sea route. Chinese records
remember Langkasuka in the north; the temple-builders of the Bujang Valley left
the earliest evidence of civilisation in old Kedah; and the maritime empire of
Srivijaya projected power across the straits — its royal line would later found
Melaka itself.

**Key moments**

- **early centuries CE** — [Langkasuka](/en/malaysia/langkasuka) — one of the earliest polities on the peninsula, remembered in Chinese and Malay records.
- **4th–14th c.** — [Bujang Valley](/en/malaysia/bujang-valley) — the candi temple complexes of old Kedah, evidence of an early Hindu-Buddhist civilisation.
- **7th–13th c.** — [Srivijaya](/en/malaysia/srivijaya) — the Sumatran sea-empire whose dynasty seeded Melaka's founding line.
- **legend & memory** — [Gangga Negara](/en/malaysia/gangga-negara) — a lost kingdom of the peninsula's Hindu-Buddhist age.

## The port that built a civilisation

Around 1400 a prince founded Melaka, and within a century it became the greatest
entrepôt in Southeast Asia — the sultanate that defined Malay kingship, law and
the coming of Islam. Its wealth also made it a target: in 1511 Portuguese cannon
ended the sultanate in ten days, and the throne carried on in the south as
Johor-Riau.

**Key moments**

- **c. 1400** — [The Melaka Sultanate](/en/malaysia/melaka-sultanate) — the port that built a civilisation and a centre for the spread of Islam.
- **15th century** — [The Malay sultanate system](/en/malaysia/malay-sultanate-system) — sultan, daulat and chiefs: how a Malay kingdom was ruled.
- **1511** — [The fall of Melaka](/en/malaysia/portuguese-melaka-1511) — ten days that ended the archipelago's richest port.
- **from 1528** — [The Johor-Riau Sultanate](/en/malaysia/johor-riau-sultanate) — how the Melaka throne continued south.

## Layered by empires: the colonial centuries

For four centuries outsiders ruled the peninsula through ports, treaties and
Residents rather than outright conquest. The Portuguese held Melaka, then the
Dutch took it and let it fade; the British built Penang and Singapore, bound them
into the Straits Settlements, and — after the 1874 Pangkor Treaty — extended
"advice" over the Malay states until the peninsula was a patchwork of Federated
and Unfederated states.

**Key moments**

- **1641** — [Dutch Melaka](/en/malaysia/dutch-melaka) — why the VOC conquered a port it then refused to develop.
- **1786** — [Penang](/en/states/penang) — Francis Light founds a British trading settlement on the island.
- **1826** — [The Straits Settlements](/en/malaysia/straits-settlements) — Penang, Melaka and Singapore become Britain's template for ruling the Malay states.
- **1874** — [Perak and the Pangkor Treaty](/en/states/perak) — tin, the Pangkor Treaty and the start of the British Resident system.
- **1895–1909** — [Federated and Unfederated Malay States](/en/malaysia/federated-unfederated-states) — the two groupings Britain governed through Residents and Advisers.

## Occupation and the road to Merdeka

Japanese conquest in 1941 shattered the myth of British invincibility. The
post-war plan to impose a Malayan Union provoked a Malay political awakening; a
multiethnic Alliance won the argument for independence; and after an armed
Emergency and hard bargaining, the Federation of Malaya reached Merdeka on
31 August 1957.

**Key moments**

- **1941–45** — [The Japanese Occupation](/en/malaysia/japanese-occupation) — eight weeks to conquer, three years eight months to rule.
- **1946–48** — [The Malayan Union and the Federation of Malaya](/en/malaysia/malayan-union) — two constitutional answers to who is sovereign and who is a citizen.
- **1955** — [The Alliance](/en/malaysia/the-alliance) — the multiethnic coalition that won the road to independence.
- **31 August 1957** — [Merdeka](/en/malaysia/independence-1957) — how independence actually happened.

## Forming Malaysia

In 1963 four territories — Malaya, Sabah, Sarawak and Singapore — were joined
into a single federation under the Malaysia Agreement. Indonesia opposed it in an
undeclared confrontation; Singapore left two years later; and in 1969
intercommunal violence after the general election reshaped the nation's politics.

**Key moments**

- **16 September 1963** — [The formation of Malaysia](/en/malaysia/formation-of-malaysia-1963) — building Malaysia out of four different territories.
- **1963** — [The Malaysia Agreement (MA63)](/en/malaysia/ma63) — the terms on which Sabah and Sarawak joined.
- **1963–66** — [Konfrontasi](/en/malaysia/konfrontasi) — Indonesia's confrontation with the new federation.
- **1965** — [Singapore's separation](/en/malaysia/singapore-separation-1965) — what the separation documents actually say.
- **13 May 1969** — [The 13 May incident](/en/malaysia/13-may-1969) — the emergency proclamation and MAGERAN, per official records.

## Modern Malaysia

The New Economic Policy remade the economy after 1969; the Barisan Nasional
coalition governed for decades of rapid modernisation; a currency crisis and
Reformasi shook the late 1990s; and in 2018 Malaysia saw its first change of
federal government since independence.

**Key moments**

- **1971** — [The New Economic Policy](/en/malaysia/new-economic-policy) — two stated objectives, one twenty-year framework.
- **from 1973** — [Barisan Nasional](/en/malaysia/barisan-nasional) — the coalition that governed Malaysia for over four decades.
- **1997–98** — [Capital controls and Reformasi](/en/malaysia/1998-reformasi) — Malaysia's response to the Asian financial crisis.
- **2018** — [GE14](/en/malaysia/2018-change-of-government) — the 14th general election and Malaysia's first federal change of government.
