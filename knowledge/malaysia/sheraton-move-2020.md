The **"Sheraton Move"** (*Langkah Sheraton*) refers to the February 2020 political
realignment that collapsed Malaysia's Pakatan Harapan (PH) federal government —
just under two years after PH won the
[2018 general election](/malaysia/2018-change-of-government). The name comes from
the **Sheraton Petaling Jaya hotel** in Selangor, where blocs of MPs and party
leaders gathered on **23 February 2020** to negotiate a new government.

## The sequence of events

Over 21–24 February 2020, a bloc of **BERSATU** and **PKR** MPs moved to leave
Pakatan Harapan, and the coalition lost its parliamentary majority. On
**24 February**, [Mahathir Mohamad](/malaysia/mahathir-mohamad) resigned as the
seventh Prime Minister; he was then appointed **interim Prime Minister** pending
the selection of a successor.

The **Yang di-Pertuan Agong (Al-Sultan Abdullah)** interviewed members of
Parliament individually to determine who commanded the confidence of a majority.
On 29 February the palace announced that **[Muhyiddin Yassin](/malaysia/muhyiddin-yassin)**
was likely to command majority support, and he was **sworn in as the eighth Prime
Minister on 1 March 2020**, forming a new **Perikatan Nasional** government.

## Significance

The Sheraton Move was the **first change of federal government in Malaysia to
occur between general elections** — effected through parliamentary defections and
a royal appointment rather than a vote. It coincided with the onset of the
COVID-19 pandemic, and analysts widely cite it as the start of a period of
political instability that saw three Prime Ministers in roughly two years.

The event remains politically contested, and different participants have given
differing accounts of how it came about; those competing claims are best treated
as attributed statements rather than established fact.
