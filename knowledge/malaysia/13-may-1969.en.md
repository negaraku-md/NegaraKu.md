Between the evening of 13 May 1969 and the morning of 22 February 1971 lay a period of roughly 21 months during which Malaysia was governed without a sitting Parliament. During that period, governing power was held by an emergency council, not the Dewan Rakyat.

This article does not retell the event as a narrative, and does not characterise any race, party or individual. It sets out what was officially recorded: dates, legislative powers, the structure of emergency administration, and the figures recorded in official reports.

## Brief Chronology

The sequence of dates below is based on the records of the National Security Council (Majlis Keselamatan Negara, MKN) and the National Archives of Malaysia (Arkib Negara Malaysia).

| Date | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 10 May 1969 | Polling for the general election held in the Peninsular states |
| 13 May 1969 | Racial riots broke out in Kuala Lumpur in the evening; a curfew was enforced |
| 15 May 1969 | The Yang di-Pertuan Agong declared a state of emergency under Article 150 of the Federal Constitution (according to MKN records) |
| 16 May 1969 | Emergency ordinance came into force; the National Operations Council (MAGERAN) was established and Parliament was suspended |
| 6 Oct 1969 | MAGERAN meeting decided to establish the National Consultative Council |
| 31 Aug 1970 | Rukun Negara proclaimed as a Royal Proclamation |
| 22 Feb 1971 | First sitting of Parliament after MAGERAN |
| 7 Jul 1971 | Cabinet agreed to formally establish the National Security Council (MKN) |

## The emergency proclamation: powers under Article 150

The 1969 emergency was implemented through the mechanism enshrined in the [Federal Constitution](/en/law/federal-constitution).

Article 150 permits the Yang di-Pertuan Agong to issue a Proclamation of Emergency if satisfied that a grave emergency exists whereby the security or economic life of the country is threatened. While the emergency is in force and Parliament is not sitting, Article 150 also permits the Yang di-Pertuan Agong to promulgate ordinances having the force of law.

According to MKN records, the Proclamation of Emergency was issued on 15 May 1969 under Article 150. Administrative power was thereafter exercised through emergency ordinances. Among these, the Emergency (Essential Powers) Ordinance No. 2, 1969 — gazetted as P.U.(A) 149 and in force from 16 May 1969 — became part of the legal framework that enabled MAGERAN to conduct the affairs of the country.

An important point to understand about this mechanism: the Constitution was not abolished. It was suspended in operation as far as an elected sitting Parliament was concerned, while law-making power passed temporarily to the executive through emergency ordinances, as permitted by Article 150 itself.

## MAGERAN: what it was, and who led it

The National Operations Council (Majlis Gerakan Negara, MAGERAN) — referred to in English as the National Operations Council — was the body established to administer the country throughout the emergency period.

MAGERAN was headed by the then Deputy Prime Minister, **Tun Abdul Razak Hussein**, who acted as *Director of Operations*. National Archives records state that he "had to take over the administration of the country in a state of emergency, becoming Director of the National Operations Council, suspending Parliament" — and was subsequently responsible for restoring parliamentary democracy.

The core function of MAGERAN, as summarised in academic studies of it, was to restore public order, restore public confidence, and prepare the conditions for the return of parliamentary government. MAGERAN combined the leadership of the civil administration with the security forces so that decisions could be made and enforced quickly under emergency conditions.

This structure was temporary by design. MAGERAN was not a permanent government and not a substitute for Parliament; it was a transitional body whose mandate ended once conditions allowed Parliament to reconvene.

## Official figures on record

The official figures below are drawn from the MAGERAN report, *The May 13 Tragedy: A Report* (1969) — an official document issued by the emergency council itself.

| Item | Official figure (1969 MAGERAN report) |
| --- | --- |
| Deaths | 196 |
| Injured | 439 |
| Arrested | 9,143 |
| Vehicles destroyed or damaged | 211 |
| Cases of arson | 753 |

It should be stated honestly that these figures are **the official government record of the time**. In later historical writing, some researchers and other sources have put forward casualty estimates that differ from this official figure. The discrepancy between the official record and other estimates is itself a documented fact in the historiography of this event. This article presents the official figures as a reference point traceable to its source, and does not break down the figures by race.

## What followed after 13 May

The MAGERAN period also became the starting point for several policies and institutions that endured afterward.

**National Consultative Council.** At the MAGERAN meeting of 6 October 1969, a decision was made to establish the National Consultative Council — a body bringing together representatives of government, political parties, religious and professional bodies, the civil service, and community groups to discuss issues of national unity.

**Rukun Negara.** From that council came [Rukun Negara](/en/malaysia/rukun-negara), which was proclaimed as a Royal Proclamation on 31 August 1970, coinciding with the 13th National Day.

**New Economic Policy (NEP).** The NEP was introduced in 1971 through the Second Malaysia Plan (1971–1975), with officially stated goals to eradicate poverty irrespective of race and to restructure society.

**Restoration of Parliament.** The first sitting of Parliament after MAGERAN was held on 22 February 1971, marking the return of the elected legislative body's operations.

**National Security Council.** MAGERAN was dissolved in 1971. The Cabinet subsequently agreed to formally establish the National Security Council (MKN) on 7 July 1971 as the national security coordinating body — an institution that still exists today and that holds part of the official record of this period.

## Common misconceptions

**"The Constitution was abolished in 1969."** No. The [Federal Constitution](/en/law/federal-constitution) remained the supreme law. The emergency was declared according to the procedure enshrined in Article 150 of that Constitution itself, and Parliament was suspended — not abolished — before reconvening in 1971.

**"MAGERAN was a military government."** MAGERAN was an emergency administrative council led by the Deputy Prime Minister, combining the heads of civil administration with the security leadership. It operated under the emergency legal framework, not as a coup.

**"All sources give the same dates and figures."** There is some variation between sources on certain dates — for example between the date of the Proclamation of Emergency and the date the emergency ordinance came into force — and between the official figures in the MAGERAN report and later researchers' estimates. This article states the source for each figure so readers can trace it themselves.

**"MAGERAN continued after 1971."** MAGERAN was dissolved in 1971 after Parliament reconvened. The security coordination function was continued by MKN, which was formally established on 7 July 1971.
