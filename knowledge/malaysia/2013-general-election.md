Malaysia's **13th general election (GE13)** was held on **5 May 2013**, after
Parliament was dissolved on 3 April. All **222 parliamentary seats** were
contested, with 112 needed for a majority. The governing
**[Barisan Nasional](/malaysia/barisan-nasional)** coalition, led by Prime
Minister [Najib Razak](/malaysia/najib-razak), faced the opposition **Pakatan
Rakyat**, led by [Anwar Ibrahim](/malaysia/anwar-ibrahim) (PKR, DAP and PAS).

## The result

Barisan Nasional retained government with **133 seats** to Pakatan Rakyat's
**89**. But for the first time, **BN won a minority of the popular vote** — about
**47.4%** to PR's roughly **50.9%** — so the governing coalition kept a majority
of seats while winning fewer votes than the opposition. **Turnout was about 85%**,
the highest recorded in a Malaysian general election to that date.

## Aftermath

At the time, GE13 was described as the strongest opposition performance against
BN since independence. The result was followed by **allegations of electoral
irregularities and constituency malapportionment** — claims made by opposition
figures and some observers, presented here as attributed rather than established.

In the immediate aftermath, the result was characterised in ethnic terms by some
commentators — framing that was **contested at the time**, with others describing
the shift as an urban, multi-ethnic one. Such characterisations are noted only as
phrases used in the period and a debate over them.
