Anwar Ibrahim (born 10 August 1947) is Malaysia's **tenth Prime Minister**, in
office since 24 November 2022. His path to the office spanned more than two
decades of political conflict, imprisonment and opposition leadership, making him
one of the most consequential figures in modern Malaysian politics.

## Rise and dismissal (1982–1998)

A prominent Islamic youth leader, Anwar joined UMNO in 1982 and rose quickly
through the cabinet, holding the education and finance portfolios and becoming
**Deputy Prime Minister in 1993** under [Mahathir Mohamad](/malaysia/mahathir-mohamad).
Widely seen as the heir apparent, he was **dismissed in 1998** amid disagreement
over the handling of the [Asian financial crisis](/malaysia/1998-reformasi).

## Reformasi, imprisonment and pardon (1998–2018)

His removal and subsequent jailing on criminal charges launched the
**Reformasi** movement — a wave of protest and calls for institutional reform.
As a matter of public record, he was released in 2004, imprisoned again in 2015,
and freed on a **royal pardon in 2018**. This article does not recount the
details of those proceedings, which are covered by court records and require
careful, sourced treatment.

## Opposition leader to Prime Minister (2018–2022)

After his 2018 release, Anwar returned to Parliament and led the reform
coalition through several turbulent years. Following the **2022 general
election**, which produced a hung parliament, he was appointed **Prime Minister
on 24 November 2022**, forming a **unity (MADANI) government** drawing together
parties that had long been rivals.
