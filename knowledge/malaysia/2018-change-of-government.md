For 61 years, from the first federal election after independence in 1957 up to 2018, every federal government of Malaysia was formed by a single coalition — the Alliance, and from the 1970s its successor, Barisan Nasional. At the 14th general election, that continuity ended: a different coalition won a majority of the seats in the Dewan Rakyat.

This article does not retell the campaign as a story, and it characterises no party, candidate or voter. It sets out what the record documents: the polling date, the seat figures, the constitutional mechanism by which the government changed, and the dates on which it happened.

## The result in numbers

Polling for the 14th general election (GE14, or PRU14 in Malay) was conducted by the [Election Commission of Malaysia (SPR)](/en/government/spr-election-commission) on **9 May 2018**. All **222 seats** of the [Dewan Rakyat](/en/government/dewan-rakyat) — the lower house of Parliament — were contested. A simple majority in a 222-seat house is **112 seats**.

The seat outcome, as recorded by the Inter-Parliamentary Union's Parline database (which compiles official national parliamentary results), was as follows.

| Coalition / party | Seats |
| --- | --- |
| Pakatan Harapan | 113 |
| Parti Warisan Sabah (ally of Pakatan Harapan) | 8 |
| **Pakatan Harapan with Warisan (combined)** | **121** |
| Barisan Nasional | 79 |
| Parti Islam Se-Malaysia (PAS) | 18 |
| Others (Sabah STAR and independents) | 4 |
| **Total** | **222** |

Pakatan Harapan together with Parti Warisan Sabah crossed the 112-seat threshold. Barisan Nasional, which had entered the election as the party forming the federal government, did not retain a majority of the seats.

*A note on the figures: the combined majority of 121 and the total of 222 are drawn from IPU Parline, which records the official results reported by Parliament; the component split is consistent across the Election Commission's declared results and standard compilations of them. The article states seat counts at coalition and party level rather than reconstructing every individual constituency.*

## Why this was a "first"

The significance of GE14 in the documented record is one of continuity broken.

From [independence in 1957](/en/malaysia/independence-1957), the federal government was formed by the Alliance Party (Parti Perikatan). The Alliance was later reconstituted and enlarged into Barisan Nasional, which was registered in the 1970s. Under one banner or the other, this coalition had formed every federal government from 1957 onward and had held a majority in the Dewan Rakyat at every general election in between.

GE14 was therefore the first general election since independence at which that coalition did not win a majority of the parliamentary seats, and the first at which the federal government passed to a different coalition. This is the plain, documented fact from which the article takes its subject — a first federal transfer of power, described without further characterisation.

## How the government changed: the constitutional mechanism

Malaysia is a federal constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary system. Who forms the government is not decided by a direct national vote for a leader; it follows from which member of the Dewan Rakyat can command the confidence of a majority of its members.

The controlling provision is in the [Federal Constitution](/en/law/federal-constitution). Article 43(2)(a) provides that the Yang di-Pertuan Agong appoints as Prime Minister a member of the House of Representatives who in his judgment is likely to command the confidence of the majority of the members of that House. The number of Dewan Rakyat seats — 222 at the time — follows from Article 46 and the constituency delineation carried out under the Constitution.

Following GE14, the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, **Sultan Muhammad V**, appointed as Prime Minister the leader put forward by the coalition holding the majority. **Tun Dr Mahathir Mohamad** was sworn in as Malaysia's **seventh Prime Minister on 10 May 2018**, the day after polling. He had earlier served as the fourth Prime Minister, from 1981 to 2003. **Dr Wan Azizah Wan Ismail** was appointed Deputy Prime Minister — the first woman to hold that office.

## Common misconceptions

**"The government changed by decree or outside the normal process."** No. The change followed the ordinary constitutional route: an election conducted by the Election Commission, seats won in the Dewan Rakyat, and the appointment of a Prime Minister under Article 43 by the Yang di-Pertuan Agong. Parliament and the Constitution operated throughout.

**"Voters directly elected the Prime Minister."** Not directly. Malaysian voters elect the members of the Dewan Rakyat for their own constituencies. The Prime Minister is the member whom the Yang di-Pertuan Agong judges likely to command the confidence of the majority of that House.

**"Pakatan Harapan won 121 seats on its own."** The 121 figure is the combined total of Pakatan Harapan and its ally Parti Warisan Sabah. Pakatan Harapan's own component parties are recorded at 113 seats, with Warisan contributing a further 8.

**"Barisan Nasional had governed since 1957 under that exact name."** The coalition that formed every federal government from 1957 was first the Alliance Party (Perikatan); it was reorganised and expanded into Barisan Nasional in the 1970s. The continuity is of the coalition and its predecessor, under successive names.
