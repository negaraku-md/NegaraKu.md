Seven kinds of flower once competed to represent a newly independent nation — and the winner was not the most fragrant or the rarest, but the one most familiar in every town and village. That flower is the bunga raya, the hibiscus.

## When and by whom was it declared?

The red hibiscus was declared Malaysia's national flower on **28 July 1960**, by the first Prime Minister, Tunku Abdul Rahman Putra Al-Haj.

The flower's scientific name is *Hibiscus rosa-sinensis*. In some places it is also known as the bunga sepatu or kembang sepatu (shoe flower).

## Why the hibiscus, and not another flower?

In late 1958, the Ministry of Agriculture proposed **seven** kinds of flower as candidates for the national flower:

| Candidate flower |
| --- |
| Hibiscus (bunga raya) |
| Ylang-ylang (bunga kenanga) |
| Jasmine (bunga melur) |
| Champak (bunga cempaka) |
| Lotus (bunga teratai) |
| Rose (bunga mawar) |
| Spanish cherry (bunga tanjung) |

The Department of Information also conducted a survey of public opinion, and people's preferences varied by region. In the end, the government chose the hibiscus based on five criteria:

- Already widely recognised throughout the country;
- Having an attractive colour and shape;
- Its name being uniform across the states;
- Easily available anywhere; and
- Not yet being the national flower of any other country at that time.

The last criterion was important: a national symbol should be unique, not shared with another country.

## What do the five petals and the red colour mean?

The five petals of the hibiscus are associated with the **five principles of the Rukun Negara**. The Rukun Negara itself was only declared ten years later, in 1970, so this association is a symbolism constructed afterwards, not the original reason for choosing the flower in 1960. The five principles are:

1. Belief in God;
2. Loyalty to King and Country;
3. Supremacy of the Constitution;
4. Rule of Law; and
5. Courtesy and Morality.

The red colour, in turn, symbolises courage in facing all challenges, as well as the nation's political and economic resilience. Overall, the hibiscus is also regarded as a symbol of unity among a people of many ethnicities.

## Where is the hibiscus used today?

As one of the national symbols, the hibiscus stands alongside the Jalur Gemilang (the flag) and the Jata Negara (the national coat of arms) as the official face of Malaysia, and its image often appears in national events and celebrations.
