## What the sector covers

Palm oil is one of Malaysia's most significant commodity exports and a major
source of rural income, particularly in Sabah, Sarawak and parts of
Peninsular Malaysia. The industry spans upstream cultivation and milling,
refining into a wide range of edible and industrial products, and downstream
manufacturing of goods such as cooking oil, oleochemicals and biodiesel.

## Key institutions and functions

- **Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB).** The main regulatory and research body,
  responsible for statistics, R&D, licensing of mills and estates, and
  industry development.
- **Malaysian Palm Oil Council (MPOC).** Promotes Malaysian palm oil in
  export markets and manages trade-related communications.
- **MSPO certification.** The Malaysian Sustainable Palm Oil scheme is
  mandatory for oil palm growers, mills and related premises in Malaysia,
  covering environmental, social and governance standards.
- **RSPO certification.** The Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil is a
  voluntary, market-driven certification scheme that many Malaysian
  producers also pursue for specific export markets.

## Who it affects

The sector affects smallholders and estate workers, mill and refinery
operators, exporters, and the state economies of major growing regions,
alongside downstream manufacturers of food, cosmetics and biofuel products
that rely on palm oil as a feedstock.
