You may once have been labelled "B40" or "T20" on a subsidy form — but that label is not your salary. It is your position in a line drawn up by the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), and that line is redrawn every time new data comes in. Understanding how the line is drawn changes how you read almost every debate about the cost of living in Malaysia.

## What do B40, M40 and T20 actually mean?

All three terms refer to **relative positions**, not fixed amounts of money:

- **B40** — the 40 percent (*bottom 40%*) of households with the lowest income.
- **M40** — the 40 percent (*middle 40%*) in the middle of the distribution.
- **T20** — the 20 percent (*top 20%*) with the highest income.

Because they are based on percentiles, the thresholds shift every time DOSM runs a survey. Someone can receive a pay rise and still stay in the same group, if other people's income rises too. This is the main reason these labels are so often misunderstood as fixed "salary classes".

The figures that set the group boundaries come from a single source: the **Household Income Survey** (HIS).

## How does DOSM collect household income data?

DOSM conducts the Household Income and Basic Amenities Survey (HIS/BA), usually paired with the Household Expenditure Survey (HES), at least twice within a five-year period. Enumerators visit a sample of selected households across every state and record gross monthly income — covering wages, self-employment income, property income, as well as gifts and transfers.

A few important points about this method:

- **The unit of measurement is the household, not the individual.** A single household may have several working members, so household income is usually higher than one person's salary.
- **Since 1989, the official figures refer to Malaysian citizens only**, even though non-citizens are also sampled.
- **The 1970 and 1974 data covered Peninsular Malaysia only**; Sabah and Sarawak began to be included in 1976.

*(The coverage and citizenship notes above are based on the OpenDOSM Household Income data catalogue.)*

It is from this income distribution that DOSM calculates the two summary values that are quoted most often: the **median** (the middle value in the line) and the **mean** (the arithmetic average). The median better represents the "typical household" because it is not pulled up by a handful of very wealthy households.

| Indicator (Malaysia) | 2022 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly median income | RM6,338 | RM7,017 |
| Monthly mean income | RM8,479 | RM9,155 |
| Annual median growth | 2.5% | 5.1% |
| Urban median | RM7,243 | RM8,139 |
| Rural median | RM4,094 | RM4,588 |

*Source: DOSM, Household Income Survey Report 2022 and 2024.*

The gap between the mean (RM8,479) and the median (RM6,338) in 2022 is itself a signal of inequality: when the average is far higher than the middle value, it means income is concentrated at the top end of the distribution.

## What are the B40, M40 and T20 thresholds?

Once the distribution is ranked, DOSM cuts it at the 40th and 80th percentiles to obtain the group thresholds. Here is the 2022 picture:

| Group | Monthly household income range (2022) | Number of households |
|---|---|---|
| B40 | Below RM5,249 | ~3.16 million |
| M40 | RM5,250 – RM11,819 | — |
| T20 | RM11,820 and above | ~1.58 million |

*Source: DOSM (cited by RinggitPlus), Household Income Survey 2022.*

Note that the names "B40" (40%) and "T20" (20%) refer to the number of households, not the share of income. When we look at how much of the country's total income each group holds, the inequality becomes clearer:

- **T20** — only 20% of households, but holding **46.3%** of total income (2022).
- **M40** — **37.6%**.
- **B40** — 40% of households, but only **16.1%** of total income.

In other words, the top one-fifth of households earns almost half of all income, while the bottom 40% share less than one-sixth.

## What is the Poverty Line Income, and how is it different?

B40 tells you who is at the bottom — but it does **not** tell you who is poor. Someone can be in the B40 without being counted as poor. Poverty is measured separately using the **Poverty Line Income** (PLI).

The PLI is the minimum income a household needs to meet its basic requirements, and it consists of two components:

1. **The food component** — based on optimal nutritional requirements and a healthy diet (meals by food category converted into prices), no longer just the minimum calorie requirement as in the old method.
2. **The non-food component** — 146 items (clothing, housing, transport, education, health and others) based on the actual spending patterns of B20 households.

In 2019, DOSM revised this methodology and raised the national PLI sharply **from RM980 to RM2,208 a month** (RM1,169 food + RM1,038 non-food). The immediate effect: the number of households recorded as poor jumped to 405,441, and the absolute poverty rate was recalculated to 5.6% (2019). By 2022, the PLI rose further to **RM2,589** (RM1,198 food component), reflecting the rising cost of living.

That 2019 revision matters: the old RM980 line had long been considered far too low compared with the current cost of living, so much so that almost no household counted as poor beneath it. Raising the line does not necessarily mean poverty increased in reality — it more reflects the use of a threshold closer to the actual cost of basic needs.

## Is Malaysia's income gap widening?

To summarise the whole distribution in a single number, DOSM uses the **Gini coefficient**. The scale is simple:

- **0** = perfect equality (every household earns the same).
- **1** = complete inequality (one household holds all the income).

The higher the value, the wider the gap. Based on gross income, Malaysia's Gini coefficient:

| Year | Gini coefficient (gross income) |
|---|---|
| 2016 | 0.399 |
| 2019 | 0.407 |
| 2022 | 0.404 |

*Source: DOSM / OpenDOSM, household inequality series.*

The movement is small — down 0.003 points from 2019 to 2022 — so it is more accurate to say Malaysia's income gap is **stable at a high level** rather than widening or narrowing significantly. The Gini in urban areas (0.393 in 2022) is also higher than in rural areas (0.351), because cities encompass both the poorest and the richest households in the same space.

## Why can your "group" change without your salary changing?

Because the B40/M40/T20 thresholds are recalculated every survey, someone can "move up" or "move down" a group without any real change in salary — simply because other people's income has changed. This is why these labels are ill-suited as a precise measure for targeting aid to particular households: they tell you a person's relative position, not whether that household genuinely needs help.

To read these statistics correctly, remember three things: (1) B40/M40/T20 are relative positions, not salaries; (2) poverty is measured separately through the PLI; and (3) the Gini summarises the overall gap, not the fortunes of any particular household.

## Next steps

- Check your own position by comparing your **household** income (not individual salary) against the 2022 thresholds in the table above.
- For the latest figures by state and district, refer directly to the **OpenDOSM** portal (open.dosm.gov.my), which provides raw data in CSV and Parquet formats.
- When reading news that claims "poverty is rising" or "the gap is widening", check whether the change comes from economic reality or from a **methodology revision** — such as the 2019 PLI increase — because both look the same in a headline but mean very different things.
