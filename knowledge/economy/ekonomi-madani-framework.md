On 27 July 2023, a few months into his premiership, Anwar Ibrahim stood up not to announce a budget or a single project, but to give Malaysia's economy a story to tell about itself.

That story is **Ekonomi MADANI** — in full, *MADANI Economy: Empowering the People* (*Ekonomi MADANI: Memperkasa Rakyat*). It is less a spending plan than a frame: a set of values, two broad ambitions and seven numbers the government says it wants to hit within roughly a decade.

## What does MADANI actually stand for?

Before it was an economy, MADANI was a word. The Anwar administration adopted *Malaysia MADANI* in early 2023 as its governing slogan, and MADANI is built as a Malay acronym — the capitalised letters inside six values spell it out.

| Letter | Malay word | English value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M | ke**M**ampanan | Sustainability |
| A | kesej**A**hteraan | Prosperity / well-being |
| D | **D**aya cipta | Innovation |
| A | horm**A**t | Respect |
| N | keyaki**N**an | Trust / confidence |
| I | **I**hsan | Care and compassion |

Ekonomi MADANI is the economic chapter of that concept: the same six values, pointed at how the country earns, shares and governs its wealth. So *Malaysia MADANI* is the wider idea, and *Ekonomi MADANI* is its economic expression — related, but not the same thing.

## The two goals: raising the ceiling and the floor

Strip away the branding and the framework rests on two ambitions that pull in different directions but are meant to move together.

The first is to **raise the ceiling** — restructure and grow the economy so Malaysia climbs back toward the front rank of Asian economies, lifting competitiveness, investment and the overall size of the pie.

The second is to **raise the floor** — improve the dignity and quality of life of ordinary people, through better wages, stronger social protection and a fairer share of national income, so growth is felt in households and not just in headline figures.

Government statements describe these as making Malaysia a leading Asian economy on one hand and elevating the quality of life for the nation on the other. The framing is deliberate: the government argues that lifting the floor and raising the ceiling are not rivals but the same project.

## What are the seven targets?

The most concrete part of Ekonomi MADANI is a set of seven medium-term targets. Anwar set a horizon of about ten years to reach them. The figures below are as reported across coverage of the launch, including a Ministry of Finance press citation of the Prime Minister's remarks.

| # | Target | Indicator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Top 30 | Among the world's 30 largest economies |
| 2 | Top 12 | Global Competitiveness Index ranking |
| 3 | 45% | Labour's share of total income |
| 4 | 60% | Female labour-force participation |
| 5 | Top 25 | Human Development Index ranking |
| 6 | Top 25 | Corruption Perceptions Index ranking |
| 7 | 3% or lower | Fiscal deficit as a share of GDP |

A few of these are worth reading twice. The **45% labour-income share** is a deliberate step up from the 40% goal carried in the 12th Malaysia Plan — a signal that the government wants more of national income flowing to workers rather than capital. The **3% fiscal deficit** ties the framework to a harder budget discipline than Malaysia ran through the pandemic years. And the two governance targets — competitiveness and the Corruption Perceptions Index — put institutional reform, not just output, on the scoreboard.

These are targets, not guarantees. By 2026, commentators tracking the framework judged progress as mixed: the fiscal deficit, female participation and competitiveness were seen as moving reasonably well, while the others lagged — an assessment attributed to analysts, not the government.

## How does it fit with Malaysia's other plans?

This is where Ekonomi MADANI is most easily misunderstood. It is **not** a new five-year plan sitting beside the others. It is the narrative stretched *over* them.

The government positions Ekonomi MADANI as the catalyst and reference point for the plans that carry the actual budgets and projects:

- the **mid-term review of the 12th Malaysia Plan**, the country's prevailing five-year development blueprint;
- the **New Industrial Master Plan 2030 (NIMP 2030)**, which sets the manufacturing and industrial agenda;
- the **National Energy Transition Roadmap (NETR)**, the plan to shift the energy mix and build green industries; and
- the **fiscal-reform agenda** — targeted subsidies, new revenue measures and the fiscal-responsibility rules meant to bring the deficit down.

Think of it as the cover and contents page of a book whose chapters were written, or rewritten, around the same time in 2023. Each plan keeps its own machinery; Ekonomi MADANI gives them a shared direction and a common set of values to be judged against.

## Common misreadings

- **Treating it as a single document with a budget.** Ekonomi MADANI is a framework and target set; the money and projects live in the Malaysia Plans and the annual federal budget.
- **Confusing Ekonomi MADANI with Malaysia MADANI.** The latter is the whole governing concept; the former is its economic slice.
- **Reading the targets as forecasts.** They are aspirations with a roughly ten-year horizon, and progress against them is debated.
- **Assuming the values are decoration.** The six MADANI values are meant to be the yardstick — trust maps to governance reform, ihsan to easing the burden on vulnerable groups, and so on.

## What's next

Ekonomi MADANI will be judged less by the speech that launched it than by the plans beneath it — whether the subsidy overhaul, NIMP 2030 and the NETR actually move the seven numbers. Watch the Ministry of Finance's annual Economic Outlook and the mid-term review of the 12th Malaysia Plan for the scorecard, and read each new budget as an instalment against these targets rather than a fresh start.
