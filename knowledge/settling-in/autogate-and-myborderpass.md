An **autogate** is an automated immigration gate: it reads the chip in your passport, matches your face to the photo, and opens — no officer, no ink stamp, usually in well under half a minute. The newer QR-code lanes are faster still, clearing a traveller in about five to seven seconds. For anyone who crosses the Malaysian border regularly, knowing which lane you are allowed to use saves real time.

The naming is where most people get confused, so start here: **MyBorderPass is for Malaysians, and foreign travellers use a different route.** This guide sorts out who can use what.

## Who can use the autogate

For years Malaysia's autogates were reserved for **Malaysian citizens, permanent residents, MM2H participants and long-term pass holders** (such as Employment Pass holders). That changed in 2024, when Malaysia opened the gates to **foreign passport holders from 63 eligible countries** — an expansion confirmed by the Home Ministry in December 2024.

| Traveller | Can use the autogate? | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malaysian citizen | Yes | Passport chip, or the MyBorderPass QR app at KLIA |
| PR / MM2H / long-term pass holder | Yes | Passport chip at the gate |
| Foreign visitor from an eligible country | Yes, after enrolment | MDAC + first-visit passport registration; MyNIISe QR where available |
| Foreign visitor from a non-listed country | Not yet | Use the staffed manual counter |

The list of eligible countries can change, so confirm your nationality on the [Immigration Department of Malaysia](/en/settling-in/immigration-department-jim/) site before relying on the gate.

## MyBorderPass vs MyNIISe

Two apps are involved, and they are not interchangeable:

- **MyBorderPass** — a QR-code app for **Malaysian citizens**, launched in January 2025 with 40 dedicated lanes at **KLIA Terminal 1 and Terminal 2**. Malaysians register their details, complete a facial check, and scan a QR code to clear in seconds.
- **MyNIISe** (the National Integrated Immigration System app) — the app being rolled out for **foreign travellers**. You scan your passport chip over the phone using NFC, take a selfie to verify your identity, then generate a QR code to scan at the gate. The QR code expires, so you regenerate it when needed.

As of early 2026, **neither app is compulsory** — the autogate and manual counters still accept a physical passport.

## Enrolling as a foreign traveller: step by step

1. **Check your eligibility.** Confirm your nationality is on the current autogate list and that your passport has at least six months of validity.
2. **Submit the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC).** File it on the official Immigration portal within three days before you arrive. This applies to almost every visit — see [who needs a visa to enter Malaysia](/en/settling-in/malaysia-entry-visa-requirements-by-nationality/).
3. **Clear the first entry at a manual counter.** On a first visit, you generally still queue at a staffed counter so your passport and biometrics are registered in the system.
4. **Use the autogate afterwards.** Once enrolled, you can use the autogate on departure and on later trips.
5. **For the QR lanes, register in MyNIISe.** Where the QR facility is live, set up the app in advance, scan your passport chip, add your selfie, and generate a QR code at the gate.

## Where the gates are

Autogates operate at **KLIA Terminal 1 and Terminal 2** and at major land and sea crossings. The foreigner QR rollout (MyNIISe) started at the busy **Johor land crossings** — **Bangunan Sultan Iskandar (BSI)** and **Kompleks Sultan Abu Bakar (KSAB)** — with expansion to KLIA, Penang, Kuching and Kota Kinabalu announced. If you are [driving to Singapore](/en/transport/driving-to-singapore-and-thailand/), the Johor causeway and Second Link are exactly where these lanes matter most.

## Common pitfalls

- **Expecting the autogate to work on your very first visit.** Plan for a manual counter the first time so your passport is enrolled.
- **Downloading the wrong app.** Foreigners do not use MyBorderPass; that one is for Malaysians. Look for MyNIISe.
- **A passport the gate cannot read.** The autogate needs a biometric (chipped) passport; an older non-chip document, a damaged chip, or a worn photo page can send you to a counter.
- **Children and families.** Age and family rules for autogate use are not uniform — be ready to use a counter with young children.
- **Leaving the apps until the airport.** Register and generate your QR code before you reach the gate; app sign-up and NFC scanning are easier on a stable connection.

## What's next

Malaysia is steadily widening automated clearance, so both the eligible-country list and the app requirements are moving targets. Before you travel, confirm the current rules with the [Immigration Department of Malaysia](/en/settling-in/immigration-department-jim/), submit your MDAC, and carry a chipped passport. For proof of identity and status inside Malaysia once you are through the border, see [MyKad and identity documents](/en/settling-in/mykad-and-identity-documents/).
