An Employment Pass holder moving to Malaysia can bring a spouse and young children on one pass. Their parents, or a child who just turned 18, need a completely different one. Same principal, same household, two separate immigration categories — and mixing them up is the single most common way a family sponsorship application stalls.

## The split: age and relationship decide the pass

Malaysia has no single "family visa" attached to an Employment Pass. Immigration splits dependants into two tracks based on who they are, according to the Expatriate Services Division's (ESD) own pass pages.

| Relationship to principal | Pass |
| --- | --- |
| Spouse | Dependant Pass |
| Children under 18 (including legally adopted children) | Dependant Pass |
| Parents or parents-in-law | Long-Term Social Visit Pass (LTSVP) |
| Children over 18 | Long-Term Social Visit Pass (LTSVP) |

The Dependant Pass is the narrower, more automatic route: it exists specifically for a spouse and minor children of an Employment Pass holder. The LTSVP is the wider catch-all — ESD uses it for the family members a Dependant Pass doesn't cover, but the Malaysian Immigration Department also issues LTSVPs in situations that have nothing to do with an Employment Pass at all, such as a foreign national married directly to a Malaysian citizen. That second use of the LTSVP runs on its own terms — for a foreign spouse of a citizen it is granted on a longer, renewable basis — and is a different pathway from the one described here for an expatriate's parents or adult children.

## Both passes are leashed to the principal

Whichever pass a family member holds, it doesn't stand on its own. ESD's guidance is explicit on both pages: validity tracks the principal's own Employment Pass, capped by whichever runs out first — the principal's pass or the dependant's own passport. If the principal's Employment Pass is renewed, the dependant's pass can be renewed alongside it; if the principal's status ends, so does theirs.

That dependency runs in only one direction. A Dependant Pass or LTSVP holder does not sponsor anyone themselves and has no independent immigration status once detached from the principal — losing the underlying Employment Pass is what breaks the whole chain, not the other way around.

## Neither pass lets you work

This is where most confusion shows up. A Dependant Pass or an LTSVP granted through an Employment Pass principal does not carry a right to work in Malaysia. ESD's own pages state it plainly for the Dependant Pass: holders "are required to apply for the Employment Pass" if they want to take up employment — the existing pass has to be set aside for a fresh Employment Pass application in the dependant's own name, sponsored by their own employer.

This is a genuinely separate rule from the LTSVP issued to a foreign spouse of a Malaysian citizen, which — per the Immigration Department's own page for that category — can carry work permission without conversion to an Employment Pass, subject to its own endorsement conditions. The lesson: "LTSVP" is not one uniform set of rights. What a specific LTSVP allows depends on which eligibility category it was granted under, not just the pass's name.

## Applying through the sponsor's ESD account

For family sponsored off an Employment Pass, both the Dependant Pass and the LTSVP are applied for the same way: through the sponsoring company's registered account on the ESD portal, not by the family member directly. That means the paperwork sits with the employer's HR or mobility team as much as with the family — a spouse or parent arriving in Malaysia cannot simply walk into an Immigration counter and apply for either pass in isolation from the principal's own Employment Pass record.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming a child "ages into" an LTSVP automatically.** Turning 18 changes which pass applies, but ESD's pages don't describe this as automatic — it needs its own application before the Dependant Pass would otherwise lapse.
- **Treating "LTSVP" as one fixed set of rules.** An adult child's LTSVP under an Employment Pass principal and a Malaysian citizen's foreign spouse's LTSVP are eligibility categories with different durations and different work rights, despite sharing a name.
- **Expecting the dependant's pass to outlast the principal's.** Because validity is capped by the principal's own Employment Pass, a dependant's pass cannot run longer than the sponsoring pass it's attached to.
- **Assuming the pass itself authorises work.** Neither pass does; taking a job means applying for an Employment Pass in the dependant's own name.
