Most people planning a move to Malaysia start with the wrong question. They ask
"what documents do I need?" and end up with a browser full of tabs — visa types,
SIM card guides, bank account rules, driving licence rules — with no sense of
which one has to happen first. Then they land, try to open a bank account on a
tourist stamp, get turned away, and lose a week figuring out why.

There's a simpler way to think about it. Almost everything you'll do in your
first weeks in Malaysia checks one thing: **the immigration pass you're
holding.** Get that decision right before you book a flight, and the rest — a
bank account, a SIM card, a driving licence — falls into a predictable order.
Get it wrong, and you'll be sequencing damage control instead.

## The decision that comes before everything else: which pass are you actually eligible for?

Malaysia's long-stay foreigner passes split into two families, and which one
applies to you is usually obvious once you ask a single question: **is a
Malaysian entity sponsoring you, or are you funding the stay yourself?**

| You are... | Likely pass | Who sponsors it | Administered by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hired by a Malaysian company for an ongoing role | **Employment Pass** | Your employer | Immigration Department / Expatriate Services Division (ESD) |
| Sent temporarily by an overseas company to work with a Malaysian company, while still paid abroad | **Professional Visit Pass** | The Malaysian company you're assigned to | ESD |
| A spouse or child (under 18) of an Employment Pass holder | **Dependant Pass** | The principal Employment Pass holder | ESD |
| A spouse, child, elderly parent, or in another qualifying family category, of a Malaysian citizen or long-term resident | **Long Term Social Visit Pass** | A Malaysian citizen/resident sponsor | Immigration Department |
| Retiring or relocating on your own funds, not tied to a job offer | **Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H)** or a similar long-stay route | Nobody — self-funded, asset/income-based | Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture (MOTAC), via the MM2H programme |

The Immigration Department is explicit that the Professional Visit Pass exists
specifically for the case where a company "provide[s] services or undergo[es]
practical training with a Malaysian company on behalf of an overseas company on
a temporary basis" — the moment you're paid by, and answerable to, the
Malaysian side instead, that's an Employment Pass matter, not a PVP. It's a
subtle line, and it's the reason "am I sponsored, and by whom" is worth
settling before anything else: PVP holders can't sponsor dependants, and their
pass is capped at no more than 12 months per issuance, so a family relocation
usually means an Employment Pass is the right target from the start, not a PVP.

The Dependant Pass has its own mechanics worth knowing early: it's applied for
through the sponsoring Employment Pass holder's own ESD account, not
independently, and its validity is tied to the principal's pass — if the
Employment Pass holder's status ends, so does the dependant's.

MM2H sits apart from all of the above because nobody is sponsoring you — the
programme evaluates your own income, assets or deposits against its own
published criteria, and it's run by a different ministry (Tourism, Arts and
Culture) rather than the Immigration Department directly, though a pass is
still issued at the end. Because MM2H's specific financial thresholds and
tiers have been revised more than once, confirm the current criteria on the
programme's own portal rather than a blog post, however recent it looks.

## What a tourist stamp will and won't get you

If you arrive on a standard short-term social visit (tourist) pass — the
default entry stamp most nationalities get on arrival or via eNTRI/eVISA — that
stamp certifies exactly one thing: that you're allowed to visit. It does not
convert into a work or long-stay pass from inside the country in most cases.
If work, family sponsorship, or a self-funded long stay is the actual plan, the
pass application generally needs to be settled — or at least well underway —
**before** the move, not treated as a first-week errand once you've landed.

Most foreign visitors, regardless of pass type, also now need to complete the
**Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)** online before arrival, through the
Immigration Department's own portal. It's worth stating plainly what it is
not: it is not a visa, it does not replace whatever pass or visa you already
hold, and it's free — the Department's own channels exist specifically because
unofficial look-alike sites have charged travellers for a form that costs
nothing at the source.

## After you land: the practical order

Once you're in the country on the right pass, four things tend to queue up —
and they queue in a specific order because each one depends on the one before
it.

**1. Somewhere to stay — temporarily first.** A short lease or serviced
apartment while you get oriented is normal, not a sign of being disorganised.
You'll need a Malaysian address for the next two steps, and it doesn't need to
be your final one yet.

**2. A bank account.** Malaysian banks map their document checklist directly
onto your pass category — an Employment Pass, Student Pass, Dependant Pass or
MM2H approval each unlock a slightly different list, and a passport or pass
that is close to expiry can be grounds for an outright decline.
This is the step most newcomers try to shortcut, and it's the one that least
tolerates shortcuts.

**3. A local SIM.** Prepaid and postpaid SIM registration in Malaysia is tied
to identity verification against your pass, in the same spirit as the bank
account step — expect to show the same documents again.

**4. A driving licence, if you'll drive.** Converting a foreign licence to a
Malaysian one has its own eligibility window tied to how long you've been in
the country and what pass you hold — worth starting on once the first three
items are settled, not left until your existing licence's recognition period
is about to run out.

## Common mistakes

- **Booking the move before confirming pass eligibility.** The pass — not the
  flight date — is the actual critical path; everything downstream stalls
  without it.
- **Assuming a Professional Visit Pass and an Employment Pass are
  interchangeable.** They serve genuinely different arrangements (temporary
  assignment vs. ongoing local employment), and only one of them lets you
  sponsor a family member.
- **Treating a tourist entry as a placeholder you'll "sort out later."** In
  most cases you can't convert it into a work or long-stay pass from inside
  Malaysia — the pass you arrive on should already be the right one.
- **Trying to open a bank account or register a SIM before the pass is
  actually issued**, using only a passport. Both processes are built around
  the pass, not just identity.
- **Using an unofficial site for the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card.** It's
  free and only valid through the Immigration Department's own portal;
  look-alike sites charging a fee are not the official channel.
