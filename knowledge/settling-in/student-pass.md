You cannot apply for your own Malaysian student visa. Even with an offer letter in hand and the fees paid, the application has to go through your institution — because the body that screens it isn't Immigration at all, at least not at first. It's a company.

## EMGS is the single window, not just another office

Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS) is a Company Limited by Guarantee under the Ministry of Higher Education, established on 13 April 2012. Its mandate, in its own words, is to be "the principal body that manages the movement of international students in Malaysia, including facilitating visa processing."

That makes EMGS a one-stop centre rather than a rubber stamp. It runs a centralised online system covering application submission, real-time status tracking, and coordination of post-arrival health screening, and it operates International Student Arrival Centres (ISAC) at KLIA terminals plus offices elsewhere in the country to support students on arrival. Institutions and students deal with one system for what would otherwise be several separate government touchpoints.

## Your institution is the applicant, not you

For higher education, the Immigration Department's own Student Pass guidance is explicit about how this runs: EMGS conducts academic screening on the application, and after approval, the institution is the one that later submits the pass for endorsement at the nearest Immigration office. Applicants for this route must be outside Malaysia when the initial application is lodged.

That's the structural point worth internalising early. A Student Pass is not something you personally negotiate with a government counter — it moves through your institution and EMGS as a pipeline, and delays in either one delay you.

## VAL, visa, and Student Pass are three different things

Guides sometimes use these terms interchangeably. Immigration and EMGS don't, and mixing them up is the fastest way to misjudge your own timeline.

| Document | What it does | Who issues it | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visa Approval Letter (VAL / eVAL) | Authorises you to travel to Malaysia as a student | Immigration Department, after EMGS screening | Before you travel |
| Single-entry visa (where required) | Lets certain nationalities physically enter Malaysia | Applied for via the eVISA portal | Before you travel, in addition to the VAL |
| Student Pass | Legal permission to study and reside in Malaysia for your programme | Endorsed on your passport by Immigration | After arrival, registration and medical clearance |

The VAL gets you to the airport. The Student Pass is what keeps you legally enrolled once you're here — and the two are issued at different stages, by different triggers.

## The application requires English proficiency evidence

EMGS's own guidelines state that a VAL application must demonstrate an acceptable level of English language proficiency. It's framed as a standing requirement of the file itself, and the guidelines note that a missing English certificate can delay processing and affect whether your pass covers your full course duration — so it's worth confirming with your institution before submission, not after a query comes back from EMGS.

## The processing clock: 14 working days, if the file is complete

EMGS states a service standard of 14 working days for VAL issuance, counted from the date a complete application is submitted. The qualifier matters more than the number — incomplete documentation is the recurring reason cited for delay, not any backlog at EMGS's end.

Plan your travel dates around a complete submission, not around the date you first sent documents to your institution.

## After you land: medical screening, then the pass itself

Arrival in Malaysia doesn't finish the process — it starts the next stage. Within 7 working days of arriving, you're required to undergo a medical screening. Only once that clearance is in, does your institution submit the final application for the Student Pass to be endorsed on your passport at the nearest Immigration office.

This is the sequence that surprises new students most: the VAL got you into the country, but you are not yet "on" a Student Pass until this second, post-arrival step closes.

## The i-Kad: your everyday ID, not a travel document

Once your Student Pass is endorsed, you're issued an i-Kad — a biometric card the size of a credit card, carrying your photo and identifying details. Per EMGS, it's meant to be carried at all times and functions as the recognised identity document for foreign nationals in Peninsular Malaysia, which is why it lets you keep your passport somewhere secure rather than carrying it daily.

Two limits are worth flagging before you assume otherwise: the i-Kad is not a substitute for international travel documents, and it does not stand in for a passport when travelling between Peninsular Malaysia and Sabah or Sarawak.

## What it costs — and what to leave unbudgeted for now

The Immigration Department publishes the statutory Student Pass fee as RM60, with an accompanying dependant's Social Visit Pass at RM90 each. Those are the confirmed government fees.

What isn't a single published figure is your total first-year cost. EMGS processing charges, medical screening fees at the clinic you're referred to, and insurance premiums are set by the parties providing those specific services, not by one centralised fee schedule — so treat any all-in estimate you see quoted elsewhere as an unofficial guide, and confirm the actual breakdown with your institution or directly with EMGS before you budget against it.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming you can apply to Immigration directly.** For higher education, EMGS screening and institutional submission are the route — there isn't a walk-in alternative.
- **Treating the VAL as the Student Pass.** The VAL gets you into Malaysia; the Student Pass is a separate, later endorsement that only happens after arrival and medical clearance.
- **Skipping the visa question because you already have a VAL.** Whether you additionally need a single-entry visa depends on nationality, and it's a separate application through the eVISA portal.
- **Missing the 7-working-day medical screening window.** The Student Pass endorsement is blocked until this clears — don't treat it as optional paperwork you can get around to.
- **Carrying your passport everywhere once you have an i-Kad.** The card is designed to reduce that risk, but it doesn't replace the passport for international travel.
- **Budgeting a total cost figure from a blog instead of your institution.** The RM60 and RM90 statutory fees are confirmed; everything else varies by provider.

## What's next

Once your Student Pass is endorsed and your i-Kad is in hand, the next practical steps are usually [opening a Malaysian bank account](/en/money-daily-life/opening-bank-account) and, if you'll be driving, sorting out a [driving licence as a foreigner](/en/transport/driving-licence-foreigners). For the agency behind the Student Pass endorsement itself, see [Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia](/en/government/imigresen).
