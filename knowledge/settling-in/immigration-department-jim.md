The **Immigration Department of Malaysia** — *Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia* (**JIM**), branded **IMI** — is the federal agency under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** that manages passports, visas, passes and border control, and enforces immigration law. If you are moving to, working in, or visiting Malaysia, JIM is the authority behind nearly every document that lets you enter, stay, or work. It is headquartered in Putrajaya and led by the **Director General of Immigration**.

## What JIM does

The department's core functions fall into three areas:

- **Travel documents for Malaysians** — issuing passports and other travel documents to citizens and permanent residents.
- **Visas, passes and permits for foreigners** — granting entry visas, employment and student passes, dependant passes and visit passes.
- **Border control and enforcement** — managing the movement of people at all authorised entry and exit points, and enforcing immigration legislation.

Its stated vision is "World Class Immigration Services," with the objective of managing immigration matters in line with national interest.

## Laws it administers

JIM enforces several statutes. The principal ones are:

| Legislation | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Immigration Act 1959/63 [Act 155] | Entry, stay, passes, removal and offences |
| Immigration Regulations 1963 | Detailed rules under the Act |
| Passports Act 1966 [Act 150] | Malaysian passports and travel documents |
| Anti-Trafficking in Persons and Anti-Smuggling of Migrants Act 2007 | Anti-trafficking and migrant smuggling |

The **Director General of Immigration** — the department's head — is appointed under **section 3 of the Immigration Act 1959/63**, and the Act gives the office powers over entry, passes and removal.

## Key online systems

Most JIM services have moved online. The ones settlers meet most often are:

- **Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)** — a free electronic arrival form that most foreign visitors must submit online, mandatory since **1 January 2024** and generally filed within three days before arrival. Malaysian citizens and permanent residents, and Singapore citizens, are among those exempt; the official MDAC page lists the full exemptions.
- **eVISA / MYVISA** — the online visa facility (at malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my) covering categories such as the Employment Pass, Professional Visit Pass, Dependant Pass and Long-Term Social Visit Pass.
- **Expatriate Services Division (ESD)** — an online portal (esd.imi.gov.my) where registered companies submit and manage expatriate immigration applications.

## Where to find it

The department operates from its headquarters in **Putrajaya** and through state and district immigration offices, entry-point counters at airports and land borders, and Malaysian missions abroad. The official website is **imi.gov.my**, which links to the online application portals above. Always start from a `.gov.my` address — many look-alike third-party sites charge fees for forms (like the MDAC) that JIM provides for free.

## What's next

If you are coming to work, read up on the specific pass you need — the Employment Pass, Professional Visit Pass or Student Pass — and check whether your employer must register with the ESD. Short-term visitors should confirm their visa-free eligibility and complete the MDAC before departure. For document renewals and appeals, use the counters and portals under imi.gov.my rather than intermediaries.
