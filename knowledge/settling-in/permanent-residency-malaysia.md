A red MyKad is the quiet finish line for a lot of long-haul lives in Malaysia — the foreign wife who has renewed the same visit pass for a decade, the engineer who never wants to file another work-permit extension, the retiree who wants to belong somewhere for good. That card, the MyPR, is what Malaysian permanent residency actually looks like in your wallet.

Permanent residency here is not a visa you renew and it is not citizenship. It is an **Entry Permit** granted under **Section 10 of the Immigration Act 1959/63** and **Regulation 4 of the Immigration Regulations 1963**, and once you hold it you can live and work in Malaysia indefinitely without a separate pass.

## What exactly is permanent residency in Malaysia?

The legal instrument is the Entry Permit. When the Immigration Department (Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia) approves it, the holder is registered as a permanent resident and issued a red MyKad — the same national identity card citizens carry, but in red rather than blue. That card is the "MyPR."

Two things make PR different from the alphabet soup of Malaysian passes:

- **It does not expire.** A work permit, a long-term social visit pass (LTSVP), or a Residence Pass all have validity periods and must be renewed. The Entry Permit does not.
- **It is issued once, on merit.** There is no automatic renewal cycle to fall out of, and no employer or spouse tie that has to be re-proved every year.

The Immigration Department describes the Entry Permit as the document that lets a foreign national "enter and reside legally in Malaysia without any time limit," and it is the instrument through which a foreign national is granted permanent resident (MyPR) status.

## Who can apply, and by which route?

The Immigration Department accepts Entry Permit applications from several classes of foreign national. In practice, most successful applicants come through one of these paths:

- **Spouse of a Malaysian citizen** — the single most common route, sponsored by the Malaysian husband or wife.
- **Skilled professional / expert** — foreign nationals with recognised expertise and a track record of employment in Malaysia, typically endorsed by a relevant Malaysian authority.
- **Investor / high-net-worth individual** — those who place a substantial long-term deposit in Malaysia.
- **Children of citizens** — biological, step, or adopted children of a Malaysian citizen who are minors.
- **Other foreign nationals on merit** — assessed against the department's criteria.

The spouse and children routes are family-based and sponsored by the Malaysian relative. The skilled, investor, and general-merit routes rest on the applicant's own profile and usually require recommendations from a Malaysian agency in the relevant field.

## What changed for foreign spouses in 2025?

For years the spouse route required the longest wait. Applicants had to accumulate **five continuous years on a long-term social visit pass** before they could even lodge a PR application, and the outcome was then run through a points-based assessment that the Foreign Spouses Support Group said was barely documented.

In January 2025 the Home Ministry announced a significant loosening. Under the reform:

- The marriage-length requirement drops to **three years** (from five).
- The applicant must have held a **long-term social visit pass for at least one year** before applying.
- The **points-based test for spouses is scrapped**, replaced by an interview and by factors such as length of marriage and the number of children the couple share.
- New applications are to be **processed within about six months**.

The ministry said these initiatives would begin no later than the end of September 2025. If you are on the spouse track, confirm the current requirements directly with Immigration, because the on-the-ground guidance has been in transition since the announcement.

## How do you actually apply?

PR is not an online process. The Entry Permit application is lodged **in person at Immigration headquarters in Putrajaya**, by the applicant, while in Malaysia.

For the spouse route, the core paperwork includes:

- The Entry Permit application forms — **IMM.4 and IMM.270**.
- A completed marriage declaration form and the marriage certificate.
- The Malaysian spouse acting as **sponsor**, with proof of income and employment.
- Proof of address (such as a utility bill), passport photographs, and a cover letter in Bahasa Malaysia.
- Original documents plus certified copies; foreign-language documents translated into English or Bahasa Malaysia.

Family Frontiers, a migrant-family advocacy group, counts about fourteen documents for a spouse file. Applicants are advised to keep renewing their existing LTSVP while the PR decision is pending, because processing has historically stretched out — in some spouse cases, up to five years, though the 2025 reform targets six months for new files.

If the application is rejected, an **appeal to the Minister of Home Affairs must be filed within 30 days** of the rejection letter, with an appeal letter, the rejection letter, and supporting documents.

## PR vs Residence Pass vs MM2H vs citizenship — how do they compare?

These four are constantly confused. They sit at different points on the permanence ladder.

| Status | Legal basis | Expires? | Leads to citizenship? | Typical holder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Permanent Residency (MyPR)** | Entry Permit — s.10 Immigration Act 1959/63; Reg. 4 Immigration Regulations 1963 | No | It is the permanent status that can precede a citizenship application | Long-settled spouses, skilled professionals, investors |
| **Residence Pass** | Reg. 16A, Immigration Regulations 1963 | Yes — RM500 for 5 years, renewable | No | Family-tied residents not (yet) on the PR track |
| **MM2H** | Long-term social visit pass programme | Yes — renewable | No | Financially self-sufficient foreigners and retirees |
| **Citizenship** | Federal Constitution | No | It is citizenship | Naturalised or registered Malaysians |

Two distinctions matter most. First, **MM2H is not PR**: it is a renewable long-stay pass with financial conditions, and it does not confer permanent status or a path to the red MyKad. Second, **PR is not citizenship**: a permanent resident keeps their foreign nationality and passport, cannot vote, and holds a status that — unlike Malaysian citizenship — can in principle be revoked. PR is, however, the settled residence base from which a citizenship application is normally built.

## What does the permit cost?

The Entry Permit fee is modest — the Immigration Department's Entry Permit page states a fixed rate of **RM120 for each issue** of the permit (forms IMM.4 and IMM.270). The real cost of a PR application is not the fee; it is the years of maintained lawful residence, the document-gathering, and the wait. Budget for repeated LTSVP renewals in the meantime and, if you use one, an agent or lawyer to assemble the file.

## What's next

- **Confirm the live spouse rules.** The three-year marriage / one-year-pass reform was announced for rollout by end-September 2025 — check the current requirement with Immigration before you file.
- **Keep your existing pass valid.** Renew your LTSVP, work pass, or Residence Pass on time throughout the PR wait; a lapse can undo years of continuous residence.
- **Assemble originals plus certified copies early.** Marriage certificate, proof of continuous residence, and sponsor income documents are the usual bottlenecks.
- **Know the appeal clock.** If refused, you have 30 days from the rejection letter to petition the Minister of Home Affairs.
- **Separate the statuses in your planning.** If your goal is eventual citizenship, PR is the base you build from; MM2H is not, however long you hold it.
