For decades, everyone in Malaysia — millionaire or rubber tapper — paid the same price for petrol at the pump. On 30 September 2025, that arrangement ended. Now a litre of RON95 may cost RM1.99 for you but RM2.60 for your neighbour, and what decides it is not the world oil price but the identity card you slot into the terminal.

This shift — from a *blanket* subsidy (everyone gets it) to a *targeted* subsidy (only the eligible get it) — is described by the government as one of Malaysia's biggest fiscal reforms in a generation. This guide explains how it works for RON95 and diesel, who is eligible, and how much money is at stake.

## Why is Malaysia ending blanket fuel subsidies?

According to the government's argument, blanket subsidies are too expensive and much of the benefit is misdirected to those who need it least.

According to the Ministry of Finance, the RON95 subsidy alone cost **nearly RM20 billion a year** in 2023 and 2024. Because that low price was given to *all* consumers, the government argued that a large share of public money also flowed to high earners who drive the most and are most able to pay the full price.

For diesel, the government argued the problem was even more stark. The government once spent **RM14.3 billion** on diesel subsidies, and stated that much of it leaked — not to citizens, but to cross-border smuggling and commercial abuse because Malaysian diesel was far cheaper than in neighbouring countries.

The reform logic put forward by the government is simple: float prices to market levels, then channel subsidies *only* to eligible citizens. The money saved can be redirected to cash aid and social safety nets.

## What is BUDI95, and who is eligible for RON95 at RM1.99?

BUDI95 (under the BUDI MADANI brand) is the targeted subsidy programme for RON95 petrol. It introduces **two-tier pricing**:

- **RM1.99 per litre** for eligible Malaysian citizens.
- **RM2.60 per litre** (the unsubsidised price) for those who are not eligible, foreigners, or after the monthly quota is used up.

The net subsidy is **RM0.61 per litre**. RON97 remains at **RM3.21 per litre** at the time BUDI95 began (the RON97 price floats with the market and can change).

Who is eligible? According to the Ministry of Finance and operators such as Petron, the conditions are:

- A **Malaysian citizen** holding a **MyKad** with an active chip.
- Holding a valid **driving licence** for an approved class.
- **Not** in the **T15** group — the top 15% of households by income, who are excluded.

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim described the target group as around 18 million car drivers and motorcyclists, including youths aged 16 and above and gig workers. As of 30 November 2025, **13.9 million citizens** — about **84% of the 16.5 million** who are eligible — had benefited, with sales reaching **RM5.16 billion** and consumption of **2.59 billion litres**.

## How do MyKad, the quota and PADU work together?

Three parts move in unison every time you fill up: identity verification, the quota check, and the database behind the scenes.

**PADU** (Pangkalan Data Utama, the Central Database) is the brain of the system. It combines data from the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN), the Road Transport Department (JPJ) and the National Registration Department (JPN) to determine whether a person falls outside the T15 group. The eligibility list is updated periodically so that no eligible citizen is left out.

**MyKad** is the key at the pump. You verify eligibility through one of several methods:

- Inserting your MyKad into a **card reader terminal** at the station.
- Using an **oil company app** such as Setel (Petronas) or the Shell app.
- **Touch 'n Go eWallet** or an Outdoor Payment Terminal (OPT).
- **Cash payment at the counter** — provided specifically for those without an e-wallet or internet access.

The **quota** limits abuse. Each eligible individual gets **300 litres a month** at the subsidised price. If the quota is used up, subsequent purchases are charged at the unsubsidised price of RM2.60. E-hailing and p-hailing drivers receive a higher allocation because of their work needs.

## How was diesel rationalised — and what changes in 2026?

Diesel led RON95 by more than a year, and its story teaches us how this reform unfolds.

On **10 June 2024**, the government floated the price of diesel in Peninsular Malaysia. The retail price jumped to **RM3.35 per litre** — in line with the market — from the previously subsidised level. Sabah, Sarawak and Labuan were **exempted** and maintained at **RM2.15 per litre**.

To protect those who genuinely need it, the government retained targeted subsidies:

- **Logistics** through the **SKDS 2.0** (Subsidised Diesel Control System) fleet card: RM2.15 per litre.
- **Public transport** (buses, ambulances, fire engines): RM1.88 per litre.
- **Fishermen**: RM1.65 per litre.

Individuals, meanwhile, received **cash aid** — two payments of RM200 a month under **BUDI Individu** (diesel vehicle owners) and **BUDI Agri-Komoditi** (smallholders).

By 30 September 2025, the Ministry of Finance reported more than **140,000** BUDI Individu recipients (RM430 million), **180,000** BUDI Agri-Komoditi recipients (RM350 million), and **121,618** companies with **355,074** vehicles approved for fleet cards.

Then came the next phase. From **1 July 2026**, the **Budi Diesel** programme allows eligible private diesel vehicle owners to buy diesel at **RM2.10 per litre** — around **700,000** vehicle owners (400,000 in the Peninsula; 300,000 in Sabah, Sarawak and Labuan). Eligibility is verified through MyKad and the JPJ database, with pickup and SUV owners able to apply for an additional 100 litres a month. The subsidised diesel applies only to **B10 and above** blends; B7 remains unsubsidised at the weekly market rate.

## How much does the government save, and where does the money go?

This is the real stake of the reform.

For **diesel**, the Ministry of Finance estimates savings of **about RM5 billion a year** since 10 June 2024 — a result of closing smuggling leaks and targeting the subsidy.

For **RON95**, Deputy Finance Minister Lim Hui Ying stated that the government is expected to save **RM2.5 billion to RM4 billion a year**, depending on the global crude oil price ranging between **US$60 and US$80 a barrel**. (A full assessment begins in 2026 because BUDI95 was only launched at the end of September 2025.)

The government states that the money saved is redirected — not lost. According to the government, fuel subsidy savings are channelled to direct cash aid such as Sumbangan Tunai Rahmah (STR) and Sumbangan Asas Rahmah (SARA) for low-income households. (The exact allocation for these cash aid programmes must be verified from official Budget documents and is not stated here because no primary source was fetched.)

## Comparison: blanket versus targeted subsidy

| Feature | Blanket subsidy (old) | Targeted subsidy (BUDI MADANI) |
|---|---|---|
| Who gets it | All consumers, including the wealthy and foreigners | Eligible citizens only; T15 excluded |
| How it is delivered | Pump price suppressed for everyone | Two-tier pricing + quota via MyKad/PADU |
| Leakage | High — smuggling, abuse (according to the government) | Reduced through identity verification |
| RON95 cost | ~RM20 billion a year (2023–2024) | Saves ~RM2.5–4 billion a year (projected) |
| Diesel cost | RM14.3 billion (before) | Saves ~RM5 billion a year |
| Fiscal flexibility | Low | Money redirected to cash aid (STR/SARA) |

## Are you eligible — and what do you need to do?

Use this simple framework for RON95:

1. **Are you a Malaysian citizen with an active-chip MyKad?** If not — you pay the unsubsidised price of RM2.60.
2. **Do you hold a valid driving licence?** This is a basic eligibility condition announced by the government.
3. **Are you in the T15 group (the top 15% of earners)?** If yes — you are excluded from the subsidy.
4. **If you pass 1–3:** you are eligible for 300 litres of RON95 at RM1.99 each month. No special registration is required for most people — simply verify your eligibility at the pump using MyKad or an app.
5. **If you are an e-hailing/p-hailing driver or a diesel vehicle owner:** check the BUDI MADANI portal for additional quotas or specific programmes (for example Budi Diesel for private diesel vehicles).

For private diesel, the additional condition is that you must **personally own** a registered diesel vehicle with valid road tax — ownership is verified through the JPJ database.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming you need to "register" for RON95.** For the majority of citizens, eligibility is verified automatically through PADU and MyKad at the pump — not through a separate form.
- **Confusing diesel and RON95 prices.** They are different programmes with their own dates, prices and mechanisms. RON95 = RM1.99 (BUDI95); private diesel = RM2.10 (Budi Diesel, from July 2026).
- **Forgetting the quota limit.** Purchases exceeding 300 litres a month (RON95) are charged the full price. There are also per-transaction limits (for example RM250 as stated by operators).
- **Assuming prices are fixed.** The *unsubsidised* price is set by the Ministry of Finance through the Automatic Pricing Mechanism (APM) and can change; only the subsidised price of RM1.99 is stable.
- **Assuming Sabah/Sarawak is the same as the Peninsula for diesel.** Diesel in Sabah, Sarawak and Labuan is not floated and remains at RM2.15 per litre.
- **Buying RON97 expecting a subsidy.** RON97 has never been subsidised and remains at the market rate (RM3.21 per litre at the time BUDI95 began).

## What's next

Malaysia's fuel subsidy system is still evolving. A few things worth watching:

- **The full 2026 assessment.** The government will calculate the actual full-year RON95 savings for the first time, which will determine whether the RM2.5–4 billion estimate is met.
- **The expansion of Budi Diesel.** The private diesel programme starting in July 2026 marks a shift from cash aid to a MyKad-based subsidy at the pump — a pattern that may be refined further.
- **PADU's accuracy.** The fairness of the whole system depends on the accuracy of income data. Watch how the government handles appeals and misclassified cases.
- **Global oil prices.** Because the *unsubsidised* price follows the market, a rise in world oil prices widens the gap between RM1.99 and the full price — making the quota more important.

For official and up-to-date information, refer to the BUDI MADANI portal and Ministry of Finance press releases. The figures in this guide are accurate as of the update date; fuel prices and programme details can change.
