Lose your passport in Lima, get arrested in Doha, or need a document
notarised in Seoul, and the office that can actually help you is one node
in a network of 111 Malaysian missions spread across 85 countries — every
one of them run out of Wisma Putra in Putrajaya.

## How big is Malaysia's overseas network?

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs breaks its overseas presence into a handful
of mission types. Together they add up to **111 missions in 85 countries**.

| Mission type | Count | What it is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Embassies | 66 | Malaysia's principal mission in a non-Commonwealth country |
| High commissions | 18 | The embassy-equivalent in a fellow Commonwealth country |
| Consulates general | 21 | A secondary mission in a major city, headed by a consul general |
| Consulate offices | 2 | A smaller consular post |
| Permanent representatives to the UN | 2 | Malaysia's missions to the United Nations |
| Permanent representative to ASEAN | 1 | Malaysia's mission to the regional bloc |
| Malaysian Friendship and Trade Centre | 1 | A de facto mission where formal ties are not maintained |

The split between "embassy" and "high commission" is the one that trips
people up most often. Both are a country's top-level mission, both are
headed by an envoy of equal rank — an ambassador or a high commissioner —
and both do the same work. Malaysia simply uses **high commission** when the
host is another Commonwealth member (the United Kingdom, India, Singapore,
Australia, and so on) and **embassy** everywhere else.

## What consular services do missions provide?

A mission's job is not only diplomacy between governments. For an ordinary
Malaysian abroad, the embassy, high commission or consulate general is the
front desk of the Malaysian government. The core consular services are
fairly consistent across missions.

| Service | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Passport | Renewal of the Malaysian international passport for citizens resident in the mission's jurisdiction |
| Emergency Certificate (SPC) | A one-way travel document to return to Malaysia when a passport is lost, expired or about to expire |
| Notarial & attestation | Certifying true copies, authenticating documents, and attesting commercial invoices and official papers |
| Registration | Registering Malaysians living in the jurisdiction through the online e-Consular system |
| Assistance in distress | Help for Malaysians who are arrested, hospitalised, victims of crime, or otherwise in difficulty overseas |

The government's own guidance is blunt about the most common emergency: if a
Malaysian loses a passport while travelling, the route home is the nearest
mission and an **Emergency Certificate (Sijil Perakuan Cemas, SPC)** — not a
same-day replacement passport. It is a single-use document valid only for
the journey back to Malaysia.

Not every service runs from every post. A small consulate office may handle
notarial work and emergencies but route full passport processing to the
embassy that supervises it, so checking the specific mission's page before
travelling matters.

## Which mission covers which country?

Each mission is assigned a **jurisdiction** — the countries and, sometimes,
the specific regions it is responsible for. A single embassy is frequently
accredited to several nearby countries at once, which is why a Malaysian in
a country without a resident embassy is served by the mission in a
neighbouring capital. Consulates general narrow this further: in large
countries, a consulate general in one city may cover only certain states or
provinces, with the embassy in the capital covering the rest.

The practical takeaway: before assuming "there's no Malaysian embassy here,"
check which mission is accredited to that country. There usually is one —
just not always inside the border.

## What about foreign embassies in Malaysia?

The traffic runs both ways. Dozens of foreign governments maintain resident
embassies and high commissions in Malaysia, overwhelmingly concentrated in
**Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya**, with a number of countries also running
consulates in regional cities such as Penang, Johor Bahru, Kota Kinabalu and
Kuching. Some states accredit their ambassador from another Asian capital
rather than keeping a resident mission in Kuala Lumpur.

The authoritative register of who is here is the **Diplomatic and Consular
List**, published by the Protocol Department of the Ministry of Foreign
Affairs. It records the accredited diplomatic and consular officials of
every foreign mission resident in Malaysia, along with honorary consuls and
senior officials of international organisations, and is issued free of charge
through the ministry's portal.

## What's next

If you are a Malaysian heading overseas, note the mission accredited to your
destination and its consular contact before you go — the moment you need it
(a lost passport, an arrest, a document to notarise) is rarely the moment to
start searching. If you are trying to place a foreign diplomat or verify a
mission accredited to Malaysia, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' Diplomatic
and Consular List is the register to consult. And if your question is about
promoting exports abroad rather than serving citizens, that runs through a
parallel network of [MATRADE](/en/government/matrade) trade offices, not the
embassies.
