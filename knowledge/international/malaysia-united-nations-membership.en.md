Just 17 days after Tunku Abdul Rahman proclaimed "Merdeka" at Stadium Merdeka, the flag of the Federation of Malaya was raised at UN headquarters in New York. On 17 September 1957, the young nation was formally admitted as a member of the United Nations — a step that affirmed its sovereignty on the world stage almost simultaneously with its birth.

Nearly seven decades later, that relationship remains a pillar of the country's foreign policy. Malaysia has sat at the Security Council table four times, sent tens of thousands of peacekeepers to the world's most dangerous conflict zones, and is now once again knocking on the Security Council's door for the 2036-2037 term.

## When and how did Malaysia join the UN?

According to the UN's official records, Malaysia's date of admission is **17 September 1957**. At the time the country was known as the Federation of Malaya; that membership continued uninterrupted under the name Malaysia after the larger federation was formed in 1963.

For a country that had only just emerged from colonial administration, UN membership was more than a symbol. It gave Malaysia an equal voice with the great powers in the General Assembly, access to technical assistance, and a platform to champion issues close to its heart — anti-colonialism, anti-apartheid, and later the question of Palestine.

## How many times has Malaysia sat on the Security Council?

The Security Council is the UN's most powerful organ — the only one that can issue binding decisions, including sanctions and authorisations to use force. It has five permanent veto-wielding members (China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States) and ten non-permanent members elected for two-year terms.

Malaysia has been elected a non-permanent member four times. As a country without veto power, its influence comes from credibility, negotiating expertise and its standing as a voice of the developing world.

| Term | Regional group | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1965 | Asia | First term, just after the era of confrontation |
| 1989–1990 | Asia | The late Cold War era |
| 1999–2000 | Asia | Included the East Timor and Kosovo issues |
| 2015–2016 | Asia-Pacific | Focus on Palestine and terrorism |

In each term, Malaysia tended to use the seat to raise issues of disarmament, Southeast Asian peace and the rights of the Palestinian people.

## What is Malaysia's candidacy for 2036-2037?

On 13 July 2026, Deputy Foreign Minister Datuk Lukanisman Awang Sauni told the Dewan Rakyat that Malaysia had submitted a candidacy to once again sit on the Security Council as a non-permanent member for the **2036-2037** term. If successful in the General Assembly election, Malaysia would serve that two-year term.

The central agenda of this campaign is reform. Lukanisman stressed that the veto power is unjust and should be abolished, or at least should not be usable in cases involving serious violations of international law such as mass killings and crimes against humanity. Malaysia also calls for expanding the membership — both permanent and non-permanent — so that every region gains fairer representation reflecting current geopolitical realities.

## What is Malaysia's record in peacekeeping operations?

Malaysia's contribution to UN peacekeeping forces began almost simultaneously with its membership. In October 1960 — just three years after independence — the Federation of Malaya sent around 3,500 personnel to the UN Operation in the Congo (ONUC). That deployment became the origin of MALBATT (the Malaysian Battalion), a name that would later become synonymous with Malaysia's presence in peacekeeping missions around the world.

According to the United Nations, since 1960 Malaysia has contributed **more than 29,000 peacekeepers** from the Malaysian Armed Forces and the Royal Malaysia Police — making it one of the consistent contributors from Southeast Asia. To support the training and deployment of personnel, Malaysia also established the Malaysian Peacekeeping Centre in 1996.

That commitment continues to this day. Malaysia joined the UN Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) in 2006, and as of 1 August 2025 contributed **830 personnel** to the UNIFIL force of 10,509. Following the Security Council's decision to extend UNIFIL's mandate for the final time, Malaysia is expected to end its participation in the mission by December 2026.

## What is the UN's agency presence in Malaysia?

This relationship is not one-way. The UN is also physically present in Malaysia through its network of agencies, funds and programmes. According to the UN in Malaysia, a total of **21 UN agencies** operate in the country under the UN Country Team, led by the Resident Coordinator.

Among the agencies operating in Malaysia are:

- **UNHCR** — the refugee agency, with a significant presence in Kuala Lumpur;
- **UNICEF**, **UNFPA** and **WHO** — children, population and health;
- **UNDP**, **UNEP**, **UN-Habitat** and **UNDRR** — development, environment and disaster risk reduction;
- **UNU-IIGH** — the UN University International Institute for Global Health, headquartered in Kuala Lumpur;
- **ILO**, **IOM**, **UNODC**, **OHCHR**, **UN Women** and others.

This presence supports national policy in areas such as the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), public health and humanitarian protection.

## What comes next?

The fate of the 2036-2037 candidacy will depend on a General Assembly vote several years before the term begins, and Malaysia will need to win a two-thirds majority of members present and voting. In the meantime, the withdrawal from UNIFIL by the end of 2026 will mark an important shift in the pattern of Malaysia's peacekeeping contributions — although leaders have expressed readiness for new missions if requested.
