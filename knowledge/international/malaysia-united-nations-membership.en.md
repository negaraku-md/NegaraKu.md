Just 17 days after Tunku Abdul Rahman proclaimed "Merdeka" at Stadium Merdeka, the flag of the Federation of Malaya was raised at UN headquarters in New York. On 17 September 1957, the young nation was formally admitted as a member of the United Nations — a step that confirmed its sovereignty on the world stage almost at the same moment as its birth.

Nearly seven decades later, that relationship remains a pillar of the country's foreign policy. Malaysia has sat at the Security Council table four times, sent tens of thousands of peacekeepers to the world's most dangerous conflict zones, and is now once again knocking on the Security Council's door for the 2036-2037 term.

## When and how did Malaysia join the UN?

According to official UN records, Malaysia's date of admission is **17 September 1957**. At that time the country was known as the Federation of Malaya; the membership continued unbroken under the name Malaysia after the larger federation was formed in 1963.

For a country that had only just emerged from colonial administration, UN membership was more than a symbol. It gave Malaysia an equal voice alongside the great powers in the General Assembly, access to technical assistance, and a platform to champion issues close to its heart — anti-colonialism, anti-apartheid, and later the question of Palestine.

## How many times has Malaysia sat on the Security Council?

The Security Council is the UN's most powerful organ — the only one that can issue binding decisions, including sanctions and authorisations to use force. It has five permanent members with veto power (China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States) and ten non-permanent members elected for two-year terms.

Malaysia has been elected a non-permanent member four times. As a country without veto power, its influence comes from its credibility, negotiating expertise and its standing as a voice for the developing world.

| Term | Regional group | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1965 | Asia | First term, just after the Confrontation era |
| 1989–1990 | Asia | Late Cold War era |
| 1999–2000 | Asia | Included the East Timor and Kosovo issues |
| 2015–2016 | Asia-Pacific | Focus on Palestine and terrorism |

In each term, Malaysia tended to use the seat to raise disarmament issues, Southeast Asian security and the rights of the Palestinian people.

## What is Malaysia's 2036-2037 candidacy?

On 13 July 2026, Deputy Foreign Minister Datuk Lukanisman Awang Sauni told the Dewan Rakyat that Malaysia had submitted its candidacy to once again sit on the Security Council as a non-permanent member for the **2036-2037** term. If successful in the General Assembly election, Malaysia will serve that two-year term.

The main agenda of this campaign is reform. Lukanisman stressed that the veto power is unfair and should be abolished, or at least should not be usable in cases involving serious violations of international law such as mass killings and crimes against humanity. Malaysia also calls for expanding the membership — both permanent and non-permanent — so that every region gets fairer representation and reflects current geopolitical realities.

## What is Malaysia's record in peacekeeping operations?

Malaysia's contribution to UN peacekeeping began almost simultaneously with its membership. In October 1960 — only three years after independence — the Federation of Malaya sent roughly 3,500 personnel to the UN Operation in the Congo (ONUC). That deployment became the origin of MALBATT (the Malaysian Battalion), a name that later became synonymous with Malaysia's presence in peacekeeping missions around the world.

According to the United Nations, since 1960 Malaysia has contributed **more than 29,000 peacekeepers** from the Malaysian Armed Forces and the Royal Malaysia Police — making it one of the consistent contributors from Southeast Asia. In the statement by the Minister of Defence Malaysia at the UN Peacekeeping Ministerial 2025, it was stated that since 1960 Malaysia has taken part in **more than 38 peacekeeping operations**, with 876 Malaysian peacekeepers currently serving in five peacekeeping operations and one special political mission. As of 31 January 2025, the UN ranked Malaysia 21st out of 120 uniformed-personnel-contributing countries. To support the training and deployment of personnel, Malaysia also established the Malaysian Peacekeeping Centre in 1996.

This commitment continues to this day. Malaysia joined the UN Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) in 2006. As of 1 July 2026 it contributes **516 personnel** to the UNIFIL force of 7,448 people, down from 830 personnel (in a force of 10,509 people) on 1 August 2025. Through Resolution 2790 (2025), the Security Council extended UNIFIL's mandate for the final time until 31 December 2026, with an orderly and safe withdrawal beginning from that date and expected to be completed within one year — that is, by the end of 2027.

## What is the presence of UN agencies in Malaysia?

The relationship is not one-way. The UN is also physically present in Malaysia through its network of agencies, funds and programmes. According to the UN in Malaysia, a total of **21 UN agencies** operate in the country under the UN Country Team, led by the Resident Coordinator.

Among the agencies operating in Malaysia are:

- **UNHCR** — the refugee agency, with a large presence in Kuala Lumpur;
- **UNICEF**, **UNFPA** and **WHO** — children, population and health;
- **UNDP**, **UNEP**, **UN-Habitat** and **UNDRR** — development, environment and disaster risk reduction;
- **UNU-IIGH** — the UN University International Institute for Global Health, headquartered in Kuala Lumpur;
- **ILO**, **IOM**, **UNODC**, **OHCHR**, **UN Women** and others.

This presence supports national policy in areas such as the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), public health and humanitarian protection.

## What comes next?

The fate of the 2036-2037 candidacy will depend on a General Assembly vote several years before the term begins, and Malaysia will need to win the support of two-thirds of the states present and voting. In the meantime, the withdrawal from UNIFIL — whose mandate ends on 31 December 2026 with the withdrawal expected to be completed by the end of 2027 — will mark an important shift in the pattern of Malaysia's peacekeeping contributions, even though leaders have expressed readiness for new missions if requested.
