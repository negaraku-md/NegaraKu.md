Every 1 January, a Southeast Asian country inherits the region's most influential seat — without a vote, without a campaign, purely by alphabetical order.

That is the deliberate design of the ASEAN chairmanship system: a simple rule that avoids power struggles and gives every member its turn to set the regional agenda.

## Who becomes Chair, and how is the turn determined?

The rule is enshrined in Article 31(1) of the ASEAN Charter: "The Chairmanship of ASEAN shall rotate annually, based on the alphabetical order of the English names of Member States."

There is no annual negotiation or ballot. After one country ends its term on 31 December, the next country in alphabetical order takes over automatically. This is the order for the 10 member states at the time the Charter was signed in Singapore on 20 November 2007 — to be distinguished from ASEAN's five founding members in 1967, since membership had grown to 10 by 2007:

| Turn | Country (English name) |
|--------|------------------------|
| 1 | Brunei Darussalam |
| 2 | Cambodia |
| 3 | Indonesia |
| 4 | Lao PDR |
| 5 | Malaysia |
| 6 | Myanmar |
| 7 | Philippines |
| 8 | Singapore |
| 9 | Thailand |
| 10 | Viet Nam |

Timor-Leste, admitted as the 11th member on 26 October 2025, slots in alphabetically after Thailand and before Viet Nam.

## What does the Chair country lead?

Article 31(2) provides that within a calendar year, the chairing country leads five groups of ASEAN bodies:

- The ASEAN Summit and related summits
- The ASEAN Coordinating Council
- The three ASEAN Community Councils
- Relevant ASEAN Sectoral Ministerial Bodies and senior officials, where appropriate
- The Committee of Permanent Representatives

Article 32, in turn, gives the chairing country a broader role — among other things, advancing ASEAN's interests, ensuring "the centrality of ASEAN", responding swiftly to crises, and representing ASEAN in relations with external partners.

## Can the alphabetical turn be skipped?

It can — by consensus, not unilateral exception. After Malaysia (2025), the next turn should have fallen to Myanmar. However, following the military coup of February 2021 and the ASEAN restrictions limiting Myanmar's participation in summits and ministerial meetings to "non-political" representatives, Myanmar relinquished its 2026 turn.

The Philippines — originally scheduled to hold the chair in 2027 — agreed in 2023 to move forward and take over 2026. This shift was confirmed by mutual agreement, keeping the alphabetical principle as the default while allowing adjustment when circumstances demand.

## Recent examples

Malaysia held the chair in 2025 under the theme **"Inclusivity and Sustainability"** — a year that also marked the 10th anniversary of the ASEAN Community's formal establishment. Malaysia had previously chaired ASEAN in 2015, the year the ASEAN Community was formally established (effective 31 December 2015).

The Philippines takes over on 1 January 2026 under the theme **"Navigating Our Future, Together"**, driving ASEAN's priorities throughout that year as chair.

## What comes next

With Timor-Leste now in the cycle, the alphabetical order will continue to determine turns in the years ahead unless members agree to adjust it, as happened for 2026. To understand the full legal framework behind this system, refer to Chapter X (Articles 31–32) of the ASEAN Charter — the official text binding on all 11 member states.
