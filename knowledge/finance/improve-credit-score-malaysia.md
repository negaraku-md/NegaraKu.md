A good credit standing is quietly one of the most valuable things you own: it decides whether you get a loan, at what rate, and how much. The good news is that it is built from a handful of habits, and almost all of it is within your control. The first step is understanding that "your credit score" is not one number.

## There is no single score

Two different things sit behind a lending decision in Malaysia:

- **CCRIS** — the **Central Credit Reference Information System**, run by **Bank Negara**. It is a **factual record** of your facilities and your repayment conduct over the **last 12 months**. It carries **no score**, makes no judgement, and — a myth worth killing — **Bank Negara blacklists no one.**
- **Credit reporting agencies (CRAs)** — **private** companies that compile a report and a **score**. The best known is **CTOS**; others include **Experian** (formerly RAM Credit Information, RAMCI) and **CRIF**. Around ten CRAs are registered with the Ministry of Finance under the **Credit Reporting Agencies Act 2010**, and a bank may pull from any of them — so there is no one universal score.

For what each system is and how they differ, see [credit reporting: CCRIS and CTOS](/finance/credit-reporting-ccris-ctos-malaysia). This guide is about **raising** them.

## What drives the CTOS score

CTOS states its score runs from **300 to 850** (higher is better) and is built from five factors:

| Factor | CTOS-stated weight | What it rewards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment history | ~45% | Paying on time; arrears, defaults and legal records hurt |
| Amounts owed / utilisation | ~20% | Using a low share of your available credit |
| Credit mix | ~14% | Managing different credit types responsibly |
| New credit | ~14% | Few recent applications |
| Length of credit history | ~7% | Older, well-managed accounts |

These weights are **CTOS's own and can change**, so treat them as direction, not gospel. But the direction is clear: **payment history and utilisation dominate.**

## The moves that actually raise it

1. **Pay on time, every time.** This is 45 percent of the score — and because CCRIS shows a rolling **12-month** window, about **a year of clean conduct rebuilds your record.** Set up standing instructions for loan instalments and at least card minimums.
2. **Lower your credit utilisation.** Keep card balances low relative to limits — "amounts owed" is 20 percent, and CTOS says the less of your available credit you use, the better. (One nuance: CTOS notes carrying a **small** balance can help more than carrying none.)
3. **Space out applications.** Each loan or card application leaves a footprint; several in a short window signals risk under the "new credit" factor.
4. **Keep older accounts open.** Closing your oldest card shortens your average account age and can raise utilisation on what remains — hurting two factors at once.
5. **Settle defaults and lodge the release letter.** Clearing a default, legal case or AKPK matter only helps once the **documentary proof** (settlement or release letter) reaches the agency, which then updates the record.
6. **Keep a healthy mix** — but do not open accounts purely to diversify; CTOS warns against that.

## Check your reports — free

- **CCRIS:** free through Bank Negara's **eCCRIS** self-service channel. It shows your facilities, 12-month conduct, any special-attention account, and recent applications — no score, no opinion.
- **CTOS / Experian:** get your report from the agency directly.

Pull them **before any major application** (mortgage, car, card), and cross-check CCRIS against your CRA file — an error in one does not appear in the other. Look for arrears you have cleared, accounts that are not yours, and settled loans still showing as outstanding.

## Fixing an error

You have the **right under the Credit Reporting Agencies Act 2010** to access your report and **dispute inaccurate data**:

- **With a CRA (such as CTOS):** lodge a dispute with supporting documents; CTOS states it updates a record within about **14 working days** of receiving the proof.
- **With CCRIS:** Bank Negara **does not alter the data itself.** You lodge a **data-verification request** (through eCCRIS or the prescribed form), and the **reporting bank** must correct a genuine error by sending the amendment to the credit bureau. The fix is **documentary** — settlement letters, discharge orders — not a phone call, and CCRIS updates monthly, so allow time.

## How long bad records last

- **CCRIS conduct:** the rolling **12-month** window means clean payments progressively displace old arrears.
- **Bankruptcy:** two different "two-year" rules — CTOS states a bankruptcy record is removed about **two years from the date of full settlement** (with an Insolvency Department release letter), while the Ministry of Finance's Registrar states an agency may not **disclose** bankruptcy more than **two years after discharge**. They anchor on different events; keep them distinct.
- **Legal / litigation records:** there is **no officially confirmed removal period** for an unresolved legal record — do not assume it simply drops off after a set time; resolve and lodge the settlement.
- **AKPK Debt Management Programme:** enrolment is **visible** (it shows under a special-attention status while you are on the programme), and completing it demonstrates responsibility; see [debt consolidation and restructuring](/finance/debt-consolidation-restructuring-malaysia).

## Myths to drop

- **Bank Negara blacklists no one.** CCRIS is factual; the lender decides.
- **Closing all your cards is not automatically good.** It can shorten your history and raise utilisation on remaining limits — both hurt the score.
- **Checking your own report does not hurt your score.** Pulling your own CCRIS or CTOS is not a lender application and leaves no adverse mark.

## What's next

Pull your CCRIS and a CRA report today, fix any error, and start a clean 12-month run — standing instructions make it automatic. Keep card balances low and resist new applications before a big one. If a loan was recently rejected, read [why loans get rejected](/finance/why-loan-rejected-malaysia) and check your [debt service ratio](/finance/debt-service-ratio-dsr-malaysia); if debt is the underlying problem, start with free [AKPK help](/finance/debt-consolidation-restructuring-malaysia) rather than a paid "credit repair" service.
