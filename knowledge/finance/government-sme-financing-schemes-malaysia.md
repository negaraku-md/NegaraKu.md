Every Budget, the government announces billions in financing for small and medium enterprises. The money is real — but it is spread across half a dozen agencies, each with its own schemes, and most SME owners never find the one meant for them. Here is the map.

## Four ways the government helps you borrow

Government support for SME financing works through four distinct mechanisms. Knowing which one you are dealing with tells you where to apply:

1. **Low-cost funds lent through banks** — Bank Negara makes money available; you borrow it from a participating bank at a subsidised rate.
2. **Guarantees** — a government body promises to cover most of a bank's loss if you default, so the bank approves a loan it otherwise would not.
3. **Direct financing from agencies** — bodies like TEKUN and MARA lend to you directly, usually small amounts to micro and small businesses.
4. **Grants and advisory** — money you do not repay, or help rather than cash, mainly from SME Corp.

## 1. Bank Negara's Fund for SMEs

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) runs a suite of revolving funds for SMEs, but it does **not** lend to you directly. The funds are channelled through **participating financial institutions** — commercial banks, Islamic banks and development banks — which still assess your application on their own credit criteria. You apply to the bank; if approved, the bank draws on the fund to give you a below-market rate.

The funds target specific needs. The **Agrofood Facility**, for instance, finances food production up to **RM5 million** at up to **3.75% a year** (including the guarantee fee) over up to eight years. Others include the **High Tech and Green Facility** (strategic and green sectors), the **SME Automation and Digitalisation Facility**, the **Low Carbon Transition Facility**, the **Micro Enterprises Facility**, the **SME Stabilisation Relief Facility** for firms hit by disruption, and **RAFt** for flood relief and resilience. Exact caps and rates vary by facility and by bank — check the current terms on **bnm.gov.my/funds4sme** or a bank's product disclosure sheet.

## 2. Guarantees: SJPP and CGC

If your business is viable but short on collateral, a guarantee is often what unlocks the loan. Two bodies provide them, and in both cases **your bank arranges the guarantee — you do not apply to the guarantor.**

**SJPP (Syarikat Jaminan Pembiayaan Perniagaan)** is owned by the Ministry of Finance and guarantees SME financing from banks. Its Working Capital Guarantee Scheme (WCGS) and its variants cover a large share of the loan:

| SJPP scheme | Financing | Guarantee coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WCGS (general) | RM100k – RM10m | 70% |
| WCGS – Start-up | RM100k – RM500k | 70% |
| WCGS – Bumiputera | RM100k – RM3m | 80% |
| WCGS – Export | RM100k – RM10m | 80% |
| WCGS – Women | RM100k – RM1m | 80% |
| Automation & Digital Guarantee Scheme | RM100k – RM10m | 80% |

SJPP also runs the government's MADANI guarantee schemes, where coverage can reach up to 90% on the first tranche of financing.

**CGC (Credit Guarantee Corporation Malaysia Berhad)**, established in 1972 and majority-owned by Bank Negara, is the other guarantor. Beyond guarantees — often as portfolio or wholesale arrangements struck with a bank to cover a whole book of SME loans — CGC also does **direct financing** itself (including Bumiputera contract-financing products) and runs **imSME**, the online marketplace below.

## 3. Direct financing: TEKUN and MARA

Two agencies lend to smaller businesses directly, largely serving Bumiputera entrepreneurs:

- **TEKUN Nasional** provides Shariah-compliant **micro-financing**. Its flagship TEKUN Niaga scheme runs from about **RM1,000 to RM100,000**, with youth and community schemes alongside it (including a separate scheme for the Indian community).
- **MARA (Majlis Amanah Rakyat)** finances Bumiputera businesses in trade, services and manufacturing through its **Business Financing Scheme (SPiM)**, commonly up to around **RM500,000**, plus professional and premises-purchase schemes.

Beyond these, **SME Bank** — one of Malaysia's development financial institutions — offers larger programme financing, including facilities for women-owned (MySMELady), Bumiputera and green-transition businesses.

## 4. Grants and advisory: SME Corp

Not every form of help is a loan. **SME Corp Malaysia** is the central coordinating agency and is mainly an advisory and grants body, not a lender. Its **Business Accelerator Programme (BAP)** has historically offered **matching grants** plus advisory — for example the version for Indian-owned enterprises offers advisory and matching grants up to around RM100,000. SME Corp also runs **SMEinfo**, a one-stop information portal listing programmes, financing and the official SME definition.

## Where to start

With so many doors, the efficient move is to use an aggregator rather than knock on each:

- **imSME (imsme.com.my)** — CGC's online loan-referral marketplace. Enter your needs once and it matches you to financing products across multiple banks and development banks. This is the closest thing to a single application front door.
- **SMEinfo (smeinfo.com.my)** — SME Corp's information directory of programmes and financing.
- **myKNP (bnm.gov.my/MyKNP)** — Bank Negara's free financing-advisory service, delivered for SMEs by CGC. It is especially worth using if a bank has already **rejected** you: it helps you understand the reason, improve your eligibility, and find an alternative.

## What's next

Shortlist the scheme that matches your sector and size, then apply through the relevant bank or agency — remembering that a bank still credit-assesses you even on a government-backed facility, so a clean credit record and documented cash flow matter. If an application has been [rejected](/finance/why-loan-rejected-malaysia), start with myKNP. And because schemes and allocations shift with every annual Budget, always confirm the current terms on the official portal before you apply.
