A profitable business can still run out of cash — because the money is sitting in invoices your customers will not pay for another 30, 60 or 90 days. Invoice financing and factoring exist to bridge exactly that gap: they turn an unpaid invoice into cash today. Here is how the products differ, what they cost, and how to tell a regulated provider from the rest.

## What it is, in one line

You sell on credit; a financier advances you most of the invoice value now and recovers it when your customer pays. You get cash flow; the financier earns a charge for the wait and the risk.

It only works for **business-to-business (B2B)** invoices — goods or services already delivered to another business that will pay later. It does not apply to consumer or cash sales.

## Factoring, discounting, invoice financing: the real difference

The three terms overlap, and Malaysian providers use them loosely, so ignore the labels and look at **two questions: who collects the debt, and is your customer told?**

- **Factoring** — the financier effectively buys your receivable and usually **takes over collecting** it, administering your sales ledger. It is typically **disclosed**: your customer is notified to pay the financier directly. Often it is whole-book (you assign all, or a defined class, of your invoices).
- **Invoice discounting** — you **keep collecting** and the arrangement is **confidential**: your customer is not told and keeps paying you, and you repay the financier. This is usually offered only to stronger, larger businesses.
- **Invoice financing** — the **umbrella term** for both, and increasingly the name for **selective, single-invoice** financing through an online platform, where you finance one invoice at a time rather than the whole book.

The practical point: terminology is not standardised here, so when you compare offers, ask who collects, whether your buyer is notified, and whether you must assign all invoices or can pick.

## How much, and what it costs

- **Advance rate:** typically **70 to 90 percent** of the invoice value upfront; the remaining slice, less fees, is released when your customer pays.
- **Cost:** two components — a **discount/financing charge** on the advanced amount (interest-equivalent, quoted per annum or per 30-day period) plus a **per-invoice service or handling fee.**
- **Tenure:** short, matched to your invoice terms — commonly **30 to 120 days** per invoice.
- **What gets you approved:** underwriting leans on **your customers' creditworthiness**, not only yours. A young SME invoicing strong corporate or government buyers can often qualify where a conventional [term loan](/finance/business-loan-facility-types-malaysia) would decline it.

These figures are market norms, not official rates — confirm the specifics on the provider's product disclosure sheet.

## Recourse vs non-recourse

This decides who carries the risk that your customer never pays:

- **Recourse** (the common form in Malaysia) — if your customer defaults, **you must buy the invoice back or repay**. Cheaper, but you keep the default risk.
- **Non-recourse** — the financier **absorbs the buyer's default loss** (within defined events), effectively bundling credit protection. It costs more.

Read which one you are signing: a "financing" that can turn into a repayment demand is a very different product from one that protects you.

## Who offers it

- **Banks (conventional and Islamic).** Invoice financing is a mainstream bank trade-finance product; the Islamic version is described below.
- **Development financial institutions.** [EXIM Bank](/finance/development-financial-institutions-malaysia) finances exporters' **receivables** and runs supply-chain financing for approved invoices — receivables financing, though not branded "factoring." See [trade finance](/finance/trade-finance-malaysia) for the export angle.
- **SC-registered peer-to-peer (P2P) platforms.** The fintech channel for selective invoice financing. Operators must be **registered with the Securities Commission Malaysia** as Recognised Market Operators — running such a platform without SC authorisation is an offence. Several SC-registered operators offer invoice financing (the SC's own SARANA scheme lists current ones); always check the SC's live register before committing.
- **Credit Guarantee Corporation (CGC).** Note what CGC is *not*: it does not advance against your invoices. It **guarantees** facilities (including invoice financing-i) so a bank will extend them — a credit-enhancement layer, covered in [government SME financing schemes](/finance/government-sme-financing-schemes-malaysia).

## The Islamic version

The Shariah-compliant structure is **factoring-i / receivables financing on Bai' al-Dayn** — the sale of a debt (your receivable) to the bank at a discount. It is mainstream in Malaysia and offered by Islamic banks and Islamic windows.

One honest caveat: the discounted sale of debt (*bai' al-dayn*) is **accepted in Malaysia** — permitted by the national Shariah Advisory Council for defined instruments — but it is **not universally accepted**; many scholars elsewhere permit transferring a debt only at face value and use other structures. If cross-border Shariah consistency matters to you, raise it with your bank.

## Is it regulated?

A point worth stating plainly, because it is counter-intuitive: **factoring as a standalone activity is largely outside Bank Negara Malaysia's licensing perimeter** — leasing and factoring companies are not supervised as financial institutions the way banks are. What *is* regulated is the **channel**:

- a **bank** offering invoice financing is a **BNM-licensed bank**;
- a **P2P invoice-financing platform** is **SC-regulated**.

A standalone non-bank "factoring company" may sit outside both. So before you sign, confirm your provider is a licensed bank or an SC-registered operator — not an unregulated lender wearing a fintech label.

## The catches to weigh

- **Cost vs an overdraft.** The all-in charge can exceed an [overdraft or term-loan rate](/finance/business-loan-facility-types-malaysia); it is cheap only when it unlocks sales you could not otherwise fund.
- **Confidentiality and your customer relationship.** Disclosed factoring means your buyer deals with the factor's collections, which can signal cash-flow stress; confidential discounting avoids that but is harder to get.
- **Recourse risk and personal guarantees.** Facilities often require directors' personal guarantees and assignment of the whole receivables book.
- **It is not full value.** An advance of 70 to 90 percent is not the whole invoice, and it relies on creditworthy B2B debtors.

## What's next

If a few large, slow-paying customers are choking your cash flow, price invoice financing against an overdraft and a [government-guaranteed facility](/finance/government-sme-financing-schemes-malaysia) before deciding. Confirm your provider is a licensed bank or SC-registered platform, read whether it is recourse, and check the all-in cost on the product disclosure sheet.
