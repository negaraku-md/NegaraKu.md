Owning a slice of Maybank or Tenaga starts with two accounts, not one — and neither of them is a bank account. Before you can buy a single share on Bursa Malaysia, you need a place to *hold* the shares and a place to *place the order*. Here is how the two fit together, and what each trade actually costs.

## What do I actually need to start?

Two separate accounts:

- A **CDS account** (Central Depository System) — an electronic account, run by Bursa Malaysia Depository, that records the shares you own. Buy a stock and it lands here; sell it and it leaves from here.
- A **trading account** with a **broker** — formally an Authorised Depository Agent (ADA). This is the firm that actually routes your buy and sell orders to the market.

The CDS account is the vault; the trading account is the counter you place orders at. You need both, and the trading account is always opened with a licensed broker.

## Who can open a CDS account, and how?

Any **individual who has reached the age of 18** as at the application date can open a direct CDS account, as either a beneficial owner or an authorised nominee. There are two routes:

1. **Bursa Anywhere app** — Bursa Malaysia's official mobile app, available on the Apple App Store and Google Play. You open the CDS account yourself, then still link a trading account with a broker.
2. **Through a broker (ADA)** — apply in person or online with your chosen brokerage, which opens the CDS and trading accounts together.

Opening a CDS account on Bursa Anywhere carries **no joining or annual maintenance fee**. During registration you upload two documents:

| Document | What to provide |
| --- | --- |
| MyKad | Clear photos of the **front and back** |
| Bank proof | A bank statement or passbook (PDF or screenshot) showing the bank name, logo, account holder name and account number |

Registering on the app does not lock you out of the branch: you can still perform CDS transactions at your broker's office whenever you prefer.

## What will each trade cost me?

Three charges apply, and each is levied on **both** the buy and the sell:

| Charge | Rate | Cap / notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brokerage commission | Set by your broker | The largest component for most retail trades; brokerage on Bursa-listed shares is currently exempt from service tax |
| Clearing fee | 0.03% of transaction value | Capped at RM1,000 per contract |
| Stamp duty | RM1 for every RM1,000 (or part) of value — about 0.1% | Capped at RM1,000 per contract |

So a RM10,000 buy incurs roughly RM3 in clearing fees and RM10 in stamp duty, plus whatever your broker charges in commission — and you pay a comparable set again when you sell. Because these costs repeat on both legs, frequent small trades are proportionately expensive.

## What's next

Compare brokers before you commit: commission rates are set by each firm, so the platform, research access and minimum charges vary widely.
