Missing repayments feels like a trap with only one exit — default, and then bankruptcy. It is not. Malaysia has several structured routes out of over-indebtedness, most of them designed to keep you paying on terms you can actually manage. The trick is matching the route to your situation, and knowing that the best first step costs nothing.

## Start with AKPK — it is free

The **Agensi Kaunseling dan Pengurusan Kredit (AKPK)** was set up by Bank Negara Malaysia in **2006** precisely to help individuals in debt, and all of its services are **free**. That free-of-charge point is also your scam detector: anyone who charges a fee to "enrol you in AKPK" or to negotiate with it on your behalf is running a scam — approach AKPK directly.

AKPK offers three things: financial **education** (the POWER! programme), free one-on-one **counselling** (available even if you never enrol in a programme), and the **Debt Management Programme (DMP)**.

**The Debt Management Programme** works like this:

- It is for **individuals** with a **regular income** and positive disposable income after expenses — not businesses, and not bankrupts.
- It covers **unsecured consumer debt** — credit cards, personal loans, overdrafts — owed to **licensed financial institutions**. It does not cover debts to unlicensed moneylenders or to friends and family.
- AKPK reviews your finances and **negotiates a single restructured repayment plan** with all your creditors. You then make **one monthly payment to AKPK**, which distributes it to them.
- The plan can mean **lower instalments, waived late fees, or reduced interest** — but it does **not reduce the principal** you owe. It is restructured, not forgiven.
- Enrolment is **recorded on CCRIS**, and while you are on the programme you are generally **restricted from taking new credit**. Completing it demonstrates financial responsibility to future lenders.

## Deal directly with your bank: rescheduling and restructuring

You do not always need a programme. You can ask your bank to rework a loan — a normal step, not an admission of crisis:

- **Rescheduling** changes the **repayment timeline** — for example stretching the tenure to lower the monthly instalment (which increases total interest paid over the life of the loan).
- **Restructuring** is a **structural change to the terms** — for instance converting a revolving overdraft into a fixed-instalment term loan.

On approval the bank issues a supplemental letter of offer with the new terms. Note that under Bank Negara's credit-risk rules, a rescheduled or restructured retail loan generally needs about **12 consecutive months of on-time payments** before the "R&R" flag clears from your CCRIS record.

## Debt consolidation loans (a commercial product)

A **debt-consolidation loan** rolls several debts into one new facility — usually a personal loan — leaving you with a single monthly instalment and a clear payoff date. Unlike AKPK, this is a **commercial product** you must still qualify for on your credit standing, and it is not free.

It can help — one simpler payment, and a potentially lower total cost **if** the new rate and tenure are genuinely better. But the risks are real: the temptation to re-borrow on the freed-up cards, fees that can erase the interest saving, and a stretched tenure that quietly increases total interest. It works only if the new instalment truly fits your budget.

## For businesses: SDRS and CDRC

Companies have their own channels:

- The **Small Debt Resolution Scheme (SDRS)**, **transferred from Bank Negara to AKPK in 2020**, helps **viable SMEs** struggling to service financing — especially those with loans across several banks — to reschedule or restructure without legal action.
- The **Corporate Debt Restructuring Committee (CDRC)**, a Bank Negara platform, handles **larger corporate debt** through voluntary, out-of-court mediation between a business and its financial creditors. Current criteria: **aggregate debt of RM10 million or more** and **at least two financial creditors**. Once lenders holding 75% of the value approve a scheme, it binds all participating lenders.

## When restructuring is not enough

If voluntary routes cannot resolve the debt, formal, court-supervised options remain: for individuals, a **Voluntary Arrangement** under the Insolvency Act 1967 as an alternative to bankruptcy; for companies, a **Scheme of Arrangement** or **Judicial Management** under the Companies Act 2016. These are last resorts, but they exist.

## What's next

If you are an individual, start with free AKPK counselling before you miss more payments — it costs nothing and opens the DMP if you need it. If you run a business, approach AKPK's SME channel or, for larger debt, ask your main lender about CDRC. Whatever route you take, avoid the trap of borrowing from an [unlicensed moneylender](/finance/licensed-moneylenders-malaysia) to plug the gap — that deepens the hole. And once you are stable, the habits that rebuild your profile are the same ones that fix a [rejected loan application](/finance/why-loan-rejected-malaysia).
