Many people have seen the "RAHMAH" umbrella logo in the window of an eatery or
at a weekend sales tent, but fewer know that RAHMAH is not a single programme.
It is in fact a **brand umbrella** that shelters several different
cost-of-living initiatives — and it is also frequently confused with a cash
aid programme that sounds almost identical by name.

## What Payung RAHMAH Is

Payung RAHMAH (the RAHMAH Umbrella) is the branding framework for a group of
initiatives aimed at easing the cost of living, run by the **Ministry of
Domestic Trade and Cost of Living (KPDN)**. It was introduced in **January
2023** under the Unity Government as part of efforts to tackle the pressure of
rising prices on lower-income groups. Rather than aid paid directly to
individuals, the RAHMAH approach focuses on **prices at the retail level** —
that is, making food and essential goods more affordable at the point where
consumers buy them.

One important thing to draw out from the start: Payung RAHMAH is **not**
[Sumbangan Tunai Rahmah (STR)](/en/money-daily-life/str-cash-aid). STR is a
cash aid channelled by the Ministry of Finance straight into the bank accounts
of eligible recipients. Payung RAHMAH, by contrast, gives no cash — it provides
cheap food, discounted goods sales and baskets of essentials under KPDN. Both
do share the word "Rahmah", but they are managed by different ministries and
work in different ways.

The RAHMAH logo itself is subject to official KPDN guidelines, and only
approved programmes and premises may use it — this helps consumers tell
legitimate offers apart from those merely imitating the brand.

## Menu RAHMAH

**Menu RAHMAH** is probably the one most people recognise. It is a **balanced
meal set** — a lunch or dinner dish — sold at a price of **no more than
RM5.00**, already inclusive of a drink and tax, according to KPDN's official
portal.

Participation is **voluntary**. Food operators — restaurants, cafes, stalls,
fast-food outlets and food courts — can join by registering on the official
portal **menurahmah.kpdn.gov.my**. Once registered, a premises is listed in the
**Menu Directory**, which can be filtered by state or type of premises so that
consumers can find a nearby location offering the RM5.00 set.

Because the price is borne by the operators themselves on a voluntary basis
(not a cash subsidy to traders), the content and type of dish varies from one
premises to another. Consumers do not need to register, carry a card, or prove
any eligibility to buy — it is enough to visit a participating premises and
order the set.

## Jualan RAHMAH MADANI (PJRM)

The second pillar is the **Jualan RAHMAH MADANI Programme (PJRM)**, launched on
**11 January 2023**. This is a **year-round sale of essential goods** at prices
lower than local market prices — according to KPDN, the saving is roughly **10
to 30 percent**.

It is important to understand that PJRM goods are **discounted** goods, not
goods directly subsidised by the government. Participating suppliers —
supermarkets, wholesalers, retailers and cooperatives — offer lower prices
through the programme. Goods commonly sold include chicken, fish and seafood,
eggs, cooking oil, flour, fresh produce, personal care and household cleaning
items, disposable diapers, school necessities and medicines.

PJRM is designed to reach across the country: it covers **all 600 State
Legislative Assembly (DUN) constituencies** as well as 40 zones in the Federal
Territories, with a target of **at least two sales a month** in each DUN. The
sales are run in three forms — sales inside premises, sales in open-area tents,
and **mobile sales**. The sales schedule (calendar) is updated on KPDN's
official portal so that consumers can plan ahead; further details can also be
obtained through KPDN's Business, Company and Direct Selling Development
Division.

Like Menu RAHMAH, consumers do not need to apply or qualify for anything — these
sales are open to the public at the announced locations and dates.

## Bakul RAHMAH

The third pillar, **Bakul RAHMAH**, works differently from the other two. It is
an initiative that provides **baskets of daily essential goods** — such as rice,
sugar, cooking oil, wheat flour, condensed milk, vermicelli and eggs — donated
monthly to target groups.

What sets Bakul RAHMAH apart is that it **receives no government allocation**.
Instead, the baskets are sponsored by **private strategic partners** through
their respective corporate social responsibility (CSR) programmes. KPDN acts as
the coordinator that brings corporate sponsors together with recipients in
need, while the cost of the goods is borne by the companies involved. This model
has also been expanded through private-sector and industry participation
initiatives under the same umbrella.

## Goals and its place in cost-of-living policy

The overall goal of Payung RAHMAH is to ease the burden of the cost of living,
especially for lower-income groups, at a time when the prices of food and
essential goods are rising. In the early stage, the focus was on the poorest
groups, before being broadened so that the benefits are open to more consumers.
KPDN also conducts an official impact study to assess public acceptance and the
delivery effectiveness of these initiatives.

Payung RAHMAH is one layer in the government's mix of measures tackling the cost
of living. It complements — rather than replaces — other measures such as
[STR cash assistance](/en/money-daily-life/str-cash-aid),
[fuel subsidies](/en/economy/fuel-subsidies), and
[subsidy rationalisation](/en/economy/subsidy-rationalisation-padu) efforts that
target aid at those who need it most. While subsidies and cash aid work at the
macro and household-income level, RAHMAH acts at the shop counter — making a
single plate of food or a single trolley of essentials cheaper on the day the
consumer buys it. Its real effect on household spending depends on how widely
the programme is reached compared with the overall rate of
[inflation](/en/economy/inflation-cpi).

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing Payung RAHMAH with STR.** RAHMAH (KPDN) is a retail price/goods
  programme; STR (Ministry of Finance) is a cash payment. The names are
  similar, but they are entirely different.
- **Thinking Menu RAHMAH is compulsory for all eateries.** Participation is
  voluntary — not every restaurant joins, and dishes vary from premises to
  premises.
- **Assuming Jualan RAHMAH goods are government-subsidised.** The prices are
  discounts from participating suppliers, not a direct cash subsidy.
- **Looking for the sales schedule on unofficial sources.** The authentic PJRM
  calendar and locations are updated on KPDN's official portal; dates circulated
  on social media may already be out of date.

## How to Access It

No application is required to buy under any of these pillars. For Menu RAHMAH,
find a participating premises through the Menu Directory at
**menurahmah.kpdn.gov.my**. For Jualan RAHMAH MADANI, check the sales calendar
on the official portal **kpdn.gov.my** for dates and locations in your area.
Bakul RAHMAH, meanwhile, is channelled through corporate sponsors to target
groups, so it is not something bought by the general public. In all cases,
KPDN's official portal is the most reliable source for current prices, schedules
and conditions, as the details are updated from time to time.
