A Malaysian citizen walks into a branch with a MyKad, signs a form, and walks out
with a working account in under half an hour. A foreigner on a tourist visa walks
into the same branch the same afternoon, passport in hand — and gets turned away.
Same bank, same counter, completely different outcome. The difference isn't the
teller's mood; it's a document checklist that depends entirely on who you are and
what pass you hold.

## If you're a citizen or permanent resident, this part is short

For Malaysians and permanent residents, the mandatory document is simple: your
**original MyKad**. That's it for most straightforward savings or current
accounts. According to CIMB's own published requirements, additional proof —
an employment letter, EPF statement, payslip, university ID, or recent bank
statements — is only requested in specific situations, such as opening a
higher-tier account, or when the address you want on file differs from the one
printed on your MyKad and the bank needs a tenancy agreement or utility bill to
confirm it.

Minors are the other exception: opening an account for someone under 18 needs
their birth certificate plus a parent or guardian's own MyKad.

## If you're a foreigner, the checklist branches by pass type

This is where most guides stop at "bring your passport" — which is true but
incomplete. Banks tie the supporting documents to the *specific reason* you're
in Malaysia, because that's what the pass on your passport actually certifies.
Based on CIMB's published foreigner requirements:

| You are | Core requirement | What else is typically asked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Working (Employment Pass / work visa) | Passport valid 6+ months, pass valid 6+ months | Employment letter; proof of monthly salary above the bank's threshold (CIMB states RM10,000) |
| Studying (Student Pass) | Passport valid 6+ months, pass valid 6+ months | Student ID card, enrolment/admission letter |
| Dependant (Dependant Pass) | Passport valid 6+ months, pass valid 6+ months | Marriage certificate, where applicable |
| MM2H participant | Passport, approved social visit pass | Conditional approval letter from the immigration authority, proof of Malaysian residential address |
| Singapore citizen | Singapore IC or driving licence | Proof of residential address matching the state of the branch |

The **Employment Pass**, **Student Pass** and **Dependant Pass** are official
categories issued by the Malaysian Immigration Department (Jabatan Imigresen
Malaysia) — the bank isn't inventing these labels; it's mapping its own
paperwork onto the immigration status you already hold. An Employment Pass
holder, for instance, can already sponsor a spouse or children under 18 for
their own Dependant Pass, and each of those dependants can then open their own
account against that pass.

Two details trip people up. First, the **six-month validity rule**: a passport
or pass with less than six months left before expiry is grounds for the bank
to decline the application outright, regardless of how much time you've
actually lived in Malaysia. Second, the bank may still ask for documents
beyond this list or run extra checks — CIMB's own page is explicit that
"additional supporting documents not listed" can be requested, and other
banks (Maybank, Public Bank, Hong Leong, RHB) publish broadly similar but not
identical lists, so the safest move is always to check the specific bank's
current page before a branch visit.

## Why a tourist visa doesn't work

A short-term social visit pass — the standard tourist entry stamp — certifies
that you're allowed to visit, not that you have an ongoing tie to Malaysia the
bank can verify: no employer, no institution, no long-term address, no
renewable status. None of the document categories above have a slot for it.
That's a deliberate, structural gap, not an oversight any one branch can wave
away — which is why "just try a different bank" rarely works for a tourist
pass, though it sometimes does work for a borderline case within one of the
categories above (a Dependant Pass close to renewal, for instance).

## The safety net applies to you regardless of nationality

Once the account is open, the money in it isn't just sitting there on trust.
**Perbadanan Insurans Deposit Malaysia (PIDM)** insures eligible deposits at
member banks up to **RM250,000 per depositor per member bank**, covering both
principal and accrued interest. According to PIDM's own FAQ, this protection
is automatic, free, and — critically for a foreigner reading this — applies
"regardless of [the depositor's] place of residence or nationality." You do
not need to apply for it, and it isn't a product you can buy more of; it's a
statutory backstop that comes with any eligible deposit account at any member
bank.

## Digital account opening exists — but usually not for you if you're new to Malaysia

Bank Negara Malaysia has a digital on-boarding policy aimed at letting
accounts be opened remotely, and several banks now offer e-KYC flows —
typically a selfie matched against your identity document with liveness
detection — for straightforward citizen applications. In practice, though,
every foreigner-facing requirement described above still routes through an
in-branch visit: CIMB and other banks require the *original* passport and pass
to be sighted in person, which a phone-camera upload can't substitute for.
If you're arriving on a long-term pass, budget for at least one branch trip
with your originals, not photocopies.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming a tourist visa is just "a formality" the bank will overlook.**
  It isn't — the pass category itself is what the bank's checklist is built
  around, and a social visit pass has no matching category.
- **Bringing photocopies or scans instead of originals.** Every list above
  specifies the *original* document; a clear photo on your phone is not a
  substitute at the counter.
- **Not checking pass validity before the appointment.** A pass or passport
  with under six months left can get the application declined on the spot,
  even if your actual stay is much longer.
- **Expecting identical requirements at every bank.** CIMB's list is a solid
  baseline, but Maybank, Public Bank and others add or vary details — a quick
  check of the specific bank's page can save a wasted trip.
- **Forgetting that a Dependant Pass needs its own paper trail.** A spouse or
  child on a Dependant Pass generally still needs the marriage or birth
  certificate tying them to the primary pass holder, not just the pass itself.
