A night-market stall owner displays a single QR code. A customer using the
Maybank app scans it. The next customer uses Touch 'n Go eWallet, scanning
the **same** QR code. Both payments land in the merchant's account within
seconds. There is no separate QR code for each bank or e-wallet — that's
DuitNow QR working quietly behind the scenes.

## DuitNow: send using a phone number, not an account number

DuitNow is an instant fund transfer service across banks and e-wallets in
Malaysia. The difference from an ordinary bank transfer: you don't need to
type a long bank account number. Instead, DuitNow matches a single **proxy
ID** to a destination account.

| Proxy ID | Suited for |
| --- | --- |
| Mobile phone number | Individuals |
| NRIC (identity card number) | Individuals |
| Passport number | Non-citizens |
| BRN (SSM business registration number) | Registered businesses |

To **send** money, you don't need to register anything — just log in to your
bank app, choose DuitNow, and type the recipient's proxy ID. To **receive**
money, on the other hand, the recipient needs to register once to link that
proxy ID to a bank account or e-wallet. A single ID can only be linked to one
account at a time, but a person can register several different IDs (for
example, a phone number and an NRIC) to the same account.

According to PayNet's official website, DuitNow transfers are **free up to
RM5,000 per transaction**. Beyond that amount, some banks charge a **50 sen**
fee, although many banks waive it as an incentive. The service is supported
by most major banks and various participating e-wallets across the country.

## DuitNow QR: one code, every bank

If DuitNow is the transfer rail, DuitNow QR is how it's displayed at the
counter. It is the same **National Standard QR Code** — when a merchant
displays a single DuitNow QR code, a customer of any participating bank or
e-wallet can scan it and pay directly, without the merchant needing to
subscribe to dozens of separate proprietary QR systems.

The limits and conditions, according to PayNet:

- **Transaction limit:** up to **RM50,000** per transaction, shared with the
  same DuitNow third-party transfer limit.
- **Transactions under RM250:** do not require additional verification at the
  customer level.
- **Fees to users:** PayNet states that paying, sending or receiving via
  DuitNow QR does not carry an additional charge to the user — although fees
  to the merchant (via the acquiring bank) may vary.

## Who's actually behind DuitNow

DuitNow and DuitNow QR are operated by **PayNet (Payments Network
Malaysia)** — the national payment infrastructure company that is
majority-owned by Bank Negara Malaysia together with major local banks. BNM
itself does not operate day-to-day transactions; its role is to set the
policy framework that requires banks and e-wallets to join the same
infrastructure.

The original framework — the **Interoperable Credit Transfer Framework
(ICTF)**, issued December 2019 — is what required DuitNow to exist in the
first place. On **30 June 2026**, BNM issued its successor: the
**Interoperable Fund Transfer Framework (IFTF)**, which goes beyond
account-to-account transfers to explicitly cover QR payments as well. Under
the IFTF, any proprietary or closed QR network still operated by a bank or
e-wallet must be **fully retired by 30 June 2028**, and institutions are
prohibited from registering new merchants to closed schemes during that
transition period.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming DuitNow QR is a feature of a specific bank.** It is shared
  infrastructure — the same QR code works regardless of which app scans it.
- **Forgetting to register to RECEIVE money.** Sending doesn't require
  registration, but your proxy ID needs to be linked first before others can
  pay you through it.
- **Assuming all DuitNow transfers are free with no limit.** It's free only
  up to RM5,000 per transaction; beyond that a small fee may be charged
  depending on the bank.
- **Confusing PayNet with Bank Negara Malaysia.** BNM sets the framework and
  supervises; PayNet is the one that builds and runs the payment rail every
  day.
