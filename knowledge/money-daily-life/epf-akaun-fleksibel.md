For almost forty years, your EPF savings lived in two boxes: Account 1, which you
could not touch until retirement, and Account 2, which you could tap for a house,
your health or your children's education. On **11 May 2024** that changed. The
Employees Provident Fund ([EPF](/en/glossary/epf), or KWSP) split every member
under the age of 55 into **three** accounts and, more importantly, carved out a
slice of every future contribution that you can withdraw whenever you like.

## The three accounts, at a glance

Going forward, each ringgit of contribution — yours and your employer's — is
divided three ways instead of the old 70/30 split:

- **Akaun Persaraan (Retirement Account) — 75%.** This is the serious retirement
  money. It is locked until you turn 55, with none of the partial-withdrawal
  windows the other accounts allow. The old Account 1 balance moved here intact.
- **Akaun Sejahtera (Wellbeing Account) — 15%.** This funds the big life-cycle
  needs — housing, medical, education — under the same kinds of rules that used to
  govern Account 2. Your old Account 2 balance moved here.
- **Akaun Fleksibel (Flexible Account) — 10%.** The new one. You can withdraw
  from it **at any time, for any reason**, with a minimum of RM50 per withdrawal.
  It started at a **zero balance** on 11 May 2024 and fills up only from the 10%
  share of contributions made from that date onward.

## Akaun Fleksibel: the part everyone asks about

Akaun Fleksibel exists for short-term needs — a car repair, a medical bill, a
cash-flow gap — without the paperwork and eligibility tests that other EPF
withdrawals require. There is no purpose to justify and no form to prove: if the
money is there, you can take it, subject to a **RM50 minimum**.

Because it only receives 10% of new contributions and began empty, the balance
builds up slowly. A member earning around RM3,000 a month accumulates only a
modest Akaun Fleksibel balance in the first year — this is a buffer for small,
occasional needs, not a second salary.

For security, larger online withdrawals trigger additional identity checks.
Reporting at launch noted that amounts above roughly **RM250** require electronic
Know-Your-Customer (e-KYC) verification such as facial recognition rather than
being a hard daily cap — so treat any specific threshold as an operational detail
to confirm in the i-Akaun app or at an EPF branch, since these limits can change.

## The one-time transfer — now closed

To give the new account a starting balance, the EPF offered a **one-time, optional**
transfer: members could move part of their Akaun Sejahtera savings into Akaun
Fleksibel. The window ran from **11 May to 31 August 2024** only, the decision could
**not be reversed**, and it is **no longer available**. If you did not opt in during
that window, your Akaun Fleksibel simply grows from the ongoing 10% share.

The EPF stressed throughout that moving funds was a **choice**, not a default — the
point being that money pulled out of long-term savings loses years of compounding.

## What happens at 55

The three-account structure applies while you are under 55. When you reach **55**,
the balances across all three accounts are consolidated into a single **Akaun 55**,
and the normal age-55 withdrawal options apply from there.

## The trade-off to weigh

Akaun Fleksibel is genuinely useful: it gives EPF members a liquid, no-questions
buffer that previously did not exist, which can keep people from turning to
high-interest debt for small emergencies. But the flexibility has a cost. Every
ringgit spent from Akaun Fleksibel today is a ringgit that will not compound inside
Akaun Persaraan for decades — and Malaysia's retirement-savings adequacy is already
a concern. The sensible use is as an **emergency buffer**, not a spending account:
the 75% locked in Akaun Persaraan is doing the real work of funding your retirement.
