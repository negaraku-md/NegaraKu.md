If you drive for an e-hailing app, sell food from a stall, take freelance design
jobs or run a one-person business, one thing never happens on payday: no employer
deducts a slice of your income for the [EPF](/en/glossary/epf) (Kumpulan Wang
Simpanan Pekerja, or KWSP). For salaried staff, that retirement contribution
happens automatically every month. For everyone outside the formal payroll system,
the retirement nest egg does not build itself — unless you choose to build it. That
choice is what **i-Saraan** is for.

## What i-Saraan actually is

i-Saraan is the EPF's **voluntary contribution scheme** for Malaysians who have no
fixed employer paying EPF on their behalf. You open it through your i-Akaun (the EPF
member portal and app) or at an EPF branch, and then you contribute whatever you
can afford, whenever you can — there is no fixed monthly amount and no penalty for
skipping a month. The money goes into your own EPF savings and, crucially, earns the
**same annual EPF dividend** that every member receives, so it compounds over the
years rather than sitting idle.

Eligibility is deliberately broad: you must be a Malaysian EPF member **under the age
of 60** who is self-employed, has no fixed income, or works in the gig economy. If you
have ever held a formal job you almost certainly already have an EPF account; i-Saraan
simply lets you keep feeding it on your own terms. There is no minimum contribution to
stay in the scheme and no obligation to contribute every year — you can pay in a lump
sum near the year's end, spread small amounts across the year, or pause entirely when
cash is tight, then resume later without re-registering.

## The government matching incentive

The feature that makes i-Saraan worth using is the **matching incentive**: the
government adds money on top of what you contribute. The incentive is **20% of your
voluntary contribution, capped at RM500 a year**, with a lifetime ceiling of
**RM5,000** (or until you reach age 60, whichever comes first). Because the rate is
20%, you reach the full RM500 by contributing **RM2,500** in a year — roughly RM208 a
month. Contribute less and you still get 20% of whatever you put in; contribute more
than RM2,500 and the extra still grows with dividends, but the incentive itself stops
at RM500 for the year.

That is effectively a guaranteed, immediate 20% return on the first RM2,500 you save
each year, before the EPF dividend is even counted — a return no ordinary savings
account can match.

## i-Saraan Plus, from Budget 2026

In **Budget 2026** (tabled on 10 October 2025), the government introduced an enhanced
tier, **i-Saraan Plus**, aimed squarely at gig workers — eligible e-hailing, p-hailing
and delivery drivers. It raises the matching incentive to **up to RM600 a year** and a
**RM6,000** lifetime cap, reached by contributing about RM3,000 in a year. A member
qualifies for **either** i-Saraan or i-Saraan Plus in a given year, not both, and the
existing i-Saraan continues unchanged for other self-employed and informal workers.

| Feature | i-Saraan | i-Saraan Plus (Budget 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Matching rate | 20% | 20% |
| Maximum incentive per year | RM500 | RM600 |
| Lifetime incentive cap | RM5,000 | RM6,000 |
| Who it targets | Self-employed, no fixed income | Eligible e-hailing, p-hailing, delivery workers |
| Nature | Voluntary | Voluntary |

## The annual contribution limit

There is a ceiling on how much you can pay into EPF voluntarily: **RM100,000 a year**,
counted in aggregate across the direct voluntary schemes (i-Saraan and i-Saraan Plus
among them), not per scheme. This was raised from RM60,000 and is far above anything a
typical informal worker contributes — but it exists, and amounts above it are refunded
rather than credited.

## Why it matters: the informal-worker retirement gap

Malaysia's mandatory EPF system covers employees with employers. It does not reach the
large share of the workforce who are self-employed or working gig and informal jobs,
and that group has historically retired with little or no formal savings. i-Saraan is
the government's main tool for closing that gap voluntarily: it keeps these workers
inside the EPF system — earning dividends, building a withdrawable balance — and uses
the matching incentive as the carrot to make saving attractive rather than compulsory.
The design reflects a basic constraint: income that is irregular cannot support a fixed
monthly deduction, so the scheme asks for flexibility and rewards consistency instead of
demanding it. For platform-based gig workers, it sits alongside the newer social-security
duties of the [Gig Workers Act 2025](/en/employment/gig-workers-act-2025), which together
begin to extend to informal workers the kind of safety net that salaried employees have
long taken for granted.

## How i-Saraan differs from three things it is often confused with

**Versus normal (mandatory) EPF.** Standard EPF is compulsory and employer-driven:
your employer deducts your share and adds its own on top, every month. i-Saraan is
voluntary, has no employer portion, and instead of an employer match you get the
government's capped incentive. Both land in the same EPF account and earn the same
dividend.

**Versus the Akaun Fleksibel restructure.** The 2024
[three-account restructure](/en/money-daily-life/epf-akaun-fleksibel) (Akaun Persaraan,
Akaun Sejahtera and the new Akaun Fleksibel) changed **how** every member's EPF savings
are split and when they can be withdrawn. That is about the structure of the account.
i-Saraan is about **getting money into** the account in the first place for people with
no employer — a different question entirely, though i-Saraan savings are then held under
the same account structure as anyone else's.

**Versus SOCSO and STR.** The SOCSO (PERKESO) self-employed scheme is **social
insurance**, not savings: you pay a small contribution and it pays benefits if you are
injured, permanently disabled or die — it is not money you get back. Sumbangan Tunai
Rahmah ([STR](/en/money-daily-life/str-cash-aid)) is a **government cash handout** under
the Budget for lower-income households; you do not contribute and you do not withdraw
savings, you simply receive aid. i-Saraan is neither: it is **your own retirement
savings**, topped up by the state. A self-employed person ideally uses all three — STR
when eligible, SOCSO for protection, and i-Saraan for retirement. For a combined view of
the savings-and-protection side, see
[EPF and SOCSO for gig and self-employed workers](/en/money-daily-life/epf-socso-gig-workers-self-employed).

## What to do now

If you have no employer paying EPF for you, register for i-Saraan through your i-Akaun
and aim for the RM2,500 a year that unlocks the full RM500 incentive — or as much of it
as you can manage. If you are an eligible e-hailing, p-hailing or delivery driver, check
whether you qualify for i-Saraan Plus instead. Because the rates and caps are set in the
annual Budget and can change, confirm the current figures on the official i-Saraan pages
at kwsp.gov.my or in your i-Akaun before you commit.
