If you rent a shop lot, an office, a warehouse or a piece of equipment for your business, your tenancy quietly changed on 1 July 2025. On that date Malaysia's **expanded SST** brought the rental or leasing of commercial property and other tangible assets into the scope of **service tax** — so a landlord above a certain size now has to add **8%** to the rent. The same reform, though, was built with a deliberate escape hatch for small businesses: a raised registration threshold and a specific exemption a small tenant can claim. Here is who has to charge the tax, who is protected from it, and the dates that decide.

## Is my rent now taxed?

From **1 July 2025**, renting or leasing property and tangible assets became a **taxable service** under the expanded SST, charged at **8%** service tax ([Ministry of Finance](https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-release/targeted-revision-of-sales-tax-rate-and-expansion-of-service-tax-scope-effective-1-july-2025); [RMCD MySST FAQ](https://mysst.customs.gov.my/faq-expansion-of-service-tax-scope-2025/)). This covers **commercial property** — offices, retail lots, warehouses and similar space — and leased **movable assets** such as machinery, vehicles and equipment.

Two things soften that headline. First, the tax only bites where the landlord or lessor is a **registered taxable person**; a landlord below the registration threshold does not charge it. Second, **residential rent is excluded** — renting a home to live in is outside the scope. So the change is aimed squarely at commercial leasing, not at ordinary housing.

*(The government has repeatedly revised the expanded SST in response to feedback; if you are pricing a lease today, confirm the current rate and thresholds on [mysst.customs.gov.my](https://mysst.customs.gov.my/faq-expansion-of-service-tax-scope-2025/) before you rely on a figure.)*

## Who has to register and charge it?

The obligation sits with the **landlord or lessor**, not the tenant — and only above a size threshold.

A provider of rental or leasing services must **register and charge service tax once its taxable rental income exceeds RM1 million in a 12-month period**. This threshold matters, because it was **raised from an originally announced RM500,000 to RM1 million** after public and industry feedback — a change the Ministry of Finance made specifically to **reduce the number of small landlords and MSMEs** pulled into the tax ([Ministry of Finance](https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-release/revision-to-the-expanded-sales-tax-and-service-tax-take-into-account-public-and-industry-feedback)).

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What is taxed? | Rental or leasing of commercial property and tangible assets |
| Rate | 8% service tax |
| Effective from | 1 July 2025 |
| Who registers/charges | The landlord or lessor, as a registered taxable person |
| Registration threshold | Taxable rental exceeding **RM1 million** in 12 months (raised from RM500,000) |
| Residential rent | Excluded |

So a landlord whose total taxable rental stays under RM1 million a year is **not required to register** and does not add service tax to the rent. It is only the larger commercial landlords who cross the threshold.

## The small-business shield: the SME rental exemption

Even where the landlord is above the threshold and would normally charge the tax, a **small-business tenant can be exempted**. This is the relief most tenants will care about.

A tenant that is a **small or micro enterprise** — one whose **annual sales do not exceed RM1 million**, based on its **latest income assessed by the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN)** — can claim exemption from the service tax on its rent. To do so, the tenant must:

- **declare and register** its small-business status through the **MyPMK** system operated by the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD); and
- **update its annual sales figure to Customs each year**, so the status stays current.

Once that declaration is accepted, the landlord does **not** charge service tax on that tenant's rent. The design intent is that the 8% falls on larger occupiers, while genuinely small tenants renting from a big landlord are shielded ([RMCD MySST FAQ](https://mysst.customs.gov.my/faq-expansion-of-service-tax-scope-2025/)). The exemption is granted under the Minister of Finance's power in the Service Tax Act 2018, so it is a formal relief a tenant claims — not something that applies automatically. If you qualify, register through MyPMK; if you do not register, the landlord is entitled to charge the tax.

## What isn't caught

Several things fall outside the rental or leasing service tax entirely:

- **Residential accommodation** — renting a home to live in.
- **Financial leases** where ownership of the asset ultimately transfers to the lessee, and **hire-purchase** arrangements — these are treated differently from an operating rental.
- **Assets located outside Malaysia.**
- **Refundable security deposits**, which are not payment for the rental service itself.

If your arrangement is one of these, the 8% does not apply even where the landlord is registered.

## How a tenant or landlord confirms whether tax applies

Rather than assume, work through it in order:

1. **Is the thing being rented residential?** If yes, no service tax. If it is commercial property or a tangible asset, continue.
2. **Is the landlord a registered taxable person** (taxable rental over RM1 million a year)? If the landlord is below the threshold and not registered, no tax is charged.
3. **Are you (the tenant) a small business** with annual sales up to RM1 million on your latest LHDN assessment? If so, register the exemption through **MyPMK** — once accepted, the landlord should stop charging the tax on your rent.
4. **Check the period, not just the invoice date.** Tax applies to rental for periods from 1 July 2025 onward; rent for earlier periods is not taxable even if billed later.

A tenant who is charged service tax but believes it is exempt should raise the MyPMK declaration with the landlord; a landlord unsure of its own position should check its rolling 12-month taxable rental against the RM1 million threshold.

## Key dates and transitional rules

- **1 July 2025** — service tax on rental or leasing services takes effect.
- **Before 1 July 2025** — rental relating to any period before this date is **not taxable**, regardless of when it is invoiced or paid ([RMCD MySST FAQ](https://mysst.customs.gov.my/faq-expansion-of-service-tax-scope-2025/)).
- **Until 31 December 2025** — the government announced that **no prosecution or penalties** would be imposed on businesses taking steps to comply, a grace period to register and adjust billing systems ([Ministry of Finance](https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-release/targeted-revision-of-sales-tax-rate-and-expansion-of-service-tax-scope-effective-1-july-2025)).

## What's next

If you are a **tenant**, the practical move is to check whether you qualify as a small business and, if so, register the exemption through MyPMK before accepting an 8% charge on your rent. If you are a **landlord**, track your 12-month taxable rental against the RM1 million threshold, register with Customs if you cross it, and be ready to honour valid SME exemption declarations from your tenants.

Because the expanded SST has been revised more than once — the threshold moved from RM500,000 to RM1 million, and the government has continued to refine the rules in response to feedback — treat any figure here as a starting point and confirm the current rate, threshold and exemption conditions on the RMCD MySST portal or with a tax adviser before you sign or price a lease.
