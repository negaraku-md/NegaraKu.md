For years, a sub-RM500 gadget ordered from an overseas web store landed on your doorstep tax-free. Since 1 January 2024, it does not.

Malaysia now charges a **10% sales tax on low value goods** — "LVG" — sold into the country from abroad. It is a quiet change with a wide reach: almost anyone who shops online across a border, and every overseas seller or marketplace of any size, is touched by it.

## What counts as a "low value good"?

The definition is deliberately narrow and price-based. A low value good is any good:

- **sold at a price of RM500 or less**, and
- **brought into Malaysia from outside the country** by land, sea or air.

In everyday terms, that is the phone case, the dress, the cable or the cosmetic you buy online from a seller abroad. The RM500 test looks at the **selling price of the goods themselves** — not the shipping, not the insurance, and not the combined value of everything in one parcel.

A handful of items are carved out entirely (see below), but otherwise the category is broad on purpose: it is meant to capture the flood of small cross-border e-commerce parcels.

## Why did Malaysia introduce it?

To close a gap that had quietly tilted the playing field.

A local shop selling a RM80 item charges sales tax on it. For years, the same RM80 item shipped from overseas arrived under the **de minimis** relief — low-value air-courier parcels were exempt from duty and sales tax at the border. Domestic sellers were, in effect, taxed while their foreign online competitors were not.

The LVG tax levels that: whether the item comes from a shop down the road or a warehouse abroad, a comparable 10% now applies.

## Who has to register and charge it?

Not every overseas seller — only those above a size threshold.

A seller (including an online marketplace operator) must register with the **Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD)** once the total value of the LVG it brings into Malaysia **exceeds RM500,000 within a 12-month period**. That total can be measured two ways:

- **historical** — the current month plus the preceding 11 months; or
- **prospective** — the current month plus the expected next 11 months.

Once liable, the seller registers within one month through the **MyLVG portal** using form **LVG-01**, and becomes a *Registered Seller* with its own registration number. Sellers below RM500,000 are not required to register or charge the tax.

## How much, and on what?

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Rate | 10% sales tax |
| Tax base | The selling price of the goods **only** |
| Excluded from the base | Overseas transport and insurance, plus any other tax or duty on the sale |
| Collected by | The registered seller / marketplace, at the point of sale |
| Charged from | 1 January 2024 |

So a RM300 item attracts RM30 of LVG tax, charged at checkout — not a calculation you do when the parcel lands. Because the 10% is on the goods price alone, freight and insurance do not inflate the tax.

## What is excluded?

Some goods are **not** low value goods, even if they cost under RM500. These stay under the ordinary import duty and excise rules instead:

- Cigarettes and tobacco products
- Intoxicating liquor
- Smoking pipes (including pipe bowls)
- Electronic cigarettes and vaping devices, and their liquids or gel preparations

If you buy any of these from abroad, do not expect the simple 10%-at-checkout treatment — they are handled at the border like other controlled or dutiable goods.

## How do registered sellers file and pay?

LVG has its own return cycle, separate from ordinary SST.

A registered seller accounts for the tax on a **three-month (quarterly) taxable period**, filing form **LVG-02** through the portal and paying the tax due. The return is lodged whether or not there was any taxable activity in the period. Late payment carries an escalating penalty (reported in the 10%–40% range), so the filing calendar matters as much as the collection.

## What it means if you are just shopping

For a consumer, the change is simple to feel and easy to misread:

- Sub-RM500 overseas online purchases now show a **10% charge at checkout** where there was none before.
- You should **not** be charged sales tax a second time when the parcel clears Customs — the tax a registered seller collects is meant to replace, not add to, border sales tax on that consignment.
- Buying from a **small overseas seller below the threshold** may mean no LVG charge at all — but the item then follows the normal import rules when it arrives, which can be less predictable than a clean 10% at checkout.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating RM500 as a per-parcel limit.** It is a per-item selling-price test; a parcel can hold several LVG items.
- **Adding shipping into the RM500 or the 10%.** Both look at the goods' price, not the delivery cost.
- **Assuming cigarettes, liquor or vapes get the 10% treatment.** They are excluded and follow import duty/excise.
- **Sellers ignoring the threshold until audited.** The RM500,000 test is rolling; crossing it triggers a one-month window to register.

## What's next

The LVG tax is now a settled part of buying across Malaysia's border, and the direction of travel globally is toward taxing low-value e-commerce rather than exempting it. If you sell into Malaysia, track your rolling 12-month LVG total against the RM500,000 line. If you shop, read the checkout: the 10% you now see on a small overseas order is this tax at work.
