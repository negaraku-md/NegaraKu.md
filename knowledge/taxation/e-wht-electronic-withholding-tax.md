For years, paying withholding tax meant a paper CP37 form, a cheque or bank slip, and a trip to an LHDN counter — or an email of scanned documents — and then a wait to see the payment reconciled against your account. That whole ritual has moved online. **e-WHT** is LHDN's electronic channel for completing withholding tax forms and paying the tax inside the **MyTax** portal, and for the forms it covers it is now the required route, not an optional convenience.

Nothing about *what* you owe changes — the rate, the payee, the one-month deadline are all the same. What changes is *how* you file and pay, and that is worth getting right, because the electronic flow issues its own bill number and receipt and leaves a clean audit trail.

## What is e-WHT?

e-WHT is an application within MyTax that lets a payer complete the **withholding tax payment form online** and settle the tax electronically. It handles the familiar **CP37-series** forms — the ones a payer uses to account for tax deducted from royalties, interest, technical fees, contract payments and similar amounts paid to a non-resident.

Before e-WHT, each of these was a printed form lodged at a counter or emailed in. LHDN introduced the electronic service to replace that manual submission, and **since 1 September 2023 the e-WHT route has been mandatory** for the covered forms. The service sits alongside LHDN's other e-services (e-Filing, e-TT, ByrHASiL) and shares the same MyTax login.

## Why did LHDN move withholding tax online?

The paper route had well-known friction: forms lost in transit, payments that could not be matched to a form, no instant proof of remittance, and manual keying at LHDN's end. e-WHT closes those gaps. When you submit an e-WHT form, the system **generates a bill number** as the single payment reference, and once you pay, the **receipt is issued immediately and the form data reaches LHDN directly** — there is no longer a separate step of emailing supporting documents for payments made through the electronic system. For the payer, that means faster confirmation and a self-documenting record; for LHDN, it means clean, structured data instead of scanned forms.

## Which payments go through e-WHT?

e-WHT covers the withholding taxes on payments to non-residents (and a few resident cases), each tied to a charging section of the **Income Tax Act 1967** and its own form. The rates are not repeated here — see the companion guide on [withholding tax rates by payment type](withholding-tax-rates) — but the form-to-section map is:

| Form | ITA 1967 section | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CP37 | s.109 | Royalty and interest paid to a non-resident |
| CP37A | s.107A | Contract payments to a non-resident contractor (and its employees) |
| CP37C | s.109C | Interest paid to a resident individual |
| CP37D | s.109B | Special classes of income under s.4A — technical and service fees, rent of movable property |
| CP37E | s.109D | Distributions from a real estate investment trust (REIT) |
| CP37F | s.109F | Income under s.4(f) — other gains or profits paid to a non-resident |
| CP37S / CP37DS | s.109 / s.109B | Deferred remittance for recurring **small-value** royalty, interest and s.4A payments |
| e-107D | s.107D | The 2 per cent deduction on commissions paid to resident agents, dealers and distributors |

Public entertainer remuneration under **s.109A** is also a withholding tax, and the electronic services carry the full CP37 range, so check the current form list in MyTax for the exact code before you file. This guide is about the *mechanism*; for which form fits a given payment, see the guide on [choosing the right CP37 form](cp37-forms), and for s.4A specifically, [section 109B special classes of income](withholding-tax-special-classes).

## How does the e-WHT process work?

The flow is the same for every covered form:

1. **Register or log in to MyTax** at `mytax.hasil.gov.my` using the payer's digital certificate.
2. Open **ezHasil Services → e-WHT** and select the relevant form (for example CP37 for a royalty, CP37D for a technical fee).
3. **Complete the e-form** — payer and payee details, the gross payment, the date paid or credited, and the tax deducted. Use a separate form for each non-resident payee.
4. **Generate the bill** — the system produces a **bill number** that is your payment reference.
5. **Pay** using that bill number, either by **FPX through the ByrHASiL portal** (any FPX-member bank) or by **Electronic Telegraphic Transfer (e-TT)** using the virtual account number LHDN assigns.
6. The **receipt is issued immediately** and the deduction data is recorded against your account — keep the receipt as your proof of remittance.

## What is the deadline, and what happens if you miss it?

e-WHT changed the plumbing, not the clock. The tax must be paid to the Director General **within one month after paying or crediting** the payee — this is written into each charging section: **s.109(1)** for royalty and interest, **s.107A(1)** for contract payments, **s.109B(1)** for special classes of income, **s.109F(1)** for s.4(f) income. Note that *crediting* can fall before actual payment, so the clock sometimes starts earlier than you expect.

Miss the month and two things happen, both unchanged from the paper era:

- **A 10 per cent increase.** Where the payer fails to pay the amount due, that amount is **increased by a sum equal to 10 per cent** of the unpaid tax, and the tax plus the increase becomes a **debt due to the Government** — for interest and royalty this is **s.109(2)**, with parallel provisions in the other sections. The increase is paid separately using **Form CP147**.
- **The expense is disallowed.** The payment you failed to withhold on is **not deductible** in the payer's own tax computation under **s.39(1)(f), (i) or (j)** until the withholding tax and the increase are paid in full.

For the full cost of getting this wrong — including how the disallowance is lifted once you pay up — see [what it costs to miss a withholding tax deduction](withholding-tax-non-compliance).

## How is e-WHT different from the old paper CP37 route?

The substance is identical; the handling is not:

- **Submission:** paper CP37 lodged at a counter or emailed, versus an e-form completed and submitted in MyTax.
- **Payment reference:** a manual payment you hoped LHDN would match, versus a **system-generated bill number** that ties payment to form automatically.
- **Proof:** a stamped copy and a wait, versus an **immediate electronic receipt**.
- **Documents:** scanned attachments sent by email, versus data captured directly by LHDN — no separate document email for payments made electronically.
- **Status:** optional in the past, but **mandatory since 1 September 2023** for the covered forms.

## What's next

If you make payments to non-residents, treat e-WHT as the default: register the payer on MyTax before the first payment falls due, so you are not scrambling within the one-month window. Work out the correct form and rate *first* — that is where the real decisions are — then the e-WHT steps are mechanical. Keep every bill number and receipt with the underlying invoice, because that receipt is your evidence both that you remitted on time and that the expense stays deductible.
