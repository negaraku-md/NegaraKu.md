The line item on your Netflix receipt, the tax on your company's AWS invoice, the few extra ringgit on a Canva or Adobe subscription — that is one specific Malaysian tax at work, and it has a name most people have never heard: SToDS.

**Service Tax on Digital Services** is how Malaysia taxes the streaming, software, cloud and advertising that pour in from servers abroad. The twist is who pays it to the government: not you, and not a local importer, but the foreign company itself, which has to register with Malaysian Customs to collect it.

## What exactly is SToDS taxing?

It taxes **digital services supplied from outside Malaysia to a customer inside it**. In practice that sweeps up most of the subscriptions a modern household or business runs on:

- Video, music and game streaming
- Software and SaaS subscriptions
- Cloud storage, hosting and platform services
- Mobile apps and in-app purchases
- Online advertising
- E-books, online media, courses and other digital content

The common thread is that the service is delivered over the internet with minimal human intervention, and the seller sits overseas. A Malaysian recipient can be identified by signals such as a Malaysian payment method, IP address, or billing address.

## Who actually collects it?

The foreign provider — but only once it is big enough in Malaysia.

A foreign service provider must register as a **Foreign Registered Person (FRP)** with the **Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD)** once the total value of digital services it provides to consumers in Malaysia **exceeds RM500,000 in a 12-month period**. That total is tested on a rolling basis — either the past 12 months or the next expected 12 months — and the RM500,000 line has been fixed since the regime began.

Registration is done through the **MySToDS** portal using form **DST-01**. Once approved, the provider becomes an FRP with its own registration number and starts charging the tax at the point of sale.

## The rate, and how it moved

| Period | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| 1 January 2020 to 29 February 2024 | 6% |
| From 1 March 2024 | 8% |

SToDS launched at **6% on 1 January 2020**. On **1 March 2024** it rose to **8%**, part of a broader increase in Malaysia's service tax. Supplies that straddle the change are generally apportioned: the part supplied before 1 March 2024 stays at 6%, and the part on or after takes 8% — which matters for the annual and multi-month subscriptions that dominate this market. Several large vendors, from cloud platforms to logistics carriers, issued customer notices confirming 8% on invoices dated from 1 March 2024.

## B2B as well as B2C

Here is a point that trips up finance teams: the digital-services regime applies to **both consumers and businesses**.

There is no blanket business-to-business carve-out of the kind that exists for some domestic service tax. So a Malaysian company subscribing to a foreign SaaS tool, cloud platform or ad network normally sees the 8% on its invoice just as a household does on a streaming plan. (Certain intra-group B2B reliefs can apply in specific cases — confirm the current rules before assuming any exemption.)

## Filing and paying

SToDS runs on its own quarterly rhythm, separate from ordinary SST filing.

An FRP accounts for the tax on a **three-month (quarterly) taxable period**, filing form **DST-02** through the MySToDS portal by the **last day of the month after the quarter ends**, and paying in **Malaysian ringgit** (typically by telegraphic transfer or FPX). The return must be filed even if there was no taxable activity in the period. Where the provider invoices in another currency, it converts the values to ringgit to report and remit.

## SToDS is not the other "digital tax" — the big one to avoid confusing

Malaysia has more than one tax with "digital" in its name, and they are easy to conflate. They are genuinely different taxes, under different laws, administered by different agencies.

| | SToDS (this article) | Withholding tax on digital services | Low value goods (LVG) tax |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type of tax | Service tax (consumption tax) | Income tax | Sales tax (consumption tax) |
| On what | Price of a digital **service** | Cross-border **payment** for digital services/royalties | Imported **goods** sold for RM500 or less |
| Who remits | Foreign provider (FRP) | The Malaysian **payer**, by deducting | Foreign seller / marketplace |
| Law | Service Tax Act 2018 | Income Tax Act 1967 | Sales Tax Act 2018 |
| Regulator | RMCD (Customs) | LHDN (Inland Revenue) | RMCD (Customs) |
| Rate | 8% | Varies by payment type | 10% |

The one to keep clearly apart is the **withholding tax on digital services**. That is *income tax*: when a Malaysian business pays a foreign party for certain digital services or royalties, it may have to withhold a slice of the payment and remit it to the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN). SToDS, by contrast, is a *consumption tax* on the service price, collected by the foreign provider and paid to Customs. A single transaction — say a Malaysian firm paying an overseas software vendor — can even attract both: SToDS added on top, and withholding deducted from the payment. See [Withholding tax on digital services](/en/taxation/withholding-tax-digital-services/) for that regime in full.

The **low value goods tax** is different again: it is a *sales tax on imported goods* under the Sales Tax Act 2018, for parcels priced at RM500 or less — physical things, not services. See [Malaysia's 10% Sales Tax on Low Value Goods](/en/taxation/low-value-goods-sales-tax/).

## The legal basis

SToDS lives inside the **Service Tax Act 2018**. The digital-services provisions were added by amendment in 2019 and took effect on 1 January 2020, creating the FRP mechanism for foreign suppliers. The rate itself is set by subsidiary order — the Service Tax (Rate of Digital Services Tax) Order 2019, amended in 2024 to raise the rate to 8% from 1 March 2024. For how the parent regime works for domestic service providers, see [the Service Tax Act 2018](/en/taxation/service-tax-act-2018/).

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking it is income tax.** SToDS is a service tax paid to Customs, not a deduction remitted to LHDN — that is the separate withholding tax.
- **Assuming a B2B exemption.** Business buyers are generally taxed too; do not strip the 8% off a foreign SaaS invoice on the assumption it is exempt.
- **Still using 6%.** The rate has been 8% since 1 March 2024; older guidance and calculators may lag.
- **Treating the RM500,000 threshold as per-customer.** It is the provider's total digital-service sales into Malaysia over a rolling 12 months.
- **Forgetting the nil return.** An FRP files DST-02 every quarter even when nothing is due.

## What's next

SToDS is now a settled, maturing part of Malaysia's tax base, and the scope of "digital services" tends to widen rather than narrow as the economy digitalises. If you sell digital services into Malaysia, track your rolling 12-month total against the RM500,000 line and register through MySToDS before you cross it. If you buy them, read your invoices: the 8% you see is this tax, and it is not the same as the withholding your finance team handles separately. See also [the Service Tax Act 2018](/en/taxation/service-tax-act-2018/) and [Withholding tax on digital services](/en/taxation/withholding-tax-digital-services/).
