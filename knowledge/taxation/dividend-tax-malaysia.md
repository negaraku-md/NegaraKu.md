faq:
  - q: "Who has to pay Malaysia's 2% dividend tax?"
    a: "Any individual who is a shareholder of a company — directly or through a nominee — and whose Malaysian-sourced dividends exceed RM100,000 in a year. Under the new paragraph 6(1)(r) of the Income Tax Act 1967 (inserted by section 4 of the Finance Act 2024), the charge applies to dividends 'deemed by virtue of section 14 to be derived from Malaysia'. The gazetted Rules apply it to both residents and non-residents, and it takes effect from year of assessment 2025. It is a charge on the individual shareholder — dividends flowing between companies are not caught."
  - q: "How is the 2% calculated if I only have dividend income?"
    a: "Part XXII of Schedule 1 charges 2% on every ringgit of the chargeable income in respect of dividend that exceeds RM100,000, and paragraph 12B(2) of Schedule 6 exempts the first RM100,000. So if your only income is RM250,000 of Malaysian single-tier dividends, the slice above the threshold is RM150,000 and the dividend tax is 2% × RM150,000 = RM3,000 (before any reliefs that may reduce chargeable income). Dividends of RM100,000 or less in the year attract no dividend tax at all."
  - q: "How is it worked out when I also have a salary or business income?"
    a: "The Income Tax (Determination of Chargeable Income of an Individual in respect of Dividend) Rules 2025 [P.U. (A) 148/2025] set a formula: chargeable income in respect of dividend = A / B × C, where A is your statutory income from dividends, B is your aggregate income, and C is your total chargeable income for the year. The 2% then applies to that dividend chargeable income above RM100,000, and the rest of your chargeable income is taxed at the normal Part I scale rates."
  - q: "Are EPF, ASNB and unit-trust dividends taxed?"
    a: "No. LHDN has clarified that distributions from the Employees Provident Fund (EPF/KWSP), the Armed Forces Fund Board (LTAT), Amanah Saham Nasional Bumiputera (ASNB) and other unit trusts are excluded — they are not taxable dividend income and are not even counted towards the RM100,000 threshold, so they do not need to be declared for this purpose. Foreign-source dividends are also outside the charge, because Part XXII only reaches dividends deemed under section 14 to be derived from Malaysia."
  - q: "How and when do I declare and pay the dividend tax?"
    a: "It is self-assessed. You report the dividend income in your annual return (Form BE for a resident, Form M for a non-resident) for year of assessment 2025 onwards, and pay the 2% together with your final tax on assessment — there is no withholding at source. From YA 2025, companies must also issue shareholders a dividend voucher showing the gross dividend, which supports your declaration."

For years the answer to "is my Malaysian dividend taxed?" was a clean no — the single-tier system taxed profits once, at the company, and paid dividends out tax-free. From year of assessment 2025 that is no longer the whole story for large shareholders: an individual whose Malaysian dividends top RM100,000 in a year now pays a 2% tax on the excess.

Here is exactly who it catches, how it is worked out, and the long list of dividends it does not touch.

## What the new tax is, and where it comes from

The charge was added by the **Finance Act 2024 (Act 862)**, gazetted on 31 December 2024 and effective from **year of assessment 2025**. It has three moving parts in the Income Tax Act 1967:

- A new charging paragraph — **section 4 of the Finance Act 2024** inserts **paragraph 6(1)(r)** into the ITA, charging income tax on an individual shareholder's dividend income "at the appropriate rate as specified under Part XXII of Schedule 1".
- The rate — **Part XXII of Schedule 1** (inserted by section 16) charges tax "at the rate of 2 per cent on every ringgit of the chargeable income in respect of such dividend" that is **in excess of one hundred thousand ringgit**.
- The floor — **paragraph 12B(2) of Schedule 6** (inserted by section 17) treats dividends of **RM100,000 or less** as exempt, so the tax only ever bites on the excess.

In short: **2% on Malaysian dividend income above RM100,000 a year**, for the individual who receives it.

## Who it applies to

The charge reaches an **individual who is a shareholder of a company**, whether the shares are held **directly or through a nominee**, on dividends "deemed by virtue of section 14 to be derived from Malaysia". Both **listed and unlisted** company dividends count.

It applies to **residents and non-residents** alike — the gazetted 2025 Rules give a separate computation limb for each. What it does *not* do is tax companies: dividends passing **between companies** in a group are outside paragraph 6(1)(r) entirely, so single-tier inter-company distributions stay exempt. This is a tax on individual shareholders, aimed squarely at those with large dividend streams.

## How the 2% is calculated

The mechanics differ depending on whether dividends are your only income.

**If dividends are your only income**, the sum is simple. The first RM100,000 is exempt, and 2% applies to the rest:

| Your Malaysian dividends in the year | Amount above RM100,000 | Dividend tax at 2% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RM80,000 | RM0 | RM0 |
| RM100,000 | RM0 | RM0 |
| RM250,000 | RM150,000 | RM3,000 |
| RM1,000,000 | RM900,000 | RM18,000 |

(Reliefs that reduce your chargeable income can lower these figures — the table shows the headline arithmetic.)

**If you also have a salary or business income**, the **Income Tax (Determination of Chargeable Income of an Individual in respect of Dividend) Rules 2025 [P.U. (A) 148/2025]** apportion your chargeable income to the dividend source with a formula:

> chargeable income in respect of dividend = **A ÷ B × C**

where **A** is your statutory income from dividends, **B** is your aggregate income, and **C** is your total chargeable income for the year. The 2% is charged on the portion of that dividend chargeable income above RM100,000; the balance of your chargeable income is taxed at the ordinary Part I scale rates. (For a married couple under a combined assessment, aggregate income includes the spouse's income.)

## What is excluded

This is where most people find they are unaffected. The following do **not** attract the 2% dividend tax, and several do not even count towards the RM100,000 threshold:

| Excluded | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **EPF / KWSP, LTAT, ASNB and other unit-trust distributions** | LHDN has clarified these are excluded and need not be declared for this purpose. |
| **Foreign-source dividends** | Part XXII only reaches dividends deemed under section 14 to be **derived from Malaysia**. |
| **Dividends of RM100,000 or less** in the year | Exempt under paragraph 12B(2) of Schedule 6. |
| **Inter-company dividends** | Paragraph 6(1)(r) charges only an *individual* — single-tier dividends between companies stay exempt. |
| **Dividends from exempt-account / pioneer / reinvestment-allowance profits, and exempt shipping profits** | These are already tax-exempt dividends, outside the Part XXII charge. |
| **Distributions by cooperatives and closed-end funds** | Excluded from the dividend tax scope. |
| **Dividends from Labuan entities** | Outside the charge. |

The practical upshot: ordinary savers holding ASB/ASNB, unit trusts or an EPF balance are not touched, and even direct share investors pay nothing until their Malaysian dividends clear RM100,000 in a single year.

## How it is declared and assessed

There is **no withholding at source**. The dividend tax runs on **self-assessment**: you report your Malaysian dividend income in your annual return — **Form BE** for a resident, **Form M** for a non-resident — for YA 2025 onwards, and pay the 2% together with your final tax on assessment. To support this, from YA 2025 companies paying dividends must issue each shareholder a **dividend voucher** stating the gross amount, so you have a record of what to declare.

## What's next

If your Malaysian dividends are comfortably under RM100,000 a year, or come mainly from EPF, ASNB or unit trusts, this change does not affect you. If you are a substantial direct shareholder — or a business owner who takes profits as dividends from your own company — start tracking your gross Malaysian dividends across all your holdings for the year, keep the new dividend vouchers, and budget the 2% on the excess above RM100,000 into your YA 2025 return. Because the computation interacts with your other income through the P.U. (A) 148/2025 formula, confirm the exact figure with a tax agent where your income is mixed, and check LHDN's latest guidance before you file.
