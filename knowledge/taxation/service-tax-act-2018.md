The **Service Tax Act 2018 (Act 807)** is the Malaysian statute that provides for the charging, levying and collecting of service tax on prescribed taxable services. It forms the service-tax half of the **Sales and Service Tax (SST)** regime that replaced the Goods and Services Tax (GST). The Act received Royal Assent on 24 August 2018, was published in the Gazette on 28 August 2018, and came into operation on **1 September 2018**. It is administered by the **Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD)**.

## What the Act does

Service tax is a **single-stage consumption tax** charged on specific ("prescribed") taxable services supplied by a registered person in the course of business. Unlike GST, there is no input-tax credit mechanism — the tax is a cost that flows through the supply chain to the final consumer. **Section 7** of the Act charges and levies service tax on taxable services, and the categories of taxable services, their thresholds and any exemptions are prescribed through subsidiary regulations and orders made under the Act (notably the Service Tax Regulations 2018).

The Act also covers registration, invoicing, returns and payment, refunds, exemptions and remission, and enforcement and offences.

## Rate of service tax

The rate is fixed by order made under the Act. At commencement the rate was **6%**. From **1 March 2024** the standard rate increased to **8%**, with several service categories deliberately kept at 6%.

| Rate | Applies to (summary) |
|------|----------------------|
| 8% (standard) | Most taxable services since 1 March 2024 |
| 6% | Food and beverage, telecommunications, parking, and logistics services |
| Specific | Credit card / charge card services are charged at a fixed amount per card |

Transitional rules apply to services spanning 1 March 2024: the 6% rate applies to services performed before that date and 8% to services performed on or after it, with apportionment where a service straddles the change.

## Who must register

Under **Section 13**, a person providing taxable services is **liable to register** when the total value of those services exceeds — or is expected to exceed — the prescribed **RM500,000** threshold within any 12-month period. Some prescribed services carry their own thresholds, and registration is done online through the **MySST portal**. The Act also brings **imported taxable services** and **digital services supplied by foreign registered persons** within the service-tax net, so overseas suppliers meeting the threshold must register and account for tax.

## Returns and payment

A registered person accounts for service tax over a **two-month taxable period** and files the **SST-02 return**, which must be submitted no later than the **last day of the month following** the end of the taxable period. Payment is due on the same date, and the return must be filed even if no tax is payable for the period. Businesses may apply to the Director General for a taxable period aligned to their financial year-end.

## Where it sits in the SST framework

Act 807 works alongside the **Sales Tax Act 2018 (Act 806)**. Sales tax applies to taxable goods (manufactured or imported); service tax applies to prescribed services. Together they make up SST, both administered by RMCD.
