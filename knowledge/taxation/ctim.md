Ask who regulates tax advisers in Malaysia and most people name CTIM. It is the
natural answer and the wrong one. CTIM trains and qualifies tax professionals;
the licence to act as a tax agent is granted by the Ministry of Finance. The gap
between the two is where this page lives.

## A short history

CTIM was established on **1 October 1991** as the **Malaysian Institute of
Taxation (MIT)**, incorporated as a company limited by guarantee. It began under
the **auspices of the Malaysian Institute of Accountants (MIA)** and became
**independent of MIA in 2001**. The change of name to the **Chartered Tax
Institute of Malaysia** was recorded when the Registrar of Companies issued the
certificate on the change of name on **12 March 2009**. It has run its
professional taxation examination since December 1995.

## Membership is not a tax-agent licence

This is the distinction the page exists to make. CTIM confers the **ACTIM** and
**FCTIM** designations on members who meet its examination and experience
requirements. Those designations evidence competence in taxation — but they are
not, by themselves, a licence to act as a tax agent.

A **tax agent** in Malaysia is approved by the **Minister of Finance under
s.153(3) of the Income Tax Act 1967**, on the recommendation of a committee, and
the approval must be renewed. CTIM membership — particularly ACTIM, which
combines the professional examination with relevant tax experience — is a widely
recognised route **toward** that approval. The approval itself is the Ministry's
to grant, not CTIM's. Holding yourself out as a tax agent without that approval
is restricted under s.153.

## What CTIM does

Beyond qualifying members, CTIM is the country's main voice on tax-practitioner
matters. It runs continuing professional development, publishes technical
material and its journal **Tax Guardian**, and engages with LHDN and the Ministry
of Finance on tax policy and administration — the recognised professional
counterpart to the revenue authority on practitioner issues.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating CTIM as the licensing body for tax agents.** The licence is a
  s.153(3) approval from the Minister of Finance.
- **Using "MIT" as the current name.** The body has been CTIM since 2009.
- **Assuming CTIM is part of MIA.** It began under MIA's auspices in 1991 but has
  been independent since 2001.
- **Reading ACTIM as automatic tax-agent status.** The designation supports an
  application; it is not the approval.
