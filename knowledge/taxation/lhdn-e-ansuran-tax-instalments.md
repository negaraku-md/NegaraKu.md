If a tax bill lands that you cannot clear in one payment, Malaysia's tax authority no longer makes you queue at a branch to arrange instalments. Since March 2025 you can apply online, in a few clicks, through **e-Ansuran** on the MyTax portal — and get approval automatically. But "paying tax in instalments" in Malaysia actually means three different things, and confusing them is where people go wrong. Here is how e-Ansuran works, and how it differs from the CP500 and CP204 instalment schemes.

## What is e-Ansuran?

**e-Ansuran** is an online service on the **MyTax portal** that lets you apply to settle a **balance of tax or tax arrears in instalments** instead of paying the whole amount at once. LHDN launched it on **5 March 2025**, and it was introduced specifically "to make it easier for taxpayers to apply to pay in instalments their tax balances or tax arrears."

The design is deliberately light-touch:

- **Automatic approval** — no supporting documents required.
- **Amount above RM300** to qualify.
- **2 to 6 instalments** to choose from.
- Applies to **income tax** (including related penalties or charges).

That is a real change from the old route, where an instalment request meant writing an appeal letter to the collection unit and attaching an income statement, bank statements and a household budget. For a straightforward tax balance, e-Ansuran removes all of that.

## Who can use it, and how do you apply?

You apply by logging in to the **MyTax portal** (`mytax.hasil.gov.my`) and selecting your role. LHDN's media statement names three roles that can use e-Ansuran:

- **Individual**
- **Director**
- **Representative Director**

So it covers both ordinary individual taxpayers and companies applying through a director. From the dashboard you open the e-Ansuran application, confirm the amount owed, choose the number of instalments (2 to 6), and submit — approval is granted automatically.

One asymmetry to note: while applying is fully online, **cancelling an e-Ansuran arrangement can only be done in person at an LHDN office**, not through the portal.

## e-Ansuran vs CP500 vs CP204: what's the difference?

This is the part worth slowing down on. All three involve paying tax in instalments, but they are not the same mechanism, and they arise at different points in the tax cycle.

| Scheme | What it is | Who it applies to | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **e-Ansuran** | An arrangement to pay a **balance of tax already owed** (or arrears) in instalments | Individuals and companies (via a director), through MyTax | After the tax is assessed and owed — spreads out a debt |
| **CP500** | An **instalment payment notice** LHDN issues, pre-paying the current year's tax | Individuals with **business or other non-employment income** (e.g. rent, a sole proprietorship) | Bi-monthly instalments across the year |
| **CP204** | A **company's own estimate** of its tax payable, paid in instalments under **section 107C** | Companies, co-operatives, trust bodies and LLPs | Monthly instalments, beginning in the **second month** of the basis period, each due by the **15th** |

The clean way to hold it in your head: **CP500 and CP204 pre-pay tax that is not yet finally due**, while **e-Ansuran spreads out tax you already owe**. A CP500 or CP204 instalment is a prepayment towards the year's eventual bill; an e-Ansuran instalment chips away at a settled debt.

A few practical points on the two prepayment schemes:

- **CP500** is set by LHDN, not by you, based on your prior returns. If your circumstances have changed you can apply to revise it (via Form **CP502**) before **30 June** of the year of assessment. When you file your return, the CP500 instalments you paid are credited against your actual tax, and any shortfall is a balance of tax to settle — which is exactly the kind of amount e-Ansuran can then spread.
- **CP204** is a company's own estimate, submitted online through the **e-Anggaran** service on MyTax (with revisions on Form **CP204A**). The estimate is paid in equal monthly instalments starting from the second month of the basis period, each due by the 15th of the month.

## Penalties for missing an instalment

Spreading payment does not soften the penalty regime — and one point catches individuals out repeatedly.

- **Individuals.** LHDN's own guidance states plainly that **even where an instalment appeal is approved, the late-payment penalty is still imposed.** A late balance of tax attracts a **10% increase** under **section 103** of the Income Tax Act 1967. In other words, an instalment plan buys you time to pay, not relief from the penalty on the amount that was already late.
- **Companies.** A missed **CP204** monthly instalment attracts a **10% increase** on the unpaid instalment under **section 107C(9)** of the Income Tax Act 1967. This is automatic and imposed without a prior notice.

Because the penalty follows the *lateness*, the discipline that matters is simple: whichever scheme you are on, pay each instalment on or before its due date.

## How you actually pay: ByrHASiL and FPX

Every instalment — whether under e-Ansuran, CP500 or CP204 — is paid through **ByrHASiL**, LHDN's online payment service. ByrHASiL lets you pay "through banks that are members of FPX online," i.e. over the **FPX** gateway straight from your bank account.

To pay, you use your **bill number**, which you can look up with the **e-Billing** application. ByrHASiL is reachable from the MyTax portal or directly at `byrhasil.hasil.gov.my`, and it is the channel LHDN points taxpayers to for income tax, CP204 and instalment payments alike.

## What's next

If you owe a balance of tax and cannot clear it at once, log in to MyTax and check whether **e-Ansuran** covers your situation — for a simple income tax debt above RM300, it is the fastest route and the approval is automatic. If instead you have received a **CP500** notice, treat it as a prepayment schedule for the current year and consider a **CP502** revision before 30 June if the estimate no longer fits your income; if you run a company, keep the **CP204** monthly instalments paid by the 15th to avoid the automatic 10% increase.

Whichever applies, remember the two things that trip people up: an instalment arrangement does not erase the late-payment penalty on tax that was already overdue, and every instalment is settled through ByrHASiL over FPX using your bill number. When the numbers are large or your situation is unusual, confirm the current position with LHDN directly or a licensed tax agent before you commit to a plan.
