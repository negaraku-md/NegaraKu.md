Every baby born at a government clinic or hospital in Malaysia goes home with more than just a temporary birth certificate — they go home with a small booklet that will be brought to *every* clinic visit for the next six years. That booklet, and the vaccination schedule it records, is part of one of the longest-running public health programmes in the country.

Many parents know that vaccinations are "free at government clinics." Fewer know that this is actually a structured programme with its own name, history and official documentation — and why a schedule copied from a WhatsApp group last year might no longer match the current version.

## What the National Immunisation Programme (NIP) is

The National Immunisation Programme (NIP) is the government's childhood vaccination programme, run by the Ministry of Health Malaysia (KKM). It has been running since the early 1950s, built on the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) framework recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO), and has since been expanded to protect Malaysian children against 13 major infectious diseases.

According to Immunise4Life — a health education initiative run jointly with KKM, the Malaysian Paediatric Association (MPA) and the Malaysian Society of Infectious Diseases & Chemotherapy (MSIDC) — the diseases covered under the NIP include:

| Disease group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Respiratory/systemic diseases | diphtheria, whooping cough (pertussis), tetanus, tuberculosis (TB) |
| Specific bacterial infections | *Haemophilus influenzae* type b (Hib), pneumococcal infection |
| Hepatitis and other viral diseases | hepatitis B, polio, measles, mumps, rubella |
| Other specific diseases | Japanese encephalitis, human papillomavirus (HPV) |

This list illustrates the programme's scope in general terms. The age, number of doses and sequence of vaccinations for each disease are set out in the National Immunisation Schedule — and that must be checked directly with KKM or a clinic, not from a general list like the one above.

## Why it's free, and where

All vaccines under the NIP are given **free** to every infant and child at government health clinics (Klinik Kesihatan) across the country. This differs from private clinics, which may provide the same vaccinations (or additional vaccines not included in the NIP) for a fee set at each clinic's own rate.

This means citizenship status or location does not block access — every baby born and raised in Malaysia is entitled to the full NIP schedule at a nearby government facility, regardless of which clinic the parents registered at during pregnancy.

## The Health Record Book: the document that connects every visit

Every baby born in Malaysia is issued an **Infant and Child Health Record Book (0-6 Years)**. This book is not just a keepsake — it's an official health document that records growth, development, and every vaccination received, complete with the vaccine type and injection date written in by the attending nurse.

Parents are encouraged to **bring this book to every clinic or hospital visit**, whether government or private. This matters for two reasons:

- It allows any healthcare staff — not just the original clinic — to quickly check the child's immunisation history.
- It prevents vaccinations from being unnecessarily repeated, or missed because of incomplete records.

If the book is lost, the clinic where the child was originally registered is usually the first place to ask about a replacement or a copy of the record.

## Common mistakes

**Keeping an old schedule chart as a permanent reference.** The National Immunisation Schedule is reviewed and updated periodically by the Health Education Division, KKM — the official infographic currently in circulation, for example, was updated in August 2023, replacing the previous version. A chart saved on a phone from a few years ago may no longer reflect the current sequence.

**Equating "free" with "the same everywhere."** The NIP is free at government health clinics. Private clinics may charge even for the same vaccines, and may also offer additional vaccines outside the NIP's scope — that's a separate choice, not part of the government programme.

**Not bringing the Health Record Book.** Without this book, clinic staff have to rely on the parents' memory or system records that may be incomplete, especially if the child has been treated at more than one facility.

## Where to check for the actual schedule

This article deliberately does not state specific vaccination ages or doses, because these details change with KKM updates and are easily thrown off if copied from an outdated third-party source. For the current, valid schedule:

- Check directly with the nurse or doctor at the health clinic during a scheduled visit.
- Check official publications on KKM's Info Sihat portal (infosihat.moh.gov.my), which periodically issues infographics and guidelines for the National Immunisation Schedule.
- Refer to the child's own Health Record Book — it records the actual schedule the child has followed and will follow.
