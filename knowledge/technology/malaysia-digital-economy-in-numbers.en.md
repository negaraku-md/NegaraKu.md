Every time someone says "Malaysia's digital economy is worth RM451 billion," the figure is correct — but it is almost never accompanied by what it actually measures. It is not online sales, not tech-company revenue, and not e-commerce transaction value. It is a value-added measure, and three different metrics are often lumped together as one.

Here are the official figures, and what each one actually means.

## How big is Malaysia's digital economy?

The Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) measures the digital economy through the ICT Satellite Account, which sets the combined contribution of the ICT and e-commerce industries to Gross Domestic Product (GDP). It consists of two parts: the ICT industry's Gross Value Added (GVAICT), and the value added by e-commerce in **non**-ICT industries.

| Year | Contribution to GDP | Value | GVAICT | E-commerce (non-ICT) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2023 | 23.5% | RM427.7 billion | 13.8% | 9.6% |
| 2024 | 23.4% | RM451.3 billion | 13.9% | 9.5% |

The contribution rose in ringgit terms even though the percentage dipped slightly in 2024 — because overall GDP also grew. Growth in ICT and e-commerce activity was recorded at 5.1 percent for 2024, compared with 3.5 percent in 2023 (both figures from the Malaysia Digital Economy 2025 release).

## What is the difference between GVAICT, e-commerce GVA and e-commerce income?

This is the biggest source of confusion in coverage of Malaysia's digital economy. Three figures exist, all official, and all measuring different things.

- **GVAICT** — value added by the ICT industry itself: RM252.0 billion in 2023. Its breakdown is ICT services (41.6%), ICT manufacturing (38.2%), ICT trade (14.2%) and content media (6.0%).
- **E-commerce GVA** — value added by e-commerce activity across **all** sectors: RM248.2 billion in 2023, or 13.6 percent of GDP. Manufacturing contributed 51.8 percent and services 45.2 percent.
- **E-commerce transaction income** — the gross transaction value, not value added: **RM1,230.1 billion** in 2024 (RM1,184.1 billion in 2023, RM1,126.9 billion in 2022).

Note the order of magnitude. E-commerce value added (RM248.2 billion) and e-commerce transaction value (over RM1.2 trillion) differ by almost fivefold because one counts the value that is **added** and the other counts the total **money that changes hands**. Citing the larger figure as "the size of the digital economy" is one of the most frequent mistakes.

## Who works in the digital economy?

ICT industry employment reached **1.24 million people** in 2023, growing 1.6 percent and representing 7.8 percent of Malaysia's total employment. Its breakdown by sector is manufacturing (35.6%), services (29.2%) and trade (22.4%) — a reminder that "the digital economy" in Malaysia is rooted in electronics manufacturing and not solely software companies.

## How many Malaysians connect to the internet?

Penetration is measured separately, through DOSM's ICT Use and Access Report. The figures for 2025:

| Indicator | 2025 rate |
| --- | --- |
| Internet use (individuals) | 98.3% |
| Household internet access | 97.1% |
| Household internet access — urban | 99.0% |
| Household internet access — rural | 90.7% |
| Mobile phone use | 99.6% |
| Computer use | 81.5% |

Individual internet use rose from 98.0 percent in 2024 to 98.3 percent in 2025 — already approaching saturation. The gap that remains is the urban–rural gap in household access: 99.0 percent in urban areas versus 90.7 percent in rural areas. Broadband penetration rates (mobile and fixed) are published separately by the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission, not by DOSM.

## Which figures are most often misused?

**Equating RM451.3 billion with online sales.** It is the combined ICT and e-commerce value-added contribution to GDP, not sales revenue.

**Adding GVAICT and e-commerce GVA together.** The two overlap — some ICT value is also counted in e-commerce — so RM252.0 billion plus RM248.2 billion is **not** the size of the digital economy.

**Citing RM1.2 trillion without the "transaction value" label.** The e-commerce income figure measures the total money flowing, not the value generated, and cannot be compared with GDP directly.

**Assuming a falling percentage means the digital economy is shrinking.** The contribution fell from 23.5 to 23.4 percent between 2023 and 2024 even though its ringgit value rose by RM23.6 billion.

## What's next

Before citing any of Malaysia's digital economy figures, first establish which metric you mean: GDP contribution (RM451.3 billion), value added (GVAICT or e-commerce GVA), or transaction value (RM1,230.1 billion). For the policy context and incentives that support the sector, see [MDEC's Digital Status](/business/mdec-digital-status) and the [Digital Free Trade Zone](/business/digital-free-trade-zone). The full ICT Satellite Account figures are updated annually by DOSM, usually in the fourth quarter, so recheck the reference year each time.
