When Malaysia decided that "digital" was too important to share with any other ministry, a new ministry was born. The Ministry of Digital is the country's technology policy control centre — home to the digital economy, cybersecurity and personal data protection under one roof.

## What is the Ministry of Digital and why does it exist?

The Ministry of Digital was established on **12 December 2023** during a Cabinet reshuffle by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. It was not a ministry built entirely from scratch: it was born by restructuring and separating the digital functions from the former Ministry of Communications and Digital, so that the delivery of the digital agenda would become more focused, holistic and efficient.

The first Minister of Digital was **Gobind Singh Deo**. He once described the establishment of the ministry as a challenge because its foundations had to be laid from the ground up, yet he confirmed that the ministry was able to carry out its duties effectively after roughly ten months of operation.

The ministry's focus is anchored on three main agendas: the digital economy, the digital society and the digital government.

## Who falls under the Ministry of Digital?

To carry out its mandate, the ministry oversees **two departments and four agencies**. One of the most notable moves was the restructuring of the Malaysian Administrative Modernisation and Management Planning Unit (MAMPU) into the **National Digital Department (JDN)**, which now leads the digital government transformation.

The placement of agencies was announced in phases. In December 2023, it was reported that MAMPU (now JDN), MDEC, MYNIC and the Personal Data Protection Department were placed under the ministry. Later, on 10 January 2024, it was announced that Digital Nasional Berhad and CyberSecurity Malaysia were also placed under the Ministry of Digital.

| --- | --- | --- |
| National Digital Department (JDN) | Department | Digital government transformation; result of the restructuring of MAMPU |
| Personal Data Protection Department (JPDP) | Department | Enforces personal data protection |

## Are MCMC and NACSA part of this ministry?

This is a common confusion. The **Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC)** — the regulator of communications infrastructure — remains under the **Ministry of Communications**, not the Ministry of Digital. The two ministries are separate even though their remits often border one another.

The **National Cyber Security Agency (NACSA)**, meanwhile, is the national lead agency for cybersecurity matters, formally established in February 2017. According to NACSA, its mandate includes formulating national-level cybersecurity policies and strategies as well as coordinating the Malaysia Cyber Security Strategy. Malaysia's cybersecurity functions are therefore shared: CyberSecurity Malaysia serves as the technical body under the Ministry of Digital, while the coordination of national-level cyber policy and strategy is led by NACSA.

## What is the ministry's real impact?

For investors and the public, the ministry is the focal point for the country's digital policy. The digital economy is reported to contribute about **23.2% of GDP**, with a target of reaching at least **25.5% by the end of 2025**. Foreign direct investment in the technology sector is also strong, with a figure of **USD16.9 billion** reported for 2024 from leading technology companies such as Amazon Web Services, Microsoft, Google and Oracle.

On the infrastructure side, DNB's 5G network coverage was reported to have reached **82.1% of populated areas as of October 2024**, up from **80.2%** the previous year — an indication of how quickly the country's digital backbone is being built.

## What's next

The Ministry of Digital is still young and its structure continues to be refined.
