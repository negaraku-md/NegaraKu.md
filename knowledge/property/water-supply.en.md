You move from Selangor to Johor. Your bank account stays the same. Your phone line can come with you. But your water account? It doesn't follow — because the company supplying water to your new home is a completely different company altogether.

Many people assume water, like electricity, is supplied by a single national entity. It isn't. Every state in Malaysia — generally speaking — has its own water company, which means every time you move between states, you need to open an entirely new water account.

## Why not a single national company

This arrangement stems from a reform of the water services industry around 2006. Before that, water was generally an entirely state matter. After the reform, a federal company named **Pengurusan Aset Air Berhad (PAAB)** took over the water infrastructure assets (treatment plants, pipes, reservoirs) from the states, while also taking over the associated loans — allowing state operating companies to focus entirely on day-to-day service rather than capital financing.

At the oversight level, the **National Water Services Commission (SPAN)** acts as the technical and economic regulator for the water and sewerage industry in Peninsular Malaysia and the Federal Territory of Labuan — setting service licences, monitoring operators' key performance indicators (KPIs), and handling consumer complaints.

But **day-to-day operations** — your tap water, meter readings, monthly bills, and customer service — remain in the hands of the water company owned or appointed by each state government. Examples listed on SPAN's official website:

| State | Water company |
| --- | --- |
| Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya | Air Selangor (Pengurusan Air Selangor Sdn Bhd) |
| Johor | Ranhill SAJ |
| Penang | Perbadanan Bekalan Air Pulau Pinang (PBAPP) |
| Perak | Lembaga Air Perak |
| Kelantan | Air Kelantan |
| Terengganu | Syarikat Air Terengganu |
| Melaka | Syarikat Air Melaka |
| Perlis | Syarikat Air Perlis |

Pahang, Negeri Sembilan and Kedah also each have their own state water company according to SPAN's official listing. One interesting point: according to Ranhill SAJ's own official website, the company that supplies water throughout the state of Johor is **the only privately-owned water supply operator in Malaysia** — every other state water company is listed as owned by its respective state government.

## Sabah and Sarawak: a separate arrangement

Sabah and Sarawak are not included in the same PAAB model as the Peninsula. According to PAAB itself, the federal government no longer provides loans to state governments for water infrastructure under this framework, **except to Sabah and Sarawak** — indicating that both states continue to operate under their own arrangements and financing, separate from SPAN's oversight, which covers the Peninsula and Labuan.

## How to open a new water account

The actual process differs slightly by company, but the general concept is similar in most states:

1. **Identify the state water company** operating at your premises' location (refer to the table above or the span.gov.my website for the full list).
2. **Register a user profile** through the relevant company's official web portal or app — for example, Air Selangor's official website and app allow online registration with verification via email and phone OTP — or in person at a customer service counter.
3. **For a new connection** (a house or premises that has never had a water account before), complete the application form together with supporting documents: a copy of the applicant's identity card, and proof of ownership or tenancy of the premises such as a sale and purchase agreement or tenancy agreement.
4. **For an existing premises** changing hands to a new owner or tenant, the relevant application is a transfer of account ownership, not a new connection registration — this process can also usually be done online at most state water companies.
5. **Pay the deposit** set by the company before supply is connected or a meter is installed. The deposit amount and any installation charges are set by the relevant water company and differ by state and premises type — check directly with your state water company for current figures.
6. Once the account is active, bills are issued periodically (usually monthly) based on meter readings, and can be checked and paid through the relevant company's portal or app.

## The concept of a water bill

A water bill is generally calculated based on **actual meter readings** — the amount of water actually used — rather than a fixed charge regardless of usage. Rate structures usually differentiate between premises types (residential versus commercial or industrial) and may involve tiered rates based on the volume consumed. However, **the exact rate is determined by each state water company** (subject to SPAN's approval for areas under its oversight), so it differs between states and is not uniform across Malaysia — which is why this article does not state any specific tariff figures.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming a single water company serves the whole of Malaysia.** Moving states means dealing with an entirely different water company, not a branch of your old one.
- **Applying for a new connection when what is actually needed is a transfer of account ownership** (for existing premises changing owner/tenant) — these are two different processes at most water companies.
- **Not preparing proof of ownership or tenancy of the premises.** A copy of an identity card alone is usually not sufficient for a new connection application.
- **Assuming the billing rate in one state matches another state.** Because rates are set by each state company individually, an estimate from your old area does not necessarily apply at your new location.
