Before you compare a single ringgit of pricing, there's a question that matters
more: does fibre even reach your front door? Sign a two-year contract before
checking that, and you may find out on installation day that the technician
can't actually pull a line into your unit.

## Coverage decides more than brand loyalty does

Malaysia's fibre rollout has been driven for years by **JENDELA** (Jalinan
Digital Negara), the national connectivity push run under the Malaysian
Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC). Its first phase set a target
of extending fibre access to millions of premises nationwide and widening
gigabit-capable coverage — a push built on top of the country's existing
High Speed Broadband (HSBB) backbone.

That backbone is owned by **Telekom Malaysia (TM)**, which is why **Unifi**
tends to have the widest reach of any single brand, including in towns and
suburbs where other ISPs haven't built out. But "wide" isn't "everywhere" —
coverage still varies address by address, which is why every major provider
puts a coverage checker front and centre on its own site rather than asking
you to just sign up and see.

## The main players

| Provider | Where it tends to be strong | What it's known for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unifi (TM)** | Nationwide — cities, suburbs and many regional towns | Widest coverage; also sells Unifi Air (wireless) for spots fibre hasn't reached, plus Fibre-To-The-Room add-ons |
| **Maxis** | Major urban areas; strongest where you're already a Maxis mobile customer | Family plans that bundle home fibre with multiple mobile lines |
| **TIME** | Grew out of high-rise condominiums, now expanding into landed housing areas across the Klang Valley, Penang, Johor and beyond | Building-focused installs and higher-speed tiers in towers it already serves |

Other names worth knowing: **CelcomDigi Home Fibre** and **Astro/YTL's Yes**
also sell fixed home broadband in overlapping areas. None of this changes the
first step — check coverage at your specific address with each provider
before you compare anything else.

## What installation actually involves

A standard installation is one technician visit. It typically includes:

1. **A quick site check** — confirming where the fibre line enters the
   building and the best path to your unit.
2. **Running the fibre to an Optical Network Terminal (ONT)** — the small box
   installed inside your home that converts the fibre signal for your router.
3. **Testing the line** before the technician leaves.

In condominiums, this usually runs through infrastructure that's already
built into the tower — a telecom room and cable risers running up through
each floor, laid out to a technical standard published by MCMC and the
Malaysian Technical Standards Forum Bhd (MTSFB). That standardisation is
exactly why more than one ISP can usually serve the same building: the riser
path already exists: no ISP has to build its own from scratch. Older
buildings that predate the standard, or landed homes in newly developed
areas, can take an extra site visit if new external cabling is needed.

## Choosing between them

- **Check coverage at your exact unit, not just your neighbourhood.** A
  building two doors down being "covered" doesn't guarantee yours is.
- **Look at the contract length, not just the speed tier.** Home fibre plans
  are commonly sold on a fixed term (24 months is typical) — cancelling early
  usually carries a penalty, so match the term to how long you expect to stay.
- **Check what you're already paying for.** If you're on a Maxis or Unifi
  mobile line, a bundled family or household plan can be worth more than a
  cheaper standalone offer elsewhere.
- **Have a fallback for fibre-poor addresses.** Wireless home broadband
  (such as Unifi Air) or a 5G home plan can bridge the gap in areas where
  fixed fibre hasn't been built yet.

## Common mistakes

- **Signing up before checking your exact address.** Marketing pages show
  speeds; only the coverage checker shows whether they apply to you.
- **Assuming a high-rise unit automatically has fibre-ready risers.** Older
  buildings, or ones built before the in-building cabling standard was
  common practice, sometimes need extra approval or wiring work.
- **Judging providers purely on advertised speed.** A slower plan on a
  provider you're already bundled with can end up more useful than a faster
  one from a brand you have no other relationship with.
- **Ignoring the contract term.** A cheap-looking monthly rate on a 24-month
  lock-in is a bigger commitment than a plan advertised at a higher price
  with no contract.
