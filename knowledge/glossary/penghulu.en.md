**Bahasa Malaysia:** Penghulu · **English:** Penghulu (head of a mukim) · **中文:** 乡长（mukim 之首长）

Many people assume a penghulu is the head of a single kampung. It is not. A
penghulu is the head of a *mukim* — a larger area that takes in several kampung
at once. So when someone calls their village head a "penghulu", they are in fact
promoting the post by one rung, wrongly. This small distinction matters because
it determines who reports to whom in Malaysia's local administration.

## The mukim and its head

A mukim is an administrative unit that sits below the district. A district is
divided into several mukim, and each mukim in turn contains several
[kampung](/en/glossary/kampung). In the Kamus Dewan, a mukim is defined, among
other things, as an area under a penghulu — and a penghulu is in turn defined as
"the head of a mukim".

In other words, a penghulu is the person who leads a mukim. In modern
administration, he is not merely a traditional chief but an officer appointed by
the state government and serving under the district office. This framework makes
the penghulu part of the machinery of
[local government](/en/government/local-government-in-malaysia), linking the
district office above with the village heads below.

## Penghulu, ketua kampung or ADUN?

These three posts are often confused because all three "look after the people"
at the local level — but their functions and the way a person holds the post
differ greatly.

| Post | Area | How the post is held | Main role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **District Officer** | One district (several mukim) | Appointed government officer | Heads the district administration |
| **Penghulu** | One mukim (several kampung) | Appointed by the state government | Government–people liaison officer at the mukim level |
| **Ketua kampung (village head)** | One kampung | Appointed | Heads a single kampung |
| **ADUN** | One state constituency (DUN) | Elected at the polls | People's representative on the political side |

The most basic difference: the penghulu and the village head are on the
appointed **administrative** side, while the
[ADUN](/en/glossary/dewan-undangan-negeri) is on the **political** side elected
by vote. A penghulu does not contest elections, and an ADUN does not administer
a mukim.

## What a penghulu handles

A penghulu's scope of work is broad because he is the point of contact between
the government and the residents of the mukim. For example, the Penghulu Grade
NP5 service scheme in Johor designates the penghulu as a liaison officer between
the government and the people for matters relating to:

- **Land administration** — assisting with land matters at the mukim level.
- **Islamic religious administration** — religious affairs within his area.
- **Security and development** — local programmes and projects.
- **Social and community welfare activities** — at the kampung, mukim, district
  and state levels.

The details of the scheme, grade and conditions of appointment can vary from
state to state, but the core of the role is the same across the Peninsula: to be
the official bridge between the district office and the residents below it.

## Common mistakes

- **Equating a penghulu with a village head.** A penghulu heads one mukim
  containing several kampung; a village head heads only one kampung. A penghulu
  is one rung higher.
- **Assuming a penghulu is elected at the polls.** A penghulu is a post
  appointed by the state government, not a political seat like an ADUN.
- **Thinking "penghulu" has only one meaning.** The Kamus Dewan also records
  other meanings, including a customary chief in the Negeri Sembilan context —
  but the meaning most commonly used today is the head of a mukim in
  administration.
