Almost every bowl of local rice in Malaysia traces back to a variety bred in a MARDI field station. Behind the country's padi, poultry, tropical fruit and processed-food shelves sits one federal institute whose job is to turn science into higher yields.

## What is MARDI, exactly?

The Malaysian Agricultural Research and Development Institute (MARDI) is a federal statutory body under the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security. It was officially established on 28 October 1969 under the MARDI Act (Act 11) and began full operations in 1971. Its founding purpose is to generate and promote "new, appropriate, and efficient technologies to advance the food, agriculture, and agro-based industries."

In plain terms, MARDI does the research so that farmers and agribusinesses do not have to. It breeds crop varieties, designs farming and post-harvest systems, and hands the results to the industry.

## How is MARDI governed and organised?

MARDI operates under policies set by the MARDI Governing Board, whose decisions require the approval of the Minister of Agriculture and Food Security. Technical quality is overseen by the MARDI Science Council. The institute's strategic direction has shifted over the decades from building basic research capacity toward technology commercialisation and a culture of excellence.

Research is grouped into ten research areas:

| Research area | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Paddy & Rice | Rice breeding, production and post-harvest systems |
| Horticulture | Fruits, vegetables and ornamentals |
| Industrial Crops | Non-food and plantation crops |
| Livestock Science | Animal production and health |
| Food Science & Technology | Processing and food product development |
| Biotechnology & Nanotechnology | Molecular breeding and advanced tools |
| Engineering | Farm machinery and automation |
| Soil Science, Water & Fertilizer | Inputs and land management |
| Agrobiodiversity & Environment | Genetic resources and sustainability |
| Economics & Social Science | Farm economics and adoption studies |

## What has MARDI produced for rice farmers?

Rice is MARDI's most visible output. The paddy and rice programme develops hybrid, inbred and specialty varieties, production systems, pest-management technology and post-harvest handling methods. Its released varieties dominate Malaysian padi fields.

A sample of MARDI rice varieties and their reported yield potential:

| Variety | Type | Reported yield |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MR 219 | Inbred white rice | up to 10.5 t/ha |
| MR 297 | Inbred white rice | 9.28-9.52 t/ha; ~50% of peninsular padi area (2021) |
| MR 326 (Kesidang) | White rice, introduced 2024 | 4.8-7.3 t/ha |
| MR 333 (Menora) | White rice, declared 2025 | 5-9 t/ha |
| KADARIA 1 | Malaysia's first hybrid rice | 9.7-12 t/ha in fertile areas |

Beyond staples, MARDI has released fragrant MRQ varieties (such as MRQ 76 and MRQ 104), glutinous types (Pulut Malaysia 1, Pulut Hitam 9), coloured rice (MRM 16 red), and herbicide-resistant Clearfield lines (MR CL3, MR CL4). MARDI also curates a national crop genebank that conserves more than 16,000 plant accessions, of which rice — about 13,020 accessions — is the largest single collection.

## Where does MARDI fit in national padi programmes?

Malaysia's flagship effort to close its rice self-sufficiency gap is the Large-Scale SMART Paddy Field programme, known as SMART SBB or "Mini Sekinchan." Led by the Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security, it consolidates individual smallholdings into systematically managed blocks using transplanters and Internet-of-Things monitoring.

The programme aims to raise yields from an average of about 3.75 t/ha toward a target of seven to ten tonnes per hectare per farmer. By mid-2023 it covered more than 11,000 hectares nationwide, involved roughly 5,000 farmers, and had lifted rice yields by more than 50 per cent in participating areas.

MARDI's role is upstream: it supplies the high-yielding seed varieties, breeding know-how and precision-farming technology that make such yield jumps possible. The institute develops the genetics; the ministry deploys them at scale.

## What's next

MARDI's trajectory points toward commercialisation and climate-resilient farming — herbicide-tolerant and hybrid rice, biotechnology-assisted breeding, and precision agriculture that feeds programmes like SMART SBB. For anyone tracking Malaysian food security, watch MARDI's variety releases and its collaboration with the ministry on national self-sufficiency targets. For primary detail, the institute's own profile and paddy-and-rice research pages are the most authoritative starting points, and the MARDI Act (Act 11) sets out its statutory mandate.
