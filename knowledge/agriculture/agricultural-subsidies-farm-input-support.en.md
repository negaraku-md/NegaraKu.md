The sack of fertiliser the paddy farmer carried to his field that morning was not bought with his own money — it came free of charge, borne by the Federal Government for decades. This is the true face of Malaysia's agricultural subsidies for paddy: not a cheque or money credited to an account, but fertiliser, pesticides and incentives that cover a large share of the costs at the farm.

These schemes are specific to the paddy industry and entirely separate from the diesel or cooking oil subsidies that the public knows better. This guide sets out the main input schemes, their rates, and who administers them.

## Why is paddy subsidised differently from other crops?

Paddy is a strategic crop — it is the foundation of the country's food security. The government aims to reduce reliance on rice imports and raise the incomes of paddy farmers, most of whom are smallholders with thin margins.

For this reason, paddy support is arranged as a bundle of schemes: some cover **input** costs (fertiliser, seed, pesticides), some support **output prices** (paddy price subsidy), and some take the form of **cash incentives** for specific operations such as ploughing. It is this combination that is reported to reach roughly RM4,300 per hectare per season according to Budget 2026.

## Which scheme covers the paddy farmer's fertiliser costs?

The core is the **Federal Government Paddy Fertiliser Scheme (SBPKP)**. According to an official reply from the Selangor State Assembly, this scheme supplies each paddy farmer with:

- **NPK compound fertiliser** — 240 kg per hectare (12 bags)
- **Urea fertiliser** — 80 kg per hectare (4 bags)

This fertiliser is distributed in physical form according to the paddy field fertilisation schedule, not as money. Compound fertiliser is usually applied in stages according to the schedule set by the area agency, depending on the formulation supplied.

Alongside it, the **Paddy Production Incentive Scheme (SIPP)** — listed by KPKM under the Paddy Industry Development Division — adds additional NPK fertiliser, liming to rehabilitate acidic soils, and pest control support. Separately, Budget 2026 sets the pest control (pesticide) incentive at **RM300 per hectare per season** according to Free Malaysia Today reports; that source does not specifically label this amount as part of SIPP, and it is higher than the RM250 per hectare recorded in a Selangor State Assembly reply in 2019.

## The main paddy input and incentive schemes

The table below summarises the paddy-specific schemes and the rates that can be verified from official sources and news reports. Note that two of the cited sources give different ploughing incentive rates — see the note after the table:

| Scheme | Type of assistance | Main rate (source) |
|---|---|---|
| SBPKP — Federal Government Paddy Fertiliser Scheme | Physical fertiliser | Compound 240 kg/ha (12 bags) + urea 80 kg/ha (4 bags) (Selangor State Assembly, 2019) |
| SIPP — Paddy Production Incentive Scheme | Additional NPK, liming of acidic soils, pest control support | Components listed by KPKM; exact rates not stated in the official sources reviewed |
| Pest control (pesticide) incentive | Input incentive | RM300/ha per season (Budget 2026, Free Malaysia Today) |
| SSHP — Paddy Price Subsidy Scheme | Output price subsidy | RM500/metric tonne (up from RM360) (MOF, Budget 2024) |
| Ploughing incentive | Cash incentive for ploughing costs | RM160/ha per season (Budget 2026, FMT) — but eCentral (May 2026) cites RM300/ha; see note |
| Harvesting incentive (new) | Assistance for harvesting diesel costs | RM50/ha per season (Budget 2026, FMT) |

**Note — ploughing rate discrepancy:** Free Malaysia Today, reporting the Budget 2026 announcement, cites a ploughing incentive of RM160/ha per season. In contrast, a third-party source (eCentral, May 2026) states the ploughing incentive amounts to RM300/ha per season with an advance payment of RM200/ha. Both figures are retained here transparently; the actual rate for the current season should be confirmed directly with KPKM.

KPKM also lists other schemes under the Paddy Industry Development Division, including **SBRPB** (Hill/Upland Paddy Fertiliser and Pesticide Scheme) for upland paddy and **IBPS** (Certified Paddy Seed Incentive) to encourage the use of quality seed.

## How large is the government's fiscal commitment?

The allocation figure rises every year. MOF announced RM2.6 billion for paddy farmer and fisherman subsidies and incentives in Budget 2024. BERNAMA reported that the allocation to paddy farmers was raised by about 5.5 percent for Budget 2025.

By Budget 2026, that figure rose to **RM2.62 billion** — described as the highest paddy farmer subsidy in the country's history. Free Malaysia Today reported that average assistance per farmer increased to roughly **RM4,300 per hectare per season** compared with RM3,790 previously, covering among other things the RM160/ha ploughing incentive, the RM300/ha pesticide incentive, and the new RM50/ha harvesting incentive for diesel costs.

## How is the paddy price also supported?

Beyond inputs, the government covers the price side. In Budget 2024, MOF announced that the paddy price subsidy was raised from RM360 to **RM500 per metric tonne**, while the guaranteed minimum price (floor price) of paddy was raised from RM1,200 to **RM1,300 per metric tonne**. This gives paddy farmers a more certain return when their harvest is sold, regardless of market price fluctuations.

## How does the assistance reach the paddy farmer?

The flow involves several layers:

1. **Registration** — the paddy farmer registers and is verified as an active paddy field operator through an agricultural agency.
2. **Input distribution** — fertiliser and pesticides are supplied physically according to the registered paddy field area through suppliers and farmers' organisations.
3. **Cash incentives** — payments such as the ploughing incentive are disbursed by season. According to a third-party source (eCentral, not an official KPKM channel), KPKM also makes an **advance payment** of the ploughing incentive before the season begins to ease the paddy farmer's initial working capital, before the balance is paid after verification — this detail still needs to be confirmed with an official KPKM announcement.

Overall administration is under KPKM, with field implementation by the Farmers' Organisation Authority (LPP) and granary area authorities such as MADA and KADA.

## What to watch out for

The rates and structure of these schemes are reviewed at each annual budget — the ploughing incentive, for example, was once recorded at RM100/ha (Selangor State Assembly, 2019) before being adjusted in the latest budget. As noted above, different sources also give differing ploughing rates for the current period (RM160/ha versus RM300/ha), so paddy farmers should refer to official announcements by KPKM and area agencies for the current season's rates.

As a matter of public policy, the paddy subsidy scheme is also not without debate. Issues such as leakage of assistance, distribution efficiency, and the continued dependence of paddy farmers on subsidies are frequently raised in policy discussions of the country's rice sector. This document focuses on the structure and rates of the schemes, not an assessment of their effectiveness or distributional impact.

For your next steps, check the latest planting season announcements on the KPKM website and with your local farmers' organisation, confirm the registration status of your paddy field, and compare the current season's rates with the figures stated here — this document is an AI-assisted draft and is not a substitute for official agency information.
