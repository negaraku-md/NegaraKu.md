Two agencies rarely mentioned in the same breath, yet both touch the fortunes of hundreds of thousands of rural families: one restores idle land, the other ensures old rubber trees are replaced before smallholders' incomes dry up. That is FELCRA and RISDA.

Both were born of Malaysia's rural development policy in the 1960s and 1970s, with the same goal — to raise the living standards of low-income rural communities — but by different paths.

## What is FELCRA's role?

FELCRA was established on **1 April 1966** under the Federal Land Consolidation and Rehabilitation Authority Act 1966. Its very name explains its mandate: two distinct but complementary core functions.

- **Consolidation:** combining smallholders' scattered and small parcels of land so that they can be worked collectively and more economically.
- **Rehabilitation:** restoring idle or problematic land so that it becomes productive again.

On **1 September 1997**, FELCRA changed from a statutory body into a corporate entity — **FELCRA Berhad**. In this corporate form, FELCRA Berhad is a government-linked company (GLC) **wholly owned by the Minister of Finance Incorporated (MoF Inc)**.

As of September 2022, FELCRA Berhad had developed **203,953.4 hectares** of land for **107,130 participants** through **1,366 projects** of consolidation and rehabilitation, in addition to owning **31,613.3 hectares** of its own commercial estates.

## What is RISDA's role?

RISDA — the Rubber Industry Smallholders Development Authority — was established on **1 January 1973** under the Rubber Industry Smallholders Development Authority Act 1972 (**Act 85**). It sits under the Ministry of Rural Development.

Unlike FELCRA, which spans multiple crops, RISDA focuses on the **rubber smallholder sector**. Among its core tasks:

- Managing **replanting** schemes (replacing old rubber trees with new, more productive clones).
- Providing technical services, training and entrepreneurship development.
- Improving the socioeconomic standing of smallholders through modernisation and organised marketing.

RISDA has stated a target of ensuring that national rubber output reaches **1 million metric tonnes a year by the end of 2030**.

## How do the two differ?

| Aspect | FELCRA (FELCRA Berhad) | RISDA |
|---|---|---|
| Established | 1 April 1966 | 1 January 1973 |
| Legal basis | Federal Land Consolidation and Rehabilitation Authority Act 1966 | Act 85 (RISDA) 1972 |
| Focus | Land consolidation & rehabilitation, multiple crops | Rubber smallholder development |
| Status | Incorporated (FELCRA Berhad, 1997) | Federal statutory body |
| Ownership / oversight | GLC wholly owned by the Minister of Finance Incorporated (MoF Inc) | Federal agency under the Ministry of Rural Development |

## Why are there proposals to consolidate these agencies?

Because of overlapping scope and smallholders split across several agencies, the idea of uniting them is not new.

On **25 August 2009**, the then Prime Minister, Datuk Seri Najib Tun Razak, called on FELDA, FELCRA and RISDA to form a consortium to explore upstream-downstream projects jointly — including sharing mills, plantation technology and marketing networks.

The issue resurfaced in **March 2023**. FGV Group Chief Executive Officer Datuk Mohd Nazrul Izam Mansor urged the government to unite the fragmented oil palm smallholders of FELDA, FELCRA and RISDA under a **single entity**. The rationale is tied to new regulatory pressures, particularly the European Union Deforestation Regulation (**EUDR**), which demands traceability of produce. According to FGV, consolidation would make it easier for smallholders to meet **MSPO** and **RSPO** certification, and thus enjoy premium prices for fresh fruit bunches.

FGV cited that there are more than **440,000 smallholders** in Malaysia with an oil palm planted area of **1.48 million hectares** — a scale that, if organised, would be easier to manage for sustainability.

Even so, this proposal remains a policy discussion. As far as the referenced sources indicate, no full merger of these agencies has been implemented.
