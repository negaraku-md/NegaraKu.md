Between the state you vote for and the village where you live, there is one layer of government that is rarely mentioned yet the one you encounter most often when dealing with land, development projects or emergency assistance: the district. This is where state policy turns into actual services, and its centre is the District Office.

## What is a district, and where does it sit in the structure of government?

Malaysia has three elected layers of government — Federal, state, and (in certain areas) local government. The district is not an elected layer; it is an administrative layer. Every state is divided into several districts so that the state government can deliver services closer to the people.

According to official data from the Department of Statistics Malaysia, there are **160 administrative districts** across the country. This figure includes four "states" that are not further subdivided into districts, namely Perlis and the three Federal Territories (Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya and Labuan).

The name of this layer is not the same everywhere. In most Peninsular states it is called a **daerah** (district). In Kelantan, the equivalent layer is called a **jajahan**. In Sabah and Sarawak, districts are grouped under a higher layer called a **bahagian** (division).

## How is a state divided into districts and mukims?

The legal framework for this division comes from the **National Land Code 1965 (NLC)**. Section 11 of the NLC, titled "Administrative areas", gives the State Authority the power — by notification in the Gazette — to:

- divide the area of the state into districts;
- divide any district into sub-districts;
- divide any district or sub-district into mukims;
- alter or amend the boundaries of any district, sub-district, mukim, town or village.

This means that district and mukim boundaries are not merely lines on a map — they are lawfully gazetted and form the basis of the country's entire land administration system. This layering can be summarised as follows.

| Layer | Common name | Main role |
|---|---|---|
| State | State / Federal Territory | State Authority; land policy and legislation |
| District | Daerah / Jajahan (Kelantan) / Bahagian (Sabah & Sarawak) | District administration and District Land Administrator |
| Mukim | Mukim / Town / Village | Land administration unit; area of the penghulu's jurisdiction |

## What does the District Office do?

The District Office is the administrative heart of this level. It is not merely a place to process forms — it coordinates almost every government function that touches the daily life of the district's residents.

The official portal of the District and Land Office lists **five core functions** carried out by a District Office:

| Function | Examples of dealings |
|---|---|
| General administration and finance | Office management, finance, district human resources |
| Social development | Community welfare, community programmes, social activities |
| Physical development | Coordination of district development and infrastructure projects |
| Mukim coordination | Linking the government with the penghulu and mukim residents |
| Security | Coordination of security and emergency/disaster response |

In terms of the individual's role, the District Officer acts as the **planner, coordinator, monitor and implementer** of programmes at the district level — thereby serving as the chief administrator of the district concerned.

## Who is the District Officer, and how is he appointed?

The District Officer is a permanent civil-service post, not a political position. According to the MyGovernment portal, a district is "led by a District Officer or Resident" who is "appointed by the State Public Service Commission (SPN) or the Malaysian Public Service".

This distinction matters. The Menteri Besar and State Assembly members are elected and may change after each election; the District Officer, by contrast, remains a career civil servant who ensures administrative continuity regardless of political change.

## How do land matters relate to district administration?

In most states, the District Office and the Land Office are combined into a **District and Land Office (PDT)**, and the same District Officer usually also holds the post of **Land Administrator** for that district.

The land division's functions are more specific. Based on the PDT's official list of functions, the Land Office's responsibilities include:

- assisting the state government to increase revenue (land is an important source of state income);
- ensuring that land development is carried out in an orderly and planned manner;
- keeping, managing and updating land title documents;
- controlling the disposal of and dealings in land in certain areas;
- advising the State Authority in the formulation of land policy.

At the lowest level, the **penghulu** exercises jurisdiction at the mukim — among other things, receiving notices from landowners about any changes to their land — while the **District Land Administrator** holds authority over related matters, including state land. It is this district–mukim–penghulu network that makes Malaysia's land administration function right down to the village level.

## Why does this district layer matter to you?

If you apply for a land grant, pay land tax, need help during a flood, or check the status of development in your area, that dealing most likely goes through the District Office. It is the layer of government closest to the land and the community — low enough to know local conditions, but structured enough to implement state policy uniformly.
