There's one question almost every Malaysian has gotten wrong at some point: who
is the Sultan of Perlis?

The answer — there isn't one. Perlis has no Sultan. It is the only state in
Malaysia whose head of state is styled **Raja**, and that title is not merely
a matter of wording. It comes from how the state was founded in 1843.

## Key facts

| | |
| --- | --- |
| State capital | Kangar |
| Land area | About 821 km² — the smallest in Malaysia |
| Population | About 0.3 million (DOSM 2025 estimate) |
| Population density | 365 people per km² (2025) vs the national average of 104 |
| Head of state | His Royal Highness the Raja of Perlis |
| State GDP | RM6.5 billion (2024), growing 3.3 percent |
| Seats | 3 parliamentary, 15 State Legislative Assembly |

## Why Raja and not Sultan

The official records of the Office of DYMM Tuanku Raja Perlis explain it
directly: in 1843, Raja Syed Hussein Jamalullail was formally installed as
**Phya Polit** in Siamese, or **Raja Perlis** in Malay. The full title
conferred by King Rama III was Phya Songkram Ramu Wichit Wilis Asmara Phya
Polit.

That Malay title — Raja — has remained in use from that day to the present.

Since that installation, Perlis has been ruled by a single family, the
Jamalullail dynasty, through seven Rajas:

| Raja | Reign |
| --- | --- |
| Raja Syed Hussein Jamalullail | 1843–1873 |
| Raja Syed Ahmad Jamalullail | 1873–1897 |
| Raja Syed Safi Jamalullail | 1897–1904 |
| Syed Alwi Jamalullail | 1905–1943 |
| Raja Syed Hamzah Jamalullail | 1943–1945 |
| DYMM Tuanku Syed Putra Jamalullail | 1945–2000 |
| DYMM Tuanku Syed Sirajuddin Jamalullail | 2000–present |

The reigning Raja of Perlis, His Royal Highness Tuanku Syed Sirajuddin ibni
Almarhum Tuanku Syed Putra Jamalullail, was proclaimed the seventh Raja of
Perlis on 17 April 2000.

## Different title, same federal standing

This is where most short explanations go wrong. A different title does not
carry a different standing within the federal structure.

The [Conference of Rulers of Malaysia](/en/government/majlis-raja-raja) states
that all Rulers and Yang di-Pertua Negeri automatically become members of the
Conference, and the chairmanship of the Conference of Rulers Meeting is held
by all nine Malay Rulers in rotation. The Raja of Perlis is one of those nine.

The record is clear in the official list of the Parliament of Malaysia. Two
Rajas of Perlis have held the office of
[Yang di-Pertuan Agong](/en/government/yang-di-pertuan-agong):

- **Tuanku Syed Putra** — third Yang di-Pertuan Agong, 21 September 1960 to
  20 September 1965
- **Tuanku Syed Sirajuddin** — twelfth Yang di-Pertuan Agong, 13 December 2001
  to 12 December 2006

Father and son, two terms, from the smallest state in the federation.

## Small, dense, and demographically unusual

At around 821 square kilometres, Perlis would fit inside many large districts
in other states. MKN lists its six main administrative areas: Kangar as the
state capital, followed by Arau, Kaki Bukit, Kuala Perlis, Padang Besar and
Simpang Empat.

But small doesn't mean sparse. DOSM recorded Perlis's population density at
365 people per square kilometre in 2025 — more than three times the national
average of 104.

Another detail rarely mentioned: in 2025, Perlis recorded a sex ratio of 99
males for every 100 females. Only two areas across Malaysia recorded more
females than males — Perlis and W.P. Putrajaya. The national ratio is 110.

Perlis's population growth rate in 2025 was 0.3 percent, below the national
rate of 0.5 percent.

## An RM6.5 billion economy

According to DOSM's 2024 GDP by State release, Perlis's economy grew 3.3
percent in 2024 (2023: 2.0 percent) with a GDP value of RM6.5 billion (2023:
RM6.3 billion).

The breakdown says a lot about the state:

- **Services** leads the state economy, up 3.3 percent, driven by the
  government services subsector along with wholesale and retail trade, food
  and beverage, and accommodation.
- **Agriculture** is the second-largest contributor — a position rarely seen
  among Peninsular states — up 2.9 percent and contributing 17.1 percent to
  state GDP. The fisheries subsector jumped 10.5 percent.
- **Construction** strengthened 16.9 percent (2023: 1.4 percent), driven by
  notable growth in the civil engineering subsector.

It should be stated plainly: in 2024, only five states recorded GDP per capita
above the national level of RM56,734 — W.P. Kuala Lumpur, W.P. Labuan,
Penang, Sarawak and Selangor. Perlis is not on that list. Compare this
structure with a manufacturing-based state such as
[Penang](/en/states/penang), or see the overview in
[Malaysia's GDP](/en/economy/gdp-overview).

## The Thailand border: a geographic liability turned asset

Perlis is the only Malaysian state whose entire northern border meets
Thailand, and its development strategy is now built on that fact.

**Chuping Valley Industrial Area (CVIA)** — a 2,482-acre industrial area in
Chuping Valley near the border town of Padang Besar, developed under the NCER
framework by the Northern Corridor Implementation Authority (NCIA). Its four
focus clusters are green manufacturing, halal industries, renewable energy
generation and electric vehicles. NCIA projects a contribution of more than
RM2.5 billion to Gross National Income, RM4.5 billion in private investment,
and over 12,000 job opportunities.

**Perlis Inland Port (PIP)** — an inland port project at Padang Besar within
the CVIA area. According to MKN, the government has allocated RM318.0 million
for external infrastructure development, with RM1.2 billion in private
investment for developing the operational area.

For the movement of people, the border is managed through Immigration,
Customs, Quarantine and Security (ICQS) complexes, including ICQS Wang Kelian
and the Padang Besar crossing. Document requirements and operating hours are
set by the [Immigration Department of Malaysia](/en/government/imigresen) and
are subject to change.

## Common mistakes

**"Sultan of Perlis."** No such office exists. The official title is His
Royal Highness the Raja of Perlis. Of the nine Malay Rulers, seven are styled
Sultan, one is styled Yang di-Pertuan Besar (Negeri Sembilan), and one is
styled Raja (Perlis).

**Assuming the title Raja means a lower rank.** It doesn't. The Raja of
Perlis is a full member of the Conference of Rulers and is eligible to be
elected Yang di-Pertuan Agong — and has been.

**Assuming Perlis is purely an agricultural state.** The services sector, not
agriculture, is the largest contributor to state GDP. Agriculture ranks
second.

**Confusing Padang Besar in Perlis with Bukit Kayu Hitam.** Bukit Kayu Hitam
is in Kedah. Perlis's main land crossings are Padang Besar and Wang Kelian.

**Getting the seat counts confused.** Perlis has three parliamentary seats
(P001 Padang Besar, P002 Kangar, P003 Arau) and 15 State Legislative Assembly
seats — the smallest DUN count in Malaysia.

## What's next

If you're evaluating Perlis for investment, start with NCIA and the state
authorities: the positioning of CVIA and the Perlis Inland Port determines
whether this border site fits your supply chain.

If you're studying Malaysia's royal institutions, Perlis is the most useful
test case — because it shows that title and constitutional power are two
different things.

And if you simply want to understand the region, the history of the northern
Peninsula begins far earlier than 1843. See
[Langkasuka](/en/malaysia/langkasuka), an early kingdom whose records predate
any Malay sultanate.
