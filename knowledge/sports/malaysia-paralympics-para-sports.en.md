When Cheah Liek Hou defended his SU5 badminton gold at Paris 2024, the triumph confirmed that Malaysian para sports now have a more solid line-up of champions, no longer an occasional achievement. Behind that moment lies a governance structure of its own, separate from the more famous Olympic world.

## Who governs para sports in Malaysia?

The governing body for high-performance para sports in Malaysia is the **Paralympic Council Malaysia (PCM)**, or in Malay, **Majlis Paralimpik Malaysia**. It serves as Malaysia's **National Paralympic Committee (NPC)** — the sole recognised body to represent the country at the Paralympic Games, affiliated with the **International Paralympic Committee (IPC)** and the **Asian Paralympic Committee (APC)**.

According to the official PCM website, the roots of this movement began in the early 1970s. The first formal organisation, the **Malaysian Council for the Rehabilitation of the Disabled**, was registered with the Registrar of Societies (ROS) on **18 May 1989**.

The name and status of this body then changed several times:

- **1996** — renamed as the Malaysian Paralympic Council / **Majlis Paralimpik Malaysia**.
- Later — rebranded as the **Paralympic Council Malaysia (PCM)**, with registration transferred from the ROS to the **Commissioner of Sports Malaysia** in line with the requirements of the **Sports Development Act 1997**.

## How does PCM differ from the Olympic Council of Malaysia?

This is a common confusion. The **Olympic Council of Malaysia (OCM)** is the country's National Olympic Committee — it manages athletes' participation in the Olympic Games, the Commonwealth Games, the Asian Games and the SEA Games. **PCM, by contrast, is a genuinely separate body** that manages the Paralympic pathway: the Paralympic Games, the Asian Para Games and the ASEAN Para Games.

The two are distinct organisations, each with its own leadership, membership and international affiliation. OCM is affiliated with the International Olympic Committee (IOC); PCM is affiliated with the IPC. What they have in common is that both operate within the framework of the **Sports Development Act 1997** and work with the Ministry of Youth and Sports as well as the National Sports Council in athlete development.

PCM is also not merely a sender of contingents. According to its official website, it hosted the **inaugural ASEAN Para Games in 2001**, and again in **2009** and **2017**. In terms of leadership, Dato' Sri Megat D. Shahriman is the President of PCM, elected in December 2019 and re-elected in December 2024.

## What does Malaysia's Paralympic medal record look like?

Malaysia has been part of the Paralympic movement since the 1970s, but medal success came more slowly. According to the official IPC database, the earliest Paralympic medal in the nation's record is a **bronze at the Beijing 2008 Paralympic Games**. The major turning point came at Rio 2016 when Malaysia claimed its first Paralympic gold.

Up to the **Paris 2024 Paralympic Games**, the official IPC record shows Malaysia has accumulated **17 medals** in total. The table below breaks down that total by edition of the Games:

| Paralympic Games | Gold | Silver | Bronze | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beijing 2008 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| London 2012 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| Rio 2016 | 3 | 0 | 1 | 4 |
| Tokyo 2020 | 3 | 2 | 0 | 5 |
| Paris 2024 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 5 |
| **Total** | **8** | **5** | **4** | **17** |

All eight golds came from just three sports — athletics, badminton and powerlifting — with athletics contributing the most.

## Who are Malaysia's Paralympic gold champions?

All eight of Malaysia's Paralympic gold medals came from five athletes across three editions of the Games. The following table lists each gold:

| Games | Athlete | Event | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rio 2016 | Mohamad Ridzuan Mohamad Puzi | 100m T36 | Among Malaysia's first three golds at Rio 2016 |
| Rio 2016 | Muhammad Ziyad Zolkefli | Shot put F20 | Athletics gold |
| Rio 2016 | Abdul Latif Romly | Long jump T20 | Athletics gold |
| Tokyo 2020 | Abdul Latif Romly | Long jump T20 | Second consecutive gold |
| Tokyo 2020 | Cheah Liek Hou | Badminton SU5 | Malaysia's first para badminton gold |
| Tokyo 2020 | Bonnie Bunyau Gustin | Powerlifting up to 72kg | First gold |
| Paris 2024 | Cheah Liek Hou | Badminton SU5 | Defending the title |
| Paris 2024 | Bonnie Bunyau Gustin | Powerlifting up to 72kg | World record 232kg |

Several moments stand out from this list:

- **Rio 2016** was Malaysia's big breakthrough. After years without gold, the country claimed **three golds in athletics** through Mohamad Ridzuan Mohamad Puzi, Muhammad Ziyad Zolkefli and Abdul Latif Romly.
- **Cheah Liek Hou** became a Paralympic badminton gold champion when he defended his Tokyo 2020 title in the SU5 category at Paris 2024.
- **Bonnie Bunyau Gustin**, an athlete from Sarawak, lifted **232kg** in the up-to-72kg category at Paris 2024 — more than three times his body weight — to break the world record while defending his powerlifting gold. This figure was confirmed by the International Paralympic Committee.

Malaysia also recorded important non-gold successes. **Chew Wei Lun** became the nation's first Paralympic boccia medallist, introducing the sport to a wider Malaysian audience.

## What categories and sports does Malaysia compete in?

Para sports use a classification system to ensure fair competition between athletes with comparable levels of impairment. Codes such as **T36** (track athletics, cerebral impairment), **T20/F20** (intellectual impairment) and **SU5** (badminton, upper limb impairment) denote an athlete's classification group, not just an event.

Malaysia's contingent typically competes across several major Paralympic sports, including athletics, badminton, powerlifting, boccia, archery and swimming. However, as far as the medal record goes, the gold contributions come from just three sports: athletics, powerlifting and badminton.
