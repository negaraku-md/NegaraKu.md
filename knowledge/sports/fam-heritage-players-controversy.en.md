Seven foreign-born players pulled on the Harimau Malaya jersey in June 2025. Less than a year later, the world's highest sports arbitration body confirmed that the documents making them eligible to play for Malaysia had been forged. The case set off a series of FIFA disciplinary proceedings, an appeal to the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS), and a police report in Malaysia.

This article sets out the facts neutrally. It describes what the authorities decided and the arguments each party put forward, without judging fault beyond those official findings. It is an AI draft that has not yet undergone human legal or editorial review.

## What happened in 2025?

According to the CAS summary, in 2025 FAM approached seven players — Facundo Tomás Garcés Rattaro, Rodrigo Julián Holgado, Imanol Javier Machuca, João Vitor Brandão Figueiredo, Gabriel Felipe Arrocha, Jon Irazábal Iraurgui and Hector Alejandro Hevel Serrano — and informed them that they might be able to lawfully apply for Malaysian citizenship and play for the national team. All seven players went through the naturalisation process and were granted Malaysian passports.

All seven players featured for Malaysia against Vietnam on 10 June 2025, in the third round of the AFC Saudi Arabia 2027 Asian Cup qualifiers, as stated in FIFA's official communication.

## What did FIFA decide?

The FIFA Disciplinary Committee found that FAM and the players had breached Article 22 of the FIFA Disciplinary Code, which relates to forgery. According to the CAS media release, the FIFA Disciplinary Committee's decision was made on 25 September 2025, namely that FAM and the players were found to have used forged documents for the naturalisation and eligibility process "even though the players had no connection with Malaysia". FIFA officially announced the sanctions through its website dated 26 September 2025.

The sanctions imposed by the FIFA Disciplinary Committee were as follows:

| Party | Sanction |
|---|---|
| FAM | CHF 350,000 fine |
| Each player (7 individuals) | CHF 2,000 fine |
| Each player (7 individuals) | 12-month suspension from all football-related activity |

According to Bernama's reporting, the CHF 350,000 fine was equivalent to roughly RM1.8 million, while the CHF 2,000 fine per player was equivalent to about RM10,560.

FAM announced its intention to appeal, stating that it would "use all available legal channels and procedures". On 3 November 2025, the FIFA Appeal Committee dismissed the appeal by FAM and the players, confirming the sanctions in their entirety.

## What did each party argue at CAS?

FAM and the players filed consolidated appeals to CAS on 5 December 2025. The CAS media summary sets out each party's position as follows.

- **FAM** accepted that "institutional shortcomings" existed and did not dispute that it might bear responsibility for the breach of the FIFA Disciplinary Code. FAM also stated that the players had a limited role in providing the documents requested by FAM, and did not prepare or alter those documents. FAM asked that the FIFA Appeal Committee's decision be set aside and that its fine not exceed CHF 50,000.
- **The players** asked that the FIFA Appeal Committee's decision be fully annulled, or in the alternative, that a proportionate penalty be imposed, on the grounds that they had not acted with intent or negligence.

The consolidated appeal was heard by a CAS arbitration panel comprising Lars Hilliger (Denmark) as President, José Luis Andrade (Portugal) and Massimo Coccia (Italy). An in-person hearing was held on 26 February 2026 at CAS headquarters in Lausanne, Switzerland.

## What was the CAS decision on 5 March 2026?

On 5 March 2026, CAS partly upheld the players' appeal and dismissed FAM's appeal.

Regarding the players, the panel found that "the offence of forging eligibility documents was established" and that the 12-month ban from playing matches was a reasonable and proportionate penalty, given their "complicit responsibility". However, in line with Article 22 of the FIFA Disciplinary Code, the panel ruled that the ban applies only to matches and not all football-related activity. This means the players may continue training with their respective clubs throughout the ban period.

The ban from playing matches began on 5 March 2026, with credit for the period from 25 September 2025 to 26 January 2026 counted as already served. Regarding FAM, the panel confirmed that the CHF 350,000 fine was "appropriate and proportionate", and FAM's appeal was dismissed.

CAS emphasised that the decision of 5 March 2026 was an operative decision without grounds, and that the full CAS Award with reasons would be issued later. For legal purposes, only the wording in that written decision is binding.

## What action did FAM take in Malaysia?

Besides the disciplinary appeal, FAM lodged a police report at the Petaling Jaya police headquarters on 24 December 2025 over the allegedly forged birth certificates of the seven heritage players. FAM Acting President Yusoff Mahadi said the association was "committed to cooperating with the police in their investigation" and described the move as reflecting FAM's commitment to integrity and governance reform. The details of the players' families' actual countries of origin were not stated in the report.

## What comes next?

Several matters remained unresolved at the time this draft was prepared.

- **Full CAS Award:** CAS will issue the complete decision with reasons. Only that written text is legally binding, and it may explain the basis of the panel's findings in greater detail.
- **AFC referral:** According to Bernama, the matter was also referred to the AFC Disciplinary and Ethics Committee for further consideration, including a possible points deduction in the 2027 Asian Cup qualifying campaign. Any such action has not been finalised.
- **Police investigation:** The police report lodged by FAM is still under investigation by the Malaysian authorities. No criminal investigation findings had been finalised as of this draft.
