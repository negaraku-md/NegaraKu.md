In 1921, Singapore beat Selangor 2-1 in the final of a new football tournament in Malaya. More than a century later, that competition — now known as the Malaysia Cup — remains the most prestigious crown in the country's most beloved sport.

Football in Malaysia is more than a game. It is a hundred-year heritage that links the British era, independence, the golden age of the 1970s and today's ambitions. To understand it, three institutions must be viewed together: the tournament (the Malaysia Cup), the governing body (FAM) and the national team (Harimau Malaya).

## How did the Malaya Cup begin and why is it linked to the warship HMS Malaya?

The tournament began in the early 1920s as the Malaya Cup, contested between states and settlements across Malaya. The inaugural final was held in 1921, and Singapore emerged champion by beating Selangor 2-1.

The link to the warship came later, not at the outset. In 1933, the tournament was renamed the HMS Malaya Cup after the warship HMS Malaya — a British Royal Navy ship funded through public contributions. That name remained until the trophy was replaced by the Malaysia Cup in 1967, a name still in use today.

Over a century, the tournament's name and form changed several times. A brief timeline:

| Year | Event |
|-------|-----------|
| 1921 | The Malaya Cup begins; Singapore beats Selangor 2-1 in the first final |
| 1933 | Renamed the HMS Malaya Cup after the warship HMS Malaya |
| 1942–1947 | Suspended throughout the Second World War |
| 1957 | Perak becomes the first team to lift the cup at Stadium Merdeka |
| 1964 | The first final broadcast on television (Singapore beats Perak 3-2) |
| 1967 | Renamed the Malaysia Cup — the name that has endured ever since |
| 1994 | Singapore's final appearance in a Malaysia Cup campaign |
| 1998 | The final held for the first time at the Bukit Jalil National Stadium |
| 2020 | Cancelled due to the COVID-19 pandemic |
| 2021 | Celebrates its 100th anniversary |

What makes the Malaysia Cup special is not just its age. It is one of the oldest football tournaments in Asia — a competition that began before most of the world's modern leagues existed, and that has kept being played through the colonial era, the Japanese occupation, independence and the professional age.

## Who is the most successful team in the Malaysia Cup?

If one name dominates the history of the Malaysia Cup, it is Selangor. The state has amassed 33 titles, an unmatched record, making it the most successful team in the competition's history.

| Team | Total titles |
|---------|------------------|
| Selangor | 33 |
| Singapore | 24 |
| Perak | 8 |
| Kedah | 5 |

Singapore's place on this list tells a larger story. With 24 titles, Singapore was once a major power in Malaysian football — a reminder that for several decades, Singaporean teams competed in the same system. Singapore made its final appearance in a Malaysia Cup campaign in 1994, before eventually parting ways with the Malaysian league.

The peak of state dominance was also seen in the late 1980s, when Kuala Lumpur became the last team to win three Malaysia Cup titles in a row (generally recorded as 1987–1989).

## What is FAM and how does it govern the game?

Behind every match is a governing body. The Football Association of Malaysia — FAM — is the body that runs the game at the national level, from the national team to the league and cup structures.

Its headquarters is located at Wisma FAM, Jalan SS5A/9, Kelana Jaya, Petaling Jaya. Within the structure of international football, FAM belongs to the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) and is one of the founders of the ASEAN Football Federation (AFF).

The AFF itself was officially established in Jakarta on 31 January 1984, with six founding associations: Brunei Darussalam, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. That membership places FAM in the core group that shaped regional football administration — the body that later produced the AFF Championship, the premier national-team competition in Southeast Asia.

One thing often disputed is the exact founding date of FAM. Some records place its founding in 1926, while others refer to 1933 — when the Singapore-based Football Association of Malaya took over the running of the Malaya Cup before its administrative centre moved to Malaya in a later era. Because the sources do not agree, the exact date is better regarded as unsettled; what can be verified is FAM's role today as the national governing body and its position within the AFC and AFF.

## When was Harimau Malaya's golden era?

Every footballing nation has its golden age. For Malaysia, it was the 1970s — the decade when Harimau Malaya not only competed in Asia but stepped onto the Olympic stage.

Its symbolic peak was qualification for the 1972 Munich Olympics. Under coaches Dave McLaren and Jalil Che Din, Malaysia beat South Korea, Japan, the Philippines and Taiwan in the qualifying round in Seoul in 1971. Striker Syed Ahmad Syed Abu Bakar scored five goals throughout that qualifying campaign, including a hat-trick in a 3-0 win over Japan — the 1968 Olympic bronze medallists.

The success continued into the Asian Games. At the 1974 Tehran Asian Games, the attacking line of Syed Ahmad, Mokhtar Dahari and Isa Bakar formed a fearsome combination; Malaysia scored 20 goals on its way to the bronze medal, after beating North Korea 2-1.

This era was also shaped by the Merdeka Tournament — an invitational competition hosted in Kuala Lumpur that served as a benchmark of a team's strength in its day. Syed Ahmad, for instance, was part of the teams that won the Merdeka Tournament in 1966, 1968 and 1974.

Names from this era remain etched in the nation's memory. Mokhtar Dahari, the scorer and striker nicknamed "SuperMokh"; Soh Chin Aun, the respected pillar of defence; and R. Arumugam, the charismatic goalkeeper — all became icons whose names are still spoken by later generations.

## What is the national team's greatest triumph so far?

After the 1970s, major success became rarer — until one night at the end of 2010 changed the mood.

In 2010, Malaysia won the AFF Suzuki Cup for the first time. Under coach K. Rajagopal, Harimau Malaya beat Indonesia 4-2 on aggregate in a two-legged final. Striker Safee Sali became one of the heroes of that campaign, scoring an important goal in the final.

That victory mattered for several reasons. It was the team's first major regional title after decades — Malaysia had previously only been runner-up in the tournament's inaugural edition in 1996 — and it was achieved by a lineup of local players that revived public interest in the national team. After years of disappointment, the 2010 title became a modern reference point for the country's footballing hopes.

## Where does Malaysia stand in world and regional football?

Ambition is one thing; the actual standing is another. As of the FIFA World Ranking update in June 2026, Malaysia stood 136th in the world with 1,086.22 points.

In the Southeast Asian context, that standing places Malaysia in the middle tier of the region:

| Country | FIFA ranking (June 2026) |
|--------|----------------------------|
| Thailand | 94 |
| Vietnam | 99 |
| Indonesia | 118 |
| Philippines | 135 |
| Malaysia | 136 |

Such rankings fluctuate with match results — and sometimes with the results of other teams, not just Malaysia's own performances. Still, they give an honest picture: Malaysia is a footballing nation with a great history but increasingly fierce regional competition, in which Thailand and Vietnam have built a marked advantage over the past two decades.

## How is Malaysian football structured today?

For new fans, the various tournaments can be easy to confuse. In fact, modern Malaysian football can be understood as three distinct but mutually supporting arenas.

| Arena | What it is | Managed by |
|------------|---------|----------------|
| National team (Harimau Malaya) | Represents Malaysia at the international level — World Cup qualifying, Asian Cup, AFF Championship | FAM |
| Domestic league (Super League) | The top-tier league competition between professional clubs across a season | The league body under FAM's auspices |
| National cups (Malaysia Cup, FA Cup) | Knockout competitions that decide the cup champion each season | FAM and the league body |

The easiest way to tell them apart: the Super League is the long battle across a season for the league crown; the Malaysia Cup and FA Cup are shorter, more dramatic knockout competitions; and Harimau Malaya is the national team that gathers the best players to represent the country. The same club can compete in all three arenas in a single season.

This structure mirrors the model of most footballing nations — a league for consistency, cups for surprises, and the national team as the pinnacle that unites them all.

## What are the most common misconceptions?

Several mistakes recur when people talk about the history of Malaysian football:

- **Assuming the Malaysia Cup was always named that way.** In fact it began as the Malaya Cup in 1921 and was only renamed the Malaysia Cup in 1967, after the formation of Malaysia.
- **Forgetting Singapore's role.** With 24 titles, Singapore is among the most successful teams in the competition's history — part of an era when Singapore competed in the same football system until 1994.
- **Confusing the Malaysia Cup with the Super League.** The Malaysia Cup is a knockout competition; the Super League is a full-season league. The two are entirely different.
- **Assuming FAM's founding date is settled.** The sources disagree between 1926 and 1933; it is safer to focus on FAM's verifiable role than on a particular year.
- **Seeing the 1970s as the only success.** Although that era was indeed a golden age, the 2010 AFF Suzuki Cup title was an important modern achievement that should not be forgotten.

## What's next

Malaysian football today lives in the tension between a proud history and increasingly fierce regional competition. The century-long heritage of the Malaysia Cup and the memories of the 1970s provide a firm foundation of identity; the 136th world ranking is a reminder that the way forward demands continuous effort.
