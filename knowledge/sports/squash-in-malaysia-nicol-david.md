For nine straight years, the best squash player on the planet came from Penang. Nicol David did not just win in Malaysia's most successful individual sport — she rewrote what dominance in it looks like, holding the world No. 1 ranking for **108 consecutive months** until September 2015, the longest unbroken reign the game has ever seen.

That figure alone would define most careers. David's did far more: it turned a niche racquet sport into a source of national pride and gave a small, well-run development system in Kuala Lumpur a global calling card.

## Who is Nicol David and why does she matter?

Born in Penang, Nicol Ann David turned professional in 2000, winning her maiden tour title that February at the Savcor Finnish Open. Within five years she was world champion, and she stayed at or near the summit for a decade.

Her career record is remarkable by any measure:

| Achievement | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Consecutive months at world No. 1 | 108, until September 2015 |
| World Open titles | 8, between 2005 and 2014 |
| British Open titles | 5 |
| World Player of the Year | 7 times (2005–2012) |
| Commonwealth Games gold medals | 2 |
| PSA Hall of Fame | Inducted 1 December 2024 |

Sources: Laureus World Sports Academy (rankings, titles and awards); PSA Squash Tour (Hall of Fame induction).

David passed Susan Devoy's previous record of 105 months at the top when she reached her 106th consecutive month as world No. 1 in the February 2015 rankings, and her reign ultimately ran to a record 108 months until September 2015. Tournament of Champions chairman John Nimick said her accomplishments placed her "among the greatest athletes in history." She was inducted into the PSA Hall of Fame on 1 December 2024, alongside squash great Jansher Khan.

## How did Malaysia produce a squash world No. 1?

David was not a lone accident of talent. She was the standout product of a deliberate pipeline built by the **Squash Racquets Association of Malaysia (SRAM)**, the sport's national governing body.

SRAM's junior development pathway produced the generation that put Malaysia on the map: Nicol David, alongside Ong Beng Hee and Mohd Azlan Iskandar.

The model has a clear shape:

- **State associations** scout and develop juniors at the local level.
- Promising players graduate to the **National Squash Centre in Bukit Jalil**, the elite training base.
- Corporate sponsorship helps sustain the top end, backing leading players and junior programmes.

It is a compact system, but a productive one for a country its size — the kind of pathway that keeps a small federation focused on developing a pipeline rather than relying on one-off stars.

## What happened to Malaysian squash after Nicol David retired?

David played her final professional matches in June 2019, closing an era. The obvious question was whether Malaysia could stay relevant without her — and the honest answer for a few years was that it slipped from the very top while a new group matured.

That group has now arrived. The Malaysian players who qualified for the 2024 World Squash Championships — the individual world championship held in Cairo — included **Sivasangari Subramaniam, Rachel Arnold, and sisters Aifa and Aira Azman**, a genuine cohort rather than a single name.

Two players in particular have pushed Malaysia back toward the elite:

- **Sivasangari Subramaniam** reached a **career-high world No. 6 in October 2025**, moving up two places from No. 8. She is Malaysia's highest-ranked woman since David and the clearest sign that the country can again contest the sport's top tier.
- **Ng Eain Yow**, the leading Malaysian man, won the **2024 ACE Malaysia Squash Cup** on home soil, beating top seed Youssef Soliman of Egypt 3–0 (11–7, 11–7, 11–7).

Neither has matched David's ceiling — few in any sport ever will — but a top-six world ranking and a home-tournament title show the pipeline that produced her is still delivering.

## Why is squash so important to Malaysia specifically?

Squash occupies an unusual place in Malaysian sport. In a country where badminton draws the biggest crowds, squash quietly became the discipline where a Malaysian was, for years, unambiguously the best in the world. That gave it outsized cultural weight relative to its participation base.

David's dominance also coincided with — and helped fuel — a long campaign to get squash into the Olympic Games. She retired before that goal was achieved, but the sport's inclusion on the programme for the 2028 Los Angeles Games gives the current generation something she never had: an Olympic stage to aim for, and a reason for SRAM's junior system to keep feeding the pathway.

## What's next

Malaysian squash is in a rebuilding-and-rising phase rather than a golden one. The near-term markers to watch are whether Sivasangari Subramaniam can push beyond world No. 6 into title contention, whether Ng Eain Yow can establish himself among the men's top 10, and whether the Azman sisters convert junior promise into senior results.

The longer arc points to Los Angeles 2028, squash's Olympic debut, where a medal would be the natural sequel to the Nicol David era. For readers tracking the sport, the useful anchors are SRAM's competition calendar and the PSA world rankings — the same measuring stick that recorded David's months at the top.
