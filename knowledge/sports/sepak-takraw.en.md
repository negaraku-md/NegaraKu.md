Picture volleyball, then take away every player's hands. What remains —
head-high bicycle-kick spikes, chest sets, serves tossed high into the air and
struck with the heel — that is sepak takraw: one of the world's most acrobatic
sports, and one of the most tightly bound to the identity of the Malay World.

## Where did this rattan-ball-kicking game come from?

Sepak takraw grew out of an old folk game known in the Malay World as
**sepak raga** — literally, kicking a ball woven from rattan. Its original form
was not a competitive game across a net, but a circle game: a group of players
stand in a ring and try to keep the ball in the air for as long as possible
using any part of the body except the hands.

Its exact origins are hazy and belong to no single nation. Similar games
developed across the Malay World and Southeast Asia, and the very terms for the
game come from two different languages:

| Name | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| sepak raga | kicking the ball with the body |
| sepak raga jaring | kicking the ball over a net with the body |
| sepak bola rotan | kicking a rattan ball |
| takraw (Thai) | rattan ball |

The word **"sepak"** means "kick" in Malay, while **"takraw"** comes from the
Thai term for a rattan ball. The combined name is itself a regional compromise —
a reflection of the fact that no single country can claim it solely as its own.

## How did a folk game become a competitive sport?

The turning point came in 1960. Delegates from Malaya, Singapore, Indonesia,
Laos and Thailand met in Kuala Lumpur to standardise the rules and formally
adopt the term **sepak takraw**, replacing the various local names. From then
on, the traditional circle game developed into the volleyball-style net sport
we know today, complete with a court, points and sets.

The ball, too, differed by era. The traditional ball was hand-woven from
rattan — which is why it was once called "sepak bola rotan"; the modern
approved competition ball is made of synthetic material, with 12 holes and
20 intersection points according to the specifications for international use.

## How is sepak takraw played?

The core format is called the **regu** — three players per team. A tekong
stands at the back and serves; two front players (left and right) stand closer
to the net to set and spike. The aim is like volleyball: send the ball across
the net so that the opponents fail to return it legally.

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Team size | 3 players (one regu) — tekong + two front players |
| Body parts allowed | Feet, knees, chest and head — **hands forbidden** |
| Court dimensions | Approximately 13.4 m x 6.1 m |
| Net height | 1.5 m (men); 1.42 m (women) |
| Touches per side | Maximum of three touches before returning the ball |
| Scoring | The first side to 21 points wins the set |
| Winning a match | Win two sets |
| Tie | If 21-21, play continues until one side reaches 25 points or leads by two points |

A point is awarded when the opposing side commits a fault — touching the ball
with a hand, letting the ball drop onto their own court, or sending the ball
out. It is the combination of the ban on hands and the three-touch limit that
produces the sport's most iconic move: the **bicycle-kick spike**, in which a
player leaps, flips their body in the air, and kicks the ball down over the net
with the sole of the foot raised well above head height.

## Who governs sepak takraw in Malaysia?

The sport's governing body in Malaysia is **PSM**. On 10 September 2025, the
Sports Commissioner of Malaysia approved an amendment changing its official name
from *Persatuan* (Association) Sepaktakraw Malaysia to *Persekutuan*
(Federation) Sepaktakraw Malaysia — a symbolic upgrade from "association" to
"federation" intended to strengthen the sport's administration and
professionalism. The PSM acronym and its logo were retained.

At the international level, the sport is governed by the **International
Sepaktakraw Federation (ISTAF)**, established through the joint efforts of
representatives from several Southeast Asian countries, with officials from
Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia playing key roles. PSM is one of its founding
members. ISTAF standardises the rules and extends the sport's global reach.
According to ISTAF's chronology, the game was first introduced in Bangkok in
1965 and included in the SEA Games in Kuala Lumpur in 1967, before making its
debut at the Asian Games in 1990 (Beijing).

## What is Malaysia's record on the world stage?

This is where the story becomes complex. Although sepak takraw is deeply rooted
in the Malay World including Malaysia, Thailand has long dominated the elite
tournaments — especially the **King's Cup**, the world's most prestigious
sepak takraw tournament, held in Thailand.

Malaysia's last King's Cup team regu title win was in **1988**. By 2024, that
wait had stretched to about 36 years. On 8 September 2024 in Nakhon Ratchasima,
Malaysia once again lost 0-2 to hosts Thailand in the team regu event,
extending the title drought.

Yet 2024 was not an entirely bleak year. A few months earlier, Malaysia had
recorded one of its biggest wins in decades, and at the King's Cup itself
Malaysia still took gold in the individual regu event:

| Event | Date & venue | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sepaktakraw World Cup — regu final | 22 May 2024, Titiwangsa Stadium, KL | **Malaysia 2-0 Thailand** (15-8, 15-12) |
| Sepaktakraw World Cup — doubles | 22 May 2024, KL | Malaysia won 2-0 (17-16, 15-13) |
| King's Cup — regu event | 2 September 2024, Nakhon Ratchasima (Korat) | Malaysia 2-0 India (gold) |
| King's Cup — team regu event (final) | 8 September 2024, Nakhon Ratchasima (Korat) | Malaysia 0-2 Thailand |

That World Cup win at Titiwangsa Stadium — a line-up of Farhan Adam (setter),
Mohammad Syahir Mohd Rosdi (tekong) and Mohamad Azlan Alias (spiker) beating
Thailand in front of home fans — ended Thailand's dominance as world champions
and avenged an earlier final defeat. It was also part of a "double triumph" on
the same day, as Malaysia also won the doubles event.

This pattern sums up the standing of Malaysian sepak takraw today: a nation
whose heritage is rooted in the sport and which can beat anyone on a good day,
but which is still chasing the consistency that lets Thailand hold on to the
most coveted titles year after year.

## Next steps

If you want to see the sport live, the national Sepak Takraw League run by PSM
is the easiest entry point, and sepak takraw events are among the liveliest at
the SEA Games. To understand why the sport's heritage is claimed by several
countries at once, start with its very name — the fusion of "sepak" (Malay) and
"takraw" (Thai) is proof that one and the same game was raised by different
cultures across Southeast Asia.
