Few street-food names in George Town are as well-worn as Penang Road Famous
Laksa, where a daytime queue forms for a single sour, fish-rich bowl that the
MICHELIN Guide calls "Worth Queueing For".

## A Bib Gourmand George Town institution

Penang Road Famous Laksa is listed in the MICHELIN Guide Kuala Lumpur & Penang as
a **Bib Gourmand**, the guide's distinction for good-quality cooking at a good
value rather than a star. It sits in the noodles category at the lowest ($) price
tier and is flagged as both "Worth Queueing For" and a place to "eat like a
local". For the wider picture, see
[the MICHELIN Guide in Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia).

## The signature laksa

The bowl is Penang-style **assam (sour) laksa** — a tamarind-soured fish broth
with no coconut, the sharp northern style explained in the
[laksa](/food-lifestyle/laksa) overview. What sets this version apart is a broth
built from **three types of fish** for depth, poured over *lai fun* (thick round
rice noodles), with a spice blend tuned to balance spicy, sweet and sour notes.
A deboned sardine fillet is offered as an add-on for those who want more fish in
the bowl. The family behind the stall describe the craft as getting "the correct
balance of the sour, sweet, and spicy flavours".

## Where it is

The MICHELIN listing gives the address as 5 Lebuh Keng Kwee, George Town, 10100,
Penang — a short lane off Penang Road, at the heart of the island's
[street-food district](/food-lifestyle/penang-food). The spot is also well known
for the cendol stall beside it, making the corner a classic George Town pilgrimage.

## Background

The shop has operated since 1970, working from a family recipe handed down over
the decades. That longevity, central location and a dish that regularly features
on "best of Penang" lists have made it one of George Town's most photographed
hawker names.

## Visiting note

Expect a queue — the MICHELIN listing says so plainly. The shop keeps daytime
hours with a mid-week closure, so it suits a lunch or afternoon visit rather than
an early breakfast. Beyond the laksa, the stall also offers char koay teow with
duck egg, but the sour laksa is what the crowds come for.
