Hainanese chicken rice is one of the quiet tests of a Malaysian kitchen: poached
or roasted chicken, and rice carrying the flavour of the bird it was cooked with.
Chee Meng, on Jalan Kelang Lama, has been passing that test since 1965 — long
enough, and well enough, to earn a MICHELIN **Bib Gourmand**, the Guide's badge
for good food at good value.

## A Bib Gourmand chicken-rice institution

The MICHELIN Guide lists Chee Meng under Malaysian cuisine in the budget ($)
tier and awards it a Bib Gourmand — a recognition that rewards value rather than
luxury, which suits a long-running chicken-rice shop. For the wider picture of
the Guide in Malaysia, see the
[MICHELIN Guide in Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia).

## The chicken and the rice

As the name promises, the house specialises in
[Hainanese chicken rice](/food-lifestyle/hainanese-chicken-rice), served with a
choice of steamed or fried chicken. A detail MICHELIN draws out: Chee Meng uses
local chicken rather than Hainan's Wenchang breed, which the inspectors say gives
leaner, softer and juicier meat. The rice is cooked in chicken fat and stock, so
it arrives fragrant rather than plain, and a set menu built for solo diners pairs
it with peanut soup and a choice of cuts. The other chicken dishes, MICHELIN
adds, are worth trying too.

## A family business across three generations

Chee Meng is a mainstay of its local dining scene, family-owned and run
continuously since 1965 — now by the second and third generations. That kind of
continuity is part of what a Bib Gourmand often recognises: a recipe held steady
and refined over decades rather than reinvented.

## Visiting

This is a neighbourhood chicken-rice shop rather than a reservations restaurant,
best approached as a walk-in for lunch. Because MICHELIN's locale listings differ
on whether bookings are handled, it is worth calling ahead if you want to be
sure, and — as with every Bib Gourmand — checking the current MICHELIN selection
to confirm the award still stands.
