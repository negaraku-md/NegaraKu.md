Away from the old-town crowds, in the seaside suburb of Tanjung Bungah, a
third-generation family stall frying char koay teow to order has earned a place
in the MICHELIN Guide.

## What it is

Awesome Char Koay Teow is a street-food stall operating from New KK99 Cafe,
recognised in the MICHELIN Guide as a **Bib Gourmand** pick — the Guide's
distinction for good-quality, good-value cooking, given freely to hawker and
street-food spots (see the
[MICHELIN Guide in Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia) for how the
distinctions work). It is tagged "worth queueing for," is cash-only, and does not
take reservations.

## The signature plate

The stall fries [char koay teow](/food-lifestyle/char-kway-teow) — the flat rice
noodles tossed hard over high heat. The MICHELIN listing says "each aromatic
plate of koay teow is fried to order and exudes wok hei," the smoky aroma that
marks a well-fired plate, and that it tastes even better with chilli sauce.
Diners can add an over-easy egg or cheese on top — a modern twist on the
traditional dish.

## Where to find it

The stall operates from New KK99 Cafe at 99 Jalan Lembah Permai, Tanjung Bungah,
George Town, 11200, Penang — northwest of the old town, toward the coast. The
MICHELIN listing shows it open Monday to Saturday, 09:00–15:30, closed Sunday,
though a hawker stall's hours shift with the cook, so confirm locally.

## Before you go

The stall is run by the third generation of the family, and each plate is fried
to order, so expect a wait at busy times. Bring cash — credit cards are not
accepted — and note there are no bookings. As with every MICHELIN listing, the
selection is reviewed each year, so check the current Guide if the distinction is
your reason for the trip.
