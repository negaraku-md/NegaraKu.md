On Jalan Sungai Besi in Pudu, a short way from central Kuala Lumpur, Minji ladles
out a bowl of bak kut teh from a recipe older than the city's skyline — and the
MICHELIN Guide has taken notice.

## What it is, and the Bib Gourmand

Minji is a bak kut teh shop recognised in the MICHELIN Guide Kuala Lumpur &
Penang with a **Bib Gourmand**, the guide's distinction for good quality at good
value, and a recent addition to the list. It is tagged **"Worth Queueing For,"**
and sits in the $$ price band. What the inspectors single out is continuity: a
family recipe carried across three generations without change.

## The dish

[Bak kut teh](/food-lifestyle/bak-kut-teh) is a Malaysian pork-rib soup simmered
with herbs and spices, and Minji's version is described by the MICHELIN Guide as
**herbal and naturally sweet**, from a recipe that has **not changed in 80
years**. You choose your cut of pork — **ribs, belly, tripe, or a combination** —
and the guide suggests dipping the meat in **soy sauce with garlic and chillies**
to round out each mouthful.

## The history

The shop began as a **hawker stall in the 1940s** and is now run by
**third-generation owners**. The MICHELIN Guide notes a worn, historic interior
that reflects the generations of diners who have passed through — part of the
appeal for a dish whose whole premise is that it tastes the same as it always
has.

## Visiting note

The "Worth Queueing For" tag is a warning as much as a recommendation: come ready
to wait at peak hours, and bring cash, since cards are not accepted. As a newer
entry on a list that is revised each year, Minji is worth confirming on the
current MICHELIN Guide before a special trip.
