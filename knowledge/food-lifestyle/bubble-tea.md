Walk through any Malaysian mall and you will pass them within minutes: bright little
counters with queues out front, staff shaking steel cups, a wall of options for
sugar and ice and toppings. **Bubble tea** — *boba*, pearl milk tea — is the country's
great modern drink craze, and the one entry on the Malaysian drinks board that is
proudly, openly *not* Malaysian in origin.

## What bubble tea is

At its heart, bubble tea is **sweet milk tea** — black or green tea with milk and
sugar — poured over a layer of **tapioca pearls**: chewy, marble-sized balls of
cooked tapioca starch, also called *boba* or simply "pearls", sucked up through a
wide straw. The "bubble" in the name originally came from the froth raised by shaking
the tea, though most people now associate it with the pearls. From that base it has
exploded into endless variety — fruit teas, cheese foam, grass jelly, pudding,
adjustable sweetness and ice — which is a large part of its appeal.

For where bubble tea sits among the country's wider drinks board, see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the deep
dive into the boba phenomenon in Malaysia.

## A Taiwanese drink, honestly

Unlike [teh tarik](/food-lifestyle/teh-tarik) or
[cincau](/food-lifestyle/cincau), bubble tea did **not** come from Malaysia. It
originated in **Taiwan in the 1980s**, and even there its exact birth is disputed —
two Taiwanese tea houses, one in Taichung and one in Tainan, both claim to have
invented it, and the question has never been cleanly settled. From Taiwan it swept
across East and Southeast Asia and then the world. Malaysia's role is as an
enthusiastic adopter that built the drink into everyday life and grew its own big
chains — a story worth telling on its own terms, without pretending the drink is
local.

## Tealive, Chatime and the 2017 split

The most Malaysian part of the bubble-tea story is the chain war. **Chatime** is a
Taiwanese brand; in Malaysia it was run by a local master franchisee, **Loob
Holdings**. When that franchise agreement collapsed in early **2017**, Loob launched
its own brand, **Tealive**, just weeks later — and Tealive grew rapidly into the
best-known home-grown chain, with hundreds of outlets nationwide. Chatime later
returned to Malaysia under a different operator. So the household name, **Tealive**,
is the local brand that rose out of that split — a genuinely Malaysian business built
on an imported drink.

## Halal, and a drink for everyone

One reason bubble tea became a drink shared across every community is **halal
certification**. Chatime Malaysia obtained **JAKIM** halal certification, and in
**2019** JAKIM publicly confirmed that Tealive was halal, putting down rumours to the
contrary. That matters in a country where the premises' certification, not the
drink's ingredients alone, is what gives Muslim diners confidence. As always, the
assurance attaches to the **specific certified outlet** — so for any given shop, look
for its displayed certificate rather than assuming every bubble-tea stall is
certified. For the deeper story of tea and coffee-shop drinks, see
[kopi, teh tarik and coffee-shop drinks](/food-lifestyle/kopi-teh-tarik-and-coffeeshop-drinks).
