On Jalan Burma in George Town, a stall that has been griddling sweet pancakes for
close to a century now carries a MICHELIN Guide Bib Gourmand — proof that a
single, perfected street snack can stand alongside the island's famous savoury
hawker fare.

## A Bib Gourmand apom stall

Ravi's Famous Apom Manis is listed in the MICHELIN Guide Kuala Lumpur & Penang as
a **Bib Gourmand**, the guide's distinction for good-quality cooking at a good
value rather than a star. It sits in the street-food category at the lowest ($)
price tier. It is one of several George Town sweet-snack stalls to earn MICHELIN
recognition; for the wider picture, see
[the MICHELIN Guide in Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia).

## The signature apom manis

*Apom manis* is a sweet pancake made from a rice-flour-and-coconut-milk batter,
cooked in a small round pan so each one emerges soft and spongy in the centre
with thin, lacy, crisp edges. The Penang version — the one Ravi's makes — adds
**egg** to the batter, which the MICHELIN write-up credits with an "intoxicating
aroma" and a richer flavour than plainer styles elsewhere. It is eaten warm, on
its own.

## Where it is

The MICHELIN listing gives the address as 319 Jalan Burma, George Town, 10350,
Penang — a busy artery on the edge of the old town's dense
[food quarter](/food-lifestyle/penang-food). It is a walk-in street stall; the
guide notes it does not take bookings.

## Background

The stall is a family business going back generations. Accounts of the exact
founding year differ — the MICHELIN restaurant listing says the stall has sold
apom "since 1920", while a MICHELIN dessert feature dates its founding to 1922
under Rajagopal — but all agree it has run for close to a hundred years, with the
recipe handed down within the family.

## Visiting note

Come early. The MICHELIN guidance is that the stall tends to sell out in the
morning, so a mid-morning visit risks an empty griddle. It is a cash-friendly,
no-frills stall rather than a sit-down café — expect to queue briefly and eat
your apom fresh off the pan.
