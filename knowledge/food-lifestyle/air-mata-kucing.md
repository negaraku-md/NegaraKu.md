Step off the crowded market lane of Petaling Street in Kuala Lumpur's Chinatown
and there is a very good chance someone nearby is clutching a plastic cup of
something pale-gold and ice-cold, sweet and faintly medicinal. That drink is
*air mata kucing* — a herbal longan cooler that has been one of the lane's fixtures
for decades, and one of the simplest pleasures in the city's drinks repertoire.

## What air mata kucing is

At heart, air mata kucing is a **chilled herbal sweet drink**. The usual build is
dried **longan**, **luo han guo** (monk fruit) and **winter melon**, simmered in
water and sweetened with **rock sugar**, then cooled. Some stalls add other
"cooling" ingredients, so the exact recipe shifts from vendor to vendor. The
result is clear, pale and deeply sweet, tasting of dried fruit and herbs rather
than of one dominant flavour — and because the fruit and monk fruit do the
sweetening, it is naturally caffeine-free and easy to drink in quantity on a hot
day.

For where it sits among the country's wider drinks board — sirap bandung,
cincau, Milo and the rest — see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the
deep dive into the longan cooler itself.

## What "mata kucing" means

The name repays a moment's attention. **"Mata kucing"** literally means
**"cat's eye"** in Malay, and it is the common local name for the **longan** — the
small, translucent fruit with a single dark seed that does, peeled, resemble an
eye. So the drink's name points to the fruit it is built on. The exact reading is
debated: some writers say "mata kucing" is simply the Malay word for longan, while
others treat mata kucing as a distinct but closely related fruit. Either way, the
dried fruit in the cup belongs to the longan family — and the name is *not*
"cat's tears", as the Malay is sometimes misheard.

## A "cooling" drink

Like grass jelly and barley, air mata kucing belongs to the Chinese-Malaysian
tradition of **"cooling" drinks** — refreshers thought to counter the body's
"heatiness" in hot weather. That is traditional dietary belief rather than
established medicine, and is best read as part of the culture around the drink.
What is beyond doubt is that an ice-cold cup is genuinely welcome in the tropical
heat. Its herbal cousin, the grass-jelly drink, is covered in
[cincau](/food-lifestyle/cincau).

## The Petaling Street institution

The most famous air mata kucing in the country is sold from a stall on **Jalan
Petaling** — Petaling Street, the heart of KL's Chinatown — where the drink is
kept ice-cold in a large metal dispenser and poured by the cupful for the crowds
working their way through the market. It has been there for decades; you will
often see a queue, and just as often see the cup bought alongside a skewer or a
packet of something from the surrounding
[pasar malam and street food](/food-lifestyle/pasar-malam-and-street-food). The
stall's exact founding year is part of local lore rather than firm record, so it
is best called simply long-running.
