Among the pale, modest drinks lined up at a Chinese coffee shop — soya bean, grass
jelly, chrysanthemum tea — there is almost always a pot of something cloudy and
faintly starchy kept warm at the back: *air barli*, barley water. Cheap, clean and
only lightly sweet, it is one of the quiet staples of the Malaysian drinks board.

## What barley water is

Barley water is exactly what it sounds like: **pearl barley** — small, polished
barley grains — boiled in water for a long stretch until the grains soften and the
water turns cloudy, then strained and sweetened, usually with **rock sugar**. Some
cooks drop in a **pandan leaf** while it boils for fragrance, and the softened grains
are sometimes left in the glass or served alongside to chew. The result is mild,
clean and naturally caffeine-free — a gentle, barely-sweet drink rather than a
bold one.

For where barley sits among the country's wider drinks board — sirap bandung,
cincau, Milo and the rest — see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the deep
dive into the barley water itself.

## Barley limau and other versions

Plain barley is the base, but two variations are everywhere:

| You say | You get |
| --- | --- |
| barley / air barli | Plain sweetened barley water |
| barley limau | Barley water with a squeeze of lime |
| barley ais / barley peng | The iced version |
| barley + winter melon | Barley with candied winter melon, sweeter and rounder |

**Barley limau** — with a squeeze of lime — is the favourite, the citrus lifting the
mild grain much as it does in [teh-O ais limau](/food-lifestyle/teh-o-ais-limau).
The pairing with **candied winter melon** gives a deeper, caramel sweetness and is a
classic Chinese coffee-shop combination.

## A "cooling" drink

Like [grass jelly](/food-lifestyle/cincau) and
[air mata kucing](/food-lifestyle/air-mata-kucing), barley belongs to the
Chinese-Malaysian family of **"cooling" drinks** — refreshers thought to counter the
body's "heatiness" in hot weather. That is traditional dietary belief rather than
established medicine, and is best read as part of the culture around the drink. What
is beyond doubt is that an ice-cold glass is genuinely welcome in the tropical heat.

One point worth clearing up: this barley has nothing to do with beer. It is simply
boiled grain water, non-alcoholic and, in that form, halal.

## Where to find it

Barley water is a fixture of **Chinese coffee shops, food courts and hawker
centres**, often kept warm in an urn and poured on request, and it is just as common
iced from a chiller. It is also one of the most widely sold ready-made drinks — the
canned barley (Yeo's among them) sits on every supermarket shelf. For the sit-down
coffee-shop world it belongs to, see
[kopitiam and mamak culture](/food-lifestyle/kopitiam-mamak-culture).
