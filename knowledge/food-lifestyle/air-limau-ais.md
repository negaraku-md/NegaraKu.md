Ask for a cold drink at almost any Malaysian mamak and one answer never fails: **air
limau ais** — fresh lime juice, sweet and sour and loaded with ice. It is among the
most-ordered drinks in the country, a simple squeeze of citrus that cuts through heat
and heavy food alike.

## What air limau ais is

At its simplest, air limau ais is **lime juice, sugar and water** over plenty of ice.
The lime is squeezed fresh, the sugar balances the sourness, and the water stretches
it into a long, thirst-quenching drink. Many stalls will make it **kurang manis** (less
sweet) on request, and some garnish the glass with a lime. Sour, sweet and cold all at
once, it is the plainest kind of refreshment — and one of the best.

For where air limau ais sits among the country's wider drinks board, see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the deep dive
into the fresh lime cooler itself.

## Which lime — and the asam boi twist

The lime that usually goes into a limau ais is **calamansi**, known here as **limau
kasturi** — small, round, fragrant and mildly sweet-tart, which makes it ideal for
drinks. Some stalls prefer the sharper **key lime** (**limau nipis**). Malaysia
actually keeps several limes apart for different jobs, and for a fresh limau ais the
calamansi is the typical, more aromatic choice.

The most popular upgrade is **limau asam boi** — the same drink with **dried sour
plum** added. The plum is steeped into a sweet syrup, then stirred through the lime and
ice, often with a few pieces of plum and a pinch of salt, giving a deeper,
sweet-salty-sour flavour. The sour plum has its own [asam boi](/food-lifestyle/asam-boi)
guide; here it is simply the classic twist.

## Not the same as lime tea

It is worth clearing up a common mix-up. Air limau ais is **not** [teh O ais
limau](/food-lifestyle/teh-o-ais-limau), which is iced **black tea** with lime. Air
limau ais has **no tea at all** — just lime, sugar and water. The two taste related
because both are citrusy and cold, but one is a lime *tea* and the other a pure lime
*juice*.

## Where to find it

Air limau ais is everywhere drinks are poured: **mamak** outlets, **kopitiam** and
hawker stalls across the country. It is a default order on a hot day, and a natural
partner to a plate of mamak food. For the coffee-shop world it belongs to, see
[kopitiam and mamak culture](/food-lifestyle/kopitiam-mamak-culture).
