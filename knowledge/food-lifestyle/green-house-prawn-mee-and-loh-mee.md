On Jalan Burma, within the coffee shop known as Restoran Old Green House, a
night-time noodle stall has quietly earned one of Penang's most coveted food
credentials. **Green House Prawn Mee and Loh Mee** holds a MICHELIN **Bib
Gourmand** — the guide's award for good-quality, good-value cooking — on the
strength of two bowls and a broth regulars have loved for decades.

## A 30-year favourite

MICHELIN describes the stall as a favourite for more than **30 years**, the kind
of place loyal regulars keep coming back to. The Bib Gourmand recognises exactly
this: honest cooking at an everyday price. As a hawker stall it sits in the
guide's lowest ($) band — proof that Penang's street food stands on the same
stage as its restaurants.

## Prawn mee, loh mee, or both

The draw is the **red prawn soup**, built from pork bones and prawn shells
simmered down to a deep, savoury umami. It carries the Hokkien **prawn mee**; the
stall also serves **loh mee** in a thick, dark gravy, and regulars often order a
**mix of both** in one bowl. About a dozen additional toppings let you build the
bowl up as you like. These dishes belong to Penang's wider family of
[Malaysian noodles](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-noodles).

## Where it is

The stall operates inside **Restoran Old Green House at 223 Jalan Burma, George
Town, 10050**, a short way from the old-town core. MICHELIN classes it as street
food and lists it as **cash only**.

## Planning a visit

This is an evening stop — the stall trades at night — so come hungry after dark
and bring cash. Hours and the exact unit can vary between listings, so it is
worth confirming before you go. For more of the island's hawker canon, see our
guide to [Penang food](/food-lifestyle/penang-food) and the wider
[MICHELIN Guide in Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia).
