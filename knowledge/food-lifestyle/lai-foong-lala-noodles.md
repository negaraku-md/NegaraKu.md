Lai Foong Lala Noodles is a Chinese noodle specialist on Jalan Sultan, in the
heart of old Kuala Lumpur, that has earned a place in the
[MICHELIN Guide Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia). It is the
kind of unfussy, single-dish stall the Guide's Bib Gourmand is designed to
spotlight.

## A Bib Gourmand clam-noodle stall

The MICHELIN Guide lists Lai Foong Lala Noodles with a **Bib Gourmand**, the
award it gives to kitchens serving good quality at good value — distinct from
its one-to-three-star ratings. The Guide places the stall in its lowest price
band ($), in keeping with its street-food roots. As with every Bib Gourmand
entry, the recognition is reviewed each year.

## The lala bihun

The house dish is **lala bihun** — clam vermicelli. *Lala* is the Malay word for
clams, and the MICHELIN Guide singles out "a bowl of flavoursome lala bihun with
prawns" as a must, with a broth it describes as aromatic with Chinese yellow
wine and ginger. The Guide notes that portions are generous. It is one of
central KL's better-known examples of a Chinese-Malaysian noodle dish built
around a single, confident flavour; for the wider family of such bowls, see
[Malaysian noodles](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-noodles).

## Where to find it

The MICHELIN Guide gives the address as 99 Jalan Sultan, Kuala Lumpur City
Centre, 50000 Kuala Lumpur — in the old commercial quarter near Petaling
Street. The listing describes a corner shop whose interior is hung with red
lanterns, walls papered with large photographs, and vintage Chinese objects such
as an abacus among the decor.

## From street stall to corner shop

According to the MICHELIN listing, the business began as a street stall that
"shot to local fame" and later expanded to occupy two shop floors on the street
corner. No precise founding year is documented in the sources consulted, so the
timeline is best treated as approximate.

## Visiting note

Lai Foong Lala Noodles is a small, popular eatery, so queues and set hours are
common and details can change. Confirm current opening times and prices before
visiting, and treat the Bib Gourmand listing — revised annually — as current
rather than permanent.
