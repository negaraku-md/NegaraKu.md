Step into the middle shop of Sek Yuen on Jalan Pudu and the decades show — worn
fittings, a kitchen that still cooks over fierce heat, and waiters who have
worked the floor for a lifetime. Open since 1948 and now carrying a MICHELIN
Guide Bib Gourmand, it is about as close as Kuala Lumpur gets to eating inside
its own Cantonese history.

## A neighbourhood institution since 1948

Sek Yuen is a Cantonese restaurant in the Pudu district, recognised by the
MICHELIN Guide with a **Bib Gourmand** — its mark of good quality cooking at
good value. The guide records that it "has been a neighbourhood favourite since
it opened in 1948," which places it among the oldest Chinese restaurants still
running in the capital. Over the years it has spread across three adjacent
shophouses on the same street; the middle one keeps the most vintage character,
and much of the staff has been there for a long time. It is listed in the $$
price band.

## What to eat

The menu is deliberately traditional — the kind of comforting, old-school
[Chinese-Malaysian Cantonese cooking](/food-lifestyle/chinese-malaysian-cuisine)
that is getting harder to find. The MICHELIN Guide calls the **pipa duck** a
must, praising its spot-on seasoning, firm flesh and crispy skin. The other
famous order is the **roast suckling pig**, which must be requested two days
ahead — a reminder that this is cooking done the slow, deliberate way.

## Visiting note

Sek Yuen is at 313 Jalan Pudu, Kuala Lumpur. It is closed on Mondays and serves
lunch and dinner the rest of the week. Bookings are handled by the restaurant
directly, and for the suckling pig you will need to plan two days in advance.
It is one of the heritage entries on the
[MICHELIN Guide Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia) Bib Gourmand
list, which is refreshed each year.
