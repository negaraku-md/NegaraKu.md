On Jalan Yew in Pudu, a row of claypots sits over glowing charcoal, each one
crisping a crust of rice at the bottom. Foong Lian has cooked claypot rice this
way since 1986, and the MICHELIN Guide now lists it with a Bib Gourmand.

## Claypot rice over charcoal since 1986

Foong Lian is a Cantonese eatery recognised by the MICHELIN Guide with a **Bib
Gourmand** — its mark of good quality cooking at good value. "A firm favourite
with the locals," the guide writes, "Foong Lian has been feeding hungry diners
claypot rice cooked over charcoal since 1986." The kitchen uses two kinds of
rice and watches the heat closely, which is what gives the dish its prized
scorched crust. It is listed in the $ (least expensive) band and tagged "Worth
Queueing For."

## What to order

The claypot rice is the reason to come. It is served with a preserved-meat
version — pork and liver sausage — or with toppings of chicken leg and salted
fish. The guide also recommends the soups and snacks, singling out the tofu skin
roll. It is a straightforward, old-school expression of
[Chinese-Malaysian Cantonese cooking](/food-lifestyle/chinese-malaysian-cuisine).

## Visiting note

Foong Lian is at 55 Jalan Yew, Pudu, Kuala Lumpur. Per the guide it does not
accept credit cards, so bring cash, and it manages its own bookings. It is one
of the value entries on the
[MICHELIN Guide Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia) Bib Gourmand
list, which is refreshed each year.
