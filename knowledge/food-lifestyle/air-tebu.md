On a hot Malaysian afternoon, few things cut through the heat like a cup of **air
tebu** — sugarcane juice, pressed fresh from the stalk, poured pale green over a
tower of ice. It is one of the simplest drinks on the street: cane in, juice out,
nothing added but maybe a squeeze of lime. And that simplicity is exactly the point.

## What air tebu is

Air tebu is the juice squeezed from **sugarcane stalks**. At the stall you will see
the machine that makes it: a motorised **roller press** that crushes length after
length of cane, sometimes feeding each stalk through two or three times to wring out
the last of the juice. What comes out is strained and poured straight over ice — a
sweet, faintly grassy, pale-green drink that tastes of nothing but the cane.

For where air tebu sits among the country's wider drinks board, see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the deep
dive into the sugarcane cooler itself.

## The squeeze of lime

Sugarcane juice on its own is **very sweet**, and left plain it can taste a little
flat. The classic fix is a squeeze of **lime** — *limau nipis* or the small, fragrant
*limau kasturi* (calamansi) — which sharpens the sweetness, adds a citrus lift and
makes the whole thing far more refreshing. Not every stall adds it, so you can order
air tebu plain or ask for the lime; some vendors also drop in a little salted plum or
a pinch of salt for contrast. However it is dressed, the drink is best **fresh**:
cane juice oxidises and ferments quickly, which is why it is pressed to order rather
than kept.

## A natural cooler, in moderation

Part of air tebu's appeal is that it is **natural** — no syrups, no colouring, no
fizz, just the juice of the plant. That makes it feel a world apart from a canned soft
drink. But it is worth remembering that it is, quite literally, **cane sugar in
liquid form**: delicious and hydrating on a hot day, yet high in sugar, so it is best
enjoyed as a treat rather than by the litre. Alongside [coconut water](/food-lifestyle/air-kelapa),
it is one of the country's great natural thirst-quenchers.

## Where to find it

Air tebu is a creature of the **street**. Look for it wherever cane stalks are stacked
beside a roller press: **pasar malam** (night markets), morning markets, roadside
hawker stalls, food courts and juice bars across the country. On a sweltering
afternoon, the sight of that green juice filling a cup is its own invitation. For the
wider world of markets and hawker food it belongs to, see
[kopitiam and mamak culture](/food-lifestyle/kopitiam-mamak-culture).
