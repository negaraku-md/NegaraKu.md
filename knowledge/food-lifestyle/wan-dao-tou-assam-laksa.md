In a city that treats assam laksa as a point of pride, Wan Dao Tou has earned a
place on the MICHELIN Guide's map of George Town — proof that one of Penang's
most everyday bowls can also be one of its most carefully made.

## A Bib Gourmand bowl of assam laksa

Wan Dao Tou Assam Laksa is listed as a **MICHELIN Bib Gourmand** in the MICHELIN
Guide Kuala Lumpur & Penang selection — the distinction the guide gives to places
serving good food at good value, a tier below the MICHELIN Star but a genuine
mark of inspector approval. Its single specialty is
[assam laksa](/food-lifestyle/laksa), the sour northern bowl built on a
tamarind-soured fish broth rather than coconut milk.

## The signature dish

MICHELIN's reviewer describes a soup "well balanced between sweet, sour and
spicy," with cucumber, shallot and pineapple cutting the heat of the broth, and
singles out the starter platter as worth ordering alongside. The laksa arrives
over thick rice noodles in the classic Penang style — the fish simmered down into
a tart, savoury stock that defines the dish across the island's best-known
[Penang food](/food-lifestyle/penang-food).

## Location and background

The stall sits at **1 Jalan Gottlieb, George Town, 10350**, on Penang island. The
recipe is a family inheritance: MICHELIN notes it dates back roughly 50 years and
is now cooked by the third-generation owner, who follows the method handed down by
his grandparents. That long continuity — one family, one dish, decades of
practice — is a large part of why the bowl tastes as settled as it does.

## Visiting

Wan Dao Tou is a cash-only street-food stall and does not accept credit cards.
MICHELIN's listing shows it closed on Mondays and open roughly 11:00 to 17:00 from
Tuesday to Sunday, though stall hours in Penang change often and popular bowls can
sell out, so it is worth confirming before you make the trip.
