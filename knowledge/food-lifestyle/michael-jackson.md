Some drinks are named for what is in them; this one is named for how it looks. Order a
**"Michael Jackson"** at a Malaysian coffee shop and you will get a glass of white soy
milk poured over black grass jelly — a drink better known on some menus by the plainer
name **soya cincau**, but forever nicknamed for its black-and-white halves.

## What it is

The Michael Jackson is a simple pairing: **soy milk** and **cincau**, or grass jelly,
sweetened with **sugar syrup** and served cold over ice. The dark, soft jelly settles
at the bottom; the pale soy milk goes over the top; and before you drink, you **stir**
— bringing the two layers into one smooth, lightly sweet, cooling glass. The jelly
lends a slippery, faintly herbal note; the soy milk, a gentle creaminess. Together
they make one of the kopitiam's most quietly satisfying coolers.

For where it sits among the country's wider drinks board, see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the deep
dive into the black-and-white drink itself.

## Where the nickname comes from

The name is pure street wordplay. The **black** grass jelly and the **white** soy milk
sit side by side, and that contrast earned the drink its nickname — popularly tied to
the Michael Jackson song **"Black or White."** It is a piece of vendor humour from
both Malaysia and Singapore, and part of its charm is that **no one can say who coined
it**: it simply caught on and stuck. For that reason it is best treated as folk
naming, with *soya cincau* the plain descriptive label underneath.

## Two Chinese-origin halves

Both parts of the drink trace back to Chinese food culture. **Soy milk** needs little
introduction — it is covered in its own [soya bean drink](/food-lifestyle/soya-bean)
guide. **Cincau**, the grass jelly, is a soft, near-black jelly set from the *Mesona*
plant (a relative of mint), long used in cooling drinks and desserts across the
region; its full story is in the [cincau](/food-lifestyle/cincau) article. The Michael
Jackson simply brings these two old favourites together in one glass.

## A drink for every table

Because it is made entirely of **plant-based** ingredients — soy milk, grass jelly and
sugar — the Michael Jackson is **vegetarian** and, in itself, **halal**, an easy order
across communities. As always, halal assurance attaches to the premises, so diners who
need certification should check the individual stall. For the coffee-shop world it
belongs to, see [kopitiam and mamak culture](/food-lifestyle/kopitiam-mamak-culture).
