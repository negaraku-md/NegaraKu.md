Order the set lunch at **Nirwana** and it arrives the way banana leaf rice
should: a glossy leaf laid flat, a mound of rice, and a rotating cast of
vegetable curries ladled around it, with the option to pile on fish, chicken or
lamb. The MICHELIN Guide lists this Bangsar Baru mainstay — widely signed and
known as *Sri Nirwana Maju* — as a Bib Gourmand address, its shorthand for
good-quality cooking at a price that does not sting.

## A Bangsar banana-leaf institution

Nirwana sits in the thick of Bangsar Baru's eating strip, by Bangsar Village,
and has long been one of Kuala Lumpur's reference points for South Indian
banana leaf rice. The MICHELIN Guide classifies it as Indian cuisine in the
`$$` band and tags it "Eat like a local" — a plain, busy, order-and-tuck-in
room rather than a dining destination. The Bib Gourmand it carries is not a
MICHELIN Star but a parallel distinction for value, and it is one of the
Malaysian entries in the [MICHELIN Guide to Kuala Lumpur and
Penang](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia).

## The food: banana leaf rice and biryani

The recommendation MICHELIN makes is direct: go for one of the set menus with
rice and vegetable curry on banana leaves, then add fish, chicken or lamb to
taste. That is the house format of [banana leaf
rice](/food-lifestyle/banana-leaf-rice) — unlimited rice and curries eaten from
the leaf, by hand in the traditional style. The one dish the Guide singles out
is the chicken biryani, praised for moist, tender meat and balanced flavours.

## Where it is

The restaurant is at 43 Jalan Telawi 3, Bangsar Baru, Kuala Lumpur 59100.
MICHELIN lists it as open daily, 11:30 to 23:00, with credit cards accepted and
bookings handled by the restaurant directly. As with any eatery, hours and
details can shift, so confirm before making a special trip.

## In the wider picture

Nirwana's Bib Gourmand places a humble banana leaf shop alongside the
peninsula's fine-dining rooms in the same Guide — the point of the Bib
Gourmand, which exists to recognise exactly this kind of unpretentious,
well-made, affordable cooking. It is a useful entry in Kuala Lumpur's map of
[Indian-Malaysian cuisine](/food-lifestyle/indian-malaysian-cuisine), where the
banana leaf meal is one of the community's most enduring contributions to the
national table.

## Visiting note

Come hungry and at a mealtime, expect a queue at peak hours, and eat the way the
room does — rice and curries from the leaf, biryani on the side. If you want the
biryani, ask, as it is prepared as its own dish rather than part of the standard
vegetable set.
