7 Chai Ipoh Hor Fun is a small, no-frills noodle shop in the Pandan Perdana
neighbourhood of Kuala Lumpur, and one of the more recent names to earn a place
in the [MICHELIN Guide](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia) Kuala Lumpur &
Penang selection.

## What it is

The shop carries a **Bib Gourmand**, the MICHELIN distinction for good food at
good value — a separate recognition from the MICHELIN Stars and one that
inspectors reserve for places offering a satisfying meal at a modest price. The
Guide files 7 Chai in its lowest ($) price tier and tags it "Eat like a local,"
a fair description of an unpretentious shop built around a single dish done well.

## The signature dish

7 Chai specialises in **Ipoh hor fun** — the smooth, slippery flat rice noodles
that are a hallmark of the town of Ipoh, in Perak. What sets the style apart is
the broth: MICHELIN describes it as "a rich broth made with chicken bones and
shrimp heads," a clear soup whose sweetness comes from long simmering rather than
heavy seasoning. The Guide recommends the shredded-chicken version — the classic
*kai si hor fun* treatment — with shrimp wontons available as an optional add-on.

## Location

The shop is at **33 Jalan Perdana 4/1, Pandan Perdana, Kuala Lumpur 55300**, in a
residential-commercial pocket on the city's eastern side. It is an informal
eatery rather than a sit-down restaurant, and the MICHELIN Guide notes that
bookings are handled by the shop directly.

## Ipoh hor fun in context

Ipoh-style hor fun is one of Perak's best-loved exports, and good versions have
spread across the Klang Valley as cooks from the region settled in Kuala Lumpur.
The dish belongs to the wider family of
[Malaysian noodle dishes](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-noodles), and its roots lie
in the celebrated food culture of [Ipoh](/food-lifestyle/ipoh-food), a town whose
mineral-rich water is often credited for the silkiness of its rice noodles and
the crispness of its bean sprouts.

## Visiting note

As an affordable, single-specialty shop, 7 Chai is best approached the way
MICHELIN frames it — as a local breakfast or lunch stop rather than a destination
dinner. Hours and queues at popular Bib Gourmand spots change often, so it is
worth confirming opening times before making a special trip, and treating the
MICHELIN listing as the authoritative record of its current status.
