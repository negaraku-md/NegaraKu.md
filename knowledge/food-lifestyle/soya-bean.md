At countless Malaysian coffee shops a single stall does nothing but soy: a vat of
warm, fresh soy milk on one side, trays of trembling soft tofu on the other, a row
of bottles and a stack of plastic cups. Order a "soya bean" and you get a glass of
that fresh milk, lightly sweet, hot or poured over ice — one of the most
unassuming and most beloved drinks in the country.

## What soya bean is

The soya bean drink is simply **fresh soy milk**. Dried soybeans are soaked,
ground with water, boiled and strained into a smooth milk, then sweetened — often
with rock or brown sugar — and sometimes scented with **pandan**. It is drunk hot
or iced. Unlike the plain, unsweetened soy milk common in the West, the Malaysian
coffee-shop version is usually enjoyed fresh and lightly sweet, with a clean,
beany, faintly nutty taste. Being plant-based, it is naturally **dairy-free**,
which makes it an easy choice for anyone who is lactose-intolerant.

For where it sits among the country's wider drinks board — sirap bandung, cincau,
Milo and the rest — see the [Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks)
hub. This article is the deep dive into the soy drink itself.

## Its many names — and tau foo fah

Part of what makes the drink confusing to newcomers is that it answers to several
names. In Hokkien it is **tau chui**; in Malay you will see **susu soya** or
**air tauhu**; on an English menu it is just "soya bean" or "soy milk". All point
to the same thing.

The other constant is its partner. The very same soy milk, set soft with a
coagulant, becomes **tau foo fah** (also spelled *tau fu fah*) — the silky
soft-tofu pudding served in a sweet, often ginger- or pandan-scented syrup. Stalls
almost always sell the drink and the pudding together, which is why the two are
hard to separate: a "soya bean and tau foo fah" is one of the classic cheap treats
of the Malaysian street.

## Soya cincau — the "Michael Jackson"

Pour the white soy milk over cubes of black grass jelly and you get **soya
cincau** — a two-tone drink whose black-and-white layers have earned it the
affectionate nickname **"Michael Jackson"** at hawker stalls across Malaysia and
Singapore. It is cheap, refreshing and a neat marriage of two coffee-shop
staples; the grass-jelly half is covered in [cincau](/food-lifestyle/cincau).

## Regional and modern versions

Like most Malaysian drinks, soy milk shifts by place and era. **Penang-style**
soya bean is often scented with pandan and sweetened with **brown sugar**, giving
it a darker, more caramel note. More recently, a wave of dedicated **soy cafés**
has pushed the drink upmarket, offering flavoured versions — black bean, black
sesame, red bean — alongside the classic. The everyday glass at the kopitiam,
though, remains the heart of it; for that institution, see
[kopitiam and mamak culture](/food-lifestyle/kopitiam-mamak-culture).
