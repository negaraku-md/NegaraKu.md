Beside the tin of Milo on every Malaysian coffee-shop shelf sits another malty
favourite: **Horlicks**. Hot and comforting, or poured over ice as **ais Horlicks**, it
is the kopitiam's other great malted drink — sweet, toasty and entirely free of
caffeine.

## What Horlicks is

Horlicks is a **malted-milk drink**. The powder is made from **malted barley and
wheat** blended with milk, and it is mixed with hot water or milk into a sweet, malty
cup. "Malted" means the grains have been sprouted and dried to bring out their sugars
and flavour — that is where the characteristic toasty-sweet taste comes from. Because
it is **caffeine-free**, it has long been sold as a soothing drink for any time of day.

For where Horlicks sits among the country's wider drinks board, see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the deep dive
into the malted cup itself.

## A brand with a long history

Horlicks is a **brand name** — a trademark — rather than a generic drink. It was
developed by the British-born brothers **James and William Horlick**, who set up a food
company in the United States in the 1870s and patented an early malted food in the
1880s. From those beginnings as a malted infant and invalid food, it grew into a
malted-milk drink sold around the world, and became a long-standing favourite across
Malaysia and Singapore. Over the decades the brand has **changed corporate hands
several times**, so it is best to treat it as the familiar malted brand rather than to
pin it to one owner.

## How it is drunk here

At a kopitiam you simply ask for **Horlicks** — hot by default, or **ais Horlicks** for
the iced version. Like its neighbours [Milo](/food-lifestyle/milo-dinosaur), kopi and
teh, it is usually sweetened and enriched with **condensed or evaporated milk**, and
you can ask for it less sweet. It is the malty alternative on a board otherwise built
on coffee and tea — a nostalgic, gentle cup many Malaysians grew up with.

## A note on halal

Horlicks is a **milk-based, non-alcoholic** drink, and packaged Horlicks sold in
Malaysia is generally produced to carry **halal certification**. As with any branded
product, the reliable check is the **halal mark on the packaging**; for a cup mixed at
a stall, the premises' own certification applies. For the coffee-shop world it belongs
to, see [kopitiam and mamak culture](/food-lifestyle/kopitiam-mamak-culture).
