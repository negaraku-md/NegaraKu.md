Char koay kak is a hawker insider's dish — cubes of rice cake charred in a
screaming wok, eaten fast and early — and on Lorong Macalister a stall run by
three sisters has carried it into the MICHELIN Guide on the strength of that
smoky char.

## A Bib Gourmand char koay kak stall

Sister Yao's Char Koay Kak holds a MICHELIN **Bib Gourmand** — the mark for
good-quality cooking at good value — in the
[MICHELIN Guide Kuala Lumpur & Penang](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia).
It is one of the city's purest hawker-to-guide stories: a single dish, cooked at
one stall, recognised on its own terms.

## The signature dish

The one dish is **char koay kak**, a Teochew-origin fried rice cake and a staple
of [Penang food](/food-lifestyle/penang-food). Steamed rice-cake cubes are
stir-fried over a very hot wok until they take on smoky **wok hei**, then tossed
with soy sauce, bean sprouts, egg and chilli, with extra hot sauce on request.

## Where it is

The stall is at **96 Lorong Macalister, George Town, 11400**, in the inner city.
The MICHELIN Guide lists it at the lowest ($) price level, and it is cash only.

## Background

The business was begun by the sisters' father in **1963**, and three daughters
now run it, continuing a recipe that traces to the family's Teochew roots. That
inheritance — a father's life's work carried on by the next generation — is a
common thread among Penang's longest-standing hawker names.

## Visiting

Sister Yao's keeps morning hours and is closed on some weekdays, so a visit is
best planned around its trading days and early in the day. It takes no bookings,
and — as with every Bib Gourmand — the distinction is reviewed each year, so
check the current MICHELIN listing before you go.
