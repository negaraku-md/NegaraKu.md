Prawn mee is one of Penang's defining bowls, and on Lebuh Pantai a long-running
stall has turned it into a MICHELIN-recognised draw — a spicy, prawn-deep broth
that now pulls queues down a quiet corner of the old town.

## A Bib Gourmand prawn-noodle stall

Bridge Street Prawn Noodle holds a MICHELIN **Bib Gourmand** — the mark for
good-quality cooking at good value — in the
[MICHELIN Guide Kuala Lumpur & Penang](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia).
Its popularity has grown noticeably since the recognition, so an early arrival
is the surest way to beat the queue.

## The signature bowl

The dish to order is **prawn mee**, one of the great
[Malaysian noodle soups](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-noodles). Diners choose
between yellow egg noodles and rice noodles, then take on a spicy prawn broth
that the MICHELIN inspectors describe as loaded with deep flavour. Worthwhile
add-ons include braised pork spareribs and fish balls, which bulk the bowl into
a full meal.

## Where it is

The stall sits at **533 Lebuh Pantai, George Town, 10300**, in the heart of the
inner city's [Penang food](/food-lifestyle/penang-food) grid. The MICHELIN Guide
lists it at the lowest ($) price level, and it is cash only.

## Background

The public record through the MICHELIN listing is light on the stall's history.
Secondary coverage describes it as a long-established, family-run business, but
those details — including any claim about how many generations have run it — are
best confirmed locally before they are stated as fact.

## Visiting

Bridge Street keeps morning-to-early-afternoon hours and is closed on some
weekdays, so the window to catch it is narrow. It takes no bookings, trading
over the counter, and — as with every Bib Gourmand — the distinction is reviewed
each year, so check the current MICHELIN listing before you make the trip.
