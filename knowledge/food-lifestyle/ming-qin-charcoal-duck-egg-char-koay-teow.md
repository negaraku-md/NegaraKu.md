On the Penang mainland in Bukit Mertajam, a plain evening noodle stall has earned
a MICHELIN Guide Bib Gourmand for doing one thing the old way — frying char koay
teow with duck egg over live charcoal.

## A Bib Gourmand mainland stall

Ming Qin Charcoal Duck Egg Char Koay Teow is listed in the MICHELIN Guide Kuala
Lumpur & Penang as a **Bib Gourmand**, the guide's mark for good-quality cooking
at a good value rather than a star. It sits in the noodles category at the lowest
($) price tier. The MICHELIN write-up is candid that there is "lack of fancy
decor" — the draw is entirely the food. It is one of the Seberang Perai mainland
entries on Penang's MICHELIN map; see
[the MICHELIN Guide in Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia).

## The signature char koay teow

[Char koay teow](/food-lifestyle/char-kway-teow) is Penang's famous stir-fried
flat-rice-noodle dish, and its holy grail is *wok hei* — the smoky char a
blisteringly hot wok imparts. Ming Qin chases it two ways: the noodles are fried
with **duck egg** for a richer body, and the wok sits over a **charcoal fire**
rather than gas, which the MICHELIN description says lends "exceptional wok hei".
Regulars add an extra over-easy egg, or start with the blanched octopus
appetiser — served warm with a tangy-sweet dip — the dish the MICHELIN inspectors
single out as a starter before the namesake noodles.

## Where it is

The MICHELIN listing places it at Jalan Betik, off Jalan Sungai Rambai, Bukit
Mertajam, Seberang Perai, 14000, Penang. Bukit Mertajam is a mainland town east
of the channel, part of the growing [mainland food scene](/food-lifestyle/penang-food)
that the MICHELIN Guide now covers alongside George Town.

## Background

By the MICHELIN account, the stall has built a loyal fan base over more than 20
years. It can stay busy into the night on Fridays and Saturdays — a neighbourhood
institution rather than a tourist stop, sustained by word of mouth and the
charcoal wok.

## Visiting note

This is an evening business — the MICHELIN listing shows evening hours and a
Monday closure — so plan for dinner rather than breakfast, and expect a wait at
the weekend. Charcoal frying is done to order, one plate at a time, so patience
is part of the experience.
