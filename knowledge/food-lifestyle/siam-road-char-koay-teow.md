Among George Town's many char koay teow stalls, the one on Jalan Siam has earned
an outsized reputation — in large part because it still fries over a charcoal
fire, the old way, and the MICHELIN Guide has taken note.

## What it is

Siam Road Char Koay Teow is a street-food stall, listed by the MICHELIN Guide as
a **Bib Gourmand** pick — the Guide's award for good-quality, good-value cooking,
given freely to hawker and street-food spots (see the
[MICHELIN Guide in Malaysia](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia) for how the
distinctions work). It is a street-food listing in the Guide's lowest price band,
tagged "worth queueing for," cash-only, and not available for reservations.

## The charcoal difference

The stall is best known for one thing: cooking its
[char koay teow](/food-lifestyle/char-kway-teow) over charcoal rather than gas.
The MICHELIN listing says the result is a "remarkable wok hei" — the smoky,
slightly scorched aroma that a fierce flame drives into the noodles, and the
single quality Penang diners most associate with a great plate. The Guide adds
that it is worth paying extra for prawns and cockles, and that the spicy version
"hits all the right notes." The stall concentrates on this one dish rather than a
broad menu.

## Where to find it

The stall is at 82 Jalan Siam, George Town, 10400, Penang. The MICHELIN listing
shows it open Tuesday to Saturday, 12:00–18:00, closed Sunday and Monday. In
practice it trades for only part of the day, and queues — often of tourists and
younger diners — tend to form before it opens, so arrive early.

## Before you go

Bring cash and budget time for the queue, as every plate is fried to order over
charcoal in small batches. There are no bookings. Because the MICHELIN selection
is refreshed each year, check the current Guide if the distinction is your reason
for making the trip.
