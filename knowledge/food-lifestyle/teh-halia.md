Take the country's most famous drink, add a warm kick of ginger, and you have **teh
halia** — ginger milk tea, the spiced cousin of [teh tarik](/food-lifestyle/teh-tarik).
Sweet, creamy and gently fiery, it is the cup Malaysians reach for when they want
something warming and soothing.

## What teh halia is

Teh halia is **strong black tea** brewed or infused with **ginger** (*halia*), then
sweetened and enriched with **condensed milk** — the same milky, sweet base as teh
tarik, plus the warm, gently spicy note of ginger. The name says it plainly: *halia* is
Malay for ginger, so *teh halia* is simply "ginger tea." The kopitiam version tends to
be quite sweet, thanks to the sweetened condensed milk, and some stalls brew fresh
ginger into the tea while others use a ginger mix.

For where teh halia sits among the country's wider drinks board, see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the deep dive
into the ginger milk tea itself.

## A member of the teh tarik family

The easiest way to understand teh halia is as **teh tarik with ginger**. It shares the
same pulled-tea base, and when it is **"pulled"** — poured back and forth between two
vessels to froth and cool it — it is called **teh tarik halia**. So rather than a
separate tradition, it is the **spiced member of the teh tarik family**, sitting on the
same board as kopi, teh and [kopi cham](/food-lifestyle/kopi-cham). For the wider logic
of coffee-shop drinks, see
[kopi, teh tarik and coffee-shop drinks](/food-lifestyle/kopi-teh-tarik-and-coffeeshop-drinks).

## Hot, iced, and a Ramadan favourite

Teh halia is served both **hot and iced**, but it is especially loved **hot**, where
the ginger's warmth carries through and makes it a soothing, comforting cup. Because
ginger is associated with warmth, many people turn to a hot teh halia when they want
something gentle — and it is a popular choice at **suhoor**, the pre-dawn meal during
Ramadan. (That warmth is a matter of comfort and tradition rather than a medical
claim.)

## Where to find it

Teh halia is poured at **kopitiam** and **mamak** counters across the country, from the
same station as teh tarik and kopi, and it is also sold as **instant ginger-milk-tea
sachets** for home. For the institution that pours it, see
[kopitiam and mamak culture](/food-lifestyle/kopitiam-mamak-culture).
