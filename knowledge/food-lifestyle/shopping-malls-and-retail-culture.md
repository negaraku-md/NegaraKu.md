On a Saturday afternoon in the Klang Valley, a single mega mall can hold more people than some small towns — and many of them are not there only to shop. Malaysian malls double as air-conditioned town squares: places to eat, watch a film, ice-skate, pray at a surau, and let children run through an indoor theme park. Understanding retail here means understanding that range, from the giant mall to the open-air night market.

## Which are Malaysia's biggest malls?

The country's largest malls cluster tightly around Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and Putrajaya. Size is usually measured by **net lettable area (NLA)** — the floor space a mall can actually rent to tenants.

| Mall | Location | Net lettable area | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| IOI City Mall | IOI Resort City, Putrajaya | ~2.5 million sq ft | Developer calls it the largest regional mall in Malaysia |
| 1 Utama | Bandar Utama, Selangor | ~2.19 million sq ft | Long the largest before IOI City Mall's expansion |
| Mid Valley Megamall | Mid Valley City, Kuala Lumpur | 1,798,148 sq ft | 556 tenancies (as of 31 Dec 2024) |
| Pavilion Kuala Lumpur | Bukit Bintang, Kuala Lumpur | 1,370,953 sq ft | 96.8% occupancy at 31 Dec 2025; luxury-led |
| Suria KLCC | KLCC, Kuala Lumpur | ~1 million sq ft retail | Over 340 specialty stores, at the Petronas Twin Towers |

Figures come from each mall's owner or developer, except the 1 Utama NLA, reported by The Edge Malaysia. The concentration is striking: the five malls above sit within one metropolitan region, and each one alone rivals the total mall space of a mid-sized city elsewhere.

## Mall, shoplot or pasar — what are the retail formats?

Malls are only the most visible layer. Everyday Malaysian retail runs across three overlapping formats:

- **Mega malls and shopping centres** — enclosed, air-conditioned, anchored by a department store, a hypermarket and a cinema. This is the weekend-destination format.
- **Shoplots (kedai)** — the two- to four-storey terraced shophouse is the backbone of every town: a ground-floor shop (kopitiam, pharmacy, hardware, mobile-phone dealer) with an office or home above. This is where much of the country actually buys its daily goods.
- **Pasar (open-air markets)** — the daytime *pasar basah* (wet market) for fresh meat, fish and produce; the *pasar tani* (farmers' market); and the *pasar malam* (night market) that rotates through neighbourhoods on a fixed weeknight, selling street food and cheap goods from tarpaulin stalls.

A single Malaysian household will typically use all three in a week — mall for a cinema outing, shoplot for a haircut, and the pasar malam for dinner and vegetables.

## How big is retail in the economy?

Retail is one of the most closely tracked consumer indicators in the country. According to the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM), the wholesale-and-retail-trade sector reached a record **RM1.77 trillion in 2024**, and within that the **retail trade sub-sector grew 6.1% to RM764.9 billion**.

The monthly numbers show the same momentum: DOSM recorded **RM150.1 billion** in total wholesale-and-retail sales in October 2024, up 5.5% year-on-year. Strong domestic spending and returning tourism are the usual drivers cited.

## Why is there no tax at the checkout?

Shoppers arriving from countries with GST or VAT often look for a tax line at the till and do not find one for most goods. That is by design. Malaysia's **Sales and Service Tax (SST) is a single-stage tax, not a value-added tax** — it is charged once, not at every step of the supply chain.

- **Sales tax on goods** is levied at **5% or 10%** (with **0%** on essential goods), charged at the manufacturing or import stage. By the time a product reaches a shelf, that tax is already embedded in the ticket price — the number on the tag is what you pay.
- **Service tax** is separate. It applies at **6% or 8%** depending on the service, and is charged by service providers such as restaurants and salons — which is why a café bill may add a service-tax line that a supermarket receipt does not.

The government has kept adjusting this structure. From **1 July 2025**, the Ministry of Finance revised sales-tax rates on discretionary and non-essential goods while keeping essentials untaxed, and expanded the service-tax scope to cover areas such as leasing or rental, construction, private healthcare, education and beauty services.

## When do the sales happen?

Retail here runs on a rhythm of sale seasons. Nationwide mega-sale campaigns cluster around the **mid-year** and **year-end** periods, and festive shopping surges before **Hari Raya Aidilfitri, Chinese New Year and Deepavali**, when malls extend hours and run bazaars. Online, the paired-digit dates (such as 11.11 and 12.12) have become their own shopping events. Because goods prices are quoted tax-inclusive, an advertised discount is a discount off the final price — there is no separate tax to add back at the counter.
