Most people meet nutmeg as a hard brown seed grated over a custard or a curry. In
Penang, it arrives in a very different form: a tall, ice-cold glass of pale,
sweet-and-tangy juice, pressed not from the spice but from the *fruit* that holds
it. *Jus buah pala* — nutmeg juice — is one of the island's signature drinks, and a
small lesson in how one fruit can live two completely separate lives.

## The fruit, not the spice

This is the thing to understand first. The nutmeg **fruit** is a pale, apricot-like
globe. Split it open and inside sits a hard seed wrapped in a lacy red web: the seed,
dried, is the **nutmeg spice**, and the red web is **mace**. The fleshy outer fruit
around them — sour and astringent when raw — is what goes into the drink. So nutmeg
juice and the nutmeg in your kitchen come from the same fruit but from entirely
different parts; the juice is the fruit, not the spice.

For where nutmeg juice sits among the country's wider drinks board, see the
[Malaysian drinks](/food-lifestyle/malaysian-drinks) hub. This article is the deep
dive into the Penang cooler itself.

## White, brown, and a sour plum

Vendors make it in two broad styles. The fresh **"white" juice** is pressed from the
raw fruit — pale, bright and tangy. The darker **"brown" version** is made by
boiling, which rounds it into something sweeter and deeper. Across both, a
**preserved sour plum (asam)** is a common addition, cutting the sweetness and
sharpening the flavour; at many stalls you can simply ask for it. The taste is sweet
and tangy with a distinctive fruity fragrance and a slightly puckering, astringent
finish — refreshing to most, an acquired aroma to some.

## A Penang drink, with a colonial root

Nutmeg is **not native to Malaysia**. The British cultivated it heavily in Penang
from the **late 18th century** as part of their spice trade, and the island has grown
it ever since — which is why nutmeg juice is so firmly a *Penang* drink rather than a
nationwide one. Much of the fruit comes from the farms around **Balik Pulau** and
**Teluk Bahang**, where long-running makers such as the **Ghee Hup** nutmeg factory
press the juice and also turn the fruit into pickled and candied nutmeg. For the
island's wider food world, see [Penang food](/food-lifestyle/penang-food).

A note of care rather than alarm: it is the nutmeg *seed*, eaten in quantity, that is
a known toxin — the drink is made from the fruit flesh, and any "cooling" or tonic
reputation it carries is folk tradition rather than proven medicine.

## Where to find it

Nutmeg juice is a Penang street and market drink, poured ice-cold at fruit-juice
stalls and food courts, and most associated with George Town and the nutmeg country
of Balik Pulau. If you cannot get to the island, the **bottled concentrate** from
Penang nutmeg makers lets you mix it at home. Alongside it on the cooling-drinks
shelf sits the herbal [air mata kucing](/food-lifestyle/air-mata-kucing).
