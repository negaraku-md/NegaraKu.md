Follow Jalan Masjid off Lebuh Chulia and you reach a working kueh kitchen with a
few tables under a glass roof, Penang-style umbrellas overhead for shade and
colour. This is **Moh Teng Pheow Nyonya Koay**, a MICHELIN Bib Gourmand address
whose whole pitch is continuity: its treats, the Guide says, are made just as
they were back in 1933.

## A kueh maker, Bib Gourmand and all

MICHELIN lists Moh Teng Pheow under "Small eats" in the `$` band — budget by the
Guide's own scale — and awards it a Bib Gourmand for good cooking at good value.
It is one of the Penang entries in the [MICHELIN Guide to Kuala Lumpur and
Penang](/food-lifestyle/michelin-guide-malaysia), and a reminder that the
distinction reaches beyond restaurants to a place that is, at heart, a cake
factory with seating.

## The food: traditional Nyonya kueh

The draw is [Nyonya kueh](/food-lifestyle/kuih-muih) — the sweet and savoury
cakes, dumplings, puddings and pastries of Peranakan kitchens, made by steaming,
deep-frying and baking. MICHELIN points to kueh lapis (the layered steamed
cake), kueh talam (a two-layer coconut-and-pandan pudding), ang koo (the
red, molded glutinous-rice cake) and Nyonya chang (Peranakan rice dumplings).
A few Nyonya dishes round out the menu, but the kueh are the reason to come.

## Where it is

The shop is on Jalan Masjid, off Lebuh Chulia, George Town 10200 — deep in the
old quarter but set back from the main street. MICHELIN lists it open daily,
10:00 to 17:00, with air conditioning and credit cards accepted. It is a
daytime, afternoon-close operation.

## Background

By MICHELIN's account the business grew out of a kueh factory the owner's father
ran from 1933; a later sit-down space let guests eat the freshly made kueh on
the spot rather than buy it to carry away. The thread the Guide keeps pulling is
the sameness of the recipes across the decades — the selling point of a shop
that measures itself against its own past.

## Visiting note

Go earlier in the day while the full range is out, since popular kueh can sell
through before closing. Eat it there under the glass roof if you can; part of the
appeal is seeing the kueh where it is made, as part of George Town's living
[Nyonya and Peranakan food](/food-lifestyle/nyonya-peranakan-cuisine) tradition.
