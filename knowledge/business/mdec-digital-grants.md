Most founders looking for MDEC funding search for "the MDEC grant" and expect a single scheme. There are really two, they fund opposite ends of a company's journey, and both share one non-obvious gate: you have to already be inside Malaysia Digital to get in. Miss that, and the amount on the page is irrelevant.

Here is how MDEC's two digital-economy grants work — what each one is for, the Malaysia Digital status door they both sit behind, and the matched, reimbursement mechanics that decide whether they actually fit your cash flow.

## Who runs these grants, and why it matters

The **Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC)** is the government agency that promotes Malaysia's digital economy. It administers **Malaysia Digital (MD) status** and, on top of that, a set of co-funding grants for digital companies. MDEC sits under the **Ministry of Digital**.

That reporting line is the reason the grants and the status are joined at the hip. MDEC's grants are not a general small-business fund — they are an incentive built onto MD status, aimed at the Malaysia Digital **promoted sectors** (digital agriculture, services, cities, health, finance, trade, content, tourism, Islamic digital economy) and the **technology enablers** (AI and big data, blockchain, cloud, cybersecurity, drone tech, IoT, robotics and XR, creative media technology, IC design and embedded software, advanced network connectivity). If your work does not sit in that map, these are not your grants.

## The two grants, and what each is actually for

The two grants fund different stages of the same journey.

| | Malaysia Digital Catalyst Grant (MDCG) | Malaysia Digital X-Port Grant (MDXG) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | Build and commercialise digital technology and IP | Take a digital product to export / global markets |
| **Stage** | Development and commercialisation | Market access and expansion abroad |
| **Typical fit** | Co-creating a solution with an end-user partner, creating new IP | An export-ready company scaling beyond Malaysia |

**MDCG — the Catalyst Grant** co-funds *developing and commercialising* a digital solution. MDEC frames it as catalysing disruptive, sustainable innovation within the Malaysia Digital sectors — building the technology, creating intellectual property, and bringing it to market, often through co-creation with an end-user partner.

**MDXG — the X-Port Grant** co-funds *going global*. It supports Malaysian technology companies that are already export-ready and want to expand internationally — market access, branding Malaysian tech products abroad, and commercialising them for the global market.

So the mental model is simple: **MDCG helps you build and sell it; MDXG helps you sell it overseas.** A company can be a candidate for one and not the other depending on where it is.

## The gateway: Malaysia Digital status

This is the part most write-ups bury, and it is the part that decides eligibility.

Both grants are **gated behind Malaysia Digital (MD) status**. MDEC's guidelines require the applicant to hold **MD status** (or legacy **MSC Malaysia status**); if you do not yet hold it, you apply for MD status as part of the grant process. The grants are, in effect, a benefit layered on top of the status.

That has two consequences worth planning around:

- **Sequence matters.** If you are not already an MD (or MSC Malaysia) company, factor the status application into your timeline — you are really running two approvals, not one.
- **The status rules carry over.** MD status has its own eligibility (incorporated under the Companies Act 2016, resident in Malaysia, carrying out an approved MD activity) and its own post-award conditions. Our separate guide on [Malaysia Digital status](mdec-digital-status) covers that in detail — read it first if you have not secured status yet.

Beyond status, MDEC applies the usual company-level filters — a minimum period in operation (around one year), a minimum issued/paid-up capital, and ownership thresholds that distinguish majority-Malaysian companies from foreign-owned ones, with the more generous terms reserved for majority-Malaysian companies. Treat the specific thresholds as things to confirm on the current grant page, because they are set per cycle.

## The mechanics: matched, and paid on reimbursement

Two features of *how* these grants pay out matter more to your cash flow than the headline amount.

**They are matched grants.** MDEC co-funds a *share* of approved project cost, not the whole thing. The published pattern is up to **50% of project cost for majority-Malaysian-owned companies** and a lower share (up to **30%**) for foreign-owned companies, each subject to a ceiling. You are expected to fund the rest yourself.

**They are reimbursement-based.** This is the one that catches people out. You do **not** receive the money up front. You incur the project cost, hit the agreed deliverables, and then claim the approved share back — with spending verified by an **independent external auditor**. In practice that means you need the working capital to run the project first and recover the grant afterwards. A grant is not a substitute for funding the project; it lowers the net cost after the fact.

Projects are time-boxed (broadly up to around 12 months), assessment includes a **pitching session**, and applications go through MDEC's **Malaysia Digital platform** online.

## Why you should not hard-plan around a figure

Grant programmes like these run in **cycles**: MDEC opens and closes application windows periodically and subject to available funds, and it revises the amounts, coverage percentages and eligibility details from round to round. A window that is open today can be closed next quarter, and the numbers attached to a 2024 round are not a promise about the next one.

So use this article for the *shape* of the thing — two grant types, the MD-status gateway, matched and reimbursement-based — and confirm every live figure, ceiling, deadline and eligibility line on **mdec.my** (the specific MDCG and MDXG grant pages) before you commit. If a source quotes you an exact amount or deadline without pointing to the current MDEC page, treat it as out of date.

## What's next

If MDEC grants are on your radar, work in this order. First, confirm you hold — or can get — **Malaysia Digital status**, because without it neither grant is open to you; start with our [Malaysia Digital status](mdec-digital-status) guide. Second, decide **which grant fits your stage**: MDCG if you are building and commercialising a solution, MDXG if you are export-ready and going global. Third, check the **current cycle** on the MDCG and MDXG pages at mdec.my — whether a window is open, the live coverage percentages and ceilings, and the exact eligibility thresholds. And budget for the **reimbursement reality**: you fund the project first and claim back a share later, against audited deliverables.
