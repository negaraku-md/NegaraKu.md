Search "government grant Malaysia" and you will get dozens of listicles promising free money for your business. The reality is more useful and more complicated: the money exists, but half of what gets called a "grant" is actually a loan, an equity investment, or a reimbursement of cash you have to spend first. The single most valuable thing you can learn before applying is how to tell them apart.

This directory maps the main government funding channels for Malaysian startups and SMEs, grouped by what they really are — and flags which figures to double-check, because in this field the numbers change every Budget.

## First, learn to read the label

Before the directory, the classification that saves you time:

| Type | What it means | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **True / matching grant** | Non-repayable; a matching grant pays a % if you fund the rest | MDEC MDCG, MSME Digital Grant MADANI, MRANTI SRF |
| **Conditional / clawback grant** | A grant that can be reclaimed if you fail conditions | Cradle CIP Spark, CIP Sprint |
| **Reimbursement** | You spend first, then claim part back | HRD Corp training grants, MATRADE MDG |
| **Customised incentive** | Negotiated support, not an outright grant | MIDA DISF |
| **Loan / debt** | Repayable financing | Malaysia Debt Ventures |
| **Equity** | Government takes shares | MTDC Business Growth Fund, Penjana Kapital |

If a page tells you a programme is a "grant" without telling you which of these it is, treat that page with suspicion.

## Early-stage tech: Cradle Fund

Cradle, under the Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation, runs Malaysia's best-known startup grants:

- **CIP Spark** — for early-stage tech (ideation to prototype/MVP): **up to RM150,000**, over a maximum of 18 months. Broadly for companies under five years old, with accumulated revenue below RM3 million and at least 51% Malaysian ownership. It is a **conditional grant** — repayable on termination.
- **CIP Sprint** — for commercialisation and market traction: **up to RM600,000**. Broadly for a *Sdn Bhd* under seven years old, revenue below RM5 million, paid-up capital of at least RM10,000, that owns its intellectual property. A **conditional, convertible** grant.

Cradle also anchors **MYStartup**, the national startup platform — more an umbrella of accelerator and ecosystem programmes than a single cash grant.

## Digital and technology: MDEC and MRANTI

**MDEC** (Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation) funds digital businesses:

- **Malaysia Digital Catalyst Grant (MDCG)** — co-funds innovative digital solutions: **up to 50% of project cost for locally-owned companies (30% for foreign-owned), capped at RM1 million**. Requires Malaysia Digital status.
- **Malaysia Digital Acceleration Grant (MDAG)** — a larger non-repayable grant (up to 70% / RM5 million) — but it is **closed to new applications** (the application window ended in 2023). Ignore any blog still listing it as open.

**MRANTI** (Malaysian Research Accelerator for Technology and Innovation) runs the **Strategic Research Fund (SRF)** — a **100% grant for startups and spin-offs, and a 65% matching grant for SMEs**, up to 36 months, for research at technology-readiness levels 2 to 9.

## Manufacturing and value-chain: MIDA and MTDC

**MIDA** runs the **Domestic Investment Strategic Fund (DISF)** — a large fund to move Malaysian-owned manufacturing and services companies up the value chain. Note it is **not an outright grant**: it is a customised incentive granted case by case on the merits of the company's investment. It generally requires at least 60% Malaysian equity.

**MTDC** (Malaysian Technology Development Corporation) offers two very different instruments:

- **Commercialisation of R&D Fund (CRDF)** — a partial grant for commercialising completed R&D (the larger tier carries a repayable component, so it is part grant, part loan).
- **Business Growth Fund (BGF)** — **equity, not a grant**: up to RM10 million via redeemable convertible preference shares, for tech companies with revenue above RM1.5 million.

## Digitalisation and export: MADANI grant, MATRADE, HRD Corp

- **MSME Digital Grant MADANI** — the current flagship digitalisation grant, with MDEC and Bank Simpanan Nasional: a **50% matching grant** (commonly up to RM5,000) for digital tools, e-invoicing and software, applied for through a registered technology solution provider.
- **MATRADE Market Development Grant (MDG)** — a **reimbursement** of eligible overseas market-promotion costs, up to a lifetime cap, for exporters. You spend first and claim back.
- **HRD Corp training grants** — funded by the employer's own **HRD levy** (1% of monthly wages for employers with 10 or more Malaysian employees). It reimburses approved training from your levy pool — so it is your own money, claimed back, and approval must be obtained *before* the training happens.

## Loans and equity (not grants) — know the difference

Two channels are widely mislabelled as grants:

- **Malaysia Debt Ventures (MDV)** provides **financing (loans and guarantees)** for ICT, green tech and biotech — repayable, not a grant.
- **Penjana Kapital** and **Khazanah's** funds are **equity** (fund-of-funds and direct investment). These belong with venture capital and private equity — see that companion guide.

## How the application usually works

Grant programmes share a rough shape, and knowing it saves wasted effort. Most agencies run their own online portal — Cradle's grant management system, MDEC's application system, and the MTDC and MRANTI portals — and the government's MyStartup national-fundings directory aggregates many of them in one place. Expect to submit a business or project proposal, proof of eligibility (SSM registration, an ownership breakdown, and financials), and, for technology grants, evidence of your solution or prototype.

Three practical realities catch first-time applicants. Grants are **competitive and capped** by the year's allocation, so a valid application is not an approved one. **Matching and reimbursement grants require you to spend first** and claim back, so you need the cash flow up front. And reimbursement or milestone grants usually require **approval before you incur the cost**, not after — spend early and you may forfeit the claim. Build the application into your plan as a process with lead time, not a cheque that arrives on demand.

## What's next

Before you apply anywhere, do two things. First, confirm the programme is **actually open and its amount is current** on the administering agency's own website — third-party lists routinely keep closed grants listed, and the numbers move every Budget. Second, be clear which *type* of funding it is: a clawback grant, a reimbursement, a loan and equity all carry obligations that free cash does not.

Match the instrument to your stage: a pre-revenue tech idea points to Cradle's CIP Spark or an MDEC grant; a digitalising SME to the MADANI grant; an exporter to MATRADE; a company ready to give up equity to MTDC's BGF or the venture-capital route. For repayable financing rather than grants, see the guide on SME financing; for equity, the guide on venture capital and private equity.
