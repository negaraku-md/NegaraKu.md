Opening a restaurant is one of the most popular ways into business in Malaysia — and one of the most licence-heavy. Between the local council, the Ministry of Health, the tax office and the labour authorities, a food business touches more regulators than almost any other small enterprise. The good news is that the sequence is well-worn; do it in the right order and it is manageable.

This playbook walks the steps from registration to opening day, and points you to the detailed guides for each licence along the way.

## Step 1: Register the business with SSM

Every food business starts at the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM). You have two routes:

- an **enterprise** — sole proprietorship or partnership — registered under the Registration of Businesses Act 1956 through SSM's **ezBIZ** portal. Cheap and simple, but you are *personally liable* for the business's debts, and it is open only to Malaysian citizens and permanent residents; or
- a **private limited company (Sdn Bhd)** — incorporated under the Companies Act 2016 through **MyCoID**. A separate legal entity with limited liability, the required route for foreign owners and for raising capital, but with a company secretary and annual filings to maintain.

Most single-outlet operators start as an enterprise and convert to a Sdn Bhd as they grow. For the full comparison, see the guide on sole proprietorship versus Sdn Bhd.

## Step 2: Get the council premise and signboard licences

This is the step new owners underestimate. The two core operating licences come from your **local council (Pihak Berkuasa Tempatan, PBT)** — DBKL in Kuala Lumpur, MBPJ in Petaling Jaya, and so on — under the Local Government Act 1976, *not* from a federal agency:

- the **business premise licence** (*lesen premis perniagaan* / food-shop licence); and
- the **signboard licence** (*lesen papan tanda*) for your shopfront sign.

Councils typically want your SSM documents, a tenancy agreement with enough term remaining, a floor plan showing the kitchen, seating and escape routes, and photos of the premises — which must sit in a commercial or mixed-use zone. See the dedicated premise-licence and signboard-licence guides for the document checklists, and the F&B-licence guide for the full food-business picture.

## Step 3: Food-handler training and typhoid vaccination

Before anyone touches food, the Ministry of Health's rules under the **Food Act 1983** and the **Food Hygiene Regulations 2009** apply:

- every **food handler** must complete a **food-handler training course** at a school recognised by the Ministry of Health; and
- every food handler must be **immunised against typhoid** by a registered medical practitioner.

These are not optional — they are conditions of operating a food premises, and inspectors check for them. Build them into your hiring so staff are certified before opening day.

## Step 4: Halal certification (optional but valuable)

If you serve Muslim customers, **halal certification** from **JAKIM** is a major commercial asset, though it is voluntary rather than a licence to operate. Applications now run through JAKIM's online **MYeHALAL** system, and involve document submission, a premises audit, and committee approval. See the halal-certification guide for the process and the common pitfalls.

## Step 5: Register for service tax (once you cross the threshold)

Restaurant food is a **taxable service**. The key numbers:

- the **service tax rate on F&B is 6%** — importantly, this is an *exception* to the general rate that rose to 8% for most services in 2024; food and drink stayed at 6%; and
- you must **register for service tax once your F&B turnover exceeds RM1,500,000** over a 12-month period. Below that, you neither register nor charge it.

Register through the RMCD **MySST** portal, and see the SST guides for how filing and the SST-02 return work.

## Step 6: Register your staff — and mind the minimum wage

Hiring brings a cluster of employer duties:

- **EPF (KWSP)** — register as an employer and contribute to the Employees Provident Fund;
- **SOCSO and EIS (PERKESO)** — register for social security and employment insurance;
- **Employment Act 1955** — the baseline terms on hours, rest days, leave and overtime; and
- the **minimum wage of RM1,700 a month** (Minimum Wages Order 2024), which from August 2025 applies to essentially all employers regardless of size.

See the EPF employer guide and the Employment Act guide for the mechanics.

## Conditional extras, depending on your concept

Not every restaurant needs these, but plan for them if they apply:

- **Liquor licence** — to serve alcohol, under the **Excise Act 1976**, involving Customs, the police and the council;
- **BOMBA fire-safety approval** for the premises;
- a **music/public-performance licence** if you play recorded music; and
- a **WRT licence** from the Ministry of Domestic Trade if the business is foreign-owned.

## What's next

Sequence the work: register with SSM first, because every other application asks for those documents; then lodge the council premise and signboard licences early, as they take the longest; get staff food-handler-certified and vaccinated before you open; and only worry about service-tax registration once you are approaching the RM1,500,000 threshold. Confirm the current fees with your specific council and the live service-tax guide with RMCD, since council fees vary and tax guidance is periodically reissued. The linked guides for each licence carry the detailed checklists.
