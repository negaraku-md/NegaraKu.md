Sooner or later a company outgrows a provider — the fees creep up, the service goes quiet, or you simply find a better fit. The instinct is to send one email and move on. But Malaysia does not treat these four professionals the same way: dropping your bookkeeper is a phone call, while removing your auditor is a formal, legally protected process. Here is how each one actually works, and how to switch without leaving a compliance gap.

## First, the one rule that applies to all four

Before you change anyone, do three things every time:

- **Verify the new provider's licence** before you commit — a company secretary's practising certificate, an auditor's approval, a tax agent's listing with LHDN, an accountant's MIA membership. If you have not already, see our guide to [choosing a firm](/en/business/choosing-accounting-secretarial-audit-tax-firm-malaysia/) and verifying it yourself.
- **Document the change by resolution.** A board (and, for the auditor, a members') resolution naming the outgoing and incoming provider and the effective date is what makes the switch clean and auditable.
- **Mind the timing.** Avoid switching in the middle of a filing — the annual return, the AGM, the audit, or a tax deadline. Line up the replacement *before* the current provider leaves so no office falls vacant.

## Switching your company secretary

A company secretary resigns by giving notice to the board under **section 237 of the Companies Act 2016**; unless the constitution or the terms of appointment say otherwise, the resignation takes effect **30 days** after the notice is lodged. A company may instead remove and replace the secretary by board resolution.

Either way, the mechanics are:

1. The board resolves to **appoint the new secretary** and to accept the resignation (or removal) of the old one.
2. **Settle outstanding fees** so the handover is not held up.
3. The change is **lodged with SSM**, and the **statutory registers and records are handed over** to the new secretary.

Do not let the office sit empty — a company must have a secretary, and the directors carry the liability if it lapses. For what the role involves and how it is licensed, see [company secretary in Malaysia](/en/business/company-secretary-malaysia/).

## Switching your auditor

This is the most regulated of the four, by design — auditor independence is protected so a company cannot quietly dismiss an auditor who asked an awkward question.

- **Removal** is by a **resolution of the members at a general meeting**, preceded by the statutory **special notice** — *not* a written resolution and not a board decision alone (the board can only propose it).
- Under **section 277**, once the company receives the special notice it must **immediately send a copy to the auditor and to the Registrar**, and the auditor has the right to make **written representations** to the members (and, in limited cases, to have them circulated or read at the meeting).
- After the resolution passes, the company **notifies the Registrar within 14 days** under **section 278**.
- An auditor may also **resign** by written notice, taking effect as stated in that notice.

If, after the change, your company now qualifies for **[audit exemption](/en/accounting/audit-exemption-criteria/)** (dormant, zero-revenue or within the thresholds), you may not need a replacement auditor at all. Otherwise, appoint the new auditor so there is no gap for the financial year under audit.

## Switching your tax agent

A tax agent must be **approved by the Minister of Finance under section 153 of the Income Tax Act 1967** and appear on LHDN's list. To change:

1. **Confirm the new agent is on LHDN's approved list** before you switch.
2. Record the change by **resolution**, with a termination/resignation note from the outgoing agent and an **acceptance** from the incoming one.
3. **Update your authorisation with LHDN** so the new agent can act and e-file on your behalf.

The exact authorisation form is updated from time to time, so confirm the current one on LHDN's site before filing.

## Switching your accountant or bookkeeper

This is the simplest — there is **no statutory removal procedure**, because bookkeeping is a private service contract. Give the notice your engagement requires, settle what is due, and focus on the **handover**:

- A complete set of **accounting records** — the company must keep them for seven years under **section 245 of the Companies Act 2016**, so make sure ledgers, trial balances and source documents transfer in full.
- **Cloud-software access** — ownership of the accounting-software subscription and its data, exported or transferred to the new provider.
- Any **payroll, SST and e-Invoice** settings and histories.

## How long will it take?

Timelines differ, so plan around the slowest one:

| Provider | Typical timeline |
|---|---|
| **Company secretary** | Resignation takes effect 30 days after notice (section 237); have the replacement start immediately so the office is never vacant. |
| **Auditor** | The longest — a general meeting with special notice, the auditor's representation window, then notice to the Registrar within 14 days (section 278). Allow several weeks. |
| **Tax agent** | Usually quick once the new agent is confirmed on LHDN's list and your authorisation is updated. |
| **Accountant / bookkeeper** | As fast as your contract notice period and a clean records handover allow. |

If you are changing more than one provider at once, **start the auditor process first** — it sets the pace, and the others can run alongside it.

## The handover checklist

Whichever provider you change, confirm you have received:

- Statutory registers and the constitution (secretary); signed audited accounts and working-paper references (auditor); tax computations, filed returns and correspondence with LHDN (tax agent); full ledgers and source documents (accountant).
- Logins and data exports for any software, and the removal of the old provider's access afterwards.
- A written confirmation of the effective date of the change.

## Common mistakes

Letting the secretary's office fall vacant while you shop around. Trying to remove an auditor by written resolution or a board vote alone. Appointing a tax agent who is not actually on LHDN's approved list. Switching mid-audit or days before a deadline. And walking away over a fee dispute without securing the records that belong to your company.

## What's next

Decide which of the four you are actually changing, line up the licensed replacement first, and pass each change by the right resolution before the current provider leaves.
