Most first-time founders reach the same wall: the idea is good, but there is no revenue yet, no track record for a bank, and giving away equity this early feels premature. Malaysia's answer to that gap is Cradle Fund — the government agency whose entire job is to put money into technology startups before anyone else will. Its Cradle Investment Programme (CIP) grants are, for many Malaysian founders, the first external funding they ever raise.

Here is who Cradle is, what its CIP grants fund, and how to think about applying — with one caveat up front: Cradle periodically rebrands and retires its schemes, so treat the programme names and amounts below as the current shape rather than a fixed promise, and confirm the live terms on **cradle.com.my** before you apply.

## Who is Cradle Fund?

**Cradle Fund Sdn Bhd** was established in **2003** and sits under the **Ministry of Science, Technology and Innovation (MOSTI)**. It describes itself as the *focal-point agency for Malaysia's early-stage startup ecosystem* — in plain terms, the government's front door for funding young technology companies.

Cradle's core vehicle is the **Cradle Investment Programme (CIP)**, and the agency says it has supported **over 1,000** Malaysian technology-based companies since inception. It is one of several government-linked bodies in the ecosystem (alongside MRANTI, MDEC and the venture-capital arms); for the wider map of who does what, see the guide to [Malaysia's startup ecosystem](/technology/malaysia-startup-ecosystem). This article focuses specifically on the CIP grants.

## What do the CIP grants fund?

The CIP is built around **stages of a startup's life**, not a single pot of money. Historically it has run as two grants that fund two different moments:

- **CIP Spark — the pre-commercialisation stage.** This is the earliest money: it funds **validating an idea and developing the product**, with the expectation that you deliver at least a **functional prototype or minimum viable product (MVP)** by the end of the funding period. Spending is weighted toward development work rather than marketing.
- **CIP Sprint — the commercialisation stage.** This funds the next step: taking a more developed technology product **to market**. The spending here is weighted toward commercialisation rather than early R&D.

In other words, Spark helps you *build something that works*, and Sprint helps you *sell it*. If you are not yet sure which stage you are at, that judgement — idea-validation versus go-to-market — is the single most useful thing to settle before you look at the eligibility rules.

As currently published on cradle.com.my, CIP Spark offers **up to RM150,000** over a maximum of **18 months**, and CIP Sprint offers **up to RM600,000**. These figures are indicative and change between cohorts — confirm the current ceiling and duration on the official page.

## The important catch: these are *conditional* grants

The CIP grants are not free cash, and misunderstanding this is the most common mistake founders make.

The money is **conditional and milestone-based**. Funds are released against agreed deliverables — typically a mix of **advance payments and reimbursement** — and if the recipient breaches the grant conditions or the project is terminated, the grant can be **clawed back**. CIP Sprint goes a step further: it is structured as a **convertible** commercialisation grant, meaning the funding can convert to another form (such as equity) under defined conditions rather than simply being written off.

The practical takeaway: read the grant agreement before you accept it. Understand the milestones you are committing to, the reporting you owe Cradle, and the circumstances that trigger clawback or conversion. A grant you cannot deliver against is a liability, not a windfall. (For how CIP compares with other Malaysian schemes — true grants, matching grants, loans and equity — see the [government grants directory](/business/government-grants-directory-malaysia).)

## Who typically qualifies?

Eligibility is where the schemes differ most, and where the details change most often, so use this as a shape to check against the live page rather than a definitive checklist. Broadly, a CIP applicant is a **Malaysian technology startup** that is:

| Criterion | CIP Spark (typical) | CIP Sprint (typical) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applicant type | Team, enterprise, LLP or Sdn Bhd | Sdn Bhd (incorporated company) |
| Age | Under 5 years since incorporation | Under 7 years since incorporation |
| Accumulated revenue | Under RM3 million | Under RM5 million |
| Malaysian ownership | At least 51% (or majority Malaysian employees) | At least 51% (or majority Malaysian employees) |
| Intellectual property | Must own or validly licence the IP | Must own or validly licence the IP |

Two conditions run across both: **no single other company may hold 25% or more** of the applicant, and the team must have a Malaysian and Malaysia-resident presence (for example, at least one Malaysian founder or director, and at least one person residing in Malaysia). CIP Sprint, being for a more established company, also expects a minimum paid-up capital and a minimum number of directors. Confirm every threshold on cradle.com.my, because Cradle adjusts them between cohorts.

## How do you apply?

Applications go through **Cradle's Grant Management System (GMS)**, its online portal — you register, submit your proposal, and pitch. Cradle indicates the journey from pitching to an approval decision takes roughly **two to four months**, so build that runway into your plans rather than treating grant money as available next week.

Before you start, have the essentials ready: a clear description of the technology and the problem it solves, evidence you hold the IP, your incorporation and ownership details, a costed project plan mapped to milestones, and a realistic sense of which stage — Spark or Sprint — you actually fit. A strong application makes the stage obvious and ties every ringgit requested to a deliverable Cradle can verify.

## What's next

If you are an early-stage Malaysian tech founder, Cradle's CIP is one of the most accessible first sources of non-bank funding — but go in understanding that it is conditional, milestone-driven and stage-specific, not a no-strings handout. Decide honestly whether you are validating (Spark) or commercialising (Sprint), get your IP and incorporation in order, and budget for a two-to-four-month decision.

Above all, **confirm the current programme names, amounts and eligibility on cradle.com.my before you apply** — Cradle rebrands and retires schemes over time, and the figures here are a snapshot, not a guarantee.
