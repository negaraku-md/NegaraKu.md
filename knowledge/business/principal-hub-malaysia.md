Multinationals do not put their regional headquarters just anywhere — they follow the incentives. For years, Malaysia's pitch to companies deciding where to base their regional management, finance and shared-services operations was the **Principal Hub** incentive. That scheme has closed, and a lot of advice online has not caught up. If you are scoping Malaysia as an operations base today, the route that actually exists is the **Global Services Hub**.

Here is the current state of play: what the Principal Hub was, why it is gone, and how its successor works.

## The Principal Hub — and why it closed

A **Principal Hub** was defined by MIDA as a locally incorporated company that uses Malaysia as a base to conduct its regional or global business and operations, and to **manage, control and support** its key functions — risk management, decision-making, strategy, finance, and human resources. MIDA described it as a "control tower" run from Malaysia.

In return, a qualifying company received a **tiered concessionary tax rate**, with the level depending on how much it committed in operating expenditure, high-value jobs and senior roles. The last version, Principal Hub 3.0 (under the Income Tax (Principal Hub Incentive Scheme) Rules 2022), offered a **two-tier 0% or 5% rate** to new applicants.

But the window for that scheme **ended on 31 December 2022**. An extension to 2025 was proposed in the October 2022 Budget, then **dropped from the re-tabled Budget 2023 and never gazetted**. So while MIDA's Principal Hub webpage still exists, the incentive is **closed to new applications**. Treat any page inviting you to apply for a Principal Hub, or quoting a "to 2025" window, as out of date.

## The live route: the Global Services Hub

Malaysia replaced the Principal Hub with the **Global Services Hub (GSH)**, introduced in Budget 2024 and administered by **MIDA**. Applications are accepted from **14 October 2023 to 31 December 2027**.

The GSH keeps the same idea — reward a company for basing substantive regional or global services operations in Malaysia — with an evolved, outcome-based structure:

| | New company | Existing company (expansion) |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate rate** | Tier 1 **0%** or Tier 2 **5%** on qualifying income, for a period of years | Concessionary rate on **value-added income**, for a period of years |
| **Key-staff individual tax** | Preferential **15%** rate for a limited number of non-citizen C-suite staff earning at least **RM35,000/month** | — |

One important caveat before you count on the rate: the GSH rates currently rest on the **MIDA guideline** while the implementing gazette order is being finalised. And for large multinational groups (consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million), Malaysia's **Domestic Top-Up Tax** — in force from 1 January 2025 under the global minimum-tax rules — can claw a sub-15% rate back up toward 15%. So model the *effective* benefit, not just the headline rate.

For a side-by-side comparison of the GSH rate against Malaysia's other zone and hub incentives, see the companion guide on special zones and hub incentives; this article focuses on how you actually set one up.

## How you qualify: it is about substance

The GSH is deliberately **outcome-based** — you do not simply tick a capital threshold, you commit to real economic activity in Malaysia and are measured against it. The setup path is:

1. **Incorporate locally.** The hub must be a Malaysian-incorporated company.
2. **Define your qualifying services.** Identify the regional or global services the hub will run from Malaysia.
3. **Commit to the substance conditions.** These include a minimum **annual operating expenditure**, a number of **high-value full-time employees**, senior **C-suite hires** at the RM35,000-a-month level, use of **local ancillary services**, collaboration with **local educational institutions**, **training of Malaysians**, and **ESG** elements.
4. **Apply to MIDA** for approval of the incentive.
5. **Report and comply annually.** Performance against the committed conditions is reviewed, and the incentive can be **clawed back** if the targets are missed — this is an ongoing obligation, not a one-time approval.

The through-line is *substance*: the incentive rewards a genuine operating hub with real jobs and spending in Malaysia, not a nameplate.

## What's next

If you are choosing where to base regional operations, start from the correct premise: **the Principal Hub is closed; the Global Services Hub is the live incentive**, open to the end of 2027 and run by MIDA. Scope your qualifying services and, crucially, whether you can meet the operating-expenditure and high-value-employment commitments — the benefit is real but conditional, and reviewed every year.

Two practical checks before you rely on the numbers: confirm the current GSH rates and per-tier thresholds directly with MIDA (the gazette order was still being finalised), and, if your group is large enough to fall under the global minimum tax, work out the effective rate after the Domestic Top-Up Tax. If your intended presence is *non-trading* — market study or coordination rather than a full operating hub — the lighter Representative/Regional Office route may fit better; see that related guide.
