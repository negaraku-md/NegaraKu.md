Rise out of the Tun Razak Exchange MRT station and the skyline tells you this was planned, not grown. A cluster of towers on 70 acres at the eastern edge of Kuala Lumpur's centre, one of them punching past 450 metres, laid out around a park that sits on the roof of a mall. This is Malaysia's attempt to build a financial district from scratch.

It is also a project with an unusually complicated backstory — and one worth telling plainly.

## What exactly is TRX?

Tun Razak Exchange is a **purpose-built international financial district** of roughly 70 acres in central Kuala Lumpur. Its master developer is **TRX City Sdn Bhd**, which the company and the Ministry of Finance describe as **wholly owned by the Minister of Finance Incorporated (MOF Inc)**.

The idea is simple and deliberate: gather banks, capital-markets firms, insurers, Islamic-finance players and the professional services around them into one dense, transit-connected quarter, the way London has the City and Canary Wharf, or Singapore its Marina Bay. TRX was earlier branded the **Kuala Lumpur International Financial District (KLIFD)** before taking its current name, and in **February 2024** it was formally relaunched as **Malaysia's International Financial Centre**.

Officials have generally positioned it as complementary to — rather than a head-on rival of — Singapore and Hong Kong, and the district is built to house both conventional and Islamic finance.

## The landmark: Exchange 106

If TRX has a face, it is **Exchange 106**.

The tower stands at about **453 metres across 106 floors**, placing it among the tallest buildings in Malaysia, with a gross floor area reported at around four million square feet. It was developed in partnership with Indonesia's **Mulia Group** and was originally known as the Signature Tower. Together with the surrounding office blocks, it supplies the grade-A floorspace the district needs to attract financial tenants.

## The incentives: why a firm would move in

A new district does not fill itself. To pull financial-services firms into TRX, the government gazetted a package of tax incentives tied to a special designation known as **TRX Marquee (TRXM) status**.

According to an EY Malaysia tax alert, the framework has included the following for TRX Marquee companies:

| Incentive | What it gives | Gazette basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industrial building allowance | 10% annual allowance on qualifying building expenditure | P.U.(A) 27/2013 (amended 2021) |
| Accelerated capital allowance | 20% initial and 40% annual allowance on specified renovation costs | P.U.(A) 29/2013 (amended 2021) |
| Relocation-cost deduction | Deduction for specified costs of moving the business to TRX | P.U.(A) 30/2013 (amended 2021) |
| Additional rent deduction | An extra deduction equal to half the rent paid for a TRX commercial building | P.U.(A) 31/2013 (amended 2021) |

Separately, **approved property developers** in TRX were offered a **70% income tax exemption** on statutory income from disposing of or renting TRX buildings (P.U.(A) 28/2013, as amended). When the district was relaunched in 2024, reporting indicated the package also featured a stamp-duty exemption on related loan and service agreements.

One important caveat: most of these incentives carried **sunset dates**. The EY alert records that the 2021 amendments limited the TRX Marquee reliefs to qualifying expenditure incurred up to **31 December 2025**, with the developer exemption running to year of assessment 2025 (disposals) and 2027 (rentals). Whether these windows have since been extended is exactly the kind of detail a firm should confirm with **LHDN (Inland Revenue)** or the current gazette rather than assume — it is flagged for verification below.

## More than offices

TRX was designed as a **mixed-use district**, not an office-only enclave — a deliberate choice to keep it alive outside banking hours.

- **The Exchange TRX** — a large retail mall that opened to the public in **November 2023**, built as a joint venture in which Lendlease holds the majority stake and TRX City the remainder.
- **The park** — a roughly **10-acre green space** laid out *above* the mall, an elevated city park rather than a conventional ground-level one.
- **The Lifestyle Quarter** — the broader residential-and-leisure component anchoring the district, which gave rise to the retail-and-park scheme now branded The Exchange TRX.
- **Residences** — **TRX Residences** towers and separate serviced-apartment developments add a resident population to the district.
- **A hotel** — a **Kimpton** hotel, reported as the brand's first in Malaysia with several hundred rooms.

## Getting there

Connectivity was built in from the start. The **Tun Razak Exchange MRT station**, open since **2017**, sits at the district and is served by two MRT lines, tying TRX directly into the Klang Valley rail network. That transit anchor is part of what distinguishes a planned financial district from a scattering of towers.

## The origin story: 1MDB and the move to MOF Inc

Here the account has to be careful and factual.

TRX was **originally developed under 1Malaysia Development Berhad (1MDB)**, the state investment fund at the centre of a major financial scandal. The developer entity now called TRX City Sdn Bhd traces back to **1MDB Real Estate**. As public scrutiny of 1MDB intensified, the TRX developer was **moved out of 1MDB and placed under the Ministry of Finance** so the project could proceed independently of the fund.

Reporting on the precise mechanics and timing differs — some accounts date the transfer to **31 March 2017**, while others describe a majority-stake acquisition in 2017 or 2018 — and official statements have discussed sums said to have been misappropriated from the developer. Because these are contested financial and legal matters, this article states only what is uncontested: the project **began under 1MDB**, the developer is **now a Ministry of Finance company (MOF Inc)**, and the government has said the development would be completed. The specific figures and dates are left to the primary MOF and court records, and are flagged for verification below.

## Common misreadings

- **Treating TRX as "owned by 1MDB today."** The developer, TRX City Sdn Bhd, is a Ministry of Finance (MOF Inc) company; the 1MDB link is to the project's origins, not its current ownership.
- **Assuming the tax incentives are open-ended.** The TRX Marquee and developer reliefs carried sunset dates around 2025; confirm the current position before relying on them.
- **Picturing TRX as just a stack of banks.** It is a mixed-use district with retail, an elevated park, homes and a hotel, not an office-only zone.
- **Reading promotional headcounts as audited facts.** Company and worker counts cited for TRX generally come from the developer's own material.

## What's next

TRX has moved from a contested construction site into a working district with a relaunch, a flagship tower, a mall and a rail link — but the parts that matter most for a business decision are the ones that change: whether the TRX Marquee and developer incentives are still live, and on what terms. If you are weighing a base there, treat the incentive timeline as something to confirm, not assume, and read the current gazette orders alongside LHDN guidance.
