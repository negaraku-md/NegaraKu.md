Plenty of small businesses know they *should* move to a proper point-of-sale system, run their accounts in software instead of a notebook, or start selling online — but the upfront cost of the tools is exactly the thing that keeps getting postponed. The MSME Digital Grant MADANI is built to remove half of that cost. It is a **matching grant**: adopt an approved digital tool, and the government pays half the bill up to a cap, while you pay the rest.

Here is how this grant *type* works, so you can judge whether it fits you and act on the current round — because the amounts and the timing change from one budget year to the next.

## What is the MSME Digital Grant MADANI?

Known in Malay as the **Geran Digital PMKS Madani** (GDPM), it is a government initiative announced under **Budget 2025** to help micro, small and medium enterprises use digital services in day-to-day operations. It is run by the **Ministry of Finance** in collaboration with **Bank Simpanan Nasional (BSN)** as the administering bank, the **Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC)**, and the **Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC)**.

The design is deliberately simple: the government provides "a matching grant of up to 50% of the invoice amount or up to RM5,000 for eligible PMKS to assist them to subscribe to digital services," in BSN's own words. It is a subsidy on a real purchase, not a cash handout — you only benefit if you actually adopt a qualifying tool.

This article is a deep dive on this one programme. For the wider landscape of Malaysian business grants and incentives, see the [government grants directory](/en/business/government-grants-directory-malaysia).

## How the matching model works

The word "matching" is the key to understanding the math. The grant covers **50% of the invoice**, and is **capped** at a maximum per business — **RM5,000 under the Budget 2025 cycle**. Put those two rules together and three cases follow:

| Invoice from appointed provider | You pay | Grant pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RM6,000 | RM3,000 | RM3,000 (50%) |
| RM10,000 | RM5,000 | RM5,000 (cap reached) |
| RM14,000 | RM9,000 | RM5,000 (cap) |

So RM10,000 is the "sweet spot" invoice at which the cap and the 50% match line up exactly. Spend less and you get half; spend more and everything above the cap is on you. The cash flow also matters: you pay your share to the provider first (typically within 14 days of approval), the provider delivers the service, and **BSN pays the grant portion to the provider afterwards** — it is a reimbursement to the provider, not money that lands in your account.

## What the grant funds

The grant is tied to specific **digital-adoption categories**, not free to spend on anything. The 2025 cycle lists nine eligible types of digital service:

- Electronic point of sale (**e-POS**)
- **HR / CRM** systems
- **Digital marketing** and sales
- **Cybersecurity**
- **ERP / accounting & tax**
- **Digital signature**
- **IoT** and smart systems
- **Artificial intelligence (AI)**
- **E-invoicing**

A business can apply for **up to three** of these service types, with one or more appointed providers. E-invoicing sits slightly apart from the rest — because Malaysia is rolling out mandatory e-invoicing in phases, it is treated more generously (see the eligibility note below).

## Who is eligible

The 2025-cycle criteria are aimed squarely at genuine, established-but-small Malaysian businesses:

- **Registered** with the Companies Commission of Malaysia (**SSM**) — or holding a business licence from a local authority (**PBT**), or registered with the Malaysian Co-operative Societies Commission (**SKM**), or with a relevant professional body (lawyers, accountants, doctors, engineers, pharmacists and the like).
- At least **60% owned by Malaysian citizens**.
- In operation for **at least six months**.
- **Annual average sales turnover of at least RM50,000**.

There is also an important "one bite" rule: a business that has **already received a digital matching grant generally cannot apply again** — with an exception carved out for **e-invoicing** solutions, so a firm that used an earlier grant for something else can still come back specifically for e-invoicing. Applications need supporting documents: the owner's or director's ID, the business registration, recent audited accounts or bank statements (the last two months), and the provider's quotation.

## How to apply

The flow is provider-led, and choosing the right partner is the first real step:

1. **Pick an appointed digital service provider** (a "Digitalisation Partner") that offers the solution you need. Only providers officially appointed and vetted for the programme qualify — a provider must itself be an approved, majority-Malaysian-owned technology company. Confirm a provider is on the **current approved list** before relying on them.
2. **Get a quotation** for the service.
3. **Apply through the appointed platform** with your documents and the quotation. Approval typically takes a few working days and you are notified by email.
4. **Pay your share** of the invoice (usually within 14 days of approval), after which the provider delivers the service and BSN settles the grant portion with the provider.

Because a private platform has been appointed to manage intake and the approved-provider list can change between rounds, always start from the **official BSN or MDEC pages** to reach the current application channel rather than a third-party link.

## Why the amount and rounds change — and what to verify

This is the part to internalise if you are reading this after 2025. The grant is **funded from a fixed government budget allocation**, and BSN states plainly that it is "subject to the allocated budget." That has two consequences:

- It runs in **periodic rounds** that open and close as budget is announced and then exhausted — it is not a permanently open facility.
- The **cap, the turnover and ownership thresholds, the eligible categories and the application channel are all set per cycle** and can be revised in each year's budget.

So treat every specific number in this guide — the RM5,000 cap, the RM50,000 turnover floor, the 60% ownership, the six-month rule, the nine categories — as **the 2025 cycle's terms**, verified against BSN's official FAQ, and confirm the *current* cycle's terms before you act. The authoritative sources are the [BSN business pages](https://www.bsn.com.my) and [MDEC's grants portal](https://www.mdec.my/grants).

## What's next

If you have been putting off a digital tool because of cost, this grant halves the barrier for exactly the kinds of adoption — e-POS, accounting, e-commerce, e-invoicing — that pay for themselves quickly. The practical sequence is: confirm a round is open, check you clear the eligibility thresholds for the current cycle, shortlist an appointed provider, and get the quotation in while budget lasts.

Do not hard-plan around any figure in this article without re-checking it — the cap and the rounds are reset each budget year on the official BSN and MDEC portals. And because this is one of many programmes, see the [government grants directory](/en/business/government-grants-directory-malaysia) to compare it against other financing and incentive options before you decide.
