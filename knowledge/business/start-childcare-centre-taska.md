Opening a childcare centre feels like a caring, low-barrier business — a house, a few cots, some warm hands. In Malaysia it is none of those things legally. The moment you take in four or more young children from different families for payment, you are running a **TASKA** (taman asuhan kanak-kanak), and a TASKA cannot open until it is registered with the government. Skip that step and you are not a small business owner; you are committing a criminal offence.

Here is what it actually takes to set up a childcare centre for the under-fours the legal way.

## Which children — and which law — are you dealing with?

First, know which regulator you fall under, because Malaysia splits early childhood in two.

- A **TASKA** cares for children **under the age of four**. It is regulated by the **Department of Social Welfare (JKM)** under the **Child Care Centre Act 1984 (Act 308)**.
- A **TADIKA** (kindergarten) educates children **aged four to six**. It is regulated by the **Ministry of Education** under the **Education Act 1996**.

They are different laws, different agencies, different licences. This guide is about the TASKA track. If you plan to take children right up to school age you may need *both* registrations. The Act defines a child care centre as a premises where **four or more children under four**, from **more than one household**, are received to be looked after **for reward** — so a centre with three children or one caring only for a single family's children falls outside it.

## The four types of TASKA

Act 308 and JKM recognise four categories, and which one you are decides your capacity, your fee and who may set you up:

| Type | Who runs it | Children |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **TASKA di Rumah** (home-based) | An operator, in their own home | **4–9** children |
| **TASKA Institusi** (institution-based) | A private operator or NGO | **10 or more** |
| **TASKA di Tempat Kerja** (workplace) | An employer, for its staff's children | **10 or more** |
| **TASKA Komuniti** (community) | A community body, with government assistance, for lower-income families | **10 or more** |

Pick the type honestly at the start — a nursery run out of your living room is a home TASKA (capped at nine), not an institution, and the requirements scale with the category.

## Register with JKM *before* you operate

This is the non-negotiable step. Under the Child Care Centre Act 1984 you must **register the TASKA with JKM before it operates**. Application is made through your **District Social Welfare Office (Pejabat Kebajikan Masyarakat Daerah, PKMD)** where the centre will be located; a complete application is processed within about **14 working days**.

The **registration fee** is **RM50** for a **home** TASKA and **RM250** for an **institution, workplace or community** TASKA, and the registration is valid for **60 months (five years)** before renewal. You register the centre with JKM in addition to registering the *business* — see SSM below.

## Caregivers must hold the PERMATA course (KAP)

You cannot staff a TASKA with willing hands alone. Every caregiver (*pengasuh*) must attend and pass the **Kursus Asuhan dan Didikan Awal Kanak-kanak PERMATA (KAP)** — the basic childcare course recognised by JKM (the earlier **KAAK** course is the predecessor equivalent). The operator or supervisor is expected to hold it, and JKM allows a newly hired caregiver to complete the KAP **within 12 months** of starting work. The course covers child development, health, hygiene, nutrition, safety and early stimulation for the 0–4 age band.

## Caregiver-to-child ratios by age

JKM fixes how many young children one adult may care for, and staffing to these ratios is a **condition of registration**, not advice:

| Child's age | Caregiver : children (typical) | With special needs |
|-------------|-------------------------------|--------------------|
| Under 1 year | **1 : 3** | 2 : 3 |
| 1 to 3 years | **1 : 5** | 2 : 5 |
| 3 to 4 years | **1 : 10** | 3 : 10 |

Younger children need far more hands — an infant room at 1:3 is expensive to staff, and your business plan has to carry that cost.

## Premises, safety and BOMBA

The physical centre is inspected before it is approved. Expect requirements around floor space per child, safe and clean facilities, hygiene and food handling, and a suitable, safe location. Two items that catch operators out:

- **Fire safety.** The premises must clear the **Fire and Rescue Department (BOMBA)** — a fire-safety inspection and approval of the building for use as a childcare centre.
- **CCTV.** JKM requires **CCTV to be installed** in the TASKA.

Depending on the premises you will also deal with the **local council (Pihak Berkuasa Tempatan, PBT)** for the business premises licence and any change-of-use or planning approval, and health-department requirements for the kitchen and sanitation. Sort the premises approvals early — a lease signed before BOMBA or the council says no is an expensive mistake.

## Register the business with SSM

Running a TASKA is a business, so register the legal entity with the **Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM)** — either a sole proprietorship/partnership under the Registration of Businesses Act 1956 or a **Sdn Bhd** company under the Companies Act 2016. An NGO-run or community TASKA may instead be operated by a society or association registered under its own governing law. JKM will expect to see your business registration as part of the TASKA application, so do this in parallel with the JKM process.

## The penalty for running an unregistered TASKA

Do not open first and register later. Operating a child care centre that is **not registered** with JKM is a **criminal offence** under the Child Care Centre Act 1984. On conviction the penalty is a **fine of up to RM10,000 or imprisonment of up to two years, or both** — with heavier penalties for a repeat offence — and JKM can order the centre closed. The registration step is the whole point of the Act; there is no informal way around it.

## What's next

Setting up a TASKA is a licensing project, not a soft launch. Sequence it: decide the **type** (home vs institution/workplace/community) and its capacity; register the **business with SSM**; secure a **premises** that can pass BOMBA and the local council; make sure you and your caregivers have — or are enrolled in — the **KAP** course; then lodge the **JKM registration** through your district welfare office and staff to the age ratios before your first child arrives. Because fees, forms and premises standards are administered locally, confirm the current requirements with your **District Social Welfare Office (PKMD)** and JKM before you commit to a lease — and register before you operate, not after.
