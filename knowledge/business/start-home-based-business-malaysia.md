You bake cookies, resell skincare, or dropship phone cases from your spare room, and the sales are real. Somewhere between the first order and the tenth, a question surfaces: is any of this *legal*, or is a home business the one kind you can just… run? The honest answer is that a home business is still a business — the state expects the same registration as a shop, plus one extra permission most people never think about: leave from your local council to run a business out of a house at all.

Here is the practical set-up, in the order you actually do it.

## Do I have to register at all? (Yes — this is the big myth)

The most expensive misconception is that selling online, or working from home, exempts you from registration. It does not. Under the **Registration of Businesses Act 1956**, "business" covers *every form of trade, commerce, craftsmanship, calling* carried on for profit — a definition wide enough to catch an Instagram store or a weekend baker.[^ssm] Registering it with the **Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM)** is not optional, and running an unregistered business is a real offence: a fine **not exceeding RM50,000, imprisonment up to two years, or both**.[^ssm]

Registration is also the gate to everything else. The national home-business guideline makes registration with SSM (or another relevant government agency) a **precondition** for the council licence you will need below.[^kuskop] So this is step one, not an afterthought.

## Sole proprietorship or Sdn Bhd — which should I register?

For most micro-entrepreneurs and online sellers, the entry point is a **sole proprietorship or partnership (an "enterprise")**, registered on SSM's **ezBiz** portal (ezbiz.ssm.com.my). It is cheap, fast, and self-service. The official SSM fees are:[^ssm]

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Register under your **personal name** (exactly as on your IC) | RM30 |
| Register under a **trade/business name** | RM60 |
| Each **branch** | RM5 |
| Business information printout | RM10 |

A new registration is **valid for one year up to a maximum of five years** per renewal, and ezBiz applications are typically approved within one working day. You must be a **Malaysian citizen or permanent resident, aged 18 or above**.[^ssm]

The trade-off: a sole proprietorship is *you* — unlimited personal liability, and profits taxed as your personal income. If you want limited liability, outside investors, or a more credible brand, you incorporate a **private limited company (Sdn Bhd)** instead, through SSM's **MyCoID** system, for an incorporation fee of **RM1,000**. That brings a company secretary, annual filing and audit obligations — heavier than most home businesses need on day one, but the right call once the business outgrows a side hustle. (For the fuller comparison, that is its own decision.)

## Can I legally run it from my house?

This is the step people miss. Registering with SSM tells the *federal* system you exist; it does **not** give you permission to operate a business from a *residential* premise. That permission comes from your **local council — the Pihak Berkuasa Tempatan (PBT)** — such as DBKL in Kuala Lumpur or your local majlis perbandaran/daerah.

To bring order to a patchwork of council rules, the Ministry (KUSKOP) issued a national **Garis Panduan Kawal Selia Perniagaan Dari Rumah** — a home-business regulation guideline for all PBTs to adopt.[^kuskop] Under it, a home business can be licensed provided it stays genuinely small and unobtrusive. The core conditions:

- **It must stay small-scale** — causing no environmental-health problems, traffic congestion, security threat or public nuisance to neighbours.[^kuskop]
- **The premises must still look like a home**, not a business, and remain lived in by the trader.[^kuskop]
- **At most 25% of the built-up floor area** of the main building may be used for the business, and only *inside* the residential building, keeping the existing residential design.[^kuskop]
- **You market mainly online.** The licence is framed around online selling; walk-in retail is treated very differently.[^kuskop]
- **Consent from those around you** — a supporting letter from immediate neighbours where relevant, written consent from the strata management body (JMB/MC) for apartments and condos, and the landlord's written consent if you rent.[^kuskop]
- **At most one (1) hired worker**, and only for a field needing particular experience or skill, at the council's discretion.[^kuskop]

Any **signboard** must be non-illuminated, no larger than **one square metre**, in Bahasa Melayu, show only the business name plus the SSM/PBT registration number (no other visuals), and be approved by the council.[^kuskop] Fees for the home-business licence itself are **set by each PBT** under the Local Government Act 1976, so check your own council's rate rather than assuming a national figure.[^kuskop]

## What can't I run from home?

The guideline lists activities that are **not allowed** from a residence at all — the ones that would turn a quiet street into a nuisance, or that carry public-safety risk:[^kuskop]

- **Repairing vehicles**, or making the home a **showroom**;
- **Unlimited walk-in retail** — opening a sales counter and receiving customers without limit;
- **Heavy mechanical equipment**, or anything producing **dust, noise or smell**;
- Selling or storing **firearms, imitation weapons, liquor, cigarettes** and the like;
- **Vape / e-cigarette liquid** and nicotine products;
- **Flammable goods, chemicals and gas**;
- **Wet goods** that pollute the surroundings, and **hoarding stock** so the home becomes a store or warehouse;
- **Controlled medicines and unnotified cosmetics** (regulated under the Poisons Act 1952, Dangerous Drugs Act 1952 and Sale of Drugs Act 1952), and spectacles/contact lenses;
- Uncertified telecommunications devices (not approved by MCMC), and anything else prohibited by written law.

By contrast, the guideline's **permitted list** is broad and welcoming to typical home operators: management/marketing offices, tailoring, tuition and consultancy, crafts and gift arrangement, beauty services, and the resale of clothing, electronics, cosmetics (as agent/stockist/dropship), household goods, stationery, baby products, packaged food and more.[^kuskop]

## Where does food need extra approvals?

Food is the one category the guideline singles out for **heavier requirements**, because it is a health matter, not just a licensing one. On top of SSM and council registration, the food-category checklist requires:[^kuskop]

- the premises to have a **grease trap** installed;
- the operator to be **listed under the home-based food category in FoSIM** (the Food Safety Information System of Malaysia, run by the Ministry of Health);
- a **food-handler training certificate** from a KKM-recognised training school; and
- proof of an **anti-typhoid vaccination**.

Some foods can't be made at home even so — infant formula and follow-up formula, milk-based products on the restricted list, and items that need their own licence such as packaged drinking water, natural mineral water and ice.[^kuskop]

## Do I need to register for tax too?

Yes, and separately from SSM. Business profit is **taxable income**, so you register with the **Inland Revenue Board (LHDN)**. Since 1 January 2024, individuals apply for a **Tax Identification Number (TIN)** online through **e-Daftar** on the MyTax portal (mytax.hasil.gov.my), choosing "Individual" and uploading the required identity documents.[^lhdn] As a sole proprietor you then declare your business income on the individual return each year (**Form B**), rather than the salaried **Form BE**. If you employ staff or cross the threshold, separate **SST** and employer registrations can also come into play — but for a one-person home business, TIN registration plus honest annual filing is the baseline.

## What's next

Sequence it in the order the system expects: **register the business with SSM** (ezBiz for a sole proprietorship/enterprise, or MyCoID for a Sdn Bhd), **apply to your PBT** for a home-business licence and read *their* specific conditions and fee, **register for tax** with LHDN via e-Daftar, and **add any sector licence** your activity needs — most commonly the food health requirements above.

Two cautions before you start trading. First, the KUSKOP guideline is a national *framework*; each council adopts and adapts it, so your DBKL, MBPJ or majlis daerah rules are the ones that actually bind you — confirm them with your own PBT. Second, "small-scale and residential-looking" is the spirit of the whole regime: the moment your home business needs a signboard bigger than a doormat, a second employee, or a steady stream of customers at the door, it has outgrown the home-business licence and belongs in commercial premises. Getting the paperwork right while you are still small is far cheaper than being compounded later.
