Ask "how do I get a warehouse licence in Malaysia?" and the honest first answer is a question back: what are you storing, and for whom? Because there is no single "warehouse licence." A plain stockroom for your own goods is licensed by the local council like any other business premises. A bonded warehouse that holds imported goods with the duty switched off is licensed by Customs under a specific section of the Customs Act. And a factory that both makes and stores goods for export is a third animal entirely. Pick the wrong one and you either over-comply — paying for a Customs bond you never needed — or under-comply and get caught holding dutiable goods without a licence.

Here is how the three paths differ, and what each one actually requires.

## Path one: a general (non-bonded) commercial warehouse

If your warehouse holds ordinary, duty-paid or locally sourced goods — retail stock, distribution inventory, a third-party logistics operation for domestic cargo — **Customs does not license it at all**. What you need is the standard local-premises stack:

- a **business premise licence** (and usually a signboard licence) from the **local authority** (*Pihak Berkuasa Tempatan*, PBT) for the area — the council that issues every shop and factory its operating licence;
- the building's **Certificate of Completion and Compliance (CCC)**, which is what makes the premises legally occupiable in the first place; and
- a **fire certificate** (*Perakuan Bomba*) from the **Fire and Rescue Department (BOMBA)** — a warehouse is a *designated premises*, so the fire certificate is not optional, and the council will lean on BOMBA's clearance before issuing or renewing your premise licence.

This is the same premise-licence process any business goes through — see the related guide on premise licences for the council side. The point to take away is that a general warehouse is a **local-council matter, not a Customs matter**. No section-65 licence, no bond, no Customs inventory system.

## Path two: a bonded Licensed Warehouse under section 65

The moment you want to store **dutiable or imported goods with the duty and sales tax suspended** until the goods are sold or moved, you cross into Customs territory. This is a **Licensed Warehouse** (*Gudang Berlesen*, GB), licensed by the **Royal Malaysian Customs Department (RMCD)** under **section 65 of the Customs Act 1967**. Duty is deferred while the goods sit in the warehouse and only becomes payable when they are released for home consumption — a real cash-flow benefit for importers and distributors.

RMCD licenses two flavours:

| Type | Who it stores for | Notable condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Public Licensed Warehouse** (Gudang Berlesen Awam, GBA) | third parties' dutiable goods (a bonded-storage service) | must be a **Sdn Bhd or Berhad** company |
| **Private Licensed Warehouse** (Gudang Berlesen Persendirian, GBP) | only the **licensee's own** approved goods | registered with SSM or the local authority |

**Applying.** You submit **JKDM Form No.1** with **Lampiran A** and the document checklist to the **Customs Operations Director or State Customs Director** for the area where the premises sit. RMCD's published **licence fee is RM2,400 a year** (RM240 a year for a government agency).

**Security and controls.** Because the state is carrying suspended duty on your stock, a Licensed Warehouse comes with a **security bond** — RMCD states **10% of the estimated annual duty** for a public warehouse and **50%** for a private one — and you must run an **electronic inventory control system** so Customs can reconcile what came in against what left. RMCD also sets **paid-up capital and minimum stored-value thresholds** that vary by warehouse type and goods, so confirm the figures that apply to you before committing to premises.

This is also where this guide meets the logistics guide: a freight-forwarding or 3PL group often wants a bonded warehouse *and* a customs agent licence *and* an operator licence. Those are separate approvals — the warehouse licence covered here is only the section-65 piece.

## Path three: a Licensed Manufacturing Warehouse (LMW) under section 65A

Section 65A covers a different case: an **export-oriented manufacturer** that wants to **warehouse and manufacture on the same premises**. A **Licensed Manufacturing Warehouse (LMW)** — *Gudang Pengilangan Berlesen* — gives **customs duty exemption on the raw materials, components and machinery used directly in producing approved goods**, from the first stage of manufacturing until the finished product is packed ready for export.

Two things make the LMW distinct from an ordinary bonded warehouse:

- **It is a factory, not a storeroom.** The premises must be a properly constructed building with segregated areas for raw materials, production, packaging and finished goods.
- **You must qualify as a manufacturer first.** In practice the applicant already holds a **manufacturing licence under the Industrial Co-ordination Act 1975** — or a **MIDA exemption letter** — and the scheme is explicitly aimed at export industries (there is an export-oriented condition on finished goods; confirm the current threshold with RMCD). Applications go to the Customs office nearest the manufacturing premises, and licensees file periodic returns to RMCD.

So an LMW suits a manufacturer exporting most of its output; it does **not** suit a business that only wants to *store* goods. If storage is all you need, you are on path one or path two.

## The overlay: dangerous and hazardous goods

Whichever path you are on, the **nature of the goods** can pull in extra regulators. Storing **hazardous or dangerous substances** brings in the **Department of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH)** — for example the control-of-industrial-major-accident-hazards regime for bulk hazardous chemicals — while **scheduled wastes or controlled chemicals** engage the **Department of Environment (DOE)**. These approvals often dictate the building and location, so scope them *before* you sign a lease, not after.

## What's next

Start by settling the only question that matters: are you storing dutiable/imported goods under duty suspension, or not? If **not**, treat this as a **premise-licence-plus-BOMBA** exercise with the local council — no Customs licence — and read the premise-licence guide. If you **are** holding bonded stock, it is a **section-65 Licensed Warehouse** application to RMCD on JKDM Form No.1, with a bond and an electronic inventory system. If you are an **export manufacturer** wanting duty-free inputs on a combined make-and-store site, it is a **section-65A LMW**, which sits on top of a manufacturing licence. Because the capital thresholds, bond basis and LMW export conditions are set administratively and change, verify the current figures directly with RMCD (and, for the manufacturing side, MIDA) before you build.
