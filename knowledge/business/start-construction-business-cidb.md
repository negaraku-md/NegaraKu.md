faq:
  - q: "Do I really need CIDB registration to do construction work in Malaysia?"
    a: "Yes. Section 25(1) of the Lembaga Pembangunan Industri Pembinaan Malaysia Act 1994 (Act 520) states that no person shall carry out or complete, undertake to carry out or complete, any construction work, or hold himself out as a contractor, unless he is registered with the Lembaga (CIDB) and holds a valid certificate of registration. Registration has been mandatory for all contractors, local or foreign, since 1995. It is separate from your SSM company registration — incorporating a Sdn Bhd does not by itself let you take construction jobs."
  - q: "What are the CIDB grades G1 to G7?"
    a: "CIDB registers every contractor by grade, which sets the maximum value of work you may tender for and carry out, and the minimum paid-up capital you must hold. Per CIDB's registration schedule, G1 covers jobs not exceeding RM200,000 (RM5,000 paid-up capital), rising through G4 (up to RM3 million, RM150,000 capital) to G7, which has no tender limit and requires RM750,000 paid-up capital. See the table below."
  - q: "What are the CIDB categories and specialisations?"
    a: "Beyond grade, you register under one or more of four categories: Civil Engineering (CE), Building (B), Mechanical & Electrical (ME), and Facilities (F). Within each are specialisation codes — for example B01–B29, CE01–CE43, M01–M23, E01–E35 and F01–F02. New applicants are generally granted categories B and CE; ME requires qualified technical personnel, and F requires a company registered as a Sdn Bhd/Bhd (or co-operative/society) at grade G4 and above."
  - q: "What is the CIDB Green Card (Kad Hijau)?"
    a: "The Green Card is the CIDB registration card for construction personnel. Under section 33(1) of Act 520, a person may not be engaged as construction personnel — from general workers up to site supervisors and project managers — unless registered with CIDB and holding a valid certificate. To register, personnel must have attended the Safety and Health Induction Course for Construction Workers (SICW) or an equivalent recognised by CIDB."

You can register a company in an afternoon, but that company still cannot legally pour a slab, wire a building, or bid for a road contract until it has a second, separate licence. In Malaysia, construction is a regulated trade: the *company* is registered with SSM, and the *contractor* is registered with CIDB. Skip the second step and the first job you take is a criminal offence, not a contract.

Here is the operator's path — what to register, in what order, and what it costs you in capital and compliance.

## Step one: incorporate the company (SSM)

Almost every construction operator sets up as a **private limited company — a Sendirian Berhad (Sdn Bhd)** — because CIDB's higher grades and its Facilities category require a body corporate, and because limited liability matters on a worksite. You incorporate under the **Companies Act 2016** through SSM's online **MyCoID 2016** portal, which combines the name reservation and the incorporation submission in one flow ([SSM](https://www.ssm.com.my/Pages/Register_Business_Company_LLP/Company/Submitting-Incorporation-Company.aspx)).

Two things to get right at this stage, because CIDB will check them:

- **Paid-up capital.** CIDB sets a minimum paid-up capital for each grade (see below), so decide your target grade *before* you fix the company's capital — a G7 contractor needs RM750,000 paid up, not the RM1 you can incorporate with.
- **Equity.** Local contractor registration (Perakuan Pendaftaran Kontraktor, PPK) is open only to companies incorporated in Malaysia with **at least 70% local equity** ([CIDB](https://www.cidb.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/1.-Definisi-_-Skop-Pendaftaran.pdf)).

## Step two: why CIDB registration is not optional

The legal centre of gravity is the **Lembaga Pembangunan Industri Pembinaan Malaysia Act 1994 (Act 520)** — "Lembaga Pembangunan Industri Pembinaan Malaysia" is CIDB's Malay name. Under **section 25(1)**:

> "No person shall carry out or complete, undertake to carry out or complete any construction work or hold himself out as a contractor, unless he is registered with the Lembaga and holds a valid certificate of registration."

Note how wide that is. It bites not only when you *do* the work but when you *undertake* to do it or merely *hold yourself out* as a contractor — so tendering or advertising unregistered is already caught. Registration has been mandatory for every contractor, local or foreign, since the Act came into force in 1995 ([CIDB](https://www.cidb.gov.my/eng/contractor-registration/)).

## Step three: choose your grade (G1–G7)

CIDB registers each contractor at a **grade** that fixes two things at once: the **maximum value of work** you may tender for and carry out, and the **minimum paid-up capital** you must hold and maintain. The tender ceilings come from CIDB's registration summary, and the capital thresholds from its financial-eligibility schedule (Jadual E).

| Grade | Tendering / work value limit | Minimum paid-up capital |
|-------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| **G1** | Not exceeding RM200,000 | RM5,000 (RM10,000 to hold a government-works certificate, SPKK) |
| **G2** | Not exceeding RM500,000 | RM25,000 |
| **G3** | Not exceeding RM1,000,000 | RM50,000 |
| **G4** | Not exceeding RM3,000,000 | RM150,000 |
| **G5** | Not exceeding RM5,000,000 | RM250,000 |
| **G6** | Not exceeding RM10,000,000 | RM500,000 |
| **G7** | No limit | RM750,000 |

Sources: [CIDB registration summary](https://www.cidb.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/1.-Definisi-_-Skop-Pendaftaran.pdf) (grade & tender limits) and [CIDB Jadual E](https://www.cidb.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Keperluan-dan-Prosedur-Pendaftaran.pdf) (paid-up capital).

The practical reading: your grade is a ceiling, not a class you sit in — a G3 contractor simply cannot be awarded a RM2 million contract. Most new firms start low and **upgrade** as their capital and completed-project track record grow. Only G7 has no upper tender limit, which is why large builders and anyone chasing major public works aim for it.

## Step four: pick your category and specialisations

Grade answers "how big"; **category** answers "what kind". CIDB has **four registration categories** ([CIDB](https://www.cidb.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/1.-Definisi-_-Skop-Pendaftaran.pdf)):

- **CE — Civil Engineering** construction (roads, drainage, earthworks, structures)
- **B — Building** construction
- **ME — Mechanical & Electrical**
- **F — Facilities**

Under each category sit **specialisation codes** — the heads that describe exactly what you are allowed to do, such as **B01–B29** (building), **CE01–CE43** (civil), **M01–M23** and **E01–E35** (mechanical and electrical), and **F01–F02** (facilities). You register for the specialisations you can actually staff and resource:

- New applicants are generally granted **categories B and CE** by default;
- **ME** is granted only if the company has qualified technical personnel with relevant certificates or academic qualifications;
- **F (Facilities)** is granted only to a company registered as a Sdn Bhd/Bhd (or co-operative/society) **at grade G4 and above**, meeting the technical-personnel criteria.

## Step five: the Green Card for site personnel

Registering the *company* is not the end — the *people* on site have their own mandate. The **CIDB Construction Personnel Registration Card**, universally called the **Green Card (Kad Hijau)**, is required under **section 33(1)** of Act 520: a person may not be engaged as construction personnel unless registered with CIDB and holding a valid certificate. That covers the whole chain — general and skilled workers, site supervisors, and project managers.

To obtain it, personnel must have completed the **Safety and Health Induction Course for Construction Workers (SICW)** (or an equivalent CIDB-recognised safety course) ([CIDB](https://www.cidb.gov.my/eng/construction-personnel-registration/)). Budget the Green Card into your mobilisation for every new hire who will set foot on a site.

## What it costs to get caught without registration

The Act is not decorative. If you undertake construction work without being registered, **section 29** makes it an offence carrying, on conviction, **a fine of not less than RM10,000 and not more than RM100,000**. Beyond the fine, **section 30** lets CIDB serve a written **stop-work notice**; failing to comply with that notice is a further offence — a fine of **up to RM5,000, plus up to RM1,000 for each day** the breach continues.

There is also an ongoing charge to know about: on any construction contract with a sum **above RM500,000**, the contractor must pay CIDB a **levy of 0.25% (one-quarter of one per cent) of the contract sum** — a running cost of doing regulated construction, not a one-off.

## What's next

The sequence is the thing to hold onto: **incorporate the company (SSM) → register the contractor (CIDB, by grade and category) → card the workers (Green Card)**, and only then bid. Size your paid-up capital to the grade you actually want, register only for specialisations you can staff, and treat the RM10,000–RM100,000 section 29 fine as the reason none of these steps is skippable.

This guide covers *setting up* to trade. Once you are on site, a different body of law governs *getting paid* — the Construction Industry Payment and Adjudication Act (CIPAA) gives contractors a fast statutory route to recover progress claims, and is worth reading before you sign your first sub-contract. For the incorporation step itself, see the guide on registering a Sdn Bhd; for the sector's economics and where public construction spending flows, see the macro overview of Malaysia's construction industry. As always, confirm current CIDB fees, capital thresholds, and category conditions on the CIDB portal before you file, since they are updated from time to time.
