Half the confusion in Malaysian company administration is vocabulary. Two registers
with similar acronyms, four portals with unrelated names, and a set of form numbers
that everyone still uses years after the forms were repealed.

Here is the decoder.

## The institution

**SSM** — *Suruhanjaya Syarikat Malaysia*, the Companies Commission of Malaysia. The
registrar for both businesses and companies, established under the Companies
Commission of Malaysia Act 2001. That Act matters beyond the name: s.20G of it is the
provision under which SSM licenses company secretaries.

**The Registrar** — the office, not a person you meet. Most duties in the Companies
Act 2016 are owed to "the Registrar".

## The two registers

This is the distinction that causes the most trouble, because the two are not
different tiers of the same thing. They are different statutes.

| | ROB | ROC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full name | Register of Businesses | Register of Companies |
| Covers | Sole proprietorships and partnerships | Sdn Bhd, Bhd, LLP-adjacent filings and foreign companies |
| Governing law | Registration of Businesses Act 1956 | Companies Act 2016 |
| Legal personality | None — the owner *is* the business | Separate legal person |
| Portal | EzBiz | MyCoID |
| Registration number style | Business registration number | Company registration number assigned under s.15(b) |

A sole proprietorship is an ROB registration. It is not "a company", and the difference
is liability, not formality.

## The portals

**MyCoID** — the ROC portal. Company name search, incorporation applications and
company filings.

**EzBiz** — the ROB portal. New registration, changes, renewal and termination of a
business registration.

**MBRS** — the *Malaysian Business Reporting System*, SSM's structured digital
lodgement platform for financial statements, annual returns and related submissions.
When a company secretary refers to submitting "through MBRS", this is what they mean.

**e-Info / SSM e-Info Services** — SSM's paid information service. This is where you
buy a company profile, a corporate search or director-involvement information. The ROC
Table of Fees lists RM10 for an electronic corporate profile copy, RM20 for company
information supplied electronically, RM50 for shareholder or director involvement
information, and RM10 to inspect a register or obtain a document copy.

**MyLLP** — the portal for limited liability partnerships under the LLP Act 2012.

## The licensing portals — two different things

These are frequently conflated because they overlap in purpose.

**BLESS** — *Business Licensing Electronic Support System*, at portal.bless.gov.my. A
federal one-stop system for applying for, tracking and obtaining licences, approvals
and permits from participating agencies. Company details are drawn from SSM rather
than re-entered.

**MalaysiaBiz** — at malaysiabiz.gov.my, operated by **SME Corp. Malaysia**. A gateway
combining business registration and licensing information with application services for
selected agencies, integrating with SSM, local councils and ministries.

Neither replaces the licensing authority. They are front doors. The licence is still
issued by the council, ministry or regulator behind them.

## The old forms and what replaced them

The Companies Act 1965 numbered its forms, and the numbers became the language. The
2016 Act abolished the forms but the vocabulary survived — which is why you will still
hear "send me your Form 49" for a company incorporated last month.

| Old form (CA 1965) | What it was | Companies Act 2016 successor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form 9 | Certificate of Incorporation of Private Company | **Notice of registration** issued by the Registrar under s.15(c). A certificate of incorporation is now issued only on application and payment of a fee under s.17 |
| Form 13 | Certificate of Incorporation on Change of Name | **Notice of registration of the new name** issued under s.28(3)(b), after a special resolution notified within 30 days |
| Form 24 | Return of Allotment of Shares | **Return of allotment** with a statement of capital, lodged within 14 days under s.78 |
| Form 44 | Notice of Situation of Registered Office and Office Hours | Registered office duty under s.46; any change notified within 14 days under s.46(3) |
| Form 48A | Statutory Declaration by a Person Before Appointment as Director | Written consent and declaration of non-disqualification under s.201 |
| Form 49 | Return Giving Particulars in Register of Directors, Managers and Secretaries | Register kept under s.57; changes notified to the Registrar within 14 days under s.58 |
| Annual Return form | Annual return | Annual return under s.68, lodged not later than 30 days from the incorporation anniversary — and not required in the calendar year of incorporation (s.68(2)) |

**Why this matters practically.** A bank, landlord or counterparty asking for "Form 9
and Form 49" is asking for proof of existence and proof of who the officers are. For a
company incorporated under the 2016 Act, give them the **notice of registration** and a
**current company profile from SSM e-Info**. Those are the modern equivalents. If they
insist on a certificate of incorporation, that can be applied for under s.17 for a fee.

## Other terms you will meet

**Sdn Bhd** — *Sendirian Berhad*, a private company limited by shares. Capped at 50
shareholders and required to restrict share transfers (s.42).

**Bhd** — *Berhad*, a public company. Not the same as listed.

**Constitution** — the replacement for the Memorandum and Articles of Association,
optional for a company limited by shares under s.31. For a pre-2016 company, the old M
and A *is* the constitution (s.34(c)).

**Superform** — the informal name for the single consolidated incorporation form in
MyCoID that replaced the separate documents required under the old Act.

**MSIC** — Malaysia Standard Industrial Classification, the Department of Statistics
Malaysia activity code you declare as your nature of business.

**Practice Directive / Practice Note** — SSM's binding administrative instruments.
Practice Directives set requirements; Practice Notes clarify interpretation. They are
where operational rules such as the audit exemption criteria actually live, which is
why an article citing only the Act can still be out of date.

**Strike off** — administrative removal from the register under s.550, distinct from
winding up.

**Lodge / lodgement** — the statutory word for filing a document with the Registrar.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating ROB and ROC as the same register.** Different Acts, different liability,
  different portal.
- **Asking a 2026-incorporated company for a Form 9.** It does not exist. The notice of
  registration under s.15 is the document.
- **Assuming you were issued a certificate of incorporation.** Under s.17 you get one
  only if you apply and pay for it.
- **Confusing BLESS with a licensing authority.** It is a submission channel.
- **Reading only the Act.** Practice Directives carry the operational detail and are
  revised more often than the statute.

## What's next

If someone has asked you for a document by an old form number, translate it using the
table above and send the modern equivalent, with a one-line note saying which section
it is issued under. That usually ends the conversation faster than arguing about the
form number.
