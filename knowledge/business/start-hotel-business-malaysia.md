Buying the building, fitting the rooms and taking the first booking is the easy part of opening a hotel. The part that stops people is that a tourist-accommodation business in Malaysia answers to several authorities at once — the companies registry, the local council, the tourism ministry and the fire department — and one of them, MOTAC, requires you to register the premises **before** you operate. Skip it and you are not merely un-graded; you are operating an unlicensed tourism business, an offence that can cost up to RM500,000 or ten years' jail.

Here is the legal set-up path, in the order it actually has to happen.

## First, register the business entity with SSM

Every business in Malaysia must be registered with the **Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM)**. For a hotel you will almost always operate through a private limited company (*Sendirian Berhad*) incorporated under the Companies Act 2016, though a sole proprietorship or partnership registered under the Registration of Businesses Act is possible for a very small operation. The entity is the legal "person" that will hold every downstream licence, sign the tenancy or own the property, and appear on the MOTAC and council applications — so it comes first.

If foreign shareholding is involved, check the equity conditions before you commit, as tourism and accommodation can attract sector-specific requirements.

## Get the local-council (PBT) licences

You cannot run premises to the public without the local authority's blessing. The **Pihak Berkuasa Tempatan (PBT)** — your city or district council — issues two things that matter here:

- a **business-premise / accommodation-premises operating licence** (often called a *lesen premis penginapan*), which is the licence that makes the premises legal to operate; and
- a **signboard (signage) licence** for any hotel sign, with each signboard measured and submitted in the application.

Requirements, supporting documents and fees are set by each council and vary, so apply to the specific PBT where the hotel sits. This licence is not optional paperwork you can leave for later — MOTAC treats a **valid local-authority accommodation-premises business licence as a prerequisite** for the next step.

## Register the premises with MOTAC — the step people miss

This is the requirement that is specific to accommodation, and it is mandatory. Under the **Tourism Industry Act 1992 (Act 482)**, any person who carries on or operates accommodation premises must apply to the **Commissioner of Tourism** to register those premises as **tourist accommodation premises**. MOTAC's guidance is explicit that this registration is **mandatory for all accommodation premises** defined under the Act, **regardless of the number of rooms** — a hotel, motel, inn, boarding house, rest house, lodging house or similar building offering sleeping accommodation to tourists for payment all fall within it.

A few practical points MOTAC sets out:

- The application is made online through MOTAC's **TOURLIST** licensing and enforcement system.
- You must supply your **SSM (or cooperative SKM) registration** and your **valid local-authority business licence** as part of the application.
- MOTAC may **inspect** the premises; the Commissioner registers them once satisfied as to suitability and the prescribed fee is paid.
- Registration is issued for a defined term (a certificate valid for three years), after which it must be maintained.

Why the ministry insists on this: registration lets it hold operators to conditions, keep a public register of legitimate accommodation, and act against the flood of unregistered conversions. Which is also why **operating without it is an offence** — up to **RM500,000 or ten years' imprisonment, or both**, under Act 482, and MOTAC runs active enforcement sweeps and issues notices to unlicensed operators.

## Fire safety: the BOMBA fire certificate and the building's CCC

Two building-safety approvals sit alongside the licences.

**The Certificate of Completion and Compliance (CCC).** Before a building can be occupied for its intended use, it must be certified complete and compliant with the approved plans and by-laws. If you are converting an existing building to hotel use, expect to satisfy the building requirements for that change of use.

**The BOMBA fire certificate.** A hotel is a **"designated premises"** under the **Fire Services Act 1988 (Act 341)**, which means it must hold a **Fire Certificate (Perakuan Bomba)** issued by the **Fire and Rescue Department (BOMBA)**, confirming the building's fire-safety systems are installed, maintained and working. BOMBA's own guidance is that the certificate is **valid for one year and must be renewed annually**, with renewals filed using **Form III not less than 30 days before expiry**, and that applications and renewals go through its **ePREMIS** online system. In practice a new premises applies for its fire certificate once the CCC (or equivalent fitness certificate) is in hand.

## Grading: star rating and Orchid rating

Baseline registration lets you operate; **grading** governs how you may present the hotel. MOTAC runs an official scheme with two tracks:

- a **Star Rating** for hotels, assessing the level of luxury, facilities and quality of service; and
- an **Orchid Rating** for budget accommodation, focused on cleanliness, safety and basic comfort.

Grading is scored as a percentage — MOTAC's bands run from **90-100% for five stars** down through the lower tiers — and hotels are also classified by concept: city, resort, highland, boutique, innovative and apartment hotels. The rating is what determines the stars you may lawfully advertise, and MOTAC has been tightening how the scheme reflects real quality. Treat grading as the layer on top of registration, not a substitute for it.

## How a homestay or short-term rental differs

"Homestay" has a specific meaning in Malaysia and it is not a synonym for a small hotel or an Airbnb. MOTAC's **Homestay (and Kampungstay) Experience Programme** is a community-based cultural-tourism scheme where guests stay **with a host family** and share in village life — it is about the experience, not just a room, and it is registered under that programme rather than as an ordinary hotel.

A commercial **short-term rental** — a unit or house let out to travellers on a booking platform — is different again. It generally does not qualify for the community homestay programme, but that does not make it unregulated: such accommodation still needs the **local-authority (PBT) licence** and, as accommodation premises offered to tourists for payment, still falls under the MOTAC accommodation-registration regime. Some local councils and states apply additional short-term-rental rules, so verify the position where the property is located before you list it.

## What's next

Sequence the set-up in the order the authorities expect: incorporate with **SSM**, secure the **PBT business-premise and signboard licences**, satisfy the **building (CCC) and BOMBA fire-certificate** requirements, then **register the premises with MOTAC** as tourist accommodation before you take a single paid booking — and only afterward pursue **star or Orchid grading** for how you market it. Because the accommodation-registration rules have been tightened and enforced in recent years, and because council requirements differ, confirm the current fees and documents directly with your local PBT, MOTAC's TOURLIST system and BOMBA before you commit timelines.
