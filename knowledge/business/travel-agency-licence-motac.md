You have found suppliers, built an itinerary and you are ready to start selling holidays — but in Malaysia the selling is the part the law reaches first. Before a single tour is advertised or a ticket is sold, the business behind it has to be licensed by the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture (MOTAC). This is not a formality bolted on afterwards; it is the gate you pass through to trade at all, and running tours without it is a criminal offence.

Here is how travel agency and tour operator licensing actually works in Malaysia — what the categories are, what MOTAC expects of you, and what it costs to get it wrong.

## What law requires the licence?

Tourism operators in Malaysia are governed by the **Tourism Industry Act 1992 (Act 482)** and, in detail, by the **Tourism Industry (Tour Operating Business and Travel Agency Business) Regulations 1992**. Together they make it an offence to carry on or operate a tour operating business or travel agency business without a licence from MOTAC.

The reach is deliberately wide. If your business plans, packages, sells or advertises tours, or sells travel tickets to the public, it is a *tourism enterprise* that needs to be licensed **before** it starts trading. Registering a company is not enough on its own, and neither is holding out that you will "sort the licence later" — the licence must be in hand before you advertise or sell.

## What are the categories of licence?

MOTAC does not issue one generic "travel agency licence". It licenses the business by the **category of activity** you actually carry on, and its own register of licensed agencies lists them as:

- **Inbound tour operating** — handling tourists coming *into* Malaysia (itineraries, ground handling, packages for foreign visitors).
- **Outbound tour operating** — arranging travel *abroad* for people in Malaysia.
- **Ticketing** — selling air and other transport tickets as a travel agent.
- **Umrah / ziarah** — organising religious pilgrimage travel, a distinct sub-category with its own scrutiny.

You apply for the categories that match your business, and a licence can cover more than one. MOTAC's public register currently lists several thousand licensed agencies nationwide, split between head offices and branches — a reminder that this is a well-policed, well-populated licensed sector, not a grey area.

## What are the steps to get licensed?

The sequence matters, because MOTAC needs to see a real, registered company before it will license it.

1. **Register the company with SSM.** Incorporate with the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM) — in practice a private limited company (*Sdn Bhd*) — with a business scope that covers tour operating and travel agency activities.
2. **Prepare the premises.** Secure a proper office (not a residential setup) that meets MOTAC's minimum standards, because inspectors will visit and check it as part of processing.
3. **Apply to MOTAC on Form 1.** Submit *Form 1 — Application for Licence to Carry on or Operate a Tour Operating Business and Travel Agency Business*, with the supporting documents on the checklist, and pay the **RM100 processing fee**.
4. **Meet the conditions and pass inspection.** MOTAC assesses your paid-up capital, the tourism experience of your directors and shareholders, and your premises before granting the licence.
5. **Then advertise and sell.** Only once the licence is granted may you market and sell tours in the licensed categories.

## What conditions must you meet?

MOTAC's licensing conditions are about proving the business is substantial and competently run, not just registered. The main ones are:

| Condition | What MOTAC looks for |
|---|---|
| **Paid-up capital** | "Sufficient" capital, set **according to the area** where you intend to operate — not a single flat national figure. Because the amounts are fixed by MOTAC policy and can change, confirm the current requirement for your category and location with MOTAC before you incorporate. |
| **Office premises** | A dedicated business premises meeting MOTAC's minimum standards, which its officers inspect. |
| **Experience / management** | Tourism experience among the directors and shareholders, and a manager competent to run the operation. |
| **Documentation** | SSM registration, company documents, and the items on MOTAC's application checklist submitted with Form 1. |

On the industry side, joining a body such as the **Malaysian Association of Tour and Travel Agents (MATTA)** is common and useful for standards, networking and bonding schemes, but membership of an association is *not* the government licence — only MOTAC's licence lets you trade legally.

Because exact paid-up-capital figures are set by MOTAC policy and differ by category and region, treat any single number you see quoted on commercial "company setup" sites with caution and **verify the current figure directly with MOTAC's Tourism Licensing and Enforcement Division**.

## What about tour guides?

A common trap: your agency licence does not license the people guiding the tours. **Tourist guides are licensed separately** under the Tourism Industry Act 1992 — an individual must hold a valid tourist-guide licence to work as a guide, and a licensed agency should engage only licensed guides. If you plan to guide tours yourself or employ guides, budget for that separate licensing track alongside the business licence.

## What is the penalty for operating unlicensed?

MOTAC treats unlicensed operators seriously and runs enforcement operations to catch them. Carrying on a tourism enterprise without a valid licence is an offence under the Tourism Industry Act 1992, and on conviction the penalty is a **fine of up to RM500,000, or imprisonment of up to 10 years, or both**. Beyond prosecution, MOTAC can suspend or revoke the licences of operators who breach the Act and regulations.

That is the real reason to license before you sell: the downside of skipping it is not a small administrative fine but a serious criminal exposure — and, in a sector where MOTAC publishes a searchable register of who is licensed, being unlicensed is easy to spot.

## What's next

If you are launching a travel business, sequence it in the right order: **incorporate with SSM, fit out and confirm the premises, then apply to MOTAC on Form 1 for the exact categories you will trade** — inbound, outbound, ticketing or umrah/ziarah — and only advertise once the licence is granted. Confirm the current paid-up-capital and any deposit conditions with MOTAC directly, since those are set by policy and vary by category and area, and line up separate tourist-guide licensing if guiding is part of the offer.
