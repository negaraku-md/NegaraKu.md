Failing to file the termination notice is itself an offence — the general penalty is a fine not exceeding RM10,000 or up to a year's imprisonment or both. And under section 5E, if the Registrar has reasonable cause to believe the business is no longer being carried on, it can give you 30 days to respond and then revoke the registration and publish the revocation in the Gazette."

Registering a sole proprietorship or partnership takes ten minutes on the ezBiz portal, so most people assume that closing one is just as informal — you stop trading and it quietly lapses. It does not. A business registered under the **Registration of Businesses Act 1956** stays on SSM's register until you actively terminate it, and while it sits there it keeps needing renewal, exposes you to penalties, and can be revoked by the Registrar. There is a proper way to close it, it is free, and there is a clock on it.

Here is how to close a sole proprietorship or partnership in Malaysia — the SSM side, the LHDN side, and why none of this looks like closing a company.

## Why stopping trading is not closing the business

A sole proprietorship or partnership is a *business registration*, not a separate legal person. But the registration is real and it has a life of its own. Under **section 5** of the Registration of Businesses Act 1956, a business is registered for a period of **up to five years**, and under **section 5A** it must be **renewed** — within 30 days before expiry — for up to five years at a time. Ignore it and the registration does not vanish; it simply falls due for renewal, and the obligation to keep your particulars current sits on you the whole time.

SSM can also act on its own. Under **section 5E**, if the Registrar has *reasonable cause to believe* a registered business is no longer being carried on, it can serve notice, give you **30 days** to make written representations, and then **revoke the registration and gazette it**. Letting your dormant business be struck by the Registrar is a far messier ending than filing the termination yourself.

## Filing the Notice of Termination on ezBiz

The clean way to close is to lodge a **Notice of Termination of Business** with SSM. According to SSM's *Guideline for Termination of Business*, a termination can be registered for four reasons:

- **cessation of business**;
- **dissolution of partnership**;
- **death**; and
- **court order**.

For a straightforward cessation of business or dissolution of a partnership, the **owner — or all of the partners — files online** through the ezBiz portal (ezbiz.ssm.com.my); you must be a registered ezBiz user first. **Registration of the termination carries no fee.** The deadline is the important part: **section 5D(1)** requires the person responsible for the business to notify the Registrar **within 30 days** of the business terminating.

One trap: the guideline notes that a business whose **registration period has already expired cannot file** a termination online — you would instead purchase a Business Expiry Confirmation Letter through SSM's e-Info or MyData portals. Terminating *before* your registration lapses keeps this simple.

## The death and court-order routes are different

Two scenarios do not go through the ordinary online flow:

- **Death of the sole owner, or of all partners.** Here the business ends by operation of the Act, and **section 5D(2)** gives the **personal representative or next-of-kin four months** from the date of death — not 30 days — to notify the Registrar. SSM's guideline requires this to be done **over the counter** by the next-of-kin, attaching the **death certificate** and the latest certificate of registration (**Form D or E**).
- **Court order.** A termination ordered by a court is also handled at the SSM counter with the court order document, rather than through the online portal.

A partnership that merely loses one of several partners while the others carry on is not a termination — that is a **dissolution/change of particulars** to update under section 5B, not a closure of the whole registration.

## Settling with LHDN

Closing the SSM registration does **not** close your tax affairs — that is a separate matter with the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN), and it is easy to overlook.

A sole proprietor's and partner's business income is **taxed as personal income**, so there is no separate "company" tax file to shut. What you do instead is:

- **file your final income tax return (Form B)** for the year in which the business ceased, declaring the business income up to the cessation date and paying any balance;
- **inform LHDN that the business source has ceased**, so your future assessments reflect that the enterprise has stopped; and
- **apply for a tax clearance letter** (*Surat Penyelesaian Cukai*, SPC) **if you are leaving Malaysia or are a foreign person** — the SPC confirms your tax is settled before you go. A resident who simply stops one business and stays in Malaysia keeps their personal tax file and normally does not need an SPC.

Settling with LHDN cleanly matters because unpaid tax follows you personally — the business was never a separate legal shield.

## Don't forget the other registrations

Because the enterprise itself is lightweight, its *attachments* are what linger. Before you consider the business fully wound down, cancel or notify:

- **SST registration**, if you were registered for sales and service tax;
- **EPF, SOCSO and EIS** employer accounts, if you had staff, and settle final contributions;
- **local council (PBT) business/signage licences** and any **sector licence or permit** (for example a food premises or WRT-type licence);
- your **business bank account**, once final receipts and payments have cleared.

## How this differs from closing a company

It is worth being blunt about this, because the two are constantly confused. A **sole proprietorship or partnership** is a registration under the Registration of Businesses Act 1956: closing it is a **free notice to SSM**, with **no liquidator, no strike-off application and no company law process**. A **Sdn Bhd** is a separate legal person under the Companies Act 2016 and can only be exited by a **strike-off** or a **winding up**, each with its own fee, conditions and statutory clocks. If your business is actually a company, the enterprise-termination route on this page does not apply — see the separate guide on closing a company.

## A short checklist

1. **Stop trading** and fix the cessation date.
2. **File the Notice of Termination on ezBiz** within **30 days** (owner or all partners; free). Death or court-order cases go **over the counter** with supporting documents.
3. **File the final Form B** with LHDN and inform them the business source has ceased; **apply for tax clearance (SPC)** if leaving Malaysia.
4. **Cancel the attachments** — SST, EPF/SOCSO/EIS, council and sector licences, and the business bank account.

## What's next

If you are winding down a sole proprietorship or partnership, do the SSM termination first and on time — it is free, it stops the renewal treadmill, and it removes the risk of the Registrar revoking and gazetting your registration under section 5E. Then close the loop with LHDN so no personal tax liability trails you, and cancel the licences and accounts that were riding on the registration. If what you are actually closing is a Sdn Bhd, use the company-closure route instead; and if you are still deciding whether to have been a sole proprietor or a company in the first place, the comparison guides on business structures cover the trade-offs. Because SSM procedures and LHDN forms are periodically updated, confirm the current ezBiz steps and tax-clearance requirements on the official portals before you file.
