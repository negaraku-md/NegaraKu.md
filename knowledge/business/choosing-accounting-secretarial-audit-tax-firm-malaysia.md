Every Malaysian company leans on a small circle of professionals it never sees on the org chart: the company secretary who keeps it legal, the accountant who keeps its books, the auditor who signs off its numbers, and the tax agent who files with LHDN. Choosing them well saves money and sleepless nights. Yet search for "best company secretary in Malaysia" and nearly every guide you find was written by a firm that sells the service. This one isn't.

## Four services, four different licences

The first thing to understand is that these are **four separate services, each with its own law and its own regulator**. A single firm may offer all four, but it needs a different authorisation for each — and you are never obliged to buy them as a bundle.

| Service | What it covers | Who may legally provide it |
|---|---|---|
| **Company secretary** | Statutory filings, registers, board and SSM compliance | A person registered with SSM holding a practising certificate under **s.241, Companies Act 2016** — a member of a prescribed body (MAICSA, MACS, MIA, the Malaysian Bar) or licensed under s.20G of the SSM Act 2001 |
| **Accounting / bookkeeping** | Day-to-day books, management and statutory accounts | A member of the **Malaysian Institute of Accountants (MIA)** with a practising certificate, under the Accountants Act 1967, for public practice |
| **Audit** | Independent audit of the financial statements | An **approved company auditor** under **s.263, Companies Act 2016**, approved by the Minister of Finance; for listed and public-interest entities, also overseen by the Audit Oversight Board |
| **Tax agent** | Tax computations, filing, and representing you to LHDN | A tax agent **approved under s.153, Income Tax Act 1967** and listed by LHDN |

Every Malaysian private company must appoint a company secretary within 30 days of incorporation. Most need bookkeeping and tax filing. Audit is required unless your company qualifies for one of SSM's **[audit exemptions](/en/accounting/audit-exemption-criteria/)** — so a dormant or very small company may need no auditor at all.

## How the market is tiered

It helps to picture the market in layers. The grouping below is a map, not a scoreboard.

> These tiers are an indicative market grouping based on scale, international reach and market positioning — not an official ranking or an assessment of service quality. Service availability varies by firm and may be delivered through separate affiliated entities.

- **The Big Four** — PwC, Deloitte, EY and KPMG. Global networks built for complex, regulated, listed and multinational work. Deepest specialism and cross-border reach; the highest fees.
- **Mid-tier international networks** — member firms of global networks such as BDO, Baker Tilly, Crowe, Grant Thornton, Forvis Mazars, UHY, RSM, Moore, HLB, PKF, Nexia and Russell Bedford. They carry international methodology and reach with more direct partner access and lower cost than the Big Four. Crowe Malaysia reported itself the fifth-largest firm in Malaysia in the International Accounting Bulletin 2024 survey.
- **Corporate-services and share-registry specialists** — firms such as Tricor, Boardroom and TMF Group concentrate on company secretarial, share registry, payroll and entity administration, often across multiple countries, rather than audit.
- **Established Malaysian firms** — home-grown practices such as YYC, Cheng & Co, KTP, KLM and 3E Accounting, strong on local knowledge and the full spread of SME compliance.
- **Boutiques and smaller practices** — single-principal and small firms, frequently the most cost-effective option for a single company, a micro-enterprise or a dormant entity, with a close personal relationship but a narrower service range.
- **Digital-first platforms** — online providers such as BossBoleh, MISHU, Sleek, Osome, Foundingbird and iCompany, which handle incorporation and ongoing filings through a web dashboard at fixed, transparent prices. They suit startups and small companies comfortable with self-service — provided the named company secretary behind the platform is properly licensed.

## How to choose: match the firm to your company

Bigger is not better; *fitting* is better. A rough guide:

- **A single, simple or dormant company** — a boutique firm or a digital platform usually covers everything you need at the lowest cost.
- **A growing SME** with financing, grants, multiple entities or sharper tax questions — an established local firm or a mid-tier network gives you room to grow into.
- **A regulated, listed, foreign-owned or multi-country group** — a mid-tier network or the Big Four, where the specialism and audit standing are worth the premium.

Before you sign, ask every candidate the same questions: *What is your licence or registration number? Who actually does the work — a partner or a junior? What exactly is in scope, and what triggers an extra fee? How is my data handled? Can you give me a reference from a company like mine?* A written fee schedule and a clear scope letter matter more than a glossy brochure.

## What will it cost?

Fees are quoted per service, not as one lump sum, so compare like with like. A company secretary is usually a modest monthly or annual retainer that covers the statutory registers and routine filings, with the annual return and specific resolutions often billed on top. Bookkeeping is priced by transaction volume or as a fixed monthly package; audit and tax are separate engagements again. The cheapest headline quote is rarely the cheapest outcome — ask precisely what is *excluded*, because add-on charges for extra filings, XBRL submission or late work are where a low retainer quietly grows. Get every number in writing before you appoint, and confirm whether SST applies.

## Verify any firm yourself

This is the step the sales-driven guides skip. A genuine licence is **checkable in a public register** — you never have to take a provider's word for it:

- **Company secretary** — confirm the practising certificate and professional-body membership (SSM's e-Secretary system; MAICSA, MACS or MIA directories).
- **Accountant / auditor** — search the MIA member and member-firm directory; an auditor must additionally be an approved company auditor (Ministry of Finance / Accountant General), and for listed-company audits, registered with the Audit Oversight Board.
- **Tax agent** — check that the agent is approved under s.153 of the Income Tax Act 1967 and appears on LHDN's list.

If a provider cannot or will not give you a registration number to check, treat that as a red flag and walk away.

## Common mistakes

Appointing on price alone and discovering the cheap quote excludes the filings you actually need. Assuming one firm must do everything. Letting a lapsed or unlicensed "secretary" file on your behalf — the directors, not the secretary, carry the legal liability. And never verifying the licence at all.

## What's next

Shortlist two or three providers at the tier that fits your company, ask each the questions above, and verify their licences in the public registers before you commit. If you are still setting up, start with the **[company secretary](/en/business/company-secretary-malaysia/)** — it is the one appointment the law requires first.
