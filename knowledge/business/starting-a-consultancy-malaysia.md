Consultancy looks like the easiest business to start — no premises, no inventory, just your expertise and a laptop. And for a general management consultant, it nearly is: register with SSM and you are trading. But two things trip people up. First, if your "consulting" is actually a *regulated profession*, a business registration is not enough — you need a licence to practise. Second, the entity you pick and the tax you fall under matter more for a services firm than for almost any other business, because your income *is* the business.

Here is how to set up a consultancy or professional services firm in Malaysia, and the two questions to settle before you take your first client.

## Step 1: Choose the right entity

For a services firm, the choice among three vehicles turns on liability, tax and credibility:

| Vehicle | Liability | Tax | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietorship / enterprise** | **Unlimited** — your personal assets are exposed | Personal graduated rates (Form B) | A solo freelancer testing the water; cheapest and fastest |
| **LLP (PLT)** | **Limited** — a separate legal entity shields partners' personal assets | Taxed as a body corporate; audit-exempt; only an annual declaration | A two-plus-partner firm wanting protection with light compliance |
| **Sdn Bhd** | **Limited** by shares | Corporate SME rates | Scaling, hiring, tendering, and credibility with corporate clients |

The **LLP** is the underrated middle option — it gives you the limited liability and separate legal personality of a company, with far lighter compliance than a Sdn Bhd. A **Sdn Bhd** is the most credible to large corporate procurement and is required if any owner is foreign. See the linked comparisons on sole proprietorship versus Sdn Bhd and LLP versus Sdn Bhd for the full trade-offs.

## Step 2: Check whether your profession is regulated

This is the caveat most guides skip, and it is the one that matters. **Registering a business with SSM gives you a business — not a licence to practise a gazetted profession.** If your field is regulated, you need the professional body's registration first, and often the practice entity itself must be registered with the regulator:

- **Accountants** — the Malaysian Institute of Accountants (MIA) under the Accountants Act 1967; "accountant" is a protected term, and public practice needs a practising certificate.
- **Engineers** — the Board of Engineers Malaysia (BEM) under the Registration of Engineers Act 1967; the engineering consultancy practice itself must be registered.
- **Auditors** — MIA plus the Audit Oversight Board for public-interest audits.
- **Lawyers** — admission to the Bar through the Legal Profession Qualifying Board.
- **Architects** — the Board of Architects Malaysia (LAM) under the Architects Act 1967.
- **Financial planners / advisers** — a Securities Commission licence and/or FIMM registration.
- **Company secretaries** — must be SSM-licensed or a member of a prescribed body.

If you are a *general* management, IT, marketing or business consultant, none of this applies and SSM registration is your baseline. If you are in one of the fields above, sort the professional licence *before* you register a business around it.

## Step 3: Register for service tax at the threshold

Professional and consultancy services are taxable. The key figures:

- the **service tax rate on professional services is 8%** — raised from 6% on **1 March 2024**; and
- you must **register once taxable turnover exceeds RM500,000** in a 12-month period.

Consultancy sits in the professionals grouping (Group G) alongside legal, accounting, engineering and IT services. One thing to get right in your own materials: this is **SST**, not GST — GST was abolished in 2018, and repeating the old "GST if turnover exceeds RM500,000" line is a common and dating error. See the Service Tax Act guide for the mechanics.

## Step 4: Sort income tax and social security

**Income tax** depends on your entity:

- a **sole proprietor** reports business profit at **personal graduated rates** on **Form B**, due 30 June;
- an **SME Sdn Bhd** (paid-up capital up to RM2.5 million and gross business income up to RM50 million) is taxed at **15%** on the first RM150,000 of chargeable income, **17%** from RM150,001 to RM600,000, and **24%** above — a meaningful advantage at higher profit, though the SME rate is lost if more than 20% of the company is foreign-owned.

**Social security** depends on whether you hire:

- **if you employ staff**, register them for EPF and SOCSO/EIS as an employer;
- **if you are a solo consultant**, you fall under the self-employed schemes — SOCSO's Self-Employment Social Security Scheme for injury protection, and the voluntary EPF **i-Saraan** channel (with a government contribution incentive) to keep building retirement savings. See the freelancer-and-gig-tax guide.

## What's next

Settle the two big questions first: **is my profession regulated** (if so, get the licence), and **which entity fits** (sole proprietor for a quick solo start, LLP for a protected small partnership, Sdn Bhd for scale and credibility). Then register with SSM, watch the **RM500,000** service-tax threshold as you grow, and pick your entity partly on the tax difference between personal rates and the SME corporate rates.

Because the service-tax rate changed in 2024 and the SME-rate conditions were tightened, confirm the current rate and thresholds with RMCD and LHDN before you rely on them. The linked guides on entity choice, service tax and freelancer tax carry the detail for each step.
