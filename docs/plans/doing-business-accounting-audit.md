## Differentiation

**Accounting.** Four seams: (1) the e-Invoice × s.245 × ITA record-keeping collision;
(2) operational MBRS/SSMxT content beyond "are you ready?"; (3) the **2027 double
transition** — MPERS (2025) and MFRS 18 land the same year and almost nothing
addresses a company facing both; (4) NSRF as an accounting obligation rather than an
ESG marketing topic. Plus maintained data pages that firm blogs never keep current.

**Audit.** Three gaps: (1) **process transparency** — every competitor is an audit
firm and none publishes what actually happens during an engagement, because opacity is
commercially useful to them; (2) **the withdrawn fee schedule** — a widely repeated
falsehood we can correct with a labelled market range; (3) **post-exemption reality** —
the corpus sells exemption as a saving and nobody costs the downside. We also win on
the phase-in table, which most competitors have simply got wrong.

## Recon notes

"MFRS vs MPERS" is saturated with near-identical 800–1,200-word firm blogs (Ecovis,
ShineWing, GSK, Premia, HashMicro) plus a PwC PDF — thin, none updated for MPERS
(2025). "MBRS 2.0" is dominated by XBRL *vendors* selling conversion; winning format is
a long step-by-step guide with screenshots, which none provide. "Accounting records
Malaysia" is won by *law* firms (Thomas Philip, RDS, Yew Huoi How) on the
director-inspection angle — **a signal to lean legal, not generic.**

"Audit exemption Malaysia" is unusually crowded and unusually **inaccurate** — top firm
results state RM3m/RM3m/30 as though current, several misdescribe the exclusion list.
SSM's own PD ranks, meaning a well-cited page can compete. "Audit fee Malaysia" is
dominated by pages reproducing the withdrawn RPG 7 tables (Boss Boleh, NBC, 3E, KLM) —
high intent, uniformly stale, **the clearest single opportunity**. "Types of audit
opinion" is won by generic international content with no Malaysian anchoring at all.
