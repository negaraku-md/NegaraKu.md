Facebook Posting Plan

How the NegaraKu.md knowledge base grows three young Facebook Pages — draining a ~1,050-article backlog and every new article — in the one format that actually earns reach, on a warm-up ramp, measured against first-party data.

01 The lever is format, not volume

Facebook suppresses posts that send people off-platform. A stream of “title + link” posts gets almost no reach no matter how many we publish — so the one decision that governs everything else is how a post is shaped, not how often.

Native photo

Every post is the article’s own OG card, uploaded as a real photo. Facebook favours native images over off-site link previews, and the card already carries the title, breadcrumb and summary.

Value-first caption

Title as the hook, the article’s summary as the payoff, then a pillar-specific question that invites a reply — comments are Facebook’s strongest reach signal.

Tappable link in caption

The article link sits on line 2, right under the title — above Facebook's "See more" fold, so it shows without expanding. One tap to the site; the photo stays the attachment (not an off-site link preview), so reach holds; UTM-attributed.

03 Which article posts next — the queue

The backlog does not drain in file order. A ranked queue front-loads proven demand and keeps the feed varied; a committed manifest guarantees nothing is ever posted twice.

Priority ranking

  • Search demand first — articles in categories already earning Google impressions (from the archived Search Console data) rank highest: proven pull.
  • Pillar rotation — the feed round-robins Doing Business → Living → Understand so no single pillar floods a Page.
  • Recency tiebreak — newer articles win within a category.
  • Seasonal injection later — tax deadlines, holidays and budget season jump the queue on the day.

No double-posts, ever

Every successful post is recorded in analytics/posted.json, keyed by article + language. The queue skips anything already there, and a partially-failed article retries only its missing languages on the next run. The manifest is committed to the repo, so state survives across runs and machines.

The already-live test article (cradle-cip-grant) is seeded as posted, so the rollout never re-posts it.

04 Cadence — a warm-up ramp

Young Pages that suddenly post in bulk trip Facebook’s spam and integrity checks and throttle all their distribution. The rate ramps up as the Pages age. One article a day means three posts — one to each language Page.

Weeks 1–2
1 article/day
Warm the Pages. 3 posts/day total. Watch that reach and reactions hold before speeding up.
Weeks 3–4
3 articles/day
9 posts/day. The steady middle gear once the Pages have a track record.
Week 5 onward
5 articles/day
15 posts/day — 5 per Page. Drains the remaining backlog in roughly seven months.

中文 /zh

Malaysian-Chinese and regional readers. Business, tax and education topics travel well; a distinct posting rhythm.

06 Turning reach into followers

Posting is only half of it. Growth comes from the loop around each post and from seeding the Pages beyond their own timeline.

On-post engagement

  • Every post ends with a question — designed to pull comments.
  • Reply to every comment quickly; it doubles a thread’s reach and signals an active Page.
  • “How-to / what you’re entitled to” framing gets shared into family and group chats — the real growth channel here.
  • Cross-promote the three Pages to one another.

Off-page seeding a Page of 2 doesn’t grow by posting alone

  • Share posts into relevant Malaysian Facebook Groups (expat, business, tax, student) — where the reach actually is for a cold Page.
  • Link the website footer / About to the three Pages, converting existing readers.
  • Optional 1company-funded boosts on the 5–10 best evergreen posts — the fastest way to break the cold-start, cheap in this market.

07 How we know it’s working

Every number this program produces is already captured by the site’s own analytics — no third-party dashboard.

Page insights

Followers, reactions, comments and shares per Page are archived weekly to analytics/facebook.json and shown on the site dashboard’s Facebook panel.

Click attribution

The UTM tags make every Facebook click land in the dashboard’s “Traffic by channel → Social”, with the source broken out — real visits, not vanity metrics.

Weekly read

Review the panel weekly; double down on the topics and times that land, drop what doesn’t. The queue’s ranking can be retuned from what wins.

09 Operating it

Preview the next batch dispatch · dry_run ✓

Run “Post backlog to Facebook” with a blank files box and Dry run ticked — logs exactly what would post, calls nothing.

Post one batch by hand dispatch · dry_run ✗

Same, unticked — posts the next ranked batch and advances the manifest. Good for a controlled start.

Start the daily drip uncomment schedule

Uncomment the schedule: block in facebook-backlog.yml and push. It runs itself from there.

Pause everything re-comment schedule

Re-comment the schedule and push. In-flight nothing; the manifest holds the place, so it resumes exactly where it stopped.

Post a specific article dispatch · files

Put its knowledge/…/slug.md path in the files box — bypasses the queue for that article.

Remove a post / refresh a preview delete workflow · FB debugger

Delete by story id via the “Delete Facebook posts” workflow; refresh a stale link card with Facebook’s Sharing Debugger “Scrape Again”.
