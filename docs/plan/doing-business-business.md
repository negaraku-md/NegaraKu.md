## Recon notes

**Who ranks.** Two SERP populations. Formation/structure queries: corporate-services
vendors (3ecpa, mishu, bossboleh, shinewing, grof, altomate, paulhypepage) plus
non-obvious generalists outranking them on head terms — DHL's small-business hub,
Curlec, Wise, Airwallex. Legal-procedure queries (winding-up, arbitration, IP,
corridors) flip to law firms and Big Four: MahWengKwai, Azmi & Associates, Richard
Wee, Baker McKenzie, PwC, EY, Crowe, plus Mondaq/Lexology syndication. Official
sources rank but are PDF-heavy with no task framing. **That gap between
authoritative-but-unusable and usable-but-commercial is our position.**

**Format that wins.** Numbered procedural guides for "how to"; comparison tables for
"X vs Y"; long structured guides with cost + timeline for foreign-investor queries.
Nearly every top result carries an explicit cost figure and timeline in the first
screen — match it or lose.

**Year-stamping.** Heavy and now table stakes. Recommend stamping only where figures
move annually, keeping evergreen procedural pages unstamped.

**Corridors hypothesis — partly wrong.** PwC, EY and MIDA hold JS-SEZ and Forest City
well; differentiation there is disambiguation and honest clawback coverage, not raw
information. The genuinely weak corridors are ECER, NCER and Sarawak/SCORE.
**The most under-served high-intent clusters are licences & permits (#22) and BNM
foreign exchange policy (#37)** — uniformly shallow incumbents, larger volume.

**Differentiation.** (1) Regulatory architecture, not lists. (2) East Malaysia.
(3) BNM FEP for SMEs. (4) Post-incorporation life — incumbents are lead-gen funnels
that stop at the certificate. (5) The uncomfortable truths: nominee directors carry
real liability, s.28 voids most non-competes, MM2H does not permit working.
(6) Corridor disambiguation.

**Existing articles.** `close-a-company` must be **split** into a hub over #62–65.
`register-sdn-bhd` needs an explicit handoff into #1. `sole-prop-vs-sdn-bhd` and
`what-is-sdn-bhd` feed the #9 comparison table.
