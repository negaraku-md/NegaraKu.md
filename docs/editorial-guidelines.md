# Editorial Guidelines

negaraku.md aims to be a **single source of truth** about Malaysia: accurate, sourced, neutral,
and respectful of the country's diversity.

## Core rules

1. **Verifiability.** Every non-obvious claim needs a citation. Articles at `reviewed` or
   `verified` status must list sources. Prefer primary sources, official statistics
   (e.g. [data.gov.my](https://data.gov.my), DOSM), and reputable references.
2. **Neutral point of view.** Present facts and, where relevant, multiple perspectives. Avoid
   promotional, partisan, or emotive language.
3. **Curate, don't accumulate.** A concise, well-structured narrative beats an undigested list.

## Sensitive topics

Malaysia has topics that are legally and socially sensitive. Take extra care with:

- **Race and ethnic relations** — including the social contract, Bumiputera policy, and
  affirmative action.
- **Religion** — especially Islam, which has constitutional status; and interfaith matters.
- **The monarchy** — the Rulers and the Yang di-Pertuan Agong.
- **Contested history and politics** — 13 May 1969, MA63, elections, ongoing disputes.

For these, an article **must**:

- stay strictly sourced and descriptive — attribute claims, don't assert contested opinions as
  fact;
- avoid content that could be defamatory or that incites hostility between communities;
- be held at `draft` until a maintainer review moves it forward.

When in doubt, describe the range of documented views with citations rather than adopting one.

## Style

- Language: author in Bahasa Malaysia first; translations follow.
- Use Malaysian spellings and proper nouns (e.g. *Kuala Lumpur*, *Sarawak*, *Yang di-Pertuan
  Agong*).
- Structure with `##` sections; keep paragraphs short.

## Corrections

Errors are fixed openly. Corrections are made via pull request so the change history remains
public and traceable.
